TITLE
Fresh pre-incision liver state versus post-TACE history and elapsed time: a nested prognostic crossover for postoperative bilirubin and INR after recorded HCC resection

CLINICAL QUESTION, IMPORTANCE, AND SUBSTANTIVE ADVANCE

For an adult with recorded HCC who has undergone TACE and then a recorded liver resection, is the most recent pre-incision bilirubin/INR state sufficient for prognosing absolute postoperative hepatic laboratory recovery, or do the acute biochemical perturbation after the last TACE and elapsed time since that TACE provide stable information beyond the fresh state?

This crossover resolves the complementary uncertainty in the two parent proposals. The acute-injury line showed that bilirubin/INR perturbation soon after TACE is densely observed and that a last-TACE clock is biologically more defensible when repeat TACE is common. The measured-state line showed that latest preoperative bilirubin/INR is much fresher than a fixed landmark value and avoids using the predictor algebraically inside the outcome. Neither established whether historical TACE injury or calendar time still contributes once the patient’s latest pre-incision state is known. The present experiment makes that question a set of identical-sample, nested prognostic comparisons rather than a scheduling contrast.

The clinically consequential advance is to distinguish three potentially interchangeable representations of recovery: (1) elapsed time since the most recent TACE, (2) the current measured state immediately before incision, and (3) the patient’s trajectory/history from before TACE through acute post-TACE perturbation to operation. If current state dominates, prospective pre-incision testing may be more useful than a calendar rule. If history or elapsed time adds reproducible information, a single fresh panel is insufficient and future studies should collect serial measurements and treatment chronology. Neither result alone establishes that changing the operation date changes outcome.

CLAIM LADDER

Strongest claim already supported by available evidence: bilirubin and INR are routinely recorded, temporally ordered measures relevant to hepatic reserve and postoperative liver dysfunction; the HCC snapshot contains enough dated laboratory and procedure data to construct pre-TACE, acute post-TACE, pre-incision, and postoperative day-5-to-7 windows. A postoperative day-5 biochemical signal is related to the laboratory component of post-hepatectomy liver failure, but clinical ISGLS grade requires management and other clinical evidence not reliably available here.

Untested primary claim: in selected patients with a recorded resection after TACE, a fresh pre-incision bilirubin/INR state improves prediction of absolute postoperative day-5-to-7 bilirubin and INR beyond elapsed time and prespecified case mix; after that fresh state is included, pre-TACE residual history and acute post-TACE perturbation either do or do not add stable, clinically meaningful information when evaluated on the identical patients. Elapsed time is tested in both directions: whether it adds beyond fresh state, and whether its apparent association attenuates after fresh state is added.

This is a prognostic and associational experiment. It does not test a treatment effect, a safe delay, an “operate now” threshold, readiness, resectability, or benefit for patients who were not resected. It cannot infer that acute injury caused postoperative dysfunction, that waiting caused recovery, or that a model should schedule surgery.

FIXED POPULATION AND TWO CLOCKS

Unit is one patient and one index operation. Construct a non-outcome-dependent eligibility ledger beginning with the earliest qualifying recorded TACE. The primary selected-resection cohort is the first qualifying recorded liver resection occurring more than 28 and no more than 180 exact elapsed 24-hour days after the earliest qualifying TACE, with a qualifying TACE strictly before the operation. The >28-day boundary is a cohort boundary, not a clinical safety threshold; a >14-to-180-day sensitivity reproduces the parent measured-state feasibility, but it is not allowed to replace the primary landmark-aligned analysis without recording the difference.

At the earliest-TACE day-28 landmark, include age >=18 in the index encounter, an explicit HCC diagnosis documented through a dated encounter no later than t0, a valid earliest TACE start time, and no qualifying liver resection or transplant through t0. Exclude impossible timestamps, intraoperative TACE, donor/transplant procedures, and operations through t0. Patients without a later recorded resection remain visible in the ledger through day 180, classified descriptively as no recorded resection, repeat TACE/other treatment, and presence or absence of a later structured institutional event. No-resection is not interpreted as waiting, survival, treatment success, or continued resectability because outside care, death, intent, and potential resectability are incompletely captured.

For the selected-resection prognostic comparisons, define O as the start time of the first qualifying resection and T as the start time of the last qualifying TACE strictly before O. Use exact elapsed days (O-T)/24 hours. This last-TACE clock is primary because each intervening TACE can reset acute biological perturbation. The earliest-TACE interval and a single-recorded-TACE restriction are sensitivities. No laboratory after an intervening TACE may be attributed to an earlier TACE. The day-28 ledger and last-TACE operation clock answer different questions and must not be conflated.

A resection with no postoperative day-5-to-7 outcome is not assigned an outcome. Missingness and institutional observation are reported rather than treated as clinical normality. All models requiring historical anchors use complete identical samples; postoperative outcomes are never imputed.

OUTCOMES: ABSOLUTE AND NOT MATHEMATICALLY COUPLED

The co-primary continuous outcomes are, for the same operation, the maximum numeric total bilirubin and maximum INR observed in the postoperative window [O+5 days, O+8 days), left-closed/right-open. Require at least one valid result for each marker in this window for the paired primary analysis; record the number and timing of measurements. The outcomes are absolute values, not postoperative/preoperative ratios or changes. Primary scoring uses log bilirubin and log INR because the distributions are positive and right-skewed, but results must also be reported in original recorded units after assay validation. The primary analysis is a pair of separate outcome models, with a prespecified joint report requiring directionally coherent findings. A gain for bilirubin alone is marker-specific and cannot be called general hepatic recovery.

The parent relative composite (postoperative bilirubin >=1.5 times latest pre-incision bilirubin AND postoperative INR >= latest pre-incision INR +0.20) is retained only as a coupled sensitivity. It is prohibited from being the evidence for incremental value of fresh bilirubin/INR because its definition embeds those predictors. A unit/reference-range-gated absolute binary signal (postoperative bilirubin >=2x validated assay ULN AND INR >=1.20 in [O+5,O+8)) may be exploratory; if ULN and assay mapping cannot be independently recovered, do not substitute a percentile and do not call this binary endpoint validated.

PREDICTOR WINDOWS AND REPRESENTATIONS

All predictors precede O and all transformations, winsorization, scaling, feature selection, and missingness models are fit inside training folds without postoperative outcomes.

Fresh current state F uses the latest numeric bilirubin and latest numeric INR in [O-7 days,O), each with exact recency, assay, specimen, and encounter retained. The primary seven-day window is feasible and represents a measured state, not necessarily a simultaneous panel. A <=24-hour paired sensitivity is required. Freshness sensitivities use [O-5,O) and [O-3,O), never carry a value older than the stated window, and report marker-specific recency. Albumin, creatinine, platelets, ALT, hemoglobin, and measurement intensity are secondary additions only after assay validation.

Pre-TACE anchors use the latest numeric bilirubin and INR in [T-7 days,T). If a marker has multiple observations, choose the latest before T, not a future value. Acute perturbation uses the maximum bilirubin and maximum INR in [T,T+7 days), excluding the right boundary. The primary acute-history features are two separate within-person quantities: log(max acute bilirubin / pre-TACE bilirubin) and max acute INR - pre-TACE INR. Require positive finite bilirubin anchors and retain the component values and recencies. Robust scaling is learned without outcomes within each training fold; do not collapse the components into an outcome-trained “injury” threshold. Quartiles are descriptive support displays only. A sensitivity uses acute maxima minus the pre-TACE anchor on the original assay scale after unit/assay validation.

Elapsed time is the exact continuous last-TACE-to-operation interval, modeled with a restricted cubic spline using training-fold empirical 10th/35th/65th/90th percentiles. Display 55/60/65-day bands only as stability diagnostics, never as the primary exposure or a safe cutoff.

IDENTICAL-SAMPLE NESTED COMPARISONS

Freeze the eligible selected-resection cohort once, then define comparison subsets by availability. Every pair below is re-estimated on exactly the same patients for both models in that pair; differing sample sizes may not be interpreted as incremental value.

- B (case-mix baseline): age, sex, calendar era, service, admission-to-operation interval, prior TACE count/spacing and other preoperative treatment/utilization intensity, validated preoperative comorbidity flags, and preoperative tumor features. Include validated operation extent/approach in a separate peri-incision specification; the primary pre-incision specification omits it, and both are reported because extent is chosen after the waiting interval and can channel prognosis.
- T = B + exact last-TACE-to-operation time spline.
- C = B + fresh current bilirubin and INR (and, in a prespecified expanded version, validated albumin/creatinine/platelets/ALT/hemoglobin). C has no interval feature.
- F = B + time spline + fresh current bilirubin and INR. T versus F asks whether fresh state adds beyond elapsed time. C versus F asks whether elapsed time adds beyond fresh state.
- H = F + pre-TACE anchor levels and the two separate residual-history terms (current minus/pre-TACE, with functional-form sensitivity using raw paired anchors). H versus F is the primary test of whether historical state adds beyond fresh state and elapsed time.
- A = H + acute post-TACE perturbation components. A versus H is secondary because acute history reduces the sample and may be redundant with residual history. Re-estimate H on the A-complete subset before comparing A to H.

Do not use postoperative labs, postoperative documents, length of stay, complications, pathology, postoperative treatment, or outcome-derived feature scaling in B through A. For prediction at the operation, actual operation extent is a prespecified case-mix sensitivity only; including it does not make any timing or state coefficient causal.

PRIMARY ESTIMAND AND VALIDATION

The primary estimand is optimism-corrected out-of-sample incremental prognosis for each absolute continuous outcome. Use repeated patient-level stratified 10-fold cross-validation or >=500 patient-level bootstrap optimism corrections, with all preprocessing, spline knots, imputation of ordinary baseline covariates, penalization, and feature transformations learned inside training partitions. Historical anchors and postoperative outcomes are never imputed. Because patients may have multiple encounters but only one index operation, resampling is by patient.

For bilirubin and INR separately, report original-scale MAE and RMSE after back-transformation, log-scale RMSE, cross-validated R-squared, calibration slope/intercept for the log outcome, and prediction-interval coverage. The primary incremental quantities are paired differences in cross-validated log RMSE, standardized RMSE, and R-squared: T->F (fresh state beyond time), C->F (time beyond fresh state), F->H (pre-TACE residual history beyond fresh state and time), and H->A (acute perturbation beyond residual history). Report paired bootstrap confidence intervals and distributions of fold-level differences, not only a fitted coefficient or in-sample likelihood ratio.

Set the prespecified stable-information threshold at >=0.05 SD reduction in cross-validated RMSE or >=0.02 increase in cross-validated R-squared for each of the two co-primary outcomes, with the paired 95% CI excluding zero, calibration slope remaining 0.80–1.20 and calibration intercept near zero, and no material degradation in the other outcome. These are model-information thresholds, not bilirubin/INR clinical cutoffs. If laboratory units are validated, additionally report clinically interpretable original-unit errors after a clinician supplies a meaningful threshold; do not invent one from this file.

Use ridge regression for continuous outcomes and low-degree penalized splines, with effective degrees of freedom capped before fitting based on the complete-sample size. No high-dimensional interactions are permitted. A secondary interaction model tests whether time’s prognostic association differs continuously by acute perturbation, but it cannot displace the nested information comparison and cannot be described as a treatment effect.

FEASIBILITY AND ACTUAL FILTERING EVIDENCE

The bounded parent scans read 338,040 raw procedure rows, retained 319,095 valid exact-deduplicated procedure rows in one reconstruction, read 105,044 encounter rows, 1,810,646 diagnosis rows, and all 28,159,928 laboratory rows. One strict day-28 landmark reconstruction found 12,731 adult patients with HCC documented by day 28 and no recorded resection through that landmark; 237, 203, and 307 had recorded resections in >28–60, >60–90, and >90–180 days, respectively, while 11,984 had no recorded resection through day 180. Only 3,076 of the latter had another structured event after day 28, demonstrating why the nonresection ledger cannot be interpreted as continued waiting or survival.

A separate deterministic selected-resection reconstruction using the last preoperative TACE found 825 adult HCC operations in >28–180 days, distributed 380/223/222 across >28–60, >60–90, and >90–180 days. The broader >14–180 scan found 846 operations, including 21 in >14–28 days. In the parent-compatible >28–180 cohort, 578 had paired current [O-7,O) and postoperative [O+5,O+8) bilirubin/INR values; 503 also had pre-TACE anchors, and 423 also had the acute post-TACE anchor. The acute extension had 61 events for the coupled relative endpoint, but the principal outcomes here are continuous and do not depend on that event count. Stricter current windows yielded 539 complete outcome pairs at five days and 391 at three days. Median current-lab recency was approximately 2.0 days for bilirubin and 2.1 days for INR; the 90th percentiles were approximately 4.6 days for both.

The two support reconstructions differ by one or more cases because their deterministic implementations differ in age/encounter linkage, intraoperative-TACE exclusion, exact duplicate collapse, and landmark definition. These are feasibility bounds, not interchangeable results. Compilation must run one frozen implementation against the exact rules above, emit a row-level exclusion ledger without identifiers in the report, and reconcile the difference record by record. The expected complete samples are approximately 578 for T->F/C->F, 503 for F->H, and 423 for H->A, but the executed counts supersede these support counts. Acute score cells are not assumed adequate merely because the total is adequate; report timing-by-history support and effective sample size.

GATES AND CONTROLS

1. Temporal/leakage gate. Unit-test strict T<O, [T-7,T), [T,T+7), [O-7,O), and [O+5,O+8) boundaries with synthetic observations exactly at each endpoint. Verify that no feature after O enters a predictor and no outcome-derived quantity enters transformations. Any violation is computational failure.

2. Identical-sample gate. For each nested pair, verify identical patient IDs, identical outcome rows, and identical fold assignments. A larger sample for a simpler model may be used descriptively but cannot be claimed as incremental validation. Refit the simpler model on the richer model’s exact complete subset.

3. Observation gate. Tabulate current-lab, historical-anchor, acute-anchor, and postoperative outcome availability by time decile, calendar year, service, admission status, assay label, measurement intensity, and case mix. Model observation probability using only pre-outcome variables and use inverse-probability weighting as a sensitivity. Do not impute postoperative outcomes. Stop interpretation if weighted ESS is <100 in either half of time support, >10% of total weight is carried by <=10 patients, or anchor availability differs by >15 percentage points after baseline adjustment.

4. Freshness/support gate. The primary T->F and C->F comparisons require >=400 complete paired patients, no empty decile of exact interval crossed with fresh-state quartile, and replication in the [O-5,O) window. F->H requires >=400 complete patients; H->A requires >=350. If these fail, the corresponding comparison is descriptive/inconclusive even if a p-value is small. A failure only in the five-day sensitivity means the result is not robust enough to call the state “fresh at operation.”

5. Laboratory/assay gate. A laboratory director or qualified laboratory expert must verify bilirubin and INR scales, routine versus 急 comparability, specimen handling, and calendar-era continuity, and inspect same-time routine/急 agreement and discontinuities. If units cannot be verified, retain continuous results as institution/assay-specific prognostic measurements, suppress absolute clinical thresholds and ULN binary interpretation, and label generalization unresolved. Run routine-only and 2018–2025 sensitivities.

6. Case-mix gate. Two blinded reviewers validate at least 200 stratified preoperative imaging reports and at least 200 procedure names for tumor diameter, multiplicity, vascular invasion/thrombus, extrahepatic disease, rupture, response, major/minor extent, and approach. Automated features require sensitivity and PPV >=0.90; otherwise fully adjudicate or declare residual tumor/procedure case-mix confounding unresolved. Report primary preoperative-only and peri-incision extent-adjusted specifications.

7. Intent/resectability gate. A hepatobiliary surgeon and interventional radiologist, blinded to postoperative labs, classify TACE and resection as planned preoperative, bridge/conversion/downstaging, definitive/palliative, emergency rupture/hemostatic, non-TACE/false match, or indeterminate, and classify potential resectability at the day-28 landmark where documentation permits. If kappa <0.70 or >30% remains indeterminate, no readiness, resectability, or scheduling interpretation is allowed. Report planned/conversion, emergency, and indeterminate strata; do not silently exclude them.

8. Negative-control and falsification controls.
   - Preoperative pseudo-outcome: predict the late pre-incision bilirubin/INR values in [O-3,O) from earlier [O-7,O-3) measurements, time, and history only. Comparable or larger gains from H/A than for the postoperative outcomes indicate monitoring/channeling or serial measurement structure, not postoperative vulnerability, and downgrade the interpretation.
   - Hemoglobin negative-control outcome: postoperative day-1-to-3 hemoglobin nadir/change, adjusted for preoperative hemoglobin and validated operation extent. A history/time gain >=0.05 SD RMSE or >=0.02 R-squared in the same pattern flags generic operative complexity or illness selection.
   - Negative-control exposure: TACE day of week, adjusted for calendar year/service. Omnibus p<0.01 or standardized effect >=0.20 on either hepatic outcome flags scheduling/service structure.
   - Pseudo-anchor: replace the true pre-TACE anchor with a prespecified pre-TACE pseudo-anchor from [T-30,T-14), when available. Similar or greater incremental value than the true anchor falsifies a specific post-TACE recovery interpretation.
   - Coupling audit: confirm that all primary outcomes are absolute postoperative maxima and contain no current-state value algebraically. If current-state gains occur only for the relative composite, the fresh-state claim is falsified.
   - Clock audit: compare last-TACE with earliest-TACE intervals and restrict to one recorded TACE. A result only under earliest TACE, despite repeat TACE, is adverse to the intended biological-history interpretation.
   - Threshold/calendar audit: 55/60/65-day splits are displays only. A finding isolated to one arbitrary cut is unstable and cannot support the crossover claim.

PRESPECIFIED INTERPRETATION BRANCHES

Supportive for fresh-state sufficiency: on the same validated complete cohort, F materially improves both absolute postoperative outcomes over T, C->F shows that time adds no clinically material information beyond fresh state, and F->H and H->A fail the stable-information threshold with confidence intervals compatible with a precise small increment. Freshness, assay, observation, case-mix, intent, pseudo-outcome, hemoglobin, day-of-week, pseudo-anchor, and clock checks pass. This supports using a fresh bilirubin/INR panel as the dominant prognostic representation among observed selected resections, while not proving readiness or a safe operation date.

Supportive for additional historical information: F improves over T, and H improves over F on the identical 503-person subset for both absolute outcomes, persists in [O-5,O) and routine-only analyses, preserves calibration, and controls pass. This supports collecting a pre-TACE reference/trajectory in prospective prognostic validation. It does not establish that TACE injury caused the outcome or that delaying surgery would help.

Supportive for acute perturbation beyond current state and residual history: A improves over H on the identical approximately 423-person subset for both outcomes, meets the stable-information threshold, replicates under the five-day freshness window, and is not reproduced by the pseudo-anchor, pseudo-outcome, hemoglobin, or day-of-week controls. This is the strongest evidence that acute post-TACE perturbation adds stable prognostic information beyond fresh state and elapsed time. If A fails while H succeeds, prefer the simpler pre-TACE residual representation.

Supportive for elapsed time beyond fresh state: C->F meets the stable-information threshold after adding the continuous last-TACE interval, with no material deterioration in calibration and controls passing. This means calendar history still carries prognostic information not captured by the latest measured bilirubin/INR. It remains an association in selected operations, not evidence that a delay is beneficial or actionable.

Adverse/opposite: fresh-state information does not improve either absolute outcome; history/time gains disappear after validated case-mix adjustment; gains occur for only one discordant marker; or time/history improves a generic hemoglobin or preoperative pseudo-outcome equally or more. This rejects the simple liver-recovery representation and points to assay, monitoring, operative selection, tumor case mix, or channeling. No scheduling recommendation follows.

Precise null: paired confidence intervals for the relevant nested increment are wholly within the prespecified no-material-information band (standardized RMSE improvement <0.05 SD and R-squared increment <0.02 for both outcomes), calibration is adequate, controls pass, and the five-day sensitivity agrees. A precise null for H versus F supports that pre-TACE residual history is unnecessary once fresh state and time are known in observed complete cases; a precise null for A versus H supports not paying its attrition cost. It does not prove biological equivalence, causal irrelevance, or generalization to unresected patients.

Inconclusive/falsified: any sample/ESS/freshness/assay/intent/case-mix gate fails; fewer than the required complete patients; poor calibration; confidence intervals span clinically material benefit and harm; component outcomes disagree; results depend on one clock, one assay label, or one arbitrary day cut; missingness differs materially; or a negative control reproduces the finding. Report the ledger and feasible descriptive distributions but no readiness, timing, or scheduling claim. If unit mapping fails, suppress ULN-based conclusions and retain only assay-specific continuous prediction.

CLINICAL LIMITS AND REQUIRED ADJUDICATION

The source does not reliably contain outside TACE/resection/death, complete mortality after loss to care, ECOG/BCLC status, longitudinal potential resectability, treatment intent, standardized Child-Pugh/ALBI with verified units, ICG clearance, portal pressure, future liver remnant, raw images/mRECIST, embolization selectivity, scheduling rationale, anesthesia/blood loss/transfusion detail, or timestamped management sufficient for ISGLS grade. Selected resection and observed postoperative testing condition on care pathways and can induce selection. The available documents and orders can support blinded review but cannot reconstruct absent facts. A prospective multicenter study with scheduled pre-TACE, acute, and pre-incision labs plus adjudicated PHLF, intent, resectability, operation extent, and outside outcomes is required before any decision rule or causal timing claim.
