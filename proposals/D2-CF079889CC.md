# LPS Priming Abolishes the Protective FLS-Suppressive Effect of MerTK+/CD206+ Macrophages in Rheumatoid Arthritis

## Question

Does inflammatory priming (LPS) of protective (MerTK+/CD206+ "Mpos") macrophages abolish their ability to suppress the FLS inflammatory program in rheumatoid arthritis?

## Why This Matters

Episodes 1 and 2 established that MerTK activity on Mpos macrophages mediates FLS suppression (ep1) and that this protective effect is dose-dependent on Mpos fraction (ep2). However, both findings were obtained under resting (non-primed) conditions. In active RA, the synovial microenvironment is rich in DAMPs and PAMPs, including LPS from gut translocation and bacterial biofilms.

[K3] Wang et al. (2026) established that MerTK+CD206+ macrophages are essential for efferocytosis and inflammation resolution in RA, are functionally impaired in disease, and that their enrichment predicts low relapse risk after biologic-free discontinuation. [K1] Wei & Xu (2026) proposed that difficult-to-treat RA represents a "failed-resolution switch" where pro-resolving macrophage subsets are depleted or dysfunctional, and reciprocal stromal-myeloid signaling locks the joint in chronic inflammation. [K2] Yi et al. (2026) reviewed how M1/M2 polarization imbalance drives RA progression, with M1 macrophages promoting inflammation via TNF-α, IL-1β, IL-6.

If LPS priming converts protective macrophages to a pro-inflammatory state that overrides their FLS-suppressive function, this would:
1. Explain why MerTK-targeted therapies may fail in active disease (the protective signal is overridden by inflammation).
2. Define a boundary condition for the protective effect: it requires a non-primed macrophage state.
3. Identify a therapeutic window: combining MerTK agonism with anti-inflammatory priming (e.g., TLR4 blockade) may be necessary for efficacy.

## Evidence from Prior Episodes

- **Ep1 (MERTK-dependence):** Mpos + MerTK inhibitor → FLS transcriptome converges toward Mneg state. MERTK activity is necessary for FLS suppression.
- **Ep2 (Dose-dependence):** 3:1 Mpos:Mneg > 1:3 Mpos:Mneg > Mneg for FLS suppression, with non-monotonic gradient (100% Mpos not always lowest). The protective mechanism has finite capacity.

## Hypothesis

**H1 (primary):** LPS priming of protective (MerTK+/CD206+) macrophages abolishes their FLS-suppressive effect by converting them to a pro-inflammatory state that overrides the MerTK-mediated protective signal. [K3]

**H2 (mechanistic):** The abolition is mediated by TLR4/NF-κB activation in macrophages, which shifts the secretome from anti-inflammatory (MerTK-dependent) to pro-inflammatory (TNF/IL-1/CXCL), overriding the protective signal at the FLS level. [K2]

**H3 (boundary):** The protective effect requires a non-primed macrophage state; under inflammatory conditions, the MerTK pathway is insufficient to maintain FLS suppression. [K1]

## Falsifiable Test

The solver will perform donor-aware permutation tests on the 23-gene FLS inflammatory panel:

1. **Primary test:** Mpos_preLPS vs Mpos (SA256A, within-donor). If LFC distribution is centered at 0 (no shift), H1 is falsified.
2. **Distinction test:** Mpos_preLPS vs Mneg_preLPS (SA256A). If the Mpos/Mneg distinction persists (significant negative LFC), H1 is falsified.
3. **Cross-donor validation:** Mneg_preLPS vs Mneg across SA258R, SA263R, SA266A. Consistent direction supports generalizability.
4. **Gene-level test:** Individual panel genes (CXCL10, IL1B, CD163) should show consistent direction across contrasts.

**Falsification criteria:**
- If contrast (a) shows no significant shift (|mean LFC| < 0.25 with permutation p > 0.05), the protective effect is LPS-resistant.
- If contrast (c) shows a significant negative LFC (Mpos still suppresses after LPS), the distinction persists.

## Competing Explanations

1. **TLR4/NF-κB override (H2):** LPS activates TLR4 → NF-κB → pro-inflammatory cytokines in macrophages, overriding MerTK signal. Prediction: TNF, IL1B, CXCL genes up; CD163, TGFB1 down. [K2]
2. **Macrophage death/loss:** LPS may kill Mpos macrophages, reducing their number below the protective threshold. Prediction: effect size proportional to surviving Mpos fraction; not testable with transcriptomics alone.
3. **FLS intrinsic LPS response:** LPS may directly activate FLS (if residual LPS remains in co-culture). Prediction: Mneg_preLPS vs Mneg should show similar magnitude to Mpos_preLPS vs Mpos.
4. **Donor-specific FLS baseline:** The inconsistent cross-donor Mneg results suggest FLS baseline activation state modulates the LPS response. Prediction: donors with higher baseline FLS activation show smaller LPS effect (ceiling effect).

## Method Comparison

**Baseline (descriptive):** Log2 fold changes on the 23-gene panel, as computed in the discovery probe. This identifies direction and magnitude but not significance.

**Learned alternative (donor-aware permutation test):** Permute sample labels within donor to generate null distribution of LFC. This accounts for donor-specific effects and provides p-values. The permutation test reveals whether the observed LFC is extreme relative to the donor's own variability, which the descriptive baseline cannot.

**What the alternative reveals:** The permutation test distinguishes true LPS effects from donor-specific FLS baseline variation. For example, the SA263R Mneg_preLPS result (−1.152) may reflect a high-baseline FLS donor where LPS has a paradoxical downregulatory effect, rather than a true anti-inflammatory response.

## Outcomes

- **Supportive:** Contrast (a) shows significant positive LFC (LPS increases inflammation in Mpos-conditioned FLS) AND contrast (c) shows no significant Mpos/Mneg distinction. This supports H1: LPS abolishes the protective effect.
- **Adverse:** Contrast (a) shows no significant shift AND contrast (c) shows significant negative LFC (Mpos still suppresses after LPS). This falsifies H1: the protective effect is LPS-resistant.
- **Inconclusive:** Mixed results across donors, or significant effects only in a subset of panel genes. This suggests the protective effect is partially LPS-resistant, with gene-specific sensitivity.

## Claim Scope

This is a **hypothesis-stage** claim based on:
- Single-donor (SA256A) for the critical Mpos contrast
- No technical replicates
- 23-gene panel selected in ep1/ep2 (selection bias)
- log2(count+1) normalization (crude for pseudobulk)
- No direct measurement of macrophage state (only FLS readout)

The claim is bounded to: "In the E-MTAB-8873 co-culture system, LPS priming of Mpos macrophages is associated with loss of FLS suppression, as measured by a 23-gene inflammatory panel." It does not establish:
- Causal mechanism (TLR4 vs other pathways)
- Generalizability to human RA synovium
- Therapeutic implications for MerTK agonists
- Dose-response of LPS priming
