# Hypothesis Proposal: Multi-Organ Recovery Discordance Predicts ICU Deterioration

## 1. Scientific Question

**Primary Question:** In critically ill patients, does the presence of *discordant multi-organ recovery trajectories*—defined as the coexistence of improving function in one organ system while function remains impaired or deteriorating in another system—after initial hemodynamic stabilization, independently predict subsequent clinical deterioration (ICU readmission, transfer to higher level of care, or death) beyond traditional severity scores and single-organ recovery metrics?

**Refined Operational Question:** After a patient achieves circulatory stabilization (sustained MAP ≥65 mmHg for ≥4 consecutive hours following a period of instability), does a lagging recovery trajectory in non-circulatory organs (renal, respiratory, hepatic) compared to the circulatory system predict subsequent deterioration with higher hazard ratios than baseline severity scores alone?

## 2. Clinical Importance and Substantive Advance

### Clinical Importance
- **Early Warning**: Discordant organ recovery may serve as an early warning signal for impending deterioration before traditional markers
- **Risk Stratification**: Could identify high-risk patients who appear stable on single-organ metrics
- **Resource Allocation**: May guide escalation of care or transfer decisions
- **Treatment Optimization**: Could inform timing of interventions

### Substantive Advance Over Existing Knowledge

**Existing Knowledge:**
- Traditional severity scores (SOFA, APACHE II) aggregate multiple organ systems but provide only a snapshot
- Single-organ recovery trajectories have been studied but rarely in concert with other systems
- Foy et al. 2022 [K1] demonstrates that *co-regulatory dynamics* of WBC-PLT predict adverse outcomes

**What This Study Adds:**
1. **Multi-system perspective**: Examines recovery across multiple organ systems simultaneously
2. **Discordance as a signal**: Tests whether *discordance* between systems is more predictive than lagging alone
3. **Temporal dynamics**: Uses longitudinal recovery trajectories rather than single timepoint assessments
4. **Clinical outcomes**: Directly tests prediction of ICU deterioration, readmission, and death

## 4. Baseline vs Alternative Approaches

### Baseline Approach
**Traditional Severity Score + Single-Organ Metrics**
- SOFA score at admission or stabilization
- Single-organ trajectory (e.g., creatinine change alone)
- Time to stabilization
- Initial severity (APACHE II)

### Alternative Approach
**Multi-Organ Recovery Discordance Score**
- Define recovery trajectory for each organ system
- Calculate "discordance index": degree to which one system's recovery diverges from others
- Test if discordance predicts outcomes better than any single metric

## 5. Falsification Criteria

The hypothesis will be **falsified** if:
1. Discordant recovery does NOT predict outcomes after adjusting for baseline severity and single-organ metrics (p > 0.05)
2. The hazard ratio for discordance is not significantly different from 1 (95% CI includes 1)
3. Discordance adds no predictive value beyond baseline scores (NRI < 5%, IDI < 1%)

## 6. Supportive, Adverse, and Inconclusive Results

### Supportive Results
- Discordant recovery associated with 2-5x higher hazard of deterioration after adjustment
- Discordance persists after controlling for baseline severity and single-organ metrics
- Discordance in specific organ pairs shows stronger association than others

### Adverse Results
- Discordance not associated with outcomes after adjustment
- Discordance is an artifact of measurement frequency
- Traditional scores remain superior predictors

### Inconclusive Results
- Discordance shows modest association but mechanism unclear
- Discordance predicts outcomes only in subgroups
- Requires larger sample size or different outcome definition
