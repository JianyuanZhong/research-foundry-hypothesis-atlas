# Episode 14 working proposal: discordant AKI recovery with an executable estimand

Status: provisional successor to [prior hypothesis]. No cohort counts or effect estimates are claimed.

## Proposed relationship and unresolved claim

Among adults in an ICU with a prespecified creatinine-defined AKI episode who remain observed at 48 hours after the AKI anchor, persistent creatinine improvement without concurrent urine-output improvement will have higher subsequent renal deterioration or in-hospital mortality than concordant recovery, after adjustment for the same 48-hour severity, treatment state, and observation intensity. The estimand is incremental prognostic information at a fixed landmark, not a causal treatment effect, mechanism, or proof of true renal recovery.

The strongest supported evidence is only that creatinine-based kidney-function assessment can be misleading during critical illness and that creatinine and urine output can be operationally discordant. The unresolved claim is whether the discordant trajectory adds reproducible information beyond current severity and measurement intensity. The leading rival is that apparent discordance is generated by illness severity, selective urine measurement, diuretic/RRT exposure, or clinician-driven treatment.

Define adults from `hosp/patients.anchor_age >= 18`, select the earliest eligible ICU stay, and use `icu/icustays` (`subject_id`, `hadm_id`, `stay_id`, `intime`, `outtime`) joined to `hosp/admissions` (`subject_id`, `hadm_id`, `admittime`, `dischtime`, `deathtime`, `hospital_expire_flag`) and `hosp/patients` by `subject_id`.

Use `hosp/labevents` serum creatinine item 50912, joined to `hosp/d_labitems` by `itemid`, with timestamps and values available before the landmark. Define AKI onset from a frozen KDIGO-compatible rule using the first qualifying creatinine rise and require at least one pre-rise and one post-rise creatinine measurement; freeze the threshold, minimum coverage, and missingness rules before fitting. The primary landmark is 48 hours after that anchor, with survival and continued observation required. Every predictor must be timestamped at or before the landmark.

Use `icu/outputevents` items 226559, 226560, 226561, 226563, 226567, and 226627, joined to `icu/d_items` by `itemid`; exclude 227489 GU irrigant/urine output from the primary definition. Treatment context comes from `icu/inputevents` (`starttime`, `endtime`, `itemid`, `amount`, `amountuom`, `rate`, `rateuom`, `statusdescription`), including norepinephrine 221906, epinephrine 221289, phenylephrine 221749, dopamine 221662, vasopressin 222315, furosemide 221794, bumetanide 229639, NaCl 0.9% 225158, and NaCl 0.45% 225159. RRT is identified in `icu/procedureevents` (`starttime`, `endtime`, `itemid`, `value`, `valueuom`, `statusdescription`) using verified items hemodialysis 225441, CRRT 225802, CVVHD 225803, CVVHDF 225809, and SCUF 225955; peritoneal dialysis 225805 is secondary. CRRT initiation is observed treatment, not an objective indication.

## Baseline, alternative, and falsification

The simple baseline is a landmark logistic/Cox model using current creatinine, creatinine slope, urine-output summary, AKI stage, age/sex, severity, treatment, and measurement intensity. The substantive alternative is a compact pre-landmark trajectory model using creatinine and urine-output sequences (slopes, nadir, persistence, and co-movement), with identical patient splits, held-out temporal evaluation, calibration, discrimination, uncertainty, and measurement-intensity sensitivity analyses. The alternative can reveal whether persistence and ordering contain information lost by a cross-sectional mismatch label; it is deferred if sequence support is inadequate. CPU is adequate for both; GPU is not required unless the trajectory implementation becomes matrix-intensive.

Support requires a prespecified directionally consistent association and reproducible incremental calibration/discrimination in held-out data, with uncertainty and sensitivity analyses showing it is not explained solely by missing urine measurements, diuretic/RRT exposure, or current severity. Adverse results are no incremental signal, reversal, or instability after these adjustments. Inconclusive results include sparse discordant cells, inadequate follow-up, or wide uncertainty. None supports mechanism or treatment benefit.

## Evidence and limitations

[K1] Haines et al., Clin J Am Soc Nephrol 2023, DOI 10.2215/cjn.0000000000000203: abstract-only evidence that creatinine-based kidney-function assessment can be inaccurate in critical illness; it does not establish the proposed association.
[K2] Li et al., Front Med 2025, DOI 10.3389/fmed.2025.1567359: full-text evidence on AKI guideline methodology and implementation gaps; it supports operational definitions but not the association.
[K3] Tang et al., Clin Exp Med 2026, DOI 10.1007/s10238-026-02305-1: full-text evidence that subsequent observed CRRT initiation is clinician-driven and should not be treated as an objective indication or causal endpoint.

The association, eligible cohort size, and measurement density remain untested. Measured GFR/cystatin C, reliable fluid-status adjudication, complete urine measurement, and clinical adjudication of AKI/recovery and RRT indication are unavailable or incomplete. Subject-specific timestamp shifts preclude cross-subject calendar alignment. The next solver must newly fit the baseline and trajectory models, estimate held-out incremental performance and uncertainty, and report supportive, adverse, or inconclusive findings linked to computed outputs.
