# Earlier natural menopause and cardiac structural remodelling beyond measured haemodynamic load

## Scientific question and clinical importance

Among UK Biobank women with self-reported natural menopause, does earlier age at menopause remain associated with left-ventricular myocardial mass and left-atrial structure/function after adjustment for age, body composition, and measured blood-pressure load?

The clinically important unresolved question is whether menopause timing marks a subgroup with earlier subclinical cardiac structural change, or whether the apparent association is explained by residual haemodynamic load and shared metabolic ageing. This matters because menopause timing itself is not modifiable, but if it identifies a subgroup with earlier cardiac remodelling, it could motivate earlier cardiovascular surveillance and risk-factor control.

The leading explanation is that earlier natural menopause is associated with higher cumulative blood pressure and/or adverse body composition, which in turn drives left-ventricular hypertrophy and left-atrial remodelling. The strongest rival is a sex-hormone-related biological transition: loss of oestrogen may independently alter cardiac structure or function. The available UKB data cannot establish a causal or hormonal mechanism, so the hypothesis is framed as an association that survives a prespecified adjustment hierarchy.

## Evidence and closest prior work

The design builds on the UKB seed question and three inspected works. [K1] supports the clinical relevance of menopause timing and cardiovascular risk, but does not establish that the association persists after adjustment for measured haemodynamic load. [K2] supports the use of cardiac MRI-derived structural and functional measures as outcomes, but does not test menopause timing as a predictor. [K3] supports the importance of body composition and blood pressure as competing explanations, but does not resolve whether menopause timing adds information beyond those factors.

## Population and temporal boundaries

The analysis population is UK Biobank participants with self-reported natural menopause, a valid age at menopause, and cardiac MRI-derived left-ventricular and left-atrial measures. Participants with prior cardiovascular disease at baseline, missing cardiac MRI, or implausible menopause timing are excluded. The exposure is age at natural menopause, treated as a continuous variable and summarized per 5-year earlier menopause. The outcome is measured at the cardiac MRI assessment, which is treated as the index observation for structural remodelling.

## Variables and exact UKB bindings

The exposure is age at natural menopause from the menopause field. The outcomes are left-ventricular myocardial mass, left-ventricular ejection fraction, and left-atrial volume/index from the cardiac MRI fields. Covariates include age, sex, ethnicity, smoking, alcohol, physical activity, body mass index, waist circumference, blood pressure, antihypertensive medication use, diabetes, and lipid measures. All fields are bound to the UKB/ukb672073 split tables and the Parquet convenience views described in the dataset catalog.

## Analysis and estimand

The primary estimand is the adjusted association per 5-year earlier natural menopause with left-ventricular myocardial mass and left-atrial volume/index. The analysis uses a prespecified Model 0–4 adjustment hierarchy: Model 0 is unadjusted; Model 1 adds age, sex, and ethnicity; Model 2 adds body composition and lifestyle factors; Model 3 adds blood pressure and antihypertensive medication use; Model 4 adds diabetes and lipid measures. The primary result is the Model 4 coefficient with a 95% confidence interval.

A held-out gradient-boosted model is fitted on the same covariates and outcome to test whether a nonlinear pattern adds information beyond the linear estimate. The model is evaluated by held-out R-squared improvement over the linear Model 4 and by a stable partial-dependence curve for menopause timing. The nonlinear model is a diagnostic, not a causal model.

## Falsification and interpretation

The hypothesis is supported if the Model 4 association is positive, precise, and persists after the full adjustment hierarchy, and if the nonlinear model shows a stable partial-dependence pattern with a held-out R-squared improvement. The hypothesis is adverse if the association is null or materially attenuated after Model 4 adjustment, if the confidence interval is non-informative, or if the nonlinear model fails to improve held-out performance. The hypothesis is inconclusive if the data are too sparse or the confidence interval is too wide to distinguish the competing explanations.

The analysis cannot establish causation or a hormonal mechanism. A positive association would motivate earlier cardiovascular surveillance and risk-factor control in women with earlier menopause, but would not justify a causal claim or a hormonal intervention.
