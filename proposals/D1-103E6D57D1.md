# Provisional HCC hypothesis — trajectory versus care-process artifact (episode 11)

## Proposed relationship
Among adults admitted with an exact documented HCC diagnosis, an ordered 7-day trajectory of liver, coagulation, renal, and tumor-marker laboratory measurements may improve held-out prediction of a subsequent, non-routine inpatient procedure beyond an admission snapshot plus measured treatment and documentation intensity. The primary endpoint is a computable procedure-based escalation proxy, not death, ICU admission, decompensation, or clinical harm.

The clinically important unresolved claim is whether temporal laboratory information contains information about later care escalation that is not already encoded by admission severity, treatment indication, routine HCC procedures, or documentation intensity. A positive result would motivate expert review of temporal monitoring; it would not establish a biological mechanism or a treatment effect.

## Grounding and strongest supported claim
The strongest claim currently supported is only that the frozen HCC snapshot contains joinable encounter, diagnosis, laboratory, and procedure tables with timed procedure records sufficient for a bounded endpoint audit. The prior source audit reported 105,044 encounter rows, 101,457 valid admission times, 104,554 exact HCC patient/admission keys, 338,040 procedure rows, 321,207 timed HCC procedure rows, 15,774 untimed HCC procedure rows, and 20,052 HCC procedure rows after day 7 and before discharge (6,779 keys). These are availability/support counts, not evidence that the endpoint is clinically valid or that any model performs well.

The unresolved claim is whether an ordered within-assay trajectory predicts a prespecified non-routine post-window procedure better than a snapshot after accounting for care-process variables. The procedure endpoint must be frozen before fitting; no model result is claimed here.

## Falsifiable experiment
Cohort: exact HCC diagnosis, nonempty admission time, and one of the four liver-tumor departments `肝肿瘤内科, 肝胆肿瘤与肝移植外科, 肝胆肿瘤内科, 肝肿瘤外科`; the unrestricted exact-diagnosis cohort is a sensitivity analysis. Freeze the routine dictionary and event counts before fitting.

Baseline: penalized logistic regression using age, sex, department, first available assay values, time-zero routine-treatment and medication indicators, and documentation counts. Alternative: a patient-level ordered sequence/state-space model using the same covariates plus within-assay values, time since admission, specimen type, and missingness. Split by `患者主索引`, use nested time-aware validation and repeated bootstrap uncertainty. Report held-out discrimination, calibration, and the incremental difference, not a causal effect.

The alternative is scientifically useful because it can reveal persistence, ordering, and cross-marker interactions that a snapshot loses. A larger neural sequence model is deferred unless event support and runtime justify it; CPU is appropriate for the tabular baseline and a bounded sequence fit.

Falsification: use timing-shifted features and within-patient order permutation as negative controls, plus routine-procedure and documentation/missingness sensitivities. Supportive results require a nontrivial held-out trajectory advantage for the non-routine endpoint, uncertainty excluding the prespecified trivial margin, stability across department/documentation/missingness/assay sensitivities, and absence of the advantage in the timing-shifted control. Adverse results include no improvement, degradation, advantage confined to routine treatment, disappearance after process adjustment, or a positive negative control. Inconclusive results include sparse non-routine events, unstable dictionary, missing timing, imprecise intervals, or inability to adjudicate clinical meaning.
