# Does hospital surveillance change the persistence of the documented hypotension–creatinine signal?

## Scientific deliverable and substantive advance

The future solver must newly estimate a hospital-practice effect-modifier estimand: whether the prompt-versus-delayed/no documented pressor-record contrast in a confirmed recorded creatinine rise depends on the hospital's pre-landmark creatinine surveillance intensity. It must construct the parent’s auditable six-hour ICU landmark cohort and 30-minute MAP ledger, derive a leakage-controlled hospital surveillance score from pre-landmark results, fit paired transparent and ordered multi-state models, and emit development and held-out-hospital estimates with uncertainty.

Required completion outputs are: (1) cohort flow, source/value/unit audits and exact stay-level ledgers; (2) any-rise and distinct-later-result-confirmed-rise outcomes; (3) the primary surveillance-dependent persistence gap, its component contrasts, and hospital-held-out transport; (4) test-opportunity, rise-given-test, confirmation, unit-death and alive-exit processes; (5) paired calibration, uncertainty and falsification sensitivities; and (6) conclusions explicitly linked to computed outputs. No model has been fitted and no clinical result is claimed here.

This is an adaptation, not a reproduction of any demonstration or expert seed. The parent already asks whether a creatinine rise persists on a later result. This successor adds one new estimand: how much that persistence contrast changes across a prespecified, outcome-free hospital monitoring regime. That can distinguish a signal useful for clinical surveillance from one that is mainly an artifact of local testing opportunity. It does not attempt to estimate a pressor treatment effect.

## Evidence-supported claim versus unresolved claim

The strongest available evidence before the experiment is data availability and schema semantics only. In the frozen eICU 2.0 snapshot, ICU-relative MAP observations, ICU-keyed laboratory results and revisions, named infusion records with offsets, unit disposition, admission descriptors, severity fields, hospital identifiers and uniquepid are joinable as specified below. A raw infusion name is a documented record, not proof that a drug was administered, started, titrated, or indicated. A recorded creatinine rise is not adjudicated KDIGO AKI or biological kidney injury. The parent proposal is a design, not a fitted result.

The unresolved claim is:

> Among adult ICU stays with a qualifying low-MAP landmark and a pre-landmark creatinine, the loss of the prompt-versus-delayed/no documented pressor-record contrast when changing from any recorded rise to a distinct later-result-confirmed rise is larger in low-surveillance than high-surveillance hospitals.

This directional hypothesis predicts that sparse local testing causes more apparent one-result timing signal to disappear on confirmation. The experiment also reports the two-sided hospital interaction; reversal or a larger loss in high-surveillance hospitals falsifies the proposed direction.

Let D_s,e be the patient-standardized 48-hour CIF difference between prompt-after-low and delayed/no-record timing groups in surveillance stratum s, for endpoint e in {any, confirmed}. Define the primary new estimand:

DeltaLoss = (D_low,any - D_low,confirmed) - (D_high,any - D_high,confirmed).

A positive DeltaLoss means the timing contrast loses more on confirmation in low-surveillance hospitals. The paired secondary heterogeneity estimand is H_confirmed = D_high,confirmed - D_low,confirmed; report H_any as context. Prompt, delayed and no-record estimates are also shown separately when overlap permits.

A practically important result is prespecified as DeltaLoss >= 3 percentage points with a 95% hospital-cluster interval excluding zero, the same directional estimate in the hospital-held-out analysis, and no essential sensitivity reversing the sign. This margin is a decision-oriented flag, not a claim that three points is a validated treatment threshold.

## Exposure-like recorded timing and endpoints

The first documented target record is the smallest infusionDrug.infusionoffset in [0,360] whose case-folded drugname contains one of norepinephrine, vasopressin, phenylephrine, dopamine or epinephrine. Assign:

- pre-low/concurrent: offset <= first raw low;
- prompt-after-low: first record > first low and <=30 minutes;
- delayed-after-low: >30 through 360 minutes; and
- no target record by 360.

Same-minute MAP and infusion are pre-low/concurrent. Rates, amounts, fluid and weight are audit fields only; a positive-rate restriction is a sensitivity. These categories describe documentation timing and cannot be interpreted as randomized strategies, administration, titration, dose, intent or treatment.

Baseline is the earliest qualifying creatinine in [0,60] by smallest labresultoffset, not the minimum value. Any-rise is the first post-landmark result at or above baseline +0.3 mg/dL. Confirmed-rise requires that first rise and a second qualifying creatinine at or above baseline +0.3 with a distinct labid, at least 360 minutes after the first rise, and no later than minute 2,880. Same-minute results, exact duplicates and revised versions of the same labid cannot confirm. The primary clock is labresultoffset; rerun with labresultrevisedoffset where present.

Represent the post-360 process as no qualifying test, qualifying test without rise, first rise, confirmed rise, unit death and alive exit, with a boundary-censored state. A first rise that never confirms is explicitly non-confirmed. Report the test-opportunity CIF, rise-given-test, confirmation-given-first-rise, death and alive-exit processes. None is a mediator or latent physiological state.

## Hospital surveillance regime and estimands

For every adult ICU stay with unitdischargeoffset >360, use only valid non-urinary creatinine rows in [0,360] to calculate the count of distinct labid results. The hospital surveillance score for an index stay is the leave-one-stay-out mean of this count among other eligible stays at the same hospitalid; require at least 30 other eligible stays for a primary score. This is a transparent proxy for pre-landmark creatinine monitoring opportunity, not a quality ranking. A sensitivity uses the leave-one-stay-out median and a patient-standardized residual score from a development-only model of that count using age, sex, baseline creatinine, minimum MAP, coverage and admission risk.

Cross-fitting is mandatory. Within each development fold, calculate each index score excluding that stay and freeze the hospital-level cutpoints using development hospitals only. For each held-out hospital, calculate its score only from other held-out stays' pre-360 records; use no post-360 values, outcomes or revisions. Define high and low surveillance as the top and bottom quartiles of hospital scores among development hospitals, with the middle half retained for descriptive plots. Apply those cutpoints unchanged to held-out hospitals. If a held-out hospital has fewer than 30 eligible non-index stays, report its overall transport estimate but omit it from the primary high/low interaction and label that component unsupported.

The principal estimand is DeltaLoss above, standardized over the same prespecified baseline covariate distribution: age, sex, ethnicity, baseline creatinine, minimum MAP, MAP burden and non-burden trajectory/coverage, unit/admission source and type, and admission-risk descriptors. The hospital context table is descriptive, not a covariate that creates a causal hospital effect. Use the same patient-level standardization in both models and endpoints.

A deterministic 80/20 hospital split is made before fitting by hash of hospitalid. If a uniquepid connects stays across hospitals, use connected hospital components so no person crosses development and holdout, report the resulting hospital/stay coverage, and do not call the result a pure hospital split. Holdout hospitals are never refit, tuned or recalibrated. Require at least 10 supported hospitals overall, at least 5 supported hospitals per high/low stratum in development, at least 10 held-out hospitals with valid scores, and at least 50 landmark stays per supported hospital. Failure is inconclusive for the heterogeneity estimand, not a negative finding.

## Matched methods

Transparent baseline. Fit regularized additive cause-specific pooled-logistic models B0–B4 on identical 30-minute intervals, with separate processes for test opportunity, first rise, confirmation, unit death and alive exit. B0 contains the fixed patient, admission, baseline creatinine, MAP trajectory/burden, coverage and interval inputs. B1 adds the prompt/delayed/no timing indicators. B2 adds the high/low surveillance indicator. B3 adds the prespecified timing-by-surveillance interaction; this interaction is the direct baseline estimate of DeltaLoss after standardization. B4 adds hospital random intercept and timing slope where supported, with no patient or hospital leakage. Fit any-rise and confirmed-rise outcomes from the same cohort and feature freeze. Compare D_any, D_confirmed, DeltaLoss, H_confirmed, component CIFs, calibration and transport—not just AUROC.

Substantive mechanistic alternative. Fit an ordered piecewise-exponential multi-state model on the identical cohort, interval grid, inputs, split, standardization and uncertainty scheme. Its transitions are test opportunity, first rise given test, confirmation given first rise, unit death and alive exit. Include the observed surveillance stratum and its interaction with documented timing in the test and confirmation intensities, plus hospital random effects only where supported; left-truncate all outcome transitions at 360 and freeze all pre-landmark MAP/pressor/creatinine history there. The model estimates whether DeltaLoss is generated mainly by low surveillance reducing the chance of a second result, by confirmation conditional on testing, or by competing unit exit. This transition decomposition is information the additive baseline loses, while the baseline remains the auditable reference for the same standardized estimand. Both methods must emit transition counts, effective samples, convergence diagnostics, interval-level predictions and uncertainty; a more complex model is not preferred merely for fit.

A learned sequence model is retained as an explicit alternative but deferred. It could encode the order and spacing of MAP, creatinine and documented infusion observations and reveal nonlinear interactions or trajectories missed by additive terms. It would use the same frozen 0–360 input ledger, hospital holdout, DeltaLoss target, calibration/Brier and cluster-bootstrap uncertainty. Revisit only if the two primary methods show reproducible shape-specific held-out miscalibration after process accounting and sufficient events; otherwise it would add prediction complexity without resolving a distinct scientific uncertainty. No GPU is required by the question.

## Analysis, uncertainty and falsification

Use development-only preprocessing, regularization and cutpoints. Estimate patient-standardized 48-hour competing-risk CIFs and all component contrasts. Use 500 hospital-cluster bootstrap replicates for primary intervals and a uniquepid-clustered sensitivity. Report paired intervals for D_any, D_confirmed, DeltaLoss, H_confirmed, component processes, between-hospital variance and held-out prediction intervals. Test overlap, effective sample size, convergence and extreme weights before interpreting estimates.

The primary hypothesis is supported only if DeltaLoss is positive, reaches the 3-point practical margin with a 95% interval excluding zero, survives hospital holdout and at least the key score/clock/duplicate sensitivities, and the component processes show the predicted greater loss of confirmation opportunity in low-surveillance hospitals. A stable positive H_confirmed with little change from any to confirmed suggests genuine heterogeneity in the recorded signal but does not identify its cause.

The hypothesis is adverse/falsified if the confidence interval excludes zero in the opposite direction, or if DeltaLoss is near zero while adequately powered and the component process is stable. A stable any-rise contrast but a null or reversed confirmed contrast supports a transient or ascertainment-sensitive explanation. Inconclusive results include sparse confirmation, unsupported score strata, inadequate overlap, fewer hospitals, extreme weighting, nonconvergence, pooled/equal-hospital disagreement, intervals spanning the margin, or material reversal across clock/duplicate/pressor-map sensitivities.

Required falsification and robustness analyses are: no MAP interpolation; no bridging; non-invasive MAP; revised laboratory clock; 3-hour and 12-hour confirmation gaps; +0.3 versus 1.5-times-baseline threshold where calculable; 30- versus 60-minute prompt cut; positive-rate pressor records; alternate target-drug map; mean versus median surveillance score; development-only versus pre-360 held-out score construction; connected-component versus ordinary hospital split; exclusion of end-of-life-plan rows only as a descriptive sensitivity if a defensible field is present; and Apache severity added versus omitted. The analysis must not treat missing lab results as confirmed absence, nor infer interface completeness, clinician intent or a biological renal null.

## Missing clinical evidence and interpretation

The study cannot establish that a documented infusion was administered or initiated, the dose or titration strategy, treatment indication, MAP target, fluid balance, renal reserve, urine-output completeness, hemoconcentration, KDIGO criteria, nephrologist adjudication, dialysis indication, or true kidney injury. The eICU intakeOutput table is therefore not used to call AKI; notes and other tables cannot substitute for adjudication. Hospital-level differences may reflect case mix, referral, documentation/interface coverage or local policy and must not be converted into hospital rankings or causal practice effects.

Computationally checkable claims are source hashes/headers, joins, offsets, filters, lab identity and confirmation logic, surveillance score construction, cross-fitting, hospital split, model convergence, standardization, CIFs, intervals, calibration, transport and sensitivities. Clinical meaning of recorded creatinine persistence, attribution to monitoring practice, administration/intent, and biological AKI require clinical adjudication, richer data, or another prospective/externally validated study.
