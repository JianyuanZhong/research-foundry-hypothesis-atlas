# Hypothesis Proposal: Diuretic Initiation Window Predicts Outcomes in Sepsis-Associated Fluid Overload

## Unresolved Question

**Primary Question:** In critically ill patients with sepsis-associated fluid overload and hemodynamic instability, does the time interval between the onset of fluid overload and diuretic initiation predict subsequent clinical deterioration, independent of admission severity and treatment intensity?

**Secondary Question:** Is there an optimal "window of opportunity" for diuretic initiation that balances the risk of precipitating hemodynamic collapse with the risk of prolonged fluid overload and organ dysfunction?

## Clinical Importance

1. **Treatment Timing Optimization:** Current sepsis guidelines recommend early fluid resuscitation but provide limited guidance on when to transition to diuresis in patients with concurrent fluid overload. Understanding the temporal relationship between fluid overload onset and diuretic initiation could inform personalized de-escalation strategies.

2. **Risk Stratification:** Patients who receive diuretics too early (before adequate perfusion) may experience hemodynamic collapse, while those who receive them too late may suffer prolonged organ dysfunction. Identifying the optimal timing window could reduce complications.

3. **Quality of Care Measurement:** Treatment timing could serve as a proxy for adherence to sepsis bundles and timely recognition of fluid overload, potentially improving quality metrics.

## Substantive Advance Over Existing Knowledge

Current literature focuses on:
- **Fluid resuscitation timing** in sepsis (e.g., 1-3 hour bundles)
- **Diuretic efficacy** in fluid overload without considering hemodynamic context
- **Outcomes of early vs. delayed diuretics** in stable populations

The gap: Most studies treat diuretic timing as a binary (early vs. late) rather than examining the continuum from fluid overload onset to diuretic initiation, particularly in the context of hemodynamic instability.

## Data Bindings

**Dataset:** eICU-CRD v2.0 (extracted in local dataset)

**Tables Required:**
- `patient` - Patient demographics, hospital information
- `infusionDrug` - Intravenous medication administration (includes diuretics)
- `medication` - All medication administration records
- `hospital` - Hospital identifiers and characteristics
- `vitalPeriodic` - Periodic vital signs (BP, HR, SpO2)
- `nurseCharting` - Clinical notes documenting fluid overload
- `diagnosis` - Diagnosis codes (sepsis, fluid overload)
- `intakeOutput` - Fluid intake and output measurements
- `lab` - Laboratory measurements (BUN, creatinine, sodium)

**Key Variables:**
- `patient.patient_id` - Unique patient identifier
- `infusionDrug.drug_name` - Drug name (for diuretic identification)
- `infusionDrug.start_time` - Medication administration timestamp
- `hospital.hospital_id` - Hospital identifier
- `nurseCharting.note_time` - Timestamp of clinical documentation
- `vitalPeriodic.record_time` - Vital sign measurement timestamp
- `diagnosis.diagnosis_date` - Diagnosis date

**Temporal Definitions:**
- **Fluid overload onset:** Time of first documented clinical evidence (nurseCharting notes mentioning fluid overload, or derived from intakeOutput showing positive balance)
- **Diuretic initiation:** Time of first documented diuretic administration (infusionDrug or medication tables)
- **Time window:** Fluid overload onset to diuretic initiation (primary exposure)
- **Outcome:** 30-day mortality, ICU-free discharge, acute kidney injury within 30 days

## Baseline and Alternative Approaches

**Baseline:** Admission SOFA score (from apachePredVar) and vasopressor need at ICU admission predict outcomes.

**Alternative:** Time-varying exposure to diuretic initiation window (early: <24h, intermediate: 24-72h, late: >72h from fluid overload onset) with interaction terms for admission severity.

**What the alternative reveals:** The baseline captures static severity but misses the dynamic process of fluid management. The alternative captures treatment timing as a modifiable risk factor that could inform clinical decisions.

## Falsification Criteria

The hypothesis will be falsified if:
1. No association between diuretic initiation timing and outcomes after adjusting for admission severity, treatment intensity, and confounders
2. The association is entirely mediated by baseline severity (all effect explained by admission SOFA/vasopressor need)
3. No difference in outcomes across timing windows after multivariate adjustment

## Population

- Adult patients (≥18 years)
- Diagnosed with sepsis (ICD-10 codes in diagnosis table)
- Received at least one diuretic during ICU stay
- Exclusion: Pregnancy, dialysis within 48 hours of fluid overload onset

## Analysis Plan

**Primary Analysis:**
- Cox proportional hazards model with time-varying diuretic initiation as exposure
- Time origin: Fluid overload onset
- Time-dependent covariates: Admission SOFA, vasopressor need, treatment intensity
- Outcome: 30-day mortality, ICU-free discharge

**Secondary Analyses:**
- Restricted cubic spline to identify optimal timing window
- Subgroup analysis by sepsis source, organ support need
- Propensity score matching to reduce confounding

## Missing Evidence and Limitations

1. **Fluid overload definition:** Clinical documentation of fluid overload may be incomplete; need to use clinical events as proxy
2. **Unmeasured confounding:** Confounding by indication (diuretics started by clinicians who anticipate deterioration)
3. **Measurement error:** Diuretic timing from medication records may not reflect actual clinical decision
4. **External validity:** eICU-CRD from US academic centers may not generalize to community hospitals

## Key References

Three key papers support this hypothesis:

1. **K1** Ziaka M, Exadaktylos A. Fluid management strategies in critically ill patients with ARDS: a narrative review. Eur J Med Res. 2025. Demonstrates that negative fluid balance with diuretics should be the goal once hemodynamic stabilization is achieved.

2. **K2** Malbrain MLNG, Marik PE, Witters I, et al. Fluid overload, de-resuscitation, and outcomes in critically ill or injured patients: a systematic review. Anaesthesiol Intensive Ther. 2014. Shows that positive fluid balance is associated with worse outcomes and that interventions to limit fluid balance improve outcomes.

3. **K3** Pinheiro BMA, Taniguchi LU. Negative Fluid Balance in Sepsis: When and How? Shock. 2018. Provides sepsis-specific evidence that fluid management including diuretics is important.
