# Does time-weighted hypotension burden add clinically meaningful information about subsequent AKI?

## Scientific opening and hypothesis

The clinically unresolved question is whether, in critically ill patients with a minimum blood pressure that would be considered low, the amount of time the patient spends below that pressure adds information about subsequent acute kidney injury (AKI) beyond the minimum pressure itself. This is not a treatment recommendation: the database does not identify an appropriate hypotension target, and hypotension is not assigned by the study.

The hypothesis is:

> Among adult ICU stays with a minimum systemic mean arterial pressure (MAP) of at least 50 mmHg, the cumulative time below 60 mmHg, 50 mmHg, and 40 mmHg during the first 48 hours after admission predicts incident AKI after adjustment for baseline severity and the minimum MAP. The incremental information from duration is stronger than a prespecified simple duration model and remains present when the analysis is split by hospital.

The strongest claim currently supported by the inspected evidence is that hypotension and AKI are related in observational ICU data. Lehman et al. (2010, PMID 22158679) used MIMIC II and reported that each additional hour of MAP below 70, 60, and 50 mmHg was associated with increased AKI risk in a target 48-hour window. That result does not establish a causal effect of hypotension or a portable clinical threshold; it is an observational association in a different dataset.

The unresolved claim this experiment tests is whether a time-weighted duration measure adds transportable, clinically interpretable information about incident AKI beyond the minimum MAP in this eICU snapshot. The substantive advance would be a data-bound test of whether duration, severity, and recording process jointly explain the risk, rather than treating duration as a generic prognostic variable.

## Population and temporal boundaries

The primary analysis includes adult ICU stays (`age >= 18`) with a nonmissing `systemicmean` value and `unitdischargeoffset > 0`. The 48-hour analysis window is `[0, 480]` minutes after `unitadmittime24`, truncated to `unitdischargeoffset`. The primary estimand is the association between time below prespecified MAP thresholds and incident AKI during the first 48 hours, conditional on baseline severity and minimum MAP. The primary analysis excludes stays with less than 200 valid MAP observations in the window, stays with more than 20% missing MAP, and stays with more than 20% missing creatinine in the 48-hour window. This is a data-availability rule, not a claim that missingness is ignorable.

A secondary analysis uses a fixed 24-hour window and a secondary outcome of creatinine increase from baseline to 48 hours. The primary outcome is incident AKI, defined as a nonmissing `labresult` for creatinine (using `labname` or `labmeasurenameinterface` after a prespecified label dictionary) with `labresultrevisedoffset` no later than 48 hours after admission, with a minimum of 24 hours between admission and the creatinine measurement. The primary creatinine value is the latest valid measurement in the window, with the exact label and value rules frozen before outcome inspection. A sensitivity analysis uses the earliest valid creatinine and excludes any stay with a creatinine measurement in the first 24 hours.

The primary analysis excludes stays with a recorded vasopressor infusion after the 48-hour window and with a recorded vasopressor infusion during the window if the primary model does not include treatment as a covariate; if treatment is included, the analysis is labeled treatment-exposed and cannot be interpreted as an effect of hypotension. A prespecified sensitivity excludes stays with vasopressor exposure during the window. The primary model does not adjust for treatment because the data do not support a causal interpretation and would change the estimand.

## Outcomes and estimand

The primary outcome is a binary indicator of incident AKI by 48 hours. The primary exposure is a vector of time below MAP thresholds: cumulative hours below 60, 50, and 40 mmHg in the window. The primary baseline model includes age, gender, ethnicity, admission height, admission weight, admission source, unit stay type, unit discharge offset, acute physiology score, APACHE score, predicted in-hospital mortality, and minimum MAP. The primary incremental model adds the duration vector. The primary comparison is the change in the area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUROC), calibration intercept/slope, and Brier score for the duration model versus the baseline model. The primary clinical contrast is the difference in predicted 48-hour AKI risk for a patient with the same minimum MAP but 12 additional hours below each threshold, reported with uncertainty and a prespecified absolute risk difference.

The primary model is a regularized logistic regression with prespecified threshold tuning. A secondary comparison is a prespecified duration-only model with the same covariates, to show whether duration adds information beyond severity and minimum MAP. A hospital-level random-effect logistic regression is a sensitivity analysis, with all hospital-specific coefficients frozen before outcome inspection and with whole-hospital leave-one-hospital-out validation. The primary split is patient-level; no patient may appear in both training and validation hospitals.

The primary analysis is exploratory and observational. It does not estimate the effect of reducing hypotension duration. The estimand is the incremental predictive association of duration with incident AKI after adjustment for baseline severity and minimum MAP, conditional on the observed recording and treatment process.

## Baseline and substantive alternative

The simple baseline is the minimum-MAP model described above: it uses only the lowest observed MAP and baseline severity covariates. This is the clinically interpretable comparator and is the primary baseline.

The substantive alternative is a duration model that represents the cumulative time below each prespecified MAP threshold. It can reveal whether the risk is concentrated in a short exposure, sustained exposure, or both, which the minimum MAP loses. It also allows a prespecified comparison with a duration-only model and a hospital-level sensitivity. This is a mechanistic-style representation of exposure burden, not a mechanistic model of kidney physiology; it does not establish that hypotension causes AKI.

A learned alternative is deferred. A sequence model such as a transformer or recurrent model could learn nonlinear interactions between MAP, time, and covariates, but it would not resolve the central confounding question and would add complexity without a clinically necessary comparison. The configured solver envelope is 16 CPUs, 262,144 MiB, and 28,800 seconds; a CPU regularized logistic model and hospital-level random effects are expected to fit within this envelope. No GPU is required. The discovery budget is 3,600 seconds and the full study is a future solver task, not a discovery result.

## Falsification and interpretation

The experiment is falsified for the proposed incremental information if the duration model has no statistically significant improvement in AUROC, calibration, or Brier score over the minimum-MAP baseline, or if the improvement is confined to a small subset of hospitals. The hypothesis is supported if the duration model improves discrimination and calibration, the incremental association is consistent across the three threshold contrasts, and the hospital-level analysis shows no clinically large heterogeneity.

An adverse result is a precise null or a result that reverses the direction, or a duration signal that disappears after controlling for minimum MAP, baseline severity, or hospital-level practice. This would argue against duration being a useful standalone risk measure in this snapshot, not against the biological association in general.

An inconclusive result is a wide confidence interval, inadequate event support, substantial missingness or recording-density heterogeneity, or a result that changes materially between the fixed 24-hour and 48-hour windows. This would justify narrowing the question or collecting more complete monitoring data; it would not establish that duration is safe or ineffective.

The strongest claim the experiment can support is a data-bound association and a comparison of predictive information. It cannot establish that hypotension caused AKI, that a duration threshold is a treatment target, or that the finding transports to other hospitals or populations. Those claims require clinical adjudication of AKI, treatment assignment and timing, missingness and recording-density validation, and external validation.
