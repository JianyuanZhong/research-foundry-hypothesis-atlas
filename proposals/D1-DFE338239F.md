# Hypothesis: Hypotension Duration as a Predictor of Multi-Organ Deterioration

## Scientific Question

**Primary Hypothesis**: Among critically ill patients with similar minimum blood pressure values, those with longer durations of hypotension experience significantly higher rates of subsequent multi-organ dysfunction (worsening in ≥2 organ systems within 24-48 hours) compared to those with brief hypotensive episodes.

**Secondary Hypothesis**: The association between hypotension duration and multi-organ deterioration is consistent across hospitals, suggesting that this is a generalizable phenomenon rather than a hospital-specific artifact.

## Unresolved Question

While existing literature has established minimum blood pressure thresholds for defining hypotension and documented associations between hypotension and mortality, there is limited evidence on:

1. **Temporal accumulation of hypotensive burden**: Does the *duration* of hypotension at a given severity level add prognostic information beyond the minimum pressure achieved?
2. **Multi-organ impact**: Does prolonged hypotension specifically predict *synchronized* deterioration across multiple organ systems, rather than just single-organ failure?
3. **Clinical intervention timing**: Can hypotension duration inform when to escalate or de-escalate hemodynamic support, independent of minimum pressure achieved?

## Clinical Importance

1. **Risk Stratification**: Hypotension duration may identify high-risk patients who require more aggressive monitoring and earlier intervention, even if their minimum blood pressure appears adequate.

2. **Treatment Optimization**: Understanding the relationship between hypotension duration and organ outcomes could inform:
   - Vasopressor titration strategies (aiming for sustained adequate perfusion, not just minimum pressure)
   - Timing of organ support escalation (e.g., renal replacement therapy)
   - ICU discharge planning (patients with prolonged hypotension may need longer recovery periods)

3. **Quality Improvement**: Hospitals could use hypotension duration metrics to evaluate the quality of hemodynamic management and identify areas for improvement in shock management protocols.

## Substantive Advance Over Existing Knowledge

This hypothesis advances beyond current understanding by:

1. **Moving from static to dynamic assessment**: Most existing risk models rely on single-timepoint measurements (e.g., admission SAP, minimum SAP during stay). This proposes using the *temporal profile* of hypotension as a risk factor.

2. **Identifying a potentially modifiable risk factor**: Unlike minimum blood pressure (which reflects the nadir of care quality), duration may be influenced by treatment timeliness, protocol adherence, and patient-specific factors like vascular resistance.

3. **Connecting hemodynamic instability to multi-organ outcomes**: While single-organ recovery has been studied, the coordinated impact of prolonged hypotension across organ systems remains unexplored.

Patients in eICU with:

- Vasopressor administration records (infusionDrug table)

### Key Variables

**Exposure**: Duration of hypotension (cumulative time below threshold, in hours)
- Definition: Sum of hours during continuous periods where SBP < 90 mmHg or MAP < 65 mmHg
- Calculation: From vitalAperiodic table, grouping by patientunitstayid

**Outcome**: Multi-organ deterioration within 24-48 hours post-hypotension onset
- Definition: Worsening in ≥2 organ systems based on predefined criteria
- Organ systems: Renal (creatinine), Liver (bilirubin, AST, ALT), Respiratory (PaO2/FiO2 ratio)
- Worsening: Increase in organ dysfunction score (e.g., SOFA component) by ≥1 point

**Covariates**:
- Demographics: Age, sex (apachePredVar)
- Comorbidities: SAPS III components (apachePredVar)
- Admission source (apachePredVar)
- Hospital region (apachePredVar)
- Vasopressor use (infusionDrug)
- Initial organ dysfunction (lab at admission)

## Analysis Plan

1. **Define hypotension episodes**:
   - Identify continuous periods where SBP < 90 mmHg or MAP < 65 mmHg
   - Handle artifacts and missing data appropriately

2. **Calculate hypotension duration**:
   - Sum of hours spent below threshold per episode
   - Total duration across all episodes during ICU stay

3. **Define multi-organ deterioration**:
   - Calculate organ dysfunction scores (e.g., SOFA components) at baseline and follow-up
   - Identify worsening in ≥2 organ systems within 24-48 hours

4. **Primary analysis**:
   - Cox proportional hazards regression
   - Exposure: Hypotension duration (hours)
   - Outcome: Time to multi-organ deterioration
   - Covariates: Age, SAPS III components, admission source, hospital region, initial organ dysfunction

5. **Secondary analyses**:
   - Hospital-level analysis with hospital fixed effects
   - Sensitivity analyses with different thresholds (SBP < 80, MAP < 60)
   - Stratification by vasopressor use and initial organ dysfunction

6. **Robustness checks**:
   - Alternative definitions of hypotension (duration only vs. duration + minimum pressure)
   - Different time windows for deterioration assessment (24h vs. 48h vs. 72h)
   - Subgroup analyses (sepsis vs. non-sepsis, cardiac vs. non-cardiac)

## Baseline Comparison

**Baseline approach**: Compare outcomes based on minimum blood pressure alone
- Groups: Minimum SBP < 90 mmHg vs. ≥ 90 mmHg
- Outcome: Mortality, multi-organ deterioration

**Proposed approach**: Compare outcomes based on hypotension duration among patients with similar minimum blood pressure
- Groups: Duration in lowest quartile vs. highest quartile
- Covariate adjustment: Match on minimum SBP

**What the alternative reveals**: If duration adds prognostic information beyond minimum pressure, it suggests that the *temporal pattern* of hemodynamic instability is more clinically relevant than the absolute nadir. This could inform more nuanced treatment protocols that focus on sustained adequate perfusion rather than just achieving a minimum threshold.

## Falsification Criteria

The hypothesis would be **falsified** if:

1. **No independent association**: Hypotension duration shows no significant association with multi-organ deterioration after adjusting for minimum blood pressure and other covariates (HR ≈ 1, 95% CI includes 1)

2. **Confounding explanation**: The association is entirely explained by confounding factors (e.g., patient severity, treatment intensity) that correlate with both hypotension duration and outcomes

3. **No temporal relationship**: Multi-organ deterioration does not occur within the specified time window (24-48 hours), suggesting the temporal relationship is not causal

## Supportive Evidence

If the hypothesis is **supported**, we would observe:

1. **Significant hazard ratios**: Hazard ratios for multi-organ deterioration significantly > 1 for longer hypotension duration (after adjusting for minimum pressure and covariates)

2. **Consistency across settings**: Similar associations observed across different hospitals and thresholds

3. **Dose-response relationship**: Gradual increase in risk with increasing duration of hypotension

## Adverse/Inconclusive Results

**Adverse** (hypothesis refuted):
- No independent association between duration and outcomes after adjustment
- Longer duration associated with *better* outcomes (suggesting selection bias or reverse causation)

**Inconclusive**:
- Weak association with wide confidence intervals
- Association only present in specific subgroups (e.g., sepsis vs. non-sepsis)
- Requires external validation or mechanistic studies

## Missing Evidence

This study requires:

1. **Clinical adjudication**: Definition of "multi-organ deterioration" needs clinical validation by experts

2. **External validation**: Results should be tested in independent cohorts (e.g., MIMIC-IV, other ICU databases)

3. **Mechanistic insight**: Whether prolonged hypotension causes organ damage through:
   - Ischemia-reperfusion injury
   - Sustained vasoconstriction
   - Activation of inflammatory pathways
   - Other mechanisms

4. **Interventional data**: Whether modifying hypotension duration (not just minimum pressure) improves outcomes in randomized trials
