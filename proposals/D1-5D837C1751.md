# Nighttime ICU discharge: unmet monitoring need (effect modification) or uniform off-hours care effect? A computable interaction + transport test in eICU 2.0

Lead working proposal — eICU island, **episode 24**. Operation: **evolve** of true parent
`[prior hypothesis]` (expert seed `[starting question]`, "Nighttime ICU discharge"),
assessed valid this episode. Status: **design owned by the Lead for compilation.** No reference/solver
solution was executed at discovery — this is design, binding and grounding work, not a solved result.

## 1. Unresolved question and clinical importance

Outside-hours ("nighttime") discharge from the ICU is associated with higher in-hospital mortality and
ICU readmission than daytime discharge ([K1] Vollam 2018, systematic review/meta-analysis). That pooled
association leaves the *structure* of the harm unresolved, and this determines the clinical response:

- **H1 (unmet monitoring need):** the harm is concentrated among patients who are still physiologically
  unstable at discharge. Discharging an unstable patient to a lower-intensity ward at night, when
  monitoring/rapid-response coverage is thinner, lets deterioration go unrecognised. If true, the
  actionable lever is **discharge-readiness screening** (do not discharge residual-instability patients
  off-hours without enhanced ward monitoring).
- **R1 (uniform off-hours care effect):** the harm is roughly homogeneous across instability strata
  because off-hours staffing/response is globally worse. If true, the lever is **unit-level staffing**,
  not individual readiness screening.

Because a pooled main-effect coefficient cannot separate these, the branch claim is a **falsifiable
interaction**: does the nighttime excess mortality odds rise with pre-discharge instability (H1), or is
it flat across instability (R1)?

## 2. Leading claim vs strongest rivals, and the separating observation

- **Leading claim H1 (falsifiable):** the nighttime-discharge effect is modified by residual
  physiologic instability at discharge, such that the nighttime-vs-daytime odds ratio for post-ICU
  in-hospital death is >1 and increasing in the instability score (interaction OR > 1), with the
  effect carried within hospitals.
- **Strongest rival R1 (uniform/care-process):** the nighttime main effect is present but the
  nighttime×instability interaction is null (OR≈1); the effect is uniform.
- **Additional rivals kept:** R2 **severity/selection** — sicker (or, alternatively, less sick) patients
  are preferentially discharged at night; controlled by hour-24 severity adjustment AND by a
  propensity/overlap check ([K2] Corrêa 2018 bounds this) rather than adjustment alone. R3
  **disposition/EOL artifact** — night discharges routed to end-of-life/hospice or 'Other ICU' transfers;
  exclude active EOL and non-ward dispositions. R4 **capacity artifact** — night discharge reflects bed
  pressure; eICU lacks real-time bed-capacity data, so this remains an **unresolvable rival stated
  honestly** (seed limitation).
- **Separating observation:** a single model entering a nighttime indicator, a continuous instability
  score, and their **product** (nighttime×instability), plus a hospital random intercept/slope, makes
  H1 and R1 non-equivalent. H1 ⇒ interaction coefficient clearly positive; R1 ⇒ interaction ≈ 0.

## 3. Baseline vs substantive alternative

- **Simple baseline (what a naive design uses):** fixed-effect logistic model for post-ICU-discharge
  in-hospital death with a single nighttime main effect, adjusted for age, gender, APACHE-IVa score /
  eICU predicted ICU mortality, admission source and unit type. This yields only the pooled association.
- **Substantive mechanistic alternative (same question):** the same model **plus** a pre-specified
  continuous **discharge-instability score** (last-24h pre-discharge vitals: heart rate, systemic mean
  BP, respiration, SpO2, temperature; ongoing vasopressors from `infusionDrug`; mechanical ventilation
  from `respiratoryCare` `ventstartoffset`/`ventendoffset`; `activeupondischarge` flags) entered as an
  **effect modifier** (nighttime×instability), with a hospital random slope on the nighttime term to
  absorb between-hospital practice.
  - **What the alternative reveals that the baseline loses:** whether harm is localised to a
    screenable subgroup (actionable discharge-readiness triage) versus a uniform care effect (staffing).
    Optionally a bounded gradient-boosted instability model is compared against the linear score to test
    whether non-linearity in the modifier changes the interaction decision.

## 4. Population, exposure, outcome, time

- **Population:** adult ICU stays (eICU `patient`; exclude age<16; eICU encodes >89 as 90) with
  `unitdischargestatus=='Alive'` and `unitdischargeoffset>0` (an actual ICU-unit discharge), excluding
  `unitdischargelocation=='Other ICU'` transfers and stays with active end-of-life care (`carePlanEOL`).
- **Exposure:** nighttime ICU unit discharge from `patient.unitdischargetime24` (HH:MM:SS time-of-day);
  primary night window 19:00–06:59, sensitivity 00:00–06:59; report time-of-day missingness.
- **Outcome:** post-ICU-discharge in-hospital death = `hospitaldischargestatus=='Expired'` among
  ICU-survivors; timing referenced to `hospitaldischargeoffset` (minutes from ICU admission).
- **Time/landmark:** instability features are taken only in the **last 24 h before `unitdischargeoffset`**
  (strict pre-discharge ordering, no post-discharge leakage); `observationoffset`/charting offsets are
  in MINUTES.

## 6. Pre-specified decision rules

- **Supportive (H1):** nighttime×instability interaction odds ratio > 1 with 95% CI excluding 1 AND the
  instability-stratified nighttime effect is monotone (highest tertile OR > lowest tertile OR).
- **Adverse (R1):** nighttime main effect present but interaction OR ≈ 1 (CI includes 1); stratified
  effect flat across instability tertiles.
- **Inconclusive:** imprecise interaction CI spanning both, or < pre-specified minimum events per
  stratum/hospital; report as underpowered, not null.
- **Transport:** hospital random slope and leave-one-hospital-out (LOHO) repetition of the interaction;
  poor LOHO stability ⇒ association not portable (weakens H1's clinical transport claim).

## 7. Honest limits

- Readiness/design only; the interaction is **not** a computed result in this episode.
- eICU has no real-time ICU bed-capacity/staffing data → the capacity rival (R4) cannot be excluded;
  causal "unmet need" and policy-grade claims require stewardship/quality-registry adjudication.
- `unitdischargetime24` reliability and timezone handling are unverified; the paper is **not** about
  treatment effect — it is an association-with-effect-modification study, and a supportive result would
  motivate, not constitute, a prospective discharge-timing trial.
- External validity is within-eICU only.
