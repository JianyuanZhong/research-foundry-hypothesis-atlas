# Cell-selective MerTK perturbation in rheumatoid synovium

## Hypothesis and clinical decision

**Hypothesis:** MerTK inhibition of LPS-activated monocyte-derived macrophages can reduce their inflammatory drive on rheumatoid arthritis fibroblast-like synoviocytes (FLS), but indiscriminate inhibition of remission-associated MerTK-positive synovial tissue macrophages can impair their FLS-supportive repair program. Therefore, any therapeutic rationale for MerTK blockade depends on cell context and selective delivery; systemic/broad MerTK inhibition should not be prioritized without functional evidence that preserves resolution-associated functions.

**Why it matters:** MerTK-positive synovial macrophage states are associated with remission and FLS repair, while inflammatory myeloid–stromal crosstalk is a plausible therapeutic target [K1]. Choosing between broad MerTK blockade, cell-selective inhibition, or deferral is a consequential target-development decision. This proposal does not claim that the hypothesis is new relative to all of K1: the full accessible publisher page and deposited data identify E-MTAB-8316/8873/8322 and supplementary MerTK-inhibitor DEGs. The advance sought is a donor- and cell-context-resolved *functional safety boundary* for target prioritization, not another expression-only confirmation of the original study.

## Evidence versus unresolved claim

- **Strongest supported claim:** Alivernini et al. report remission-associated MerTK-positive STM populations with anti-inflammatory signatures, inflammation-resolving lipid mediators and FLS repair response in vitro; their paper links low MerTK-positive STM proportion to flare after treatment cessation [K1]. The E-MTAB-8316 protocol independently documents LPS stimulation of monocyte-derived macrophages, UNC106238 pretreatment and 48-hour FLS transwell coculture. E-MTAB-8873 documents sorted MerTK/CD206 macrophage-FLS coculture and inhibitor-labeled conditions. These are observed study designs and published findings, not results computed in this proposal.
- **Unresolved claim to test:** Does cell-restricted MerTK inhibition reduce inflammatory FLS output when applied to LPS-activated monocyte-derived macrophages while preserving repair-associated FLS and macrophage functions when MerTK-positive remission-associated STMs are left untreated? Expression differences alone do not establish this therapeutic window or direct MerTK engagement.
- **Strongest rival:** UNC106238 has off-target effects, or observed shifts reflect donor pairing, LPS activation, coculture composition, sample handling or targeted-panel measurement rather than MerTK-dependent signaling. Alternatively, repair-associated expression does not translate to matrix function; apparent anti-inflammatory changes may coexist with impaired efferocytosis or tissue repair.
- **Prior-work boundary:** K2 describes macrophage–FLS crosstalk but uses broad polarization framing and does not establish this cell-selective therapeutic window. K3 reviews inflammatory resolution and remission mechanisms, underscoring that a transcript shift is not clinical remission. Both bound the rationale, not prove it. The closest experiment in K1 makes the specific added endpoint (orthogonal target engagement plus distinct functional harm/benefit readouts) essential; it is not safe to claim the MerTK inhibitor expression effect itself is unstudied.

## Harbor computation and future experiment

**Population and window:** In the computational screen, include all 12 E-MTAB-8316 matrix samples passing exact sample-ID join, then analyze the six within-experiment pairs in which LPS-stimulated monocyte-derived macrophages were untreated/vehicle or pretreated with UNC106238; the endpoint is FLS RNA after 48-hour transwell coculture. Preserve monocyte donor, FLS donor, specimen, and run/batch fields. No random cell split or inference from 12 as independent donors. Keep the three verified E-MTAB-8873 Mpos inhibitor/control pairs and lone Mneg pair separate as contextual/safety description only.

**Baseline estimand:** For each matched experiment, calculate treated-minus-vehicle effect on a prespecified inflammatory FLS score (only genes present in the matrix and fixed before outcomes; e.g., IL6, CXCL8, CCL2, MMP3 after checking identifiers) and a separate repair/ECM score (prespecified measured matrix features such as COL1A1, COL1A2, TGFBI where actually present). Normalize raw counts with a validated bulk RNA-seq workflow; report sample-level expression, per-pair differences, mean paired effect and uncertainty at donor-pair level, leave-one-pair-out sensitivity, and multiplicity-adjusted transcript-level exploratory outputs. Use a blocked/regression model with exposure and prespecified donor terms only if estimable from these six experiments; otherwise emphasize paired contrasts, do not overfit random effects. No computed estimates are asserted here.

**Substantive mechanistic alternative, deferred as computational model:** Use a signed, literature-prior constrained macrophage-to-FLS interaction model on the exact same measured genes/contrasts to test whether effects align with coherent MERTK/effector-resolution and inflammatory/repair programs rather than a few large genes. Inputs: the same sample matrices, design covariates, and frozen signed ligand–receptor/pathway annotations limited to measured features. Fit pathway weights in donor-grouped training folds and evaluate score direction/coherence in held-out donor-pair experiments, comparing with the paired-score baseline and a shuffled-edge/null model; report uncertainty and stability across held-out donors. Six pairs and strong donor reuse are too small for reliable independent validation, and transcript abundance cannot identify target engagement or mediation. Defer model fitting until a larger independent donor set exists; do not invent model complexity or use GPU. A future *mechanistic perturbation* is more informative: randomized vehicle, UNC106238 and orthogonal MERTK knockdown/rescue in separate inflammatory MoM and MerTK-positive STM arms, with cell-specific target engagement, viability and efferocytosis readouts plus FLS cytokine secretion, matrix degradation and collagen deposition. This reveals functional selectivity and off-target effects that the simple expression baseline loses.

**Future donor-level design:** Recruit/obtain independently identified RA FLS and macrophage donors, with active-disease LPS-activated blood-derived MoMs and sorted MerTK-positive/CD206-positive remission-associated STMs tested separately. Randomize macrophage pretreatment to vehicle versus a dose-verified UNC106238 range and orthogonal MERTK perturbation; wash before coculture to distinguish macrophage from direct FLS exposure. Include matched FLS-alone vehicle and drug-exposure controls, untreated macrophage, LPS-only, positive inflammatory, and MerTK-positive untreated arms; cross donors rather than pool, block on FLS and macrophage donor, and measure at baseline and 24/48 hours. Primary joint outcomes: FLS secreted inflammatory cytokines/chemokines and matrix-damage activity; co-primary safety outcomes: FLS repair/matrix function and macrophage efferocytosis/viability. Blind sample processing and define a biologically meaningful margin with rheumatology/stromal biology review before enrollment. This is a proposed experiment, not executed data.

## Result interpretation and stopping rules

- **Supportive of cell-selective development:** inhibition in inflammatory MoMs reduces the inflammatory FLS functional endpoint versus matched vehicle, while leaving MerTK-positive STM-associated repair and efferocytosis within prespecified non-inferiority margins; on-target evidence is reproduced by orthogonal MERTK perturbation. This supports further selective-delivery development only, not patient benefit.
- **Adverse / abandon broad blockade:** reduction of inflammatory output is accompanied by impaired MerTK-positive STM repair/efferocytosis, or pharmacologic effects fail orthogonal target validation. Do not advance broad/systemic MerTK blockade on transcript reduction alone; reassess selective delivery or abandon the target.
- **Inconclusive:** donor heterogeneity, uncertain annotations, wide confidence intervals, absent target engagement, or imprecise functional margins leaves the therapeutic window unresolved; replicate/clarify before choosing. A non-significant transcript effect in six pairs is not evidence of no effect.
- **Computationally checkable:** source-to-table joins, measured feature availability, QC, pairwise estimates, uncertainty, robustness, and whether solver conclusions numerically follow from computed outputs.
- **Requires human/external adjudication:** what constitutes a clinically meaningful repair benefit/non-inferiority margin, donor annotation conflicts, target-engagement specificity, cell-state classification, and interpretation of tissue repair. Clinical efficacy/safety requires separate in-vivo/clinical studies with treatment and patient outcomes. No automatic verifier can establish these.

## Method choice and resource note

Simple baseline selected for the historical-data screen because the experimental unit count is six paired donor experiments and the purpose is an auditable effect/uncertainty description. The mechanistic model could reveal coherence across prespecified pathways rather than just a weighted marker score, but available data do not support held-out biological confirmation and include no target-engagement labels; it is deferred. Computational fitting if later justified is CPU (approximately 4 CPUs, <=16 GiB, <30 minutes for count normalization, paired models and pathway scoring; estimate not measured here). Future orthogonal perturbation is wet-lab, outside the compute envelope. Discovery budget is separate from solver planning limits; no GPU probe or full screen was run, and this proposal reports no computed results.

## Prior directions not selected

- D2-T1 broad “context-dependent intervention response” is narrowed to an explicit inhibitor-associated response and target-selective safety question; its proposed GSE95588 extension is deferred until the assay registry identifies donor-aware perturbation contrasts.
- D2-T2 generic “protective versus pathogenic states” is not treated as a novel discovery; its benefit/harm distinction is retained as outcome structure, but there is no proof that transcript states mean functional protection.
- The three clinical research demonstrations are not adapted: molecular assays do not provide longitudinal EHR histories, cancer outcomes/images, or EHR-genetic longitudinal trajectories required for faithful adaptation. Their model/compute lessons do not justify importing unrelated methods.
- Revisit pathway/network fitting only after an independent, mapped donor cohort with measured MERTK engagement and functional response endpoints; revisit broad vs targeted MerTK drug development only after cell-specific perturbation data.
