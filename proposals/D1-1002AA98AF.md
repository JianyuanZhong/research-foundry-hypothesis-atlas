# Provisional HCC hypothesis: post-hepatectomy laboratory trajectory and 30-day deterioration

Status: provisional checkpoint for episode 8. This is a proposed relationship and experiment, not a completed analysis or clinical conclusion.

## Proposed relationship

Among adults with a recorded liver resection in the HCC snapshot, the early post-operative trajectory of liver-related laboratory abnormalities (initially operationalized as bilirubin, INR, ALT, and albumin when available) will be associated with a clinically important deterioration signal during the subsequent 30 days, beyond baseline disease severity and operative context. The unresolved claim is whether a reproducible trajectory, rather than a single post-operative value, identifies a distinct recovery/deterioration pattern that could inform surveillance or escalation research.

This association is not established. The HCC catalog and prior board records support the presence of longitudinal procedure, laboratory, encounter, diagnosis, medication, examination, pathology, and clinical-document data. They do not establish the proposed association, endpoint validity, causal mechanism, or clinical utility.

## Clinical importance and substantive advance

A positive result would distinguish an early warning pattern that merits prospective validation from the simpler explanation that a single post-operative value or baseline severity is sufficient. It would be a substantive advance if the trajectory adds reproducible, temporally valid information after accounting for operative context and measurement practice. An adverse result would show that the apparent signal is explained by baseline severity, timing, missingness, or observation practice. An inconclusive result would identify the measurement or adjudication evidence needed before a treatment decision is considered.

## Planned discriminating experiment

Population: adults in the HCC snapshot with a procedure-table record matching a liver-resection label, restricted to records with a usable procedure time and a defined 30-day follow-up window. Time zero is the procedure start time; records with missing or ambiguous times are excluded in the primary analysis and retained in a sensitivity analysis.

Inputs: the procedures table for surgery label and time; the laboratory table for the specified analytes and timestamps; encounters and transfers for post-operative care context and deterioration proxies. Diagnoses, medications, pathology, examinations, and clinical documents may be prespecified covariates or adjudication aids, but cannot substitute for missing primary fields.

Baseline: a prespecified model using baseline severity, operative context, and the first post-operative laboratory value. Alternative: a low-dimensional trajectory model (for example, slope/curvature or a regularized functional/latent trajectory summary) fitted to the same analytes over a fixed early post-operative window. Compare held-out temporal predictions and calibration, with uncertainty estimates and a negative control based on a pre-procedure or otherwise temporally invalid window.

Outcome: a prespecified 30-day deterioration signal derived from available encounter/transfer and, if feasible, clinically adjudicated documentation. The exact endpoint must be frozen after inspecting actual fields and must distinguish death, ICU/transfer, readmission, or other deterioration only when those labels are genuinely present.

Falsification: the trajectory model is not supported if it adds no reproducible information beyond baseline plus the first value, if its signal disappears after timing/missingness controls, if it fails temporal holdout, or if the negative-control window performs comparably. A null result is not proof of no biological mechanism.

## Deliverable and alternatives

The future solver must newly fit the baseline and trajectory models, estimate held-out performance and uncertainty, produce the prespecified endpoint and negative-control analyses, and report whether the trajectory claim is supportive, adverse, or inconclusive. The simple baseline is scientifically necessary; the trajectory alternative is retained because it can reveal ordered recovery information lost by a single value. A mechanistic causal model is deferred because treatment assignment and confounding are not adequately identified in these sources.
