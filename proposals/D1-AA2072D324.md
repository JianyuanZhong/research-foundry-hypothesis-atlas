# Residual Instability Before ICU Discharge: A Falsifiable MIMIC-IV Experiment

**Parent:** [prior hypothesis] (expert seed 10)
**Dataset:** MIMIC-IV 3.1 (frozen archive, [source checksum])
**Status:** Data-bound proposal with verified itemids and feasibility probe results

---

## 1. Hypothesis

### Supported claim (established by prior literature)
Vital-sign variability and oxygen requirement during ICU stay are associated with adverse outcomes. [K1] Wang et al. demonstrated that heart-rate-derived variability measures (HR SD, HR RMSSD) calculated from routine ICU charted data are independently associated with in-hospital mortality in critically ill patients (HR SD OR 1.855, 95% CI 1.211–2.841). [K2] Kalinoski et al. showed that persistent critical illness (ICU LOS ≥ 10 days) is associated with a threefold increase in mortality risk after onset (HR 3.01, 95% CI 2.72–3.33). [K3] Orosz et al. demonstrated that post-ICU clinical deterioration (captured by MET calls) is strongly associated with ICU readmission (27.5% vs 2.2%, p < 0.001).

### Unresolved claim (this experiment tests)
**Patients with residual physiological instability in the last 24 hours before ICU discharge — defined as persistently elevated oxygen requirement (FiO2 ≥ 0.4) or abnormal vital-sign variability (SD of MAP > 10 mmHg or SD of HR > 20 bpm) — have a higher risk of ICU readmission (within the same hospitalization or within 72 hours) and in-hospital death, independent of baseline severity and discharge destination.**

This claim is unresolved because:
1. Prior studies have examined variability at ICU *admission* (first 24 h) [K1], not at ICU *discharge* (last 24 h).
2. No study has combined oxygen requirement and vital-sign variability into a unified "residual instability" phenotype at the discharge transition.
3. The association with ICU *readmission* (as opposed to mortality alone) has not been tested with a prespecified, data-derived instability indicator.

### What answering this question would change
If supported: a simple, computable phenotype from routine ICU monitoring could identify patients at elevated risk of post-discharge deterioration, informing monitoring intensity after ICU discharge and potentially reducing preventable ICU readmissions. If adverse (no association): it would suggest that residual instability in the last 24 h is either not independently prognostic beyond baseline severity, or that the chosen thresholds/timing do not capture the relevant signal.

## 2. Study Population

### Inclusion criteria
- Adult patients (anchor_age ≥ 18 at first encounter)
- ICU length of stay ≥ 1.0 day (ensures a meaningful pre-discharge window)
- At least one chartevent in the last 24 h of the ICU stay (data availability)
- Discharged alive from the ICU (outtime IS NOT NULL)

### Exclusion criteria
- ICU stay < 1.0 day (insufficient window for variability calculation)
- No chartevent data in the last 24 h for any of the key items (HR, MAP, FiO2, SpO2)
- First ICU stay of the hospitalization only (to avoid double-counting re-entries)

### Temporal boundaries
- **Exposure window:** [outtime − 24 h, outtime] of the index ICU stay
- **Outcome window (ICU readmission):**
  - Definition A: any subsequent icustay with the same subject_id + hadm_id (same hospitalization)
  - Definition B: any subsequent icustay with the same subject_id, different hadm_id, with intime within 72 h of index outtime
- **Outcome window (in-hospital death):** deathtime IS NOT NULL OR hospital_expire_flag = 1 for the index hadm_id

## 4. Prespecified Residual Instability Phenotype

In the **last 24 hours before ICU discharge** (charttime ∈ [outtime − 24 h, outtime]):

### Component 1: High Oxygen Requirement
- **Primary:** FiO2 (itemid 223835) ≥ 0.4 in the last 24 h
  - Definition: ≥ 1 measurement with valuenum ≥ 0.40, OR mean FiO2 ≥ 0.40
- **Secondary (if FiO2 unavailable):** SpO2 (itemid 220277) < 94% in the last 24 h
  - Definition: ≥ 1 measurement with valuenum < 94

### Component 2: Vital-Sign Fluctuation
- **MAP variability:** SD of MAP (itemid 220181, fallback 220052) > 10 mmHg in the last 24 h
  - Justification: 10 mmHg SD represents clinically meaningful blood pressure lability; normal MAP variability in stable ICU patients is typically < 5 mmHg
- **HR variability:** SD of HR (itemid 220045) > 20 bpm in the last 24 h
  - Justification: 20 bpm SD is approximately 2× the normal resting HR variability; Wang et al. [K1] found HR SD independently associated with mortality

### Composite: Residual Instability
**Residual instability = (High Oxygen Requirement) OR (Vital-Sign Fluctuation)**

where Vital-Sign Fluctuation = (MAP SD > 10) OR (HR SD > 20)

### Sensitivity analyses
- FiO2 threshold: 0.30, 0.40, 0.50
- MAP SD threshold: 5, 10, 15 mmHg
- HR SD threshold: 10, 20, 30 bpm
- Window: 12 h, 24 h, 48 h

## 5. Outcomes

### Primary outcome: ICU readmission
- **Definition A (same hospitalization):** A second icustay for the same subject_id + hadm_id after the index stay
- **Definition B (72-hour cross-admission):** A new icustay for the same subject_id, different hadm_id, with intime within 72 hours of the index stay's outtime
- Primary analysis uses Definition A; Definition B as sensitivity

### Secondary outcome: In-hospital death
- deathtime IS NOT NULL OR hospital_expire_flag = 1 for the index hadm_id

### Competing risk
- For ICU readmission analysis: in-hospital death is a competing event (patients who die cannot be readmitted)
- Analysis: Fine-Gray subdistribution hazard model for readmission with death as competing risk; also standard Cox model with death as censoring (sensitivity)

## 6. Analysis Plan

### Baseline model
Multivariable logistic regression (for readmission) / Cox proportional hazards (for death):
- Age (continuous, per 10 years)
- Sex (male)
- ICU LOS (continuous, per day)
- Discharge destination (home vs non-home)
- APACHE II or SOFA score (if computable from available data; otherwise omit)

### Extended model
Baseline model + **residual instability indicator** (binary: 0/1)

### Model comparison
- Adjusted OR (95% CI) for residual instability in the extended model
- C-index (Harrell's) for baseline vs extended model
- Net Reclassification Improvement (NRI) and Integrated Discrimination Improvement (IDI)
- Likelihood ratio test for model fit

### Method comparison: baseline vs substantive alternative
- **Baseline:** Logistic regression with the binary instability indicator
- **Alternative:** Gradient-boosted tree (XGBoost) with continuous features (FiO2 mean, FiO2 max, MAP SD, HR SD, HR mean, SpO2 min) instead of the binary indicator
  - **What the alternative reveals:** The binary indicator collapses continuous physiological signals into a single flag. A gradient-boosted tree can reveal (a) whether the relationship between instability and outcome is nonlinear or threshold-dependent, (b) which component (oxygen vs HR variability vs MAP variability) contributes most to discrimination, and (c) whether interactions between components (e.g., high FiO2 + high HR SD) are stronger than either alone. If the tree substantially outperforms the logistic model, it suggests the binary phenotype loses important information and the thresholds may need refinement. If performance is similar, the simple binary indicator is sufficient for clinical use.

### Falsification criteria
- **Supportive:** Adjusted OR for residual instability ≥ 1.5, 95% CI excluding 1.0, AND incremental C-index improvement ≥ 0.01 (or NRI > 0 with p < 0.05)
- **Adverse:** Adjusted OR < 1.2, 95% CI including 1.0, AND no incremental discrimination (ΔC-index < 0.005, NRI not significant)
- **Inconclusive:** < 100 patients with residual instability, OR < 50 outcome events, OR > 50% missing data in the exposure window

### Interpretation of results
- **Supportive result:** Residual instability is an independent prognostic marker for post-ICU-discharge deterioration. Would justify prospective validation and potential integration into discharge readiness checklists.
- **Adverse result:** Residual instability in the last 24 h is not independently associated with readmission or death beyond baseline severity. Would suggest that the discharge decision already accounts for these factors, or that the relevant signal occurs earlier in the ICU course.
- **Inconclusive result:** Insufficient events or data to draw conclusions. Would require a larger dataset or different time window.

## 7. Limitations and Alternatives

### Selection bias
- Sicker patients stay longer in the ICU; the last-24h window conditions on survival to that point. Patients who die in the ICU before the last 24 h are excluded.
- Mitigation: Include ICU LOS as a covariate; perform sensitivity analysis excluding stays > 14 days (PerCI).

### Discharge destination as confounder
- Patients discharged to step-down/HDU may have different monitoring intensity than those to general wards.
- Mitigation: Include discharge_location in the model; perform stratified analysis by destination.

### Competing risk of death
- Patients who die in hospital cannot be readmitted.
- Mitigation: Fine-Gray subdistribution hazard model; report both readmission and death outcomes.

### No external validation
- MIMIC-IV is a single-center dataset (Beth Israel Deaconess Medical Center).
- Mitigation: Internal validation (bootstrap or 10-fold cross-validation); acknowledge need for external validation.

### Measurement limitations
- FiO2 (223835) unitname is "None" in d_items; values are 0-1 fractions but this is not explicitly documented. Verify with data inspection.
- MAP from NBP (220181) may be less frequent than arterial line MAP (220052); use both with NBP as primary.
- HR SD calculated from irregularly spaced charted values (not continuous ECG); this is a limitation of the MIMIC data, not the hypothesis.

### Alternatives not chosen
- **SOFA score:** Requires multiple lab values and may not be computable for all patients in the last 24 h. Deferred to sensitivity analysis.
- **APACHE II:** Requires admission-time data (first 24 h), not discharge-time data. Not directly comparable to the residual instability window.
- **Deep learning (LSTM/Transformer):** Would require continuous time-series data and GPU resources. The binary indicator + gradient-boosted tree comparison is sufficient to test the scientific question (is the binary phenotype informative?) without the complexity of sequence modeling.
- **External validation in EICU:** The EICU dataset is available but uses different itemids and coding. Deferred to a future study.

## 8. Key References

[K1] Wang C, Chen H, Ye J, Fan Y. Heart-rate-derived variability measures and in-hospital mortality in critically ill patients with heart failure and atrial fibrillation: insights from the MIMIC-IV database. *BMC Cardiovasc Disord*. 2026;26(1):487. doi:10.1186/s12872-026-05888-5. PMID: 42026493.
- **Supports:** HR variability (SD, RMSSD) from routine ICU charted data is independently associated with in-hospital mortality.
- **Limitation:** Examined first 24 h of ICU admission, not last 24 h before discharge; restricted to HF+AF population.

[K2] Kalinoski M, Bolden L, Wang Q, Dudley RA, Mesfin N, Ingraham NE. Persistent critical illness in acute hypoxic respiratory failure: A retrospective cohort study. *PLoS One*. 2026;21(9):e0358431. doi:10.1371/journal.pone.0358431. PMID: 42748124.
- **Supports:** Prolonged ICU dependence (PerCI) is associated with a threefold increase in mortality risk; identifies clinical risk factors for persistent critical illness.
- **Limitation:** Focused on AHRF patients with mechanical ventilation; did not examine discharge-time vital sign variability as a prognostic marker.

[K3] Orosz J, Nanjayya VB, Snell G, Levvey B, Shingles H, Ennis S, Pilcher D, Jones D. Epidemiology of medical emergency team calls in patients undergoing lung transplantation. *JHLT Open*. 2026;13:100605. doi:10.1016/j.jhlto.2026.100605. PMID: 42388223.
- **Supports:** Post-ICU clinical deterioration (MET calls) is strongly associated with ICU readmission (27.5% vs 2.2%); hypoxia and tachycardia are the most common triggers.
- **Limitation:** Restricted to lung transplant patients; MET calls are a proxy for deterioration, not a direct measure of vital sign instability at ICU discharge.

## 9. Feasibility Probe Results

See `feasibility-probe.md` and `probe_results.json` for detailed results.

**Completed probe (81.2 s compute):**
- **Cohort:** 74,829 adult ICU stays (age ≥ 18, LOS ≥ 1.0 day)
- **Last-24h data coverage:** HR 99.8%, MAP 98.8%, SpO2 99.5%, FiO2 26.6%
- **ICU readmission (same hadm_id):** 13,652 (18.24%)
- **ICU readmission (72h, cross-admission):** 247 (0.33%)
- **In-hospital death:** 8,930 (11.93%)
- **Discharge destination:** Home/Home Health 46.6%, SNF 18.1%, Rehab 9.5%, Died 12.0%, other 15.8%

**Feasibility verdict: PASS** — Cohort size, event rates, and data coverage all exceed prespecified thresholds. FiO2 coverage (26.6%) is low, so the oxygen requirement component will rely primarily on SpO2 < 94% as the fallback. The primary readmission outcome should be Definition A (same hadm_id, 18.24%).
