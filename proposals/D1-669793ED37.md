# Transition-specific safety signal after ICU discharge: persistent instability, discharge timing, and lower-acuity handoff

## Proposal status and clinical decision

This is an episode-10 substantive successor of assessed valid `[prior hypothesis]`, selected as the parent after the eICU seeds were assessed. The clinically consequential decision is whether a patient being transferred from ICU to lower-acuity care during an off-hours window should receive additional discharge-readiness and handoff review when their recorded physiology remains abnormal, rather than treating all nighttime discharges as equally risky.

The primary hypothesis is:

> Among adult ICU stays discharged alive to a lower-acuity internal destination, persistent late six-hour recorded physiologic abnormality is associated with a larger 48-hour hospital-death risk difference for nighttime versus daytime ICU discharge than resolving or low recorded abnormality, and this interaction is reproducible across hospitals after accounting for pre-discharge care-pathway indicators and observation density.

The null is no interaction, or an interaction that disappears after pathway/density adjustment or varies arbitrarily across hospitals. This is a prespecified observational association and transportability experiment. It does not estimate the effect of delaying discharge, adding staff, changing handoff, or increasing monitoring.

The substantive advance over candidate-68 is to test the clinically narrower transition in which ICU discharge hands a patient to lower-acuity care, and to distinguish a transition-specific physiologic signal from a generic time-of-day association. The design uses `unitdischargelocation` only to define this downstream transition stratum and as a separately reported effect-modification audit; it is never treated as a pre-discharge confounder, mediator-adjusted causal covariate, or quality label. `unitadmitsource`, `unitstaytype`, and `unitvisitnumber` are available before the index discharge and are used as pathway/context variables.

## What is already supported and what remains unresolved

The strongest external claim located and read is Azevedo et al. (BMC Health Services Research 2015; DOI `10.1186/s12913-015-1044-4`; frozen source ID and provenance are recorded in `work/episode-10-support.md`). In five Canadian ICUs, nighttime discharge was associated with higher adjusted hospital mortality (OR 1.29, 95% CI 1.14–1.46). The authors also discuss inconsistent findings and the possibility that premature discharge, ICU capacity, patient flow, staffing, and goals of care explain part of the association. Their data did not resolve whether risk is concentrated in patients who remain physiologically abnormal at the transition, and it is not eICU validation.

The local audit supports only that the eICU snapshot contains patient-level ICU/disposition fields, ICU-relative offsets, time-stamped structured vital streams, hospital identifiers, and EOL records. It establishes neither an association nor adjudicated instability. The unresolved claim is therefore exact and falsifiable: conditional on an observed lower-acuity discharge transition, does a persistent-versus-resolving recorded physiology state modify the nighttime-versus-daytime 48-hour hospital-death association across hospitals?

The outcome is `hospitaldischargestatus = Expired` within a documented 48-hour interval, not post-ICU death from a known mechanism. The exposure is a recorded clock category, not staffing or darkness. The state is an algorithm over structured measurements, not clinical adjudication.

## Dataset, population, and time

Use eICU snapshot `[source checksum]` and catalog `[internal dataset path]` (catalog [source checksum]). Source files are read-only ordinary gzip CSVs; no nested archive member and no cross-dataset join is used.

Primary eligibility:

1. adult ICU stays with numeric `patient.age >= 18`; report the eICU `>89` age convention and missing-age flow;
2. `patient.unitdischargeoffset >= 360`;
3. `patient.unitdischargestatus = Alive`;
4. `unitdischargelocation` exactly in the audited lower-acuity internal whitelist: `Floor`, `Acute Care/Floor`, `Telemetry`, or `Step-Down Unit (SDU)`;
5. at least two valid physiologic streams contributing observations in each of the early and late halves of the final six ICU hours, with the fixed density rules below.

The lower-acuity whitelist is fixed before modeling. `Death`, `Other ICU`, `ICU`, `Other ICU (CABG)`, `Other Hospital`, `Other External`, `Home`, `Skilled Nursing Facility`, `Nursing Home`, `Rehabilitation`, `Other`, `Other Internal`, `Operating Room`, empty and any unrecognized value are not in the primary population. Their counts, outcomes, and state coverage are reported in a disposition audit. A secondary destination audit may compare the lower-acuity whitelist with the other-ICU whitelist if event and overlap gates pass, but that comparison is descriptive effect modification, not a causal contrast.

The index unit is `patientunitstayid`; retain eligible repeated stays and cluster by `patienthealthsystemstayid` and `uniquepid` in sensitivity analyses. A primary one-index-per-hospitalization analysis using the earliest eligible lower-acuity discharge is also required if repeated 48-hour windows overlap materially. Do not use future ICU readmission, hospital discharge destination, or any post-discharge feature as a predictor.

Define the final ICU window as `[unitdischargeoffset - 360, unitdischargeoffset]`, using only observations with ICU-relative `observationoffset` in that interval. Divide it into early `[unitdischargeoffset - 360, unitdischargeoffset - 180)` and late `[unitdischargeoffset - 180, unitdischargeoffset]`. Require at least 60 covered minutes and two flagged 30-minute bins in each half. Carry each accepted stream forward for at most 30 minutes; do not bridge longer gaps. Values outside prespecified physiologic ranges are invalid and become missing. The primary state algorithm is fixed before fitting:

- persistent late instability: at least 60 flagged minutes and at least two flagged bins in the late half, and late flagged minutes no more than 30 minutes lower than early flagged minutes;
- resolving instability: at least 60 flagged minutes and at least two flagged bins in the early half, but late flagged minutes below 60 or at least 30 minutes lower than early flagged minutes;
- low recorded instability: neither half meets the high-instability rule;
- unknown: either half lacks the required coverage or has invalid/unusable state data; never recode unknown as low.

A flagged bin is one carried-forward accepted stream with noninvasive MAP <65 mmHg or heart rate >120; the primary rule requires the thresholds to be met in at least two distinct streams over the six-hour window. The one-stream rule is a prespecified sensitivity, not a post hoc rescue. The continuous late-minus-early flagged-minute change is also reported.

Define nighttime from the recorded `patient.unitdischargetime24`: 19:00:00 through 23:59:59 or 00:00:00 through 06:59:59. Daytime is 07:00:00 through 18:59:59. Invalid or missing clock values are excluded from the clock comparison and reported. Calendar dates and time zones are unavailable; this is a clock-time exposure only.

Follow-up begins strictly after `unitdischargeoffset` and ends at 2,880 minutes after ICU discharge or the available hospital disposition, whichever is earlier. The primary outcome is documented hospital death when `hospitaldischargestatus = Expired` and `0 < hospitaldischargeoffset - unitdischargeoffset <= 2880`. Assign every otherwise eligible stay to one mutually exclusive ascertainment state: death within 48 hours; documented alive hospital discharge by 48 hours; documented hospital disposition after 48 hours (known non-event for this endpoint); or unknown/inconsistent follow-up because status/offset is missing, nonpositive, or cannot establish the interval. Unknown is not survival. An expired disposition after 48 hours is a known 48-hour non-event. Report the full-hospital-death sensitivity separately, without replacing the primary endpoint.

## Estimands, baselines, and analysis

The primary estimand is the hospital-standardized 48-hour hospital-death risk-difference interaction among the lower-acuity transition population:

`[P(death|night,persistent) - P(death|day,persistent)] - [P(death|night,nonpersistent) - P(death|day,nonpersistent)]`.

Here nonpersistent combines resolving and low for primary power; the three-state analysis is secondary. Standardization uses the prespecified eligible population, with hospital fixed effects for the descriptive association and hospital-clustered uncertainty.

Use transparent nested models:

- `M0`: age, gender, ethnicity, hospital admission source, unit admission source, unit type, unit stay type, unit visit number, pre-discharge coverage/recency, and nighttime indicator;
- `Mstate`: M0 plus early/late flagged minutes, state and measurement-density summaries;
- `Minteraction`: Mstate plus nighttime × persistent-state interaction (primary);
- `Mdensity`: M0 plus density, last-observation recency and gap features without physiology;
- `Mlearn`: bounded ridge logistic model using the same permitted pre-discharge variables; a fixed-depth shallow tree is secondary only if event and overlap gates pass.

Do not adjust for `unitdischargelocation` in the primary model because it defines the target transition and is downstream of discharge selection. Do not adjust for ICU length of stay in the primary model; it is a selection-related discharge variable. Label any addition of length of stay or destination as sensitivity/stratification only.

Use five grouped hospital folds from a fixed hash of `hospitalid`; all stays from a hospital remain in one fold. Preprocessing, imputation, tuning, calibration and any threshold selection are learned in training hospitals only. Report the primary interaction from the transparent model plus paired held-out predictions for M0, Mstate, Mdensity and Mlearn. Report Brier score, calibration intercept/slope, AUROC, precision-recall area, and sensitivity/false-negative rate at a sensitivity target selected in training hospitals. The interaction estimand remains primary even if model learning improves discrimination.

Report by hospital, fold, destination category, state and clock category: flow counts, unknown-state and unknown-outcome counts, event rates, coverage, overlap, standardized risk differences, interaction contrast, and hospital-specific estimates. Use hospital-cluster bootstrap intervals; add `patienthealthsystemstayid`- and `uniquepid`-clustered sensitivities and leave-one-hospital-out estimates where cells support them. Do not present hospitals as rankings.

## Falsification and interpretation

The hypothesis is computationally weakened or falsified if:

- the interaction is absent, reverses across hospitals, is driven by one hospital, or vanishes in leave-one-hospital-out and health-system-clustered analyses;
- the interaction disappears after adding pre-discharge pathway variables or density-only features, indicating care selection or measurement intensity rather than a persistent physiology signal;
- the same or larger interaction appears in the earlier six-hour window `[unitdischargeoffset-720, unitdischargeoffset-360]`, in a pre-discharge pseudo-outcome, or after nighttime labels are permuted within hospital and density strata;
- the interaction persists with a deliberately impossible time ordering, revised lab timestamps, post-discharge features, or leakage, indicating implementation error;
- the association is reproduced among other-ICU transfers without a lower-acuity transition-specific pattern, or is confined to a destination category with inadequate overlap;
- sensitivity to one-stream versus two-stream state definitions, 15/120-minute thresholds, carry-forward gap rules, or exclusion of EOL records changes the direction materially;
- the density-only model matches the physiology model while the physiology state adds no stable held-out information.

Supportive results require the pre-fit gates to pass, a positive and directionally consistent interaction in the standardized risk-difference table, hospital-clustered uncertainty excluding no interaction or a declared clinically meaningful margin, persistence in hospital-held-out and pathway/density sensitivities, and conclusions linked to computed estimates and uncertainty. This supports a reproducible recorded association that could justify prospective review of lower-acuity ICU handoffs for patients with persistent abnormal recordings. It does not support delaying discharge, changing staffing, calling a patient unstable, or claiming that the interaction is causal.

Adverse results include no interaction, reversal, a stronger effect in resolving/low states, or a signal explained by density, EOL documentation, pathway variables, or a single hospital. These argue against the proposed transition-specific subgroup and may identify selection or ascertainment mechanisms. Inconclusive results reflect missingness, sparse cells, unknown follow-up, poor overlap, or unstable state definitions; they are not evidence that the hypothesis is false.

## Claims beyond computation

Computationally checkable claims are source-row and join accounting, clock classification, discharge-destination classification, vital-state algorithm, outcome ascertainment, hospital-standardized interaction, held-out model performance, uncertainty, robustness, and whether conclusions follow from those outputs.

Clinical adjudication or another study is required to determine whether abnormal recordings represent true instability, whether discharge was premature, whether a handoff failed, whether deaths were preventable, whether ICU readmission would have been appropriate, and whether changing discharge time or adding review improves outcomes. A prospective multicenter study or target-trial emulation with discharge readiness, staffing, occupancy/bed pressure, care limitations, treatment intent, ward resources, handoff quality and post-ICU monitoring is required for a causal policy claim. This eICU experiment cannot provide those data.
