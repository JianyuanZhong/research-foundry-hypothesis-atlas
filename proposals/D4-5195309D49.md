# Prospective muscle-fat signal and falls in UK Biobank

## Scientific question and claim boundary

A recent UK Biobank analysis reports broad associations between MRI thigh muscle fat infiltration (MFI), mortality and incident diseases [K1]. A separate UK Biobank MRI study reports associations between thigh muscle-volume distribution, grip weakness and falls [K2]. Neither inspected abstract establishes that a temporally prior muscle-fat measure predicts a later falls report independently of visceral adiposity (VAT), baseline function and prior falls. The underlying biological interpretation is also disputed: myosteatosis may mark age-related vulnerability or result from disease/disuse, and its imaging measurement lacks a gold standard [K3].

**Hypothesis to test:** Among UKB MRI visit-2 participants with a later visit-3 falls response at least 366 days after visit 2, higher bilateral posterior-thigh MFI at visit 2 will be associated with reporting at least one fall in the year before visit 3, conditional on MRI VAT, muscle volume, general adiposity, age, sex, smoking/activity, and visit-2 grip, walking pace and falls history. The experiment tests an incremental conditional prognostic association, not whether reducing MFI prevents falls or whether MFI is the biological cause.

Falls can cause injury, fracture, disability and loss of independence. A stable temporal MFI signal beyond VAT and baseline function would justify external validation of muscle-quality measures as a way to identify people for functional assessment; a precise null would argue against adding MFI to fall-risk phenotyping despite broad disease associations. Predictive association alone would not establish a screening threshold or treatment decision. Novelty is limited to the specific lagged, head-to-head MFI-versus-VAT and baseline-function contrast; this is not an exhaustive novelty search.

**Evidence versus hypothesis:** The current literature and dataset support field availability and general associations. The independent, time-ordered MFI-to-falls claim remains unresolved. No outcome result has been fit in discovery.

## Rival explanations, falsification, and interpretation

The leading explanation is that posterior-thigh MFI marks muscle-quality vulnerability not represented by abdominal VAT. The strongest rival is that MFI reflects overall adiposity, age, low activity/disuse, pre-existing weakness, chronic disease, recall/measurement practices, or selection into and return to MRI; any association can be confounded or reverse-causal. Adjusting baseline function, falls and adiposity plus the nonoverlapping report window separates some observable predictions, but it cannot remove unmeasured/time-varying confounding or healthy-attender selection. UKB imaging returnees are not a representative clinical cohort.

- **Supportive:** a positive, reasonably precise adjusted MFI association remains after VAT and baseline function/history, with stable direction in prespecified sensitivities; the MFI term improves held-out calibration/risk estimation beyond the conventional covariate model. This supports a distinct prognostic association in selected UKB returnees and motivates external validation, not a mechanism, causality, treatment effect, or clinical threshold.
- **Adverse:** with adequate events and precision, the adjusted MFI association is null/opposite or too small to meet a clinician-prespecified meaningful margin, while VAT/baseline function/history accounts for the signal and the flexible alternative finds no stable nonlinear/subgroup pattern. This weakens this particular added falls-risk signal, not all biology of myosteatosis.
- **Inconclusive:** wide intervals, sparse outcomes, uncertain code mapping, poor overlap, unstable estimates, or substantial nonresponse/selection sensitivity. Do not treat an imprecise null as refutation.

Computationally checkable outputs include cohort filters, field joins, code use, model fitting, estimates, uncertainty calculations and whether conclusions accurately follow from computed output. Automatic verification cannot decide whether a self-report corresponds to a clinically adjudicated fall, whether the effect is clinically meaningful without an expert-set margin, or whether MFI causes functional decline. Stronger claims need adjudicated fall dates/severity or prospective surveillance, MFI algorithm/quality review, physical-activity and time-varying disease evidence, an external cohort, and ultimately intervention or mechanistic evidence.

## Method comparison, compute, and deferred paths

**Simple baseline:** adjusted logistic regression with linear standardized predictors and an explicit comparison of the model without versus with MFI. Inputs are the exact visit-2 fields above; fitting target is verified visit-3 any-fall self-report. Use the complete eligible participant cohort for inference, bootstrap intervals, and participant-level stratified fivefold prediction evaluation. This is transparent and clinically interpretable for a single prespecified question.

**Substantive alternative:** GAM with smooth terms for MFI and VAT plus an interaction, same participant rows/input variables/target/folds, and nested preprocessing. It could reveal thresholds or an MFI effect concentrated at high VAT that linear main effects miss. Uncertainty and calibration/Brier plus discrimination are reported; predictive gain alone is not a clinical-value argument. If the outcome coding/support cannot sustain spline degrees of freedom, defer the alternative rather than changing population or endpoint.

A neural sequence model is deferred for a question-specific reason: two sparse imaging/function visits and one later recall item do not provide dense longitudinal sequences that would distinguish disease ordering; it would not solve reverse causation or selection. Delphi's disease-sequence method and ALADYNOULLI longitudinal latent trajectories remain revisit candidates only if a later question has adequate dated trajectories. The Oncoformer demonstration is not pursued because this is not a cancer/image-payload question; its unavailable main-paper methods are not assumed from the supplement. These are dispositions, not general method bans.

## Key works

[K1] Zhang et al. report broad associations of UKB thigh MFI with mortality/incident diseases and multi-omics signals. This already bounds broad novelty; the added question is the lagged falls-specific MFI-versus-VAT contrast adjusted for baseline function. Only the Europe PMC abstract was inspected; acquired full-text XML was unreadable in discovery, so no methods/full-text claim is made.

[K2] Kim et al. report a neck-to-knee MRI muscle-volume/distribution framework and associations with grip, falls and mortality in UKB. This supports thigh phenotyping relevance but does not establish the proposed MFI-specific temporal contrast or causality. Publisher abstract/introduction excerpt inspected, not full paper.

[K3] Roe et al. review myosteatosis evidence and state that disease/disuse versus aging remains uncertain and that imaging has no gold-standard measure. Relevant abstract/section-1 excerpt inspected; this bounds interpretation, not direct evidence for this hypothesis.
