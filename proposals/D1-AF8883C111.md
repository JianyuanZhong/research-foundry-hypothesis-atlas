# Episode 58 result: observed late lactate has a positive modeled increment, but its +1-per-100 materiality remains unresolved

## Resolution, clinical importance, and advance

At a 24-hour reassessment after an early recorded norepinephrine entry, clinicians already know the pressor-entry lactate and may have access to an admission-severity estimate. The consequential unresolved question was whether the observed 12-to-24-hour lactate adds enough cross-hospital mortality-risk information to justify prospective evaluation of a repeat-lactate-informed review strategy, rather than merely showing statistical association.

The frozen primary hypothesis was:

> Among APACHE-IVa-complete, repeatedly lactate-measured adults alive and still hospitalized 24 hours after early recorded norepinephrine entry, adding the observed late lactate to a model containing retrospective APACHE IVa predicted hospital mortality and pressor-entry lactate increases uniformly threshold-averaged net benefit across mortality-risk thresholds 0.30–0.50 by at least 1 net true-positive-equivalent review per 100 people.

This result-bearing successor preserves the parent’s population, clocks, variables, A0/A1 models, estimand, baselines, 2,000 copied-hospital refits, guardrails, and classification rules. It changes no scientific design element. The only implementation repair is use of the protocol-specified bootstrap seed 20260951; the attached parent pilot executable retained its pilot-era constant 20260949.

The strongest claim supported by the definitive computation is narrower than the primary hypothesis. In the selected 1,084-person, 31-hospital EICU cohort, adding the already observed late lactate produced a positive cross-hospital modeled increment under the prespecified 0.30–0.50 review-threshold scenario: point ΔINB +1.982 per 100, with a 2,000-refit copied-hospital percentile interval +0.483 to +3.351. All 31 fixed-prediction site deletions were positive, and discrimination and proper scores improved. This supports a positive modeled increment in this selected cohort and scenario.

The unresolved materiality claim remains **inconclusive**, because +1 lies inside the interval: its lower bound is below +1 while its upper bound is above +1. The favorable point estimate, an interval excluding zero, and passing guardrails do not satisfy the locked supportive rule. This does not support or refute the utility of ordering repeat lactate, changed care, cost-effectiveness, or patient benefit.

The substantive advance is resolution of the parent’s prohibited 20-refit pilot into a definitive, independently arithmetic-audited result. It separates a positive modeled signal from the stronger, still-unresolved clinical materiality claim and thereby identifies the next evidentiary need: prospective external validation with timestamps for what clinicians knew, explicit review actions and burdens, and adjudicated threshold preferences.

## Existing evidence and evidence boundary

The full-text XML of Baysan et al. (2022; DOI 10.1097/CCE.0000000000000750; PMCID PMC9444407; frozen source ID `[source checksum]`; [source checksum]) was inspected. It reports external validation in 4,440 critically ill adults with sepsis: adding 24-hour lactate to recalibrated APACHE IV increased the C-statistic from 0.62 to 0.64, and admission plus 24-hour lactate had the smallest AIC. This supports modest serial prognostic information, not decision-curve value, a vasopressor-defined phenotype, multi-hospital transportability, or benefit from obtaining the test. Supplementary material was not inspected.

The full HTML of the decision-curve guide by Vickers et al. (PMCID PMC6261531; frozen source ID `[source checksum]`; [source checksum]) was inspected. It supports net-benefit comparison against treat-all and treat-none over clinically reasonable thresholds and emphasizes that thresholds encode benefit-harm preferences. It does not validate 0.30–0.50, uniform weighting, or +1 per 100 for this mortality-review decision.

The three research-ambition demonstrations and their explicit availability limits were reviewed through `references/research-ambition/README.md`; none is used as substantive evidence for this hypothesis.

## Frozen variables, models, estimand, and baselines

A0 contains age and age squared; Female and Male indicators with Other/Unknown omitted; `t0/1440` and its square; baseline collection lag `(t0-B_offset)/360`; `ln(B)` and its square; and `logit(predictedhospitalmortality)` and its square. A0 is retrospective APACHE-IVa risk recalibration plus pressor-entry lactate.

A1 adds only follow-up measurement age `(L24-F_offset)/720`, `ln(F)`, and `ln(F)^2`. Baseline lactate remains in both models. Binary clearance is not a predictor, estimand, or rescue analysis.

Validation is leave-one-hospital-out. Within each training fold, every continuous feature is winsorized at training 1st/99th percentiles and standardized using training mean and population SD; fixed training transformations are applied to the held-out hospital. Both models are unweighted L2 logistic regressions with intercept, `C=1`, `lbfgs`, `max_iter=10000`, `tol=1e-8`, no class weighting, and model seed 20260924. Held-out outcomes do not affect fitting, preprocessing, thresholds, or calibration.

At each `p` in {0.30,0.31,…,0.50}, compute `NB=TP/N-FP/N*p/(1-p)`. Treat-none is zero and treat-all uses pooled prevalence. A0 is the primary comparator; training-site prevalence prediction is a sanity baseline.

The primary estimand is:

`ΔINB = 100/(0.50-0.30) × integral_0.30^0.50 [NB_A1(p)-NB_A0(p)] dp`

using the trapezoid rule over all 21 thresholds. The +1-per-100 margin and uniform threshold weighting are transparent working criteria, not validated preferences.

For uncertainty, sample the 31 hospitals with replacement 2,000 times using seed 20260951. Copies of an original hospital remain one held-out group; all copies are excluded from that hospital’s training fold; multiplicity weights both training and held-out contributions. Refit preprocessing and both models in every replicate. Percentile 95% intervals are computed from finite converged replicates.

## Falsification classification and meaning

The frozen hierarchy is:

- **Supportive:** ΔINB 95% lower bound >=+1 and all calibration/default/threshold/site guardrails pass.
- **Adverse to materiality:** 95% upper bound <+1 but >=0.
- **Opposite:** 95% upper bound <0.
- **Inconclusive:** interval includes +1 or zero without another branch; also mixed if the lower bound clears +1 but a guardrail fails.
- **Not computable:** any required source, flow, sample, fit, bootstrap, deletion, or threshold gate fails.

The observed interval [+0.483,+3.351] includes +1, so the definitive primary label is **inconclusive**. A numerically correct report calling the materiality hypothesis “supported” must fail verification. Conversely, “no prognostic value,” “no effect,” “repeat lactate is harmful,” and “repeat testing should be abandoned” are also unsupported.

Supportive results would have established only a material out-of-hospital modeled review increment under the frozen scenario utilities in selected, repeatedly measured, APACHE-complete EICU survivors. Adverse results would have argued against this +1 target, not all prognostic information. Opposite results would have indicated worse modeled decisions, not harm caused by testing. The current inconclusive materiality result preserves uncertainty while its interval above zero supports only the narrower positive modeled increment.

Threshold narrowing, alternative weighting, binary clearance, the pilot interval, and sparse exploratory models cannot rescue or reclassify the primary result.
