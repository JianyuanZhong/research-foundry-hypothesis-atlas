# Working proposal (Generation, episode 17) — negative-control-outcome discriminator

Branch deliverable (distinct from the competing-risks/dose-response branch): attack the
**confounding-by-indication / reverse-causation rival R** directly with a *target-trial-emulation
negative-control-outcome* design, plus a double-robust IPTW estimate and a naive→adjusted ladder.
Parent: `[prior hypothesis]` (Lead, valid, refs complete). Origin: unused expert seed
`[starting question]`. Status: design frozen; **no cohort outcome was computed in discovery** —
every prevalence/power figure below is a prescribed compile-time probe, not an observed result.

## 1. Unresolved question, leading claim, rival
After the post-stabilization landmark t\* (first time sustained MAP≥65 mmHg and vasopressor-free ≥2 h
while still invasively ventilated) in MIMIC-IV ICU stays, does sustained deep sedation (charted RASS
itemid 228096 in [-5,-3]) causally delay **successful liberation** from mechanical ventilation?

- **Leading claim (C):** sedation depth is on the causal path — continued deep sedation after t\*
  prolongs time to successful liberation. [K1] is the closest prior evidence (adjusted HR 0.90 for
  time to extubation).
- **Strongest rival (R):** confounding by indication / reverse causation — patients kept deeply
  sedated are systematically sicker and more encephalopathic; the charted RASS depth that is treated
  as "exposure" partly **is** the severity/arousal state. [K3] shows RASS depth carries a strong
  mortality gradient (aOR 4.93 at RASS ≤-4), so depth plausibly marks severity.

These make **different predictions on a negative-control outcome** (below), which is what makes the
test discriminating rather than merely predictive.

## 2. The discriminating test

### 2a. Primary — negative-control outcome (the R-attack)
A negative-control outcome is a post-t\* event that continued deep sedation should **not** plausibly
*mechanistically* cause, but that shares the same severity/indication pathway as death (the R pathway).
If "deep sedation" is only a marker of severity, it should predict the negative control with a similar
effect size as the primary outcome; if deep sedation is causal for liberation, the negative-control
effect should be near-null.

- **Negative-control outcome (NCO):** *new renal-replacement-therapy (RRT) initiation or vasopressor
  escalation in the 24 h window (t\*, t\*+24h]* among patients **not** already on that therapy at t\*.
  - New RRT = first `icu/procedureevents` row after t\* with itemid ∈ {225802 Dialysis-CRRT,
    225803 Dialysis-CVHD, 225809 Dialysis-CVHHDF, 225955 Dialysis-SCUF, 225441 Hemodialysis} for a
    stay with no such row in (t\*-24h, t\*].
  - Vasopressor escalation = new/↑ `icu/inputevents` norepinephrine (itemid 221906) or vasopressin
    (222315) rate at a time > t\* for a stay not on either agent at t\*.
  - Composite NCO = RRT-initiation OR vasopressor-escalation within 24 h.
- **Prespecified equivalence/reference margin:** estimate the NCO effect (risk difference RD_NCO and
  OR_NCO) on the same adjusted scale as the primary outcome. **R is weakened** if the deep-sedation
  effect on NCO is within the equivalence margin **|RD_NCO| ≤ 0.03** (equivalently OR_NCO ∈
  [0.75, 1.33]) with a 90% CI inside the margin. **R is supported** if OR_NCO ≥ 1.33 (deep sedation
  predicts the unrelated organ outcome with a similar magnitude to the primary effect). Margin is
  prescribed here and may be revised by the Lead before compilation.

### 2b. Secondary — double-robust target-trial emulation (IPTW + outcome model)
Emulate a trial under a stated assignment protocol: **"at t\*, arm = maintain deep sedation
(fraction of RASS observations in [-5,-3] over (t\*, t\*+12h] ≥ 0.5) vs. arm = lighter sedation
(< 0.5)"**, with treatment assigned at t\* and outcome followed from t\*.
- Propensity score e(X) via logistic regression on the M2 covariate set; IPTW weights = 1/e for the
  deep arm, 1/(1-e) for the light arm; trim at the 1st/99th percentile.
- Doubly-robust estimate: weighted outcome regression (IPTW-weighted logistic/Cox) → report RD and HR
  with bootstrap 95% CI. Report both the IPTW-only and outcome-model-only estimates.
- **Naive→adjusted ladder:** M0 (naive, no adjustment) → M1 (+ severity: SOFA-proxy components, age,
  sex, admission type, Charlson-via-ICD) → M2 (+ GCS itemid 220739/223900/223901 + sedative-drug
  class from inputevents: propofol 222168, midazolam 221668, fentanyl 221744, dexmedetomidine
  225150/229420, morphine 225154, hydromorphone 221833). The **attenuation of the effect across
  M0→M2** quantifies how much raw association is confounded; the residual M2 effect is the causal
  estimate under exchangeability.

## 4. Population, temporal boundaries, outcome
- Adults (anchor_age ≥ 18) with an invasive-ventilation stay; landmark t\* = first timestamp where
  sustained MAP ≥ 65 (≥2 consecutive readings) **and** vasopressor-free ≥ 2 h **and** still invasively
  ventilated (invasive-vent itemid 225792 / mech vent 226260; extubation 227194; unplanned
  225468/225477).
- Time zero = t\*. Exposure window (t\*, t\*+12h]. Follow-up 7 d from t\*.
- **Primary outcome:** successful liberation = live extubation/vent-discontinuation with no re-intubation
  within 48 h, within 7 d of t\*. (This branch keeps the parent's binary primary endpoint; the sibling
  branch replaces it with the competing-risks estimand — that is the distinct deliverable split.)
- **Permuted-label control:** reassign exposure labels at random (preserving prevalence) 1000×;
  the observed effect must lie outside the permuted distribution (prespecified α=0.05).
- Censoring at ICU discharge/death handled by the outcome model; sensitivity: treat death as
  competing event in a cause-specific Cox check (does not replace the sibling's primary estimand).

## 5. Baseline vs. alternative — what the comparison resolves
- **Baseline (B0):** single naive/newly-adjusted logistic or Cox model of liberation on the binary
  deep-sedation exposure.
- **Alternative (A0 = this branch's core):** target-trial-emulation IPTW + outcome model, evaluated
  jointly on the primary outcome **and** the negative-control outcome.
- **Scientific uncertainty resolved:** the baseline cannot tell whether a positive association is
  causal or is shared severity confounding. A0's NCO contrast can: a near-null NCO effect alongside a
  real liberation effect supports C; a similar-magnitude NCO effect supports R. This is not a chase
  for a small predictive gain — it changes which explanation the data support.
- *Recorded deferrals:* the sibling branch's dose-response gradient contrast and the competing-risks
  cumulative-incidence estimand are deliberately **not** repeated here (avoid duplication); a time-varying
  MSM/g-computation alternative was also considered and deferred to keep this branch's A0 self-contained.

## 6. Prespecified interpretation rules
- **Supportive of C:** M2-adjusted liberation RD/HR shows a meaningful deep-sedation delay (e.g.
  HR ≤ 0.90 or RD ≥ 0.05 later liberation) **and** NCO within equivalence margin. → continue toward
  causal claim, propose confirmatory design.
- **Adverse / supports R:** NCO effect similar in magnitude to the primary effect (OR_NCO ≥ 1.33) →
  report that much of the raw association is shared severity confounding; revise C toward "marker".
- **Inconclusive:** wide CIs overlapping both the null and the margin; or NCO estimable only in a small
  subset with unstable weights. → state missing evidence, do not claim either.
- Permuted-label effect must be null; if not, treat the pipeline as misspecified and report as inconclusive.

## 7. Falsification criteria
C is falsified (for this data) if, after M2 adjustment, the deep-sedation effect on successful
liberation is null **while** the NCO effect is non-null and comparable — then depth behaves as a
severity marker. R is falsified if the NCO effect is within the equivalence margin **and** the
liberation effect is non-null and survives IPTW.

## 8. Honest limitations / what needs adjudication or another study
- Single-centre retrospective; no randomisation → exchangeability is assumed, not guaranteed. No causal
  truth is claimed.
- Charted RASS is intermittently sampled and nurse-driven; deep charted RASS may reflect an unmeasured
  indication (agitation, ARDS, ICP, status epilepticus). **Clinical adjudication of sedation indication
  is required** and unavailable in structured data — the NCO only probes *shared* confounding, not
  the specific indication.
- The NCO equivalence margin is prescribed, not externally validated; a wrong margin can mislabel R.
- RRT/vasopressor escalation are themselves treatment decisions (confounded by indication), so NCO is
  a *partial* probe. A truly transportable causal answer needs a multicentre, protocolised or
  instrumental-variable / target-trial-with-reason design — another study.
- Death-vs-failed-liberation conflation is handled here only by sensitivity analysis; the definitive
  competing-risks estimand is the sibling branch's deliverable.
