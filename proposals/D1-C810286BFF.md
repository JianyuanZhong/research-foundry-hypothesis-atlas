# Hypothesis Proposal: TACE Timing and HCC Progression

## Unresolved Question

**Primary Question:** Does the time interval between initial HCC diagnosis and first-line TACE (transarterial chemoembolization) treatment predict progression-free survival (PFS), independent of tumor burden and patient characteristics?

**Secondary Question:** Is there an optimal "window of opportunity" for TACE initiation that maximizes PFS, beyond a simple binary comparison of early vs. delayed treatment?

## Clinical Importance

1. **Treatment Timing Optimization**: Current HCC guidelines recommend TACE for intermediate-stage disease, but optimal timing relative to diagnosis is not well-defined. Understanding whether delayed treatment (due to patient preference, availability, or perceived stability) compromises outcomes could inform shared decision-making.

2. **Resource Allocation**: TACE requires specialized expertise and resources. Identifying patients who benefit most from prompt intervention vs. those who can safely delay could optimize healthcare delivery.

3. **Prognostic Value**: Treatment timing itself may serve as a surrogate marker for disease aggressiveness or patient selection quality.

## Substantive Advance Over Existing Knowledge

Existing literature on HCC treatment focuses on:
- Stage at diagnosis [K1]
- Child-Pugh score
- BCLC classification
- Tumor burden metrics

This proposal addresses the **temporal dimension** of treatment initiation, which is:
- Understudied in HCC management
- Potentially modifiable (unlike stage at diagnosis)
- May capture unmeasured factors (patient preferences, healthcare access, disease natural history)

The approach draws inspiration from [K1] which demonstrates that longitudinal disease trajectory learning from structured EHR data can identify meaningful disease progression patterns. By isolating treatment timing as a distinct temporal feature, this proposal tests whether similar longitudinal learning principles apply to HCC treatment sequencing.

## Data Requirements and Bindings

### Primary Dataset: HCC Clinical Registry

**Source Files:**
- `HCC/data_诊断_7504718184492840569.csv` - Diagnosis records (患者主索引，就诊号，诊断名称，诊断类型)
- `HCC/data_手术_8024330590283626027.csv` - Procedures including TACE (患者主索引，就诊号，手术，开始时间，结束时间，手术来源)
- `HCC/data_用药_5693407050835159466.csv` - Medications (患者主索引，就诊号，用药，开始时间，结束时间，用药方式，药品类型)
- `HCC/data_检验_609065997844652188.csv` - Laboratory results (患者主索引，就诊号，检查，定性结果，定量结果，目标类型，检查时间)

**Key Variables:**
1. **Primary Exposure**: Time from first HCC diagnosis to first TACE procedure
   - Categorized: 0-30 days (early), 31-90 days (intermediate), 91+ days (delayed)
   - Continuous: days elapsed

2. **Outcome**: Progression-free survival (PFS)
   - Defined as time from TACE to next progression event (diagnosis of recurrence, new tumor, or treatment failure)
   - Categorical: PFS ≤ 6 months, 6-12 months, > 12 months, or censored (no progression at study end)

3. **Covariates:**
   - Tumor burden: Number of tumors, largest tumor size (from diagnosis records)
   - Child-Pugh score (from lab results: bilirubin, albumin, INR, creatinine)
   - Performance status (from clinical documents or encounters)
   - Concomitant therapies: Sorafenib, lenvatinib, immunotherapy (from medications)
   - Age, sex (from basic info)

4. **Time Field**: All timestamps are in format `YYYY-MM-DD HH:MM:SS`

### Population Definition

- **Inclusion**: Patients with confirmed HCC diagnosis (诊断名称 contains "肝癌" or "肝细胞癌")
- **Exclusion**:
  - No TACE procedure performed
  - Missing follow-up data after TACE
  - Pre-existing cirrhosis with non-HCC malignancy
- **Temporal Boundaries**:
  - Study start: 2000-01-01 (earliest diagnosis date in dataset)
  - Study end: 2015-12-31 (latest diagnosis date in dataset)

## Analysis Plan

### Baseline Approach
**Cox proportional hazards model** comparing PFS across TACE timing categories:
```
hazard ~ timing_category + tumor_burden + child_pugh + age + sex + concomitant_therapy
```

### Learned Alternative
**Random forest survival model** or **DeepSurv neural network**:
- Advantages: Non-parametric baseline, handles non-linearity, provides feature importance
- Scientific information gained: May reveal complex interactions between timing and other factors that linear models miss

### Key Comparisons
1. Early vs. Delayed TACE (binary)
2. Continuous timing effect (per 30-day increment)
3. Non-linear timing effect (quadratic term)

## Falsification Criteria

**Hypothesis will be falsified if:**
- No significant association between TACE timing and PFS (p > 0.05 after adjustment)
- Delayed TACE shows superior or equal PFS compared to early TACE
- The association is entirely explained by confounding (e.g., delayed TACE patients have better baseline characteristics)

**Supportive Evidence:**
- Early TACE (0-30 days) significantly improves PFS compared to delayed TACE
- Optimal timing window identified (e.g., 31-90 days performs best)
- Effect persists after adjusting for tumor burden, Child-Pugh, and other covariates

**Inconclusive:**
- Weak association with wide confidence intervals
- Effect modified by subgroup (e.g., only in Child-Pugh A patients)

## Missing Evidence and Clinical Adjudication

1. **Tumor Size at Diagnosis**: Not directly measured in diagnosis records; may need to infer from imaging reports in clinical_documents or pathology records.

2. **Progression Definition**: Need to verify that "diagnosis of recurrence" truly represents progression and not new primary tumor.

3. **Concomitant Therapy Confounding**: Patients receiving systemic therapy may have different timing patterns; need to stratify or adjust for this.

4. **External Validation**: This analysis is internal to the HCC dataset; external validation would require another cohort.

## Expected Impact

If supported, this hypothesis could:
- Guide shared decision-making about treatment timing
- Inform clinical pathways for HCC management
- Identify high-risk patients who need prompt intervention

## Method Comparison Rationale

**Baseline (Cox model)**: Provides interpretable hazard ratios, handles censoring, establishes clear null hypothesis.

**Learned Alternative (DeepSurv/Random Forest)**:
- Could reveal non-linear timing effects (e.g., optimal window at 45 days, not just early vs. late)
- May identify important interactions (e.g., timing × Child-Pugh)
- Provides feature importance for clinical prioritization

**Scientific Information Gained**: The learned model may reveal that the relationship between timing and PFS is U-shaped or has an optimal point, which a linear Cox model would miss. This would fundamentally change the clinical recommendation from "treat early" to "treat within X days."
