# Bone–muscle discordance and incident fracture in UK Biobank

## Scientific question and hypothesis

Among UK Biobank participants with assessment-instance 2 DXA and grip measurements, does low femoral-neck BMD combined with low relative grip strength identify excess incident fracture risk beyond low BMD alone? The primary hypothesis is additive synergy: the joint low-BMD/low-grip group has a higher 5-year incident fracture risk than predicted by the sum of the low-BMD and low-grip effects. A compatible secondary observation is a higher subsequent self-reported fall rate, but falls are not treated as a causal mediator.

The clinically important unresolved claim is whether muscle-function discordance adds information to a BMD-centered fracture-risk decision. Existing evidence supports a cross-sectional joint association and motivates an integrated bone–muscle–function perspective, but does not establish prospective synergy. The closest systematic review found only imprecise, nonsignificant additive and multiplicative interaction in a small prospective evidence base. This experiment therefore tests a boundary of that explanation rather than assuming osteosarcopenia is a distinct diagnostic entity.

The primary outcome is first incident fracture identified from inpatient diagnosis fields `41270-0.*` and `41280-0.*`, restricted to prespecified fracture ICD-10 prefixes (S12, S42 and T17) and requiring a diagnosis date after index. Secondary outcomes are 1-, 2- and 5-year cumulative incidence, hip/proximal-femur fracture, and subsequent self-reported falls from `2296-3.0` if available. Do not use same-assessment falls as a mediator.

The exploratory prior audit found 45,125 instance-2 participants with BMD plus grip and 137 post-assessment first-fracture participants after excluding prior registry fracture, including 32 within one year and 118 within five years. These are feasibility counts, not results for the final cohort or clinical adjudication.

## Baseline and substantive alternative

The baseline is a transparent cause-specific Cox model for fracture with death as a competing event, including continuous femoral-neck BMD, continuous relative grip (best grip divided by body mass if body mass is available in the verified assessment schema; otherwise use the prespecified available grip measure and state the limitation), age, sex, and assessment-instance covariates. Estimate 5-year cumulative incidence and the additive interaction contrast:
`RERI = risk(low BMD, low grip) - risk(low BMD only) - risk(low grip only) + risk(reference)`.
Use robust standard errors and a patient-level train/validation split fixed by `eid`; no outcome-derived threshold selection in the validation set.

The substantive alternative is a joint frailty/competing-risk model that estimates participant-level frailty and correlated fall susceptibility, with BMD and grip entering as time-varying or baseline exposures and subsequent falls as a secondary process. It can reveal whether the apparent joint association is explained by a shared latent frailty/fall-prone state that the baseline loses. Fit it only if event support and convergence permit; otherwise report the baseline and defer the alternative rather than changing the estimand. A neural sequence model is not selected: with approximately 137 exploratory incident events and a single dated assessment, it would add flexibility without resolving the scientific uncertainty.

## Analysis, uncertainty and falsification

Primary analysis: additive and multiplicative interaction estimates for 5-year incident fracture, with 95% confidence intervals and calibration of cumulative incidence. Report missingness, follow-up, event counts, and sensitivity analyses excluding events within 90 days, restricting to hip/proximal-femur codes, and excluding prior falls. A secondary model includes prior fall status (`2296-2.0`) and activity/health covariates only when available; this is a rival-explanation analysis, not proof of mediation.

Supportive result: the joint group has materially higher observed 5-year risk than the additive baseline prediction, with a positive interaction estimate whose interval excludes zero, stable across code definitions and early-event exclusions, and the joint frailty model does not fully explain the excess. This supports incremental risk stratification, not a mechanism.

Adverse result: no interaction, or the excess disappears after prior-fall/frailty adjustment, or the joint model shows the association is shared frailty. This argues against using discordance as an independent fracture-risk signal.

Inconclusive result: wide intervals, sparse events, unstable code sensitivity, or nonconvergence. Do not call this a null mechanism; repeat with adjudicated fracture data or a larger cohort.

Clinical adjudication is required for non-inpatient fractures, fracture site specificity, fall causality, and treatment decisions. The available registry can support a computable incident-code association and uncertainty, but not establish osteosarcopenia, mediation, or clinical benefit.

The solver must produce the frozen cohort and event table, baseline interaction/cumulative-incidence estimates with uncertainty, the joint frailty fit or a documented convergence failure, sensitivity tables, and a conclusion classified as supportive, adverse or inconclusive. Completion is the fitted estimates and uncertainty, not confirmation of the hypothesis.

Deferred alternatives: (1) a learned sequence model is deferred because the available dated measurement structure and event count do not make its extra flexibility scientifically informative; revisit with repeated longitudinal outcomes or substantially larger event support. (2) A mediation analysis of falls is deferred because same-assessment and subsequent self-reported falls cannot identify causal mediation without richer exposure timing and clinical adjudication. (3) A cross-sectional replication of the NHANES joint stratification is not the primary advance because it would not resolve the prospective uncertainty.
