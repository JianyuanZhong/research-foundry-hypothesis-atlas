# Proposal: history- and capture-specificity of concordant post-TACE recorded pathways

## Episode, parent, and scientific deliverable

This Episode-30 successor starts from assessed-valid parent `[prior hypothesis]`. It preserves the parent’s one-first-index HCC population, encounter-constrained timed TACE-like index, all-index early-transition accounting through day 45, L=14 and L=45 information boundaries, component-resolved AFP/albumin/total-bilirubin profile, procedure-plus-same-encounter-order C2 endpoint, procedure-only/broad/diagnostic/non-liver/observation-loss states, patient-level temporal split, clustered uncertainty, and noncausal recorded-care boundary.

The distinct remaining uncertainty is specificity and history dependence. C2 is more defensible than a procedure-only record, but a patient who is easier to observe locally, has a dense prior episode history, or receives many generic procedures may be more likely to have both a procedure and a corroborating order recorded. The parent’s capture covariates and negative endpoints are useful safeguards; this child makes the scientific comparison explicit: does the laboratory profile predict a liver-directed concordant recorded pathway more than it predicts generic local procedure capture, and does that contrast persist across prior recorded-care history?

The actual future deliverable is newly constructed, fitted, and estimated:

1. A row-level provenance and index manifest for every eligible patient, including patients with early events, incomplete labs, and no later local record.
2. Frozen profile, pre-index episode-history strata, source-specific capture features, and mutually exclusive C2/C1/C0/diagnostic/non-liver/observation-loss transitions.
3. Standardized CIF contrasts for each outcome, plus the primary liver-specificity contrast, history interaction, overlap/positivity diagnostics, and observation-weight sensitivity.
4. A transparent nested competing-risk baseline and a substantive joint marked-point-process alternative fitted to the same split and endpoints.
5. Locked test predictions, calibration and dynamic scoring, patient-clustered intervals, permutation/placebo results, and an interpretation file linking every statement to an output, interval, model, boundary, endpoint state, split, and frozen criterion.

Completion means these estimands and falsification checks are computed or explicitly marked infeasible, with conclusions limited to recorded local evidence. A supportive result is not required.

## Evidence, unresolved question, and falsifiable hypothesis

The strongest available evidence supports only data availability and an operational endpoint, not an association. The frozen HCC snapshot has dated encounters, procedures, non-drug orders, examinations, medications, and laboratory records linked by patient/encounter identifiers. The procedure-plus-order endpoint is computable as a strict recorded concordance. The catalog also establishes that diagnosis rows have no native time, clinical documents/pathology have no usable row time, and vitals/transfers/front_page are identifier-only. No fitted result or clinical adjudication is available.

The unresolved claim is:

> After retaining all index patients and their early competing transitions, does a favorable early AFP/albumin/total-bilirubin profile have a larger association with a later C2 concordant liver-directed recorded episode than with a comparable non-liver recorded procedure, after standardization to the same pre-index episode history and local observation opportunity?

The primary hypothesis is directional and noncausal: the favorable profile will have a lower standardized 320-day C2 CIF than the discordant profile, while its contrast for non-liver procedure capture will be smaller; equivalently, the liver-specificity contrast will be negative and practically meaningful. The primary design margin is an absolute 3 percentage-point difference for C2-minus-non-liver specificity, with a patient-level 95% interval excluding zero. This is a prespecified decision margin, not an expected or observed result.

The secondary history hypothesis is that the C2 contrast differs by prior recorded liver-directed episode history. A stable C2-minus-non-liver contrast across history strata supports transport across observed local histories; concentration in one stratum, especially dense prior care, supports a history/capture explanation. History is recorded local history, not complete treatment history.

Clinical importance: a profile that predicts a liver-directed recorded pathway beyond generic local contact could justify prospective evaluation of whether early laboratory monitoring should trigger an adjudicated reassessment workflow. A profile that predicts generic local procedures or only C2 among densely observed patients would warn against treating the association as disease-specific. Neither outcome establishes that TACE was intended, delivered, technically successful, beneficial, or followed by response/progression.

## Outcome and measurement ontology

Aggregate each child source before joining on the exact two-key `(patient master index, encounter number)`. Define mutually exclusive first post-boundary states with an absorbing early-transition version from t0:

- C2: a strict timed TACE-like procedure episode with `Surgery Start Time`, linked on both keys to an `orders` row whose `Non-drug Order` passes the training-frozen TACE-like order screen, with `Order Time` or `Start Time` within [-24,+24] hours of the earliest valid procedure episode time. This order corroborates local documentation; it is not intent, completion, or technical-success evidence.
- C1: strict timed TACE-like procedure episode without the C2 order concordance.
- C0: timed broad/ambiguous/mixed liver-directed procedure episode not classified C1/C2.
- D: diagnostic/technical-only episode.
- N: timed non-liver procedure episode, defined by the training-frozen procedure ontology as neither liver-directed nor D. N is a generic recorded-procedure control, not a benign clinical control.
- O_loss: no observed local record after the prespecified observation gap.
- Retain other/unknown timed procedural labels in an explicit audit and do not silently force them into N.

The primary specificity estimand uses N, not an invented biological negative outcome. A secondary workflow tier adds a liver/upper-abdominal examination from `examinations` using `Examination` and `Start Time`; `Examination Findings` and `Examination Diagnosis` are narrative sensitivity-only fields, never radiology adjudication. Status-sensitive C2 variants use exact observed `Order Status` values and a declared non-canceled subset; status semantics must be reported, not assumed.

Primary matching is same encounter and [-24,+24] hours. Mandatory sensitivity analyses use ±6 and ±72 hours, same-patient cross-encounter matching, order `order time` versus `start time`, and each exact status rule. Preserve source row IDs/labels and all candidate matches in `source_concordance_audit.csv`. Do not use the order that defines C2 as a predictor, plan covariate, or capture variable for that same endpoint.

## Estimands and analysis

Let X be favorable versus discordant profile, H the pre-index history stratum, and O the pre-index source-specific observation vector. For outcome k in {C2,C1,C0,D,N,O_loss}, estimate the predictive standardized CIF contrast:

`Delta^L_k(h) = CIF_k(h | X=favorable, standardized to the observed joint distribution of H,O) - CIF_k(h | X=discordant, same distribution of H,O)`.

Estimate separately for L in {14,45}, with h=30,90,320 and post-L through day 365. The primary child estimand is:

`S^L(h) = Delta^L_C2(h) - Delta^L_N(h)`

with primary `S^45(320)`. Report `Delta_C2`, `Delta_C1`, `Delta_C0`, `Delta_N`, `Delta_D`, and `Delta_O_loss` alongside it. Use absolute differences as primary; report ratios only with stable denominators.

History heterogeneity is:

`I_H^L(h) = Delta^L_C2(h | H1) - Delta^L_C2(h | H0)`

and, as the specificity check, the corresponding difference-in-differences `[Delta_C2(H1)-Delta_N(H1)]-[Delta_C2(H0)-Delta_N(H0)]`. Use common-support restrictions declared from training only. H2 and sparse strata remain explicitly reported or are labeled infeasible; they are not collapsed into H0 without a frozen rule.

The full-index estimand is the vector of first-transition probabilities from t0 through day 45, including C2/C1/C0/D/N/O_loss. The day-45 continuity estimate is reported for parent comparison, not substituted for the all-index estimand. None of these is causal or a treatment effect; early-event patients are not “recovered” into the post-L analysis.

Use 2010–2022 fit, 2023 tuning, and 2024-01-01–2025-01-01 locked test when event support permits; otherwise declare the deterministic patient-hash split before fitting. No patient crosses splits. Fit all lexical dictionaries, assay pairing rules, profile rules, history bins, standardization, observation weights, margins, and common-support rules on training only. Use patient-clustered bootstrap (at least 500 patient resamples, or a declared equivalent) for intervals. The test set remains locked until all choices are frozen.

### Transparent baseline B_specificity

Fit nested regularized discrete-time cause-specific hazard/multi-state models on the same episode-time grid and competing states:

- B0: age, sex, index encounter time/department, diagnosis ascertainment, pre-index encounter and source-specific capture counts, H, days since/count of prior liver-directed episodes, and time-varying observation opportunity.
- B1: B0 plus the L-boundary component values, changes, exact measurement delays, qualitative/inequality flags, counts and missingness for AFP/albumin/bilirubin.
- For every k, compute standardized CIFs under X=favorable and X=discordant using the same H/O distribution. The order defining C2 is excluded from predictors; post-L source rows are not baseline inputs.
- Fit the full-index transition model with absorbing early states, and the parent-compatible day-45 continuity model separately.
- Report coefficients, C2-minus-N specificity, history interactions, calibration, Brier score, dynamic log score, overlap/positivity and patient-clustered intervals.

B0 is essential: if B1’s apparent C2 contrast is matched by a non-liver contrast, the signal is generic capture rather than liver-directed specificity. B1 is transparent and auditable, not a claim that adjustment identifies a biological effect.

### Substantive alternative M_marked_history

Fit, on the same patients, L boundaries, states, split, and uncertainty procedure, a joint irregular-time marked point-process/multi-state model. A patient-specific local observation intensity generates opportunities for any recorded source; conditional on an opportunity, a marked event process generates liver-directed C2/C1/C0, diagnostic/technical, or non-liver marks. Include:

- time-varying exposure to H and O, with H-specific baseline intensity and a shared observation frailty;
- source-specific procedure/order/examination emission probabilities and timing kernels for the C2 measurement layer;
- profile effects on liver-directed marks and on generic non-liver marks, with a prespecified profile-by-H interaction;
- an absorbing observation-loss process after the declared gap;
- weakly informative priors or penalization fixed before test access, plus prior sensitivity because no gold-standard adjudication identifies source sensitivity/specificity.

Fit target: joint training likelihood of dated source streams and first-transition marks, with held-out source/mark prediction and calibrated CIFs as evaluation targets. The scientific output is the estimated profile contrast in the liver-specific mark after separating shared observation intensity, not a latent treatment-response estimate. M_marked_history can reveal whether source discordance, prior-history frailty, and generic contact explain a hard C2 label; B_specificity cannot represent these as a joint event-generation mechanism. Prefer it scientifically only if it converges, passes posterior/predictive checks, improves held-out mark log score/Brier/calibration or materially narrows/clarifies `S` without prior-dominated intervals, and gives qualitatively stable history contrasts. Otherwise retain B1 as the primary report and mark M as deferred/inconclusive.

CPU is the default. The transparent baseline is a tabular fit; M is repeated likelihood optimization or sampling over aggregated patient episodes. Discovery used no model fit and no GPU allocation. ## Falsification, interpretation, and uncertainty gates

1. **Parent continuity gate.** Reproduce the parent day-45 procedure-only strict contrast, C2/C1 accounting, plan/context summaries, and all-index early transitions. Failure is a definition/package problem, not evidence about specificity.
2. **Generic-capture negative control.** Require the same profile contrast for N and for any post-L local encounter/source contact. A similar or larger `Delta_N` than `Delta_C2`, or `S` near zero, is adverse to a liver-directed interpretation.
3. **History transport.** Report C2, N, and S within H0/H1/H2 where supported. A materially different S by H, reversal, or concentration only in H1 is adverse to a history-invariant interpretation. Sparse/unsupported H strata are inconclusive, not null.
4. **Outcome-measurement checks.** Recompute C2 at ±6/24/72 hours, `order time` versus `start time`, same-encounter versus cross-encounter, and exact status/non-canceled rules. Similar profile contrasts after order-time permutation within patient, preserving order names and encounter structure, are adverse to the time-concordant interpretation.
5. **Endpoint-tier specificity.** Compare C2, C1, C0, workflow-supported C2, D and N. If an association is present only in C1/C0/D or procedure-only records, it does not support a concordant liver-directed claim.
6. **Profile timing placebo.** Permute eligible lab times within patient while preserving assay/specimen/count structure, and use pre-index placebo windows. Preserved `S` favors timing/capture artifact.
7. **Observation-process sensitivity.** Compare unweighted, observation-weighted, and complete-pair analyses; vary the 30/90/180-day observation gap; inspect weight truncation, positivity, overlap, and loss-to-observation CIF. No analysis may treat O_loss as death or silently remove it.
8. **Early-transition integrity.** Compare L=14 and L=45 while retaining all t0–45 absorbing events. A result that exists only after conditioning on reaching L is not a full-index result.
9. **Data-quality and lexical falsification.** Verify duplicate-key aggregation, invalid times, assay vocabulary and status stability by split; rerun with qualitative/inequality values treated as missing. If the dictionary or timestamps are unstable, report infeasibility/inconclusive.
10. **Alternative integrity.** Require M convergence, posterior/predictive checks, held-out source/mark metrics, calibration, and prior-sensitivity intervals. Nonconvergence, nonidentification, or prior domination cannot support a biological or pathway claim.

Supportive evidence requires parent continuity; a practically meaningful negative `S^14(320)` and `S^45(320)` with compatible intervals; smaller/null N and generic-contact contrasts; stable C2 timing/status sensitivities; adequate overlap/positivity; no material placebo signal; interpretable H0/H1 estimates; and a converged M result that is not prior-dominated. Even then, the conclusion is only that the profile is specifically associated with a corroborated local liver-directed recorded-care tier in this snapshot, motivating prospective clinical adjudication.

Adverse evidence includes similar N/generic-contact contrasts, `S` near zero or reversal, dependence on dense H1 history, C2 collapse under status/time sensitivity, C1/C0/D concentration, placebo persistence, poor overlap, or observation-weight instability. These favor generic workflow, history, coding, or measurement explanations.

Inconclusive evidence includes sparse C2 or N cells, no valid same-key matches, unstable vocabulary/status/timestamps, unsupported history strata, positivity failure, missing follow-up, split failure, nonconvergent/prior-dominated M, or intervals crossing the design margin. Inconclusive does not establish absence of response, progression, benefit, recurrence, liver failure, death, or treatment effect.

The verifier can check source paths, schema IDs, ordinary-file members, headers, two-key aggregation, time windows, lexical freezing, profile leakage, history/capture lookback, early transitions, CIFs, standardization, weights, patient splits, predictions, calibration, bootstrap, permutations, and conclusion-to-output mappings. It cannot adjudicate intent, completion, technical success, radiologic/pathologic response, viable tumor, recurrence/progression, liver failure, mortality, survival, outside care, appropriateness, utility, or clinical benefit. Those require expert chart/radiology/pathology adjudication, linked mortality and outside-care data, and prospective or external validation.
