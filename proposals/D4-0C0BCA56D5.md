# Episode 15 proposal — UKB17 posterior-thigh MFI and later falls report

## Scientific opening and hypothesis

UK Biobank work already links thigh muscle volume distribution with falls/function [K2], while broader thigh muscle fat-infiltration work reports disease and mortality associations [K1]. Myosteatosis reviews emphasize unresolved aging versus illness/disuse explanations and measurement limitations [K3]. These findings do not establish whether posterior-thigh MFI precedes or adds an association with a later falls response beyond fat distribution and measured function. The closest inspected overlap is the UKB thigh-volume/falls work; this proposal does not claim novelty for thigh MRI and falls generally. It tests a more specific, fixed-time MFI contrast.

Hypothesis: among UK Biobank `ukb672073` body-MRI visit-2 participants who return at least 366 days later and provide a classifiable visit-3 answer, higher visit-2 bilateral posterior-thigh muscle fat infiltration (MFI) is positively associated with any visit-3 self-reported fall after adjustment for measured VAT, thigh-muscle distribution, baseline falls and function. Falls can cause injury, disability, and loss of independence; evidence for an independent association would justify prospective event-validated replication of muscle-quality phenotyping, not clinical deployment.

The strongest rival is that MFI marks pre-existing frailty, inactivity/disuse, illness or prior falls, compounded by selective MRI attendance/return and differential recall/reporting. Full-versus-reduced measured-proxy adjustment and a same-cohort flexible model probe this rival only partially. Neither attenuation nor persistence identifies a mechanism or removes latent confounding, reverse causation, or selection bias. K1 supports broad disease relevance but is abstract-only and does not establish falls; K2 is the closest overlap but addresses thigh distribution and self-report rather than this MFI contrast; K3 bounds mechanistic and measurement interpretations but is a narrative review, not a patient-level causal test.

## Interpretation and falsification

Supportive: a positive, reasonably precise adjusted association stable to measured prior-fall/function contrasts and compatible with the prespecified flexible model supports a respondent-conditional association worth prospective event-validated replication. It does not show cause, mechanism, true falls risk, treatment benefit, screening performance or clinical utility.

Adverse: precise null/negative results that exclude the prespecified positive association in both models would lower the priority of this particular self-report marker. It would not prove equivalence or absence of association with objectively dated falls. Inconclusive: intervals include null and plausible positive effects, support is sparse, estimates are unstable, quality/coding gates fail, or selection/missingness materially changes interpretation; this does not refute the hypothesis. Curvature/heterogeneity is descriptive evidence requiring replication, not an actionable threshold.

Computationally checkable: cohort construction, date arithmetic, code handling, joins, model convergence, estimates, uncertainty, output/result linkage, and whether conclusions match computed results under prespecified rules. Not automatically adjudicable: clinical truth/severity/timing of falls, adequacy of an MRI QC criterion absent validated documentation, nonresponse mechanism, unmeasured confounding, causal mechanism, transportability, or clinical utility. These need clinical expert review, missing evidence and/or an independent prospective study. Continue only if cohort and QC/code gates yield interpretable support; revise if a defensible quality/coding dependency changes design; defer if that evidence is unavailable; abandon the report-level MFI direction if precise adverse estimates make further validation low value. An imprecise null is not a falsification.
