TITLE
Observable cross-marker persistence of cystatin-C/creatinine discordance in UK Biobank repeat attenders: a two-direction, certification-bounded experiment

SUBSTANTIVE REPAIR AND CLINICAL ADVANCE
This child retains the clinically consequential question in [prior hypothesis] but changes what is claimed and what decides the result. A discrepant cystatin-C/creatinine pair can affect CKD staging, drug dosing, monitoring, and referral, yet may reflect stable or transient non-GFR determinants, biological variation, or assay error. With only two protocol visits, the available UK Biobank data cannot identify a latent GFR trajectory or decompose stable participant renal level, stable marker-specific non-GFR components, visit-specific biological variation, assay error, cross-marker measurement-error covariance, and cross-visit measurement-error covariance. The parent's empirically “calibrated no-decline” simulation is therefore removed from the primary decision rule. Preserving the empirical cross-lag covariance in such a null could put the association being tested into the null itself.

The repaired experiment estimates only directly observable cross-marker persistence: whether one baseline renal marker, unexpectedly high relative to the other, predicts the other marker at the repeat visit after conditioning on that other marker's baseline value. Its advance is a falsifiable determination of whether baseline discordance contains a reproducible cross-marker association among eligible repeat attenders, rather than an unidentifiable claim of latent renal decline. Any external-parameter measurement-error simulation is optional, assumption-indexed, and cannot upgrade or veto the primary observable finding.

SCIENTIFIC QUESTION
P1: Among eligible UK Biobank repeat attenders, does baseline cystatin-C unexpectedly high relative to baseline creatinine predict higher repeat creatinine after conditioning on baseline creatinine?

P2, the prespecified reciprocal direction: Does baseline creatinine unexpectedly high relative to baseline cystatin-C predict higher repeat cystatin-C after conditioning on baseline cystatin-C?

The strongest supportive conclusion is: baseline cystatin-C/creatinine discordance showed a positive, longitudinally persistent cross-marker association in both directions among protocol-biomarker-eligible UK Biobank repeat attenders. It is not measured GFR decline, CKD progression, causality, clinical utility, benefit from ordering cystatin-C, or population transportability.

OBSERVABLE EXPOSURES, OUTCOMES, AND ESTIMANDS
All continuous preprocessing is training-fold-only. Use natural-log concentrations after positivity checks. Assign one participant to one of 10 folds before residualization.

P1 exposure R_cys|cr: in each training fold, regress baseline log cystatin-C on a restricted cubic spline of baseline log creatinine, recruitment age, sex, age-by-sex, and baseline center if field 54 is accepted. Predict held-out baseline log cystatin-C, calculate observed minus predicted, and scale by the training-fold residual SD. Positive R_cys|cr means cystatin-C is higher than expected given creatinine and certified core variables.

P1 outcome and estimand: repeat log creatinine. Fit the stacked held-out estimating model with R_cys|cr, baseline log creatinine spline, recruitment age, sex, age-by-sex, elapsed-time spline, fold indicators, and accepted baseline/repeat center terms. Standardize repeat log creatinine using training-fold mean/SD for a symmetric effect scale. Beta1 is the adjusted difference in repeat-log-creatinine SD per one training-fold SD higher R_cys|cr among eligible repeat attenders.

P2 exposure R_cr|cys: analogously residualize baseline log creatinine on baseline log cystatin-C and the same certified core variables, then training-fold standardize.

P2 outcome and estimand: standardized repeat log cystatin-C, conditioned on baseline log cystatin-C and the same covariates. Beta2 is the adjusted difference in repeat-log-cystatin-C SD per one SD higher R_cr|cys.

Fit one stacked inferential regression per direction; never average fold coefficients. Report beta, full-pipeline participant-bootstrap 95% CI, HC3 robust CI as a diagnostic, partial R-squared, and held-out change in RMSE and log predictive density versus the corresponding baseline-marker-only model. At least 1,000 participant bootstrap replicates rerun fold-specific preprocessing; fixed original fold assignments may be retained within bootstrap. All model terms and spline knots are fixed before inspecting P1/P2 estimates. Richer covariates such as BMI, smoking, diabetes, inflammation, and medications are separately labeled, coding-gated sensitivities and cannot determine core feasibility.

MULTIPLICITY AND SYMMETRIC CONFIRMATION
P1 and P2 form one prespecified two-direction family. Test one-sided nulls H1: beta1<=0 and H2: beta2<=0 with Holm familywise alpha 0.05 using full-pipeline bootstrap p-values. A shared cross-marker persistence claim requires both Holm-adjusted one-sided tests to reject and both direction-specific simultaneous lower confidence bounds to exceed zero. A result in only one direction is not called a shared signal.

The provisional smallest relevant standardized association is 0.05 outcome SD per exposure SD. This is not a validated clinical threshold. If both directions pass the multiplicity rule but either point estimate is below 0.05 or its adjusted interval includes 0.05, report “statistically supported but of uncertain or small substantive magnitude,” never clinical usefulness. A “material bidirectional association” label requires both adjusted lower bounds to exceed 0.05. Report two-sided 95% intervals regardless of the one-sided directional family.

MATCHED-SAMPLE NEGATIVE CONTROLS
Direct LDL (30780-0.0/1.0; mmol/L) and vitamin D (30890-0.0/1.0; nmol/L) are prespecified diagnostic control outcomes, not guaranteed biological nulls. For each control separately, intersect the primary renal cohort with participants having finite positive baseline and repeat control concentrations. Keep the original folds. On that exact intersection:
1. rerun P1 and P2 renal models;
2. fit repeat standardized log control concentration conditional on its baseline log concentration and the same certified core covariates, once with R_cys|cr and once with R_cr|cys;
3. compare each renal-control coefficient difference using a paired participant bootstrap that reruns both models.

Control and renal estimates from different selected samples must never be compared. Apply Holm correction across the four prespecified renal-versus-control contrasts as a diagnostic family. A control association not demonstrably smaller than its matched renal association weakens specificity; it cannot by itself prove the renal association false because LDL and vitamin D can share health, season, medication, and selection pathways.

No empirical covariance decomposition, reliability estimate, or “calibrated no-decline” null is used as a gate. Cross-fitting and cross-marker ANCOVA reduce direct mathematical coupling but do not eliminate correlated biological or assay errors.

PLACEBOS AND ROBUSTNESS
1. Permute each held-out residual exposure within fold, sex, age decile, baseline outcome-marker decile, and center where feasible; use at least 1,000 permutations. A gross mismatch between nominal and permutation inference is adverse to implementation validity.
2. Date/selection diagnostic: test whether each baseline residual predicts elapsed interval conditional on baseline date, demographics, and center. Material prediction requires explicit discussion and agreement of weighted/unweighted directions.
3. Center checks, only if field 54 is accepted: same-center-only analysis, baseline-center leave-one-out analyses, and cluster-by-center inference. A sign reversal or concentration in one center is adverse.
4. Every-positive-interval sensitivity and continuous interval interaction. A sign reversal across the observed interval support is adverse.
5. Same-marker change analyses are descriptive regression-to-the-mean illustrations only and cannot support the main claim.

FALSIFICATION AND RESULT BRANCHES
Supportive for observable bidirectional persistence requires all of the following: source/date/field integrity passes; both Holm-adjusted directional tests reject with adjusted lower bounds above zero; estimates are not sign-reversed by attendance weighting or every-positive-interval analysis; center checks do not show one-center dependence when available; and matched controls are weaker or, if not statistically distinguishable, the lack of specificity is explicitly retained. Only if both lower bounds exceed 0.05 may the association be called material, still without a clinical-utility claim.

Adverse/falsifying for a shared persistence hypothesis: either direction has an adjusted upper bound at or below zero; P1 and P2 have conflicting signs; a correctly implemented permutation test contradicts nominal inference; the association reverses under the prespecified temporal window sensitivity; or it is attributable to one center. Conclude that these data do not support a bidirectional persistent cross-marker association among eligible repeat attenders. A precise adjusted upper bound below 0.05 in either direction also falsifies a material bidirectional association even if a smaller positive effect remains.

Inconclusive: integrity or required field/date certification fails; intervals include both zero and relevant effects; one direction is positive but the other is imprecise; weight positivity/ESS is unacceptable; weighted and unweighted results materially conflict without a defensible target explanation; control associations are similar and their paired differences are imprecise; or center/date structure cannot be resolved. Inconclusive is not interpreted as absence.

A negative control equal to or larger than the matched renal association is adverse to renal specificity, but because controls are not guaranteed nulls it yields “nonspecific observable persistence,” not proof that beta1/beta2 are artifacts. A very small but precise bidirectional result supports statistical persistence while falsifying the prespecified material-magnitude claim.
