# HCC repeat-TACE P-beyond-B successor: downstream recorded-care translation and selection audit

## Research question and substantive advance

This is a substantive child of `[prior hypothesis]` and `[prior hypothesis]`, preserving the mature HCC repeat-TACE design. The frozen scientific question remains whether the contemporaneous pre-repeat assay P improves short-horizon prediction of the subsequent assay Y beyond remote baseline assay B and the locked pre-event covariates among selected repeat-TACE episodes. The primary endpoint, cohort, clocks, annual forward validation, locked nested-ridge estimand, outcome-blind support, assay-separated missing-Y bounds, and strict noncausal limits are unchanged.

The new, decision-relevance-directed question is narrower and explicitly secondary: among BP-eligible episodes, is the *pre-specified out-of-sample forecast discrepancy* attributable to adding P associated with a temporally subsequent, objectively recorded care-process proxy after the Y window? Specifically, do episodes in which the locked M1 versus M0 forecast differs more show a different rate of a valid additional encounter in days 4–30 after the repeat event, with same-assay reassessment and other recorded activity as secondary proxies? This can test whether the surrogate forecast is at least operationally connected to subsequent documented follow-up, rather than silently presenting laboratory prediction as patient benefit.

This is not a treatment-effect, treatment-response, utility, quality, safety, mortality, hepatic-failure, or patient-benefit analysis. A post-event encounter may represent planned surveillance, deterioration, outside-care substitution, access, coding, or documentation behavior. The strongest possible result is a reproducible association between a frozen pre-event forecast discrepancy and a timed recorded-care proxy in this selected calendar mixture. Even a supportive result does not justify an action threshold or claim that acting on P improves care. The substantive advance is an evidence-bounded bridge audit plus negative-control and selection diagnostics; it does not invent a clinical endpoint that the source lacks.

No new empirical HCC finding is claimed here. The inherited executed audit supports at most a local selected 2022 prognostic comparison; later blocks have sparse and differential Y observation. All downstream results in this proposal are unexecuted.

## Forecast-discrepancy exposure for the downstream audit

For every future BP-eligible episode and assay, retain frozen predictions `p0_i` and `p1_i` from the pre-origin models. Define the exposure before looking at any downstream endpoint:

* `D_i = |p1_i - p0_i| / SD_train(Y)`, the absolute incremental forecast discrepancy, only if finite and `SD_train(Y)>0`;
* `S_i = (p1_i-p0_i) / SD_train(Y)`, the signed discrepancy, only as a secondary directional coordinate.

These are model coordinates, not validated clinical thresholds. If the training SD is zero/nonfinite, or either prediction is nonfinite, mark the downstream translation exposure non-estimable for that assay/origin/block and do not substitute a scale. Do not use future Y, future care records, or full-source quantiles to define D categories.

Define assay-specific low/middle/high D categories using the 33rd and 67th percentiles of finite D among pre-origin BP-eligible training events, with a deterministic quantile rule frozen before reading future downstream endpoints. If a future D is outside the training range, retain its raw value and report extrapolation; do not clip or silently discard it. The primary contrast is high versus low; the middle category is descriptive and tests monotonicity. If training cutpoints are undefined or any future category has fewer than 10 episodes, report only descriptive exposure distributions and mark the category contrast non-estimable.

## Downstream temporal outcomes and exact semantics

All primary downstream outcomes are evaluated using raw event timestamp `t_event`, not encounter date alone. To avoid contaminating the translation audit with the assay-Y window, the primary downstream window is the open/closed interval `(t_event + 72 hours, t_event + 30 calendar days]`, operationalized as day 4 through day 30 after the event under one declared timezone and parser. The boundary at exactly +72 hours belongs to neither downstream window nor the Y window; exactly +30 days is included. If event time or the relevant source time is invalid, that row is not eligible for that endpoint and the missingness is reported, never converted to zero.

The primary care-process proxy is `additional_encounter_4_30`: at least one encounter row with finite `就诊时间` in the downstream interval and composite key different from the selected repeat-event encounter (`患者主索引`,`就诊号`). This is the most temporally defensible downstream proxy because it has a native time and a distinct encounter key. It means only a recorded encounter; it does not mean completed follow-up, clinical need, admission, emergency care, treatment response, or benefit.

Prespecified secondary proxies, each reported separately rather than hidden in an unvalidated composite, are:

1. `same_assay_recheck_4_30`: at least one finite, uncensored numeric row for the same assay (`白蛋白` or `总胆红素`) with `检验时间` in the interval;
2. `any_examination_record_4_30`: at least one examination row with valid `开始时间` in the interval;
3. `any_procedure_record_4_30`: at least one procedure row with valid `开始时间` in the interval, with counts separated into recorded repeat-TACE versus other procedure using only the prespecified name matching;
4. `any_medication_record_4_30`: at least one medication row with valid `开始时间` in the interval;
5. `any_nondrug_order_4_30`: at least one non-drug order with valid `开立时间` or `开始时间` in the interval, reporting the clock separately and not treating an order as administration;
6. `any_timed_activity_4_30`: the union of the five timed proxies above, with row-level source provenance, used only as a broad sensitivity endpoint and never as a clinical outcome.

The union must deduplicate at the patient-event level. Do not use clinical-document text or untimed documents to create any timed outcome. A same-assay lab recheck is a recorded assay event, not evidence that Y was observed, that the test was clinically indicated, or that the numerical result improved. A procedure, medication, or order is a recorded row, not proof of completion or intent.

For ascertainment/selection auditing, define the pre-event negative-control activity using the same source semantics in `(t_event-30 calendar days, t_event-72 hours]`, excluding the TACE2 and selected event encounter keys and excluding the B/P windows where applicable. The negative control is `pre_event_additional_encounter_30`: any distinct encounter with valid `就诊时间` in that interval. Secondary pre-event controls use the analogous examination, same-assay lab, procedure, medication, and non-drug-order presence. This is not assumed to have a true null association because underlying disease and access may persist; a positive result is a warning that D may be tagging baseline care intensity rather than downstream translation. It cannot, by itself, prove bias.

For a temporal falsification control, repeat the primary analysis in the delayed window `(t_event+30 days, t_event+60 days]`, always labeling it delayed recorded activity. A stronger or weaker delayed association is not clinically interpretable without additional data, but a result confined to untimed/document or delayed records is classified as selection-sensitive rather than supportive of immediate care-process translation.

## Analysis, baselines, and uncertainty

The unit is one selected repeat event per frozen event-selection rule, with patient clustering retained. Analyze albumin and bilirubin separately, and report systemic-record versus comparator arms separately before any overall standardization. No patient may contribute multiple selected events unless the inherited reconstruction explicitly does so; if it does, all events remain linked to the patient for cluster-robust uncertainty.

For every origin, future block, assay, arm, and downstream endpoint, report the full denominator funnel `selected event -> has_B -> has_P -> BP_eligible -> valid event time -> endpoint evaluable`, plus Y-observed/missing status. The endpoint audit uses all endpoint-evaluable BP rows, including missing-Y rows; it never imputes Y or conditions on Y observation. Require the inherited support gate (at least 20 BP-eligible episodes overall and at least 10 per arm) for an inferentially labeled block. If the downstream endpoint has fewer than 10 positives or fewer than 10 negatives overall, or fewer than 5 in either high/low D category, report raw counts/fractions only and mark the contrast non-estimable. Cells below five are suppressed or marked as sparse.

The primary downstream estimand is an assay-specific, future-block-specific high-versus-low D risk difference for the primary proxy:

`RD_4_30 = Pr(additional_encounter_4_30=1 | high D) - Pr(additional_encounter_4_30=1 | low D)`.

This is a descriptive/associational risk difference in the selected event population, not a causal effect of P or D. Report crude rates, exact/binomial or Wilson 95% intervals for each category, risk difference and ratio where estimable, and a patient-cluster bootstrap interval as the primary uncertainty procedure. The bootstrap samples patients with replacement within arm, retains all their selected events, and recomputes category rates and contrasts with fixed predictions/cutpoints; it does not refit M0/M1, impute Y, or resample source rows. Declare the seed and B=2000; if fewer replicates are completed because of a recorded resource failure, report that fact and downgrade the uncertainty result. Zero denominators, nonfinite estimates, no positive/negative outcome variation, or fewer than two patient clusters make the interval non-estimable, not zero.

The category result must be accompanied by a prespecified baseline comparison. Fit, only as an associational adjustment and not as a causal model, a binomial model for the binary proxy with predictors high/middle/low D, arm, calendar block, age, sex, TACE2-to-repeat days, P-lead hours, and B, using training-derived transformations and no outcome-based selection. Report standardized predicted-probability contrasts over the future block covariate distribution and robust patient-cluster uncertainty. If separation, nonfinite coefficients, insufficient events, or a failed support gate occurs, do not replace the adjusted result with a different model; retain crude descriptive rates and mark the adjusted contrast non-estimable.

Baselines are required and are not optional model competitions:

* `Q_null`: arm-only and calendar-block-only rates, showing the underlying process rate;
* `Q_core`: pre-event core adjustment without D (B, arm, age, sex, TACE2-to-repeat days, P-lead hours);
* `Q_D`: Q_core plus D category (and S only in a separate directional sensitivity, never jointly selected by performance).

Report whether D changes standardized risk prediction relative to Q_core using a prespecified Brier-score difference only when an out-of-sample process prediction is available. Do not use in-sample fit improvement as evidence. The clinical translation conclusion rests on the pre-specified high-versus-low association and its falsification checks, not a small predictive metric.

Preserve the inherited primary P-beyond-B RMSE and partial-identification outputs adjacent to, but separate from, the downstream audit. A downstream association cannot rescue a failed/unsupported primary prognostic block, and a positive RM difference cannot be used to impute missing Y. If the primary block has failed support, only a clearly labeled downstream descriptive audit may be emitted; it cannot be called a validated translation.

Use the inherited outcome-blind ascertainment categories (`exam+followup`, `exam_only`, `followup_only`, `document_only`, `none`) to stratify downstream reporting, but do not replace the global endpoint estimand with a range of stratum estimates. Report endpoint rates by category only when each cell has at least 10 BP rows and at least five positives/negatives; otherwise suppress or mark non-estimable. These categories are pre-Y recording/opportunity proxies and do not prove completed assay follow-up.

## Negative controls and falsification rules

The verifier and compiler must execute both favorable and adverse branches. The translation audit is supportive only if all of the following hold in an adequately supported future block: source/hash/schema and duplicate-safe reconstruction pass; the inherited complete-case and all-BP missing-Y status is explicitly reported; the primary D exposure and endpoint are finite and estimable; the high-versus-low association is prespecified-direction positive with a confidence interval excluding zero; the middle category is directionally compatible or explicitly inconclusive; the same result is not explained by arm/calendar imbalance after Q_core adjustment; the pre-event negative-control association is absent or materially smaller; and the result is present for the temporally valid primary encounter proxy rather than only an untimed document/order proxy. Even then, label it `supportive_recorded-care_association`, not clinical utility or benefit.

Classify as adverse to the translation hypothesis if the primary encounter association is prespecified-direction negative with an adequately supported interval excluding zero, or if repeated supported blocks show no association and the exposure is estimable. This challenges the claim that the surrogate forecast is operationally linked to documented follow-up; it does not prove that P has no prognostic value or that care is inappropriate.

Classify as selection-sensitive if a favorable association is accompanied by a similar or larger pre-event negative-control association, is present only for document/order/medication records, differs sharply by arm or ascertainment category, reverses across assay or calendar block, depends on extrapolation beyond training D range, disappears under Q_core adjustment, or occurs only in delayed activity. A positive result with a wide interval crossing zero is inconclusive, not supportive.

Classify as inconclusive for failed gates, sparse endpoint cells, unsupported or degenerate training Y range, missing/nonfinite frozen predictions, zero training Y SD, failed event clock, low transport/observation ESS, influential clusters, no endpoint variation, out-of-range future assay values invalidating the inherited bound, or any inability to establish native timestamps. Do not pool albumin and bilirubin to overcome sparsity.

Required computational negative-control tests are:

1. Permute D within assay-by-origin-by-arm-by-calendar strata using a declared seed. The permuted high-versus-low association must be labeled a null calibration check; a large association indicates implementation or stratification failure, not a clinical finding.
2. Shift the downstream window to the pre-event negative-control window. A positive signal there is a selection warning and must not be called downstream translation.
3. Verify that exactly +72 hours is excluded from downstream and is retained only under the inherited Y clock, while exactly +30 days is included and +60-day delayed activity is separate.
4. Remove the selected repeat-event encounter key from additional-encounter counts. If the primary endpoint changes when the event encounter is retained, fail the endpoint implementation.
5. Test synthetic rows with documents but no native time; they must never enter a timed endpoint. Test medication/order rows with only order times; they must retain the order-record label.
6. Test favorable observed-case RMSE gain with global Delta crossing zero: classify primary prognostic evidence as selection-sensitive and do not let a favorable care proxy override it.
7. Test a positive care-proxy association with a nonpositive prognostic RMSE result: retain adverse prognostic and separately describe the care association; never infer that the care proxy validates P.
8. Test sparse high/low cells and model separation: emit descriptive counts and `non-estimable`, never a zero effect or a fallback model.
9. Test albumin and bilirubin with incompatible numeric scales: confirm no pooling, shared threshold, or cross-assay effect is generated.
10. Test a procedure/medication/order row after the window without a valid native timestamp: exclude from the timed endpoint and report timing missingness.

## Fail-closed rules and interpretation precedence

The following precedence is mandatory: source/schema/hash failure -> computationally invalid; frozen cohort/clock assertion failure -> stop all scientific interpretation; failed support gate -> descriptive only; endpoint timestamp/exposure failure -> endpoint-specific non-estimable; sparse or separated endpoint -> non-estimable; out-of-range future Y -> inherited temporal bound unsupported; positive care association with negative-control or ascertainment warning -> selection-sensitive; only a clean, adequately supported association can be labeled supportive, and that label remains restricted to recorded-care association.

Never use downstream records to select the repeat event, define B/P/Y, choose alpha, choose a model, set D cutpoints, repair missing Y, or widen the training Y range. Never report a downstream association as evidence of treatment response, causal effect, clinical benefit, utility, quality, completed follow-up, or an action rule. Do not infer mortality, hepatic failure, tumor progression, imaging response, admission severity, urgency, or patient intent. `就诊科室` is descriptive only. A record of an assay or encounter does not establish that the care was clinically indicated or completed.

Computationally checkable outputs are source hashes and headers, duplicate counts, frozen cohort/event counts, exact clock boundaries, BP and Y funnels, fixed pre-origin predictions, D cutpoints, endpoint row-level provenance without identifiers, category counts, crude and adjusted rates, bootstrap bookkeeping, permutation checks, and status labels. Clinical adjudication is required for assay units/reference ranges, specimen validity, severity, tumor burden, imaging response, TACE intent/administration, actual care completion, outside-care capture, mortality, hepatic failure, patient-important outcomes, and whether any recorded process represents appropriate care. An independent external or prospective cohort with validated laboratory metadata and patient-important outcomes would be required to establish clinical utility or benefit.

## Expected result meanings

A supportive result would mean only that the frozen incremental forecast discrepancy is associated with a temporally subsequent recorded encounter in the tested selected population and is less compatible with a simple pre-existing documentation artifact. It would motivate prospective evaluation of whether clinicians could use a validated assay and outcome definition, but it would not justify changing TACE management.

An adverse result would weaken the proposed operational bridge even if the primary P-beyond-B RMSE gain is positive; it would suggest that the surrogate forecast is not detectably linked to the measured care process. It would not prove irrelevance to patient outcomes.

A selection-sensitive result would show why a favorable complete-case surrogate signal cannot be translated: the same pattern is present before the event, in recording-only endpoints, in a single sparse calendar/arm, or only after conditioning on observed Y. This should be treated as a useful falsification, not a failed study.

An inconclusive result would preserve uncertainty due to missing Y, sparse downstream activity, invalid/absent timing, or non-estimable contrasts. It should trigger data acquisition or clinical adjudication, not analytic relaxation.

No empirical result is asserted in this proposal. The retained parent artifact `[internal dataset path]` remains historical support only and does not contain executed results for this downstream audit.
