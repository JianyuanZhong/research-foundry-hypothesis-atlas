# Same-current HbA1c history and transition to cardiometabolic multimorbidity

Parent: `[prior hypothesis]`  
Seed: `[starting question]`, “Metabolic deterioration and multimorbidity”  
Dataset: UK Biobank snapshot `[source checksum]`  
Status: prospective observational hypothesis; no fitted result is claimed.

## Unresolved question, strongest evidence, and advance

The strongest claim supported by the evidence available for this proposal is narrower than the proposed hypothesis: static metabolic or atherogenic measures are associated with incident cardiometabolic multimorbidity. The accessible demonstrations and a targeted current literature search support the value of time-aware disease histories and calibrated risk estimation, but do not establish that a prior HbA1c trajectory adds clinically material information once the current HbA1c is known. The search was not a systematic novelty review.

The unresolved clinical decision is whether two people with the same current HbA1c should receive different near-term risk interpretation because one arrived there from a lower prior HbA1c. A positive result could support a narrowly targeted repeat-measurement and prevention-risk assessment strategy; it would not show that offering repeat testing or acting on it improves outcomes.

Primary hypothesis:

> Among UKB participants with valid, strictly ordered baseline and repeat HbA1c measurements who are alive and free of inpatient-coded type 2 diabetes, CKD, and heart failure at the repeat landmark, a recent HbA1c rise is associated with a clinically material increase in 5-year risk of acquiring a second distinct disease family among those three, conditional on the same current HbA1c and prespecified baseline covariates.

The substantive advance is a prespecified same-current-level history contrast, not another static association or an unconstrained prediction competition. A separate secondary contrast tests ordinary deterioration at a fixed baseline HbA1c. These estimands must not be conflated: holding current HbA1c fixed, a larger rise means a lower prior HbA1c; holding baseline fixed, a larger rise means a higher current HbA1c.

## Exposure, outcome, baselines, and analysis

After verification, preserve raw HbA1c and define current C = repeat HbA1c, baseline B = baseline HbA1c, and change Delta = C - B in verified NGSP percentage points. If conversion to NGSP cannot be justified, use a standardized within-cohort contrast and do not claim a 0.5-point clinical contrast.

Primary estimand: standardized 5-year cumulative incidence difference for the same-current history C-0.5 versus C, averaged over the observed current HbA1c/covariate distribution and restricted to common empirical support. This compares a 0.5 percentage-point rise from the implied prior level with a stable history at the same current level. The secondary estimand compares C=B+0.5 with C=B at fixed B. Report absolute risk difference, risk ratio, 95% participant-bootstrap intervals, cause-specific hazard ratio, and cumulative-incidence curves.

Use cause-specific Cox modeling with a flexible joint surface in C and B (restricted cubic splines, complexity fixed before outcome inspection) and Aalen–Johansen cumulative incidence with death competing. Fit nested models in the identical landmark cohort:

1. current HbA1c plus baseline covariates (age spline, sex, BMI, SBP, DBP; renal estimates only after unit/equation verification);
2. current plus baseline HbA1c and baseline covariates;
3. current plus change and baseline covariates, only as an equivalent parameterization for the fixed-baseline secondary contrast.

Do not put unrestricted current, baseline, and change terms together. Assess proportional hazards, calibration, Brier score, optimism-corrected incremental performance, empirical support, and participant-level bootstrap uncertainty. A repeat-selection inverse-probability-weighted sensitivity model uses baseline variables only, fixed truncation, and reports positivity/effective sample size. Multiple imputation, if needed, occurs within resampling partitions. A bounded elastic-net Cox model is optional and secondary; it cannot substitute for the prespecified association analysis or establish clinical utility.

Secondary outcomes are first component, each component separately, all-cause death, and an expanded endpoint adding CHD I20–I25 and stroke I60–I69. They cannot replace the primary second-distinct-family endpoint if it is infeasible.

## Falsification and interpretation

Set clinical materiality in advance at a 2-percentage-point absolute 5-year risk difference; this is a decision-relevance gate requiring expert review, not a validated treatment threshold. Support for the expected directional hypothesis requires verified gates, at least 100 primary second-family events overall, at least 10 in each displayed trajectory group, common support, a positive primary contrast with a 95% interval excluding zero and point estimate at least 2 points, broadly consistent washout/repeat-weighted/interval-restricted/competing-risk results, and no equally large positive association for fixed negative-control appendicitis K35.x (with its event count reported).

A negative/opposite contrast is adverse to the expected direction but would still support a trajectory-history association. A null with adequate events and an interval excluding the 2-point materiality threshold supports current HbA1c sufficiency for this endpoint in this selected population. A strong current-level association without a history increment supports only current-level stratification. Disappearance after washout or repeat weighting suggests reverse causation or measured selection.

Results are inconclusive if any field-unit/date/code/death gate fails; events, support, or negative-control counts are sparse; weights are unstable; or uncertainty includes both a material increase and a trivial/no increase. A failure of the primary endpoint cannot be silently replaced by a secondary outcome.

Computation can establish field availability, filter counts, inpatient-code-defined disease order, competing-risk estimates, calibrated association/prediction metrics, uncertainty, and gate status. It cannot establish outpatient disease, adjudicated diagnoses, disease severity/stage, treatment effects, causal benefit of repeat testing, representativeness, or clinical utility. Those require validated dictionaries and dates, outpatient labs/medications, adjudication, external validation, expert review, and a prospective testing-and-management study.
