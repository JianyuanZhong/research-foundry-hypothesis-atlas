# Proposal: encounter-constrained and dynamic reassessment pathways after TACE

## Episode 15 successor and scientific deliverable

This is a substantive child of assessed valid parent `[prior hypothesis]` (Episode 14, highest-mu assessed HCC candidate). It preserves the parent’s HCC population, first recorded TACE-like index, day-45 landmark, days 46–365 horizon, diagnostic-only versus therapeutic ontology, measurement-selection estimands, capture-inclusive observation state, temporal locking, competing pathway checks, and strict limits on clinical interpretation.

The repair addresses a remaining endpoint-identification problem. The parent groups procedure rows for a patient within 24 hours. That can merge distinct `Encounter Number` values when a diagnostic angiography encounter and a therapeutic embolization encounter occur close together. Because `hepatic arteriography` is common and therapeutic records often contain bundled angiography, this ambiguity can change whether diagnostic contact is an intermediate state or part of a therapeutic episode. The primary episode identity is therefore encounter-constrained; the parent’s patient-time grouping is a locked sensitivity. The repair also makes the dynamic question explicit: a diagnostic contact after day 45 is a time-varying gateway, not a fixed baseline subgroup.

The actual scientific deliverable is newly fitted, leakage-safe B0, Bobs and B2 competing-transition models plus a dynamic post-diagnostic-landmark model and, if supported, an irregular-time M3. Completion requires an episode identity audit, cohort and split manifests, transition-specific locked predictions, cumulative-incidence and calibration outputs, alert curves at prespecified sensitivity, episode-definition contrast, observation/positivity diagnostics, patient-clustered bootstrap intervals, dynamic landmark outputs and an interpretation file linking claims to computed fields. The episode is not claiming to solve the clinical decision during discovery.

## Strongest supported claim and unresolved hypothesis

The strongest available evidence supports only data availability and coding structure. The HCC snapshot contains linkable encounters, procedures, diagnoses, labs and examinations. A prior read-only audit counted 105,044 encounter rows, 338,040 procedure rows, 1,810,646 diagnosis rows, 344,383 rows with `hepatocellular carcinoma` in the diagnosis name, and 106,963 rows matching the parent’s TACE lexical rule; 15,874 procedure rows lacked `start time`. Direct inspection of the procedure source found `hepatic arteriography` as the most frequent TACE-family-related label (33,332 rows), alongside `transcatheter hepatic arterial embolization` (27,411), `arterial chemoembolization` (26,385), and many composite angiography/embolization labels. These facts do not establish therapeutic intent, recurrence, viable tumour, radiologic response, benefit, mortality or outside care.

The unresolved clinical-research question is:

> After a first recorded TACE-like episode for HCC, does an early AFP/hepatic trajectory contain reproducible information about which patients subsequently enter a therapeutic TACE-containing recorded pathway, beyond measurement and local-care capture, and does that information remain specific when diagnostic contact is represented as a time-ordered gateway rather than merged with nearby procedures?

The primary hypothesis is:

> Among adults in the exact day-45 risk set who have no encounter-constrained therapeutic liver-directed episode through day 45, adding the early trajectory and its missingness pattern to an observation/pathway baseline will reduce the locked-test alert fraction at 90% sensitivity for a first therapeutic TACE-containing episode with a prior local reassessment opportunity by at least 10 percentage points, after training-only full-index standardization, with a patient-bootstrap 95% interval excluding zero. The same trajectory gain will remain at least 5 percentage points on the dynamic post-diagnostic-contact landmark relative to its observation-only comparator, while no comparable gain appears for diagnostic-only contact, non-liver capture, or a pre-index placebo. The primary gain and the parent’s 24-hour patient-time episode sensitivity will not differ by more than 5 percentage points in either direction; otherwise the endpoint is definition-sensitive and the clinical interpretation is adverse or inconclusive.

These are reproducible local recorded-care criteria, not a treatment threshold, causal effect, utility estimate or recommendation to repeat TACE.

## Population and time boundary

Use one index per patient. Aggregate every child table to (`Patient master index`, `encounter number`) before joining to `encounters`; never make a many-to-many child join.

1. Parse `procedures.start time`. The primary procedure episode consists of rows with the same `patient master index` and same `encounter number`, ordered by start time; rows within 24 hours within that encounter may be grouped. Retain raw `surgery`, `start time`, `end time`, `surgery source`, row count, family hits, composite status and missing-time flags. Rows without parseable start time cannot define an event and are reported only.
2. Define the primary index as the earliest timed encounter-constrained episode containing literal `TACE`, a name containing `transarterial chemoembolization`, or both `hepatic artery` and `embolization`. Preserve the frozen vocabulary audit and exact ties.
3. The parent sensitivity constructs patient-level 24-hour groups regardless of `encounter number`. A second sensitivity requires same encounter and exact same-start-date without 24-hour grouping. Cross-encounter procedures remain ordered observations in the primary analysis, never silently merged.
4. Require age >=18 and nonmissing `Sex` from the index encounter.
5. Require a diagnosis name containing `Hepatocellular Carcinoma` on an encounter joined by the two keys within 180 days before through 7 days after index. `diagnoses` has no diagnosis timestamp; inherit joined `encounters.Visit Time` only for this cohort criterion and report a sensitivity excluding uncertain same-encounter ordering.
6. Restrict index timestamp to <= 2025-01-01 00:00:00 for a nominal 365-day horizon. Show maximum valid follow-up for every index. The complete-horizon primary analysis requires day-365 local representation; a right-censored analysis is explicitly secondary.
7. Exclude from the day-45 risk set any encounter-constrained therapeutic liver-directed episode after the first 24 hours through day 45, and report this early pathway separately. Diagnostic-only contact is not automatically excluded.
8. Stop all predictors at day 45. Primary follow-up is days 46–365. Use patient-level temporal splits: 2010–2022 fit, 2023 tuning, and 2024 through the cutoff locked test, provided minimum index/outcome support is met. If calendar support fails, use deterministic participant hashing and label it internal. No participant crosses splits.

## Endpoint ontology and estimands

Create the ontology before modeling using a frozen vocabulary audit.

- `therapeutic_TACE`: encounter-constrained episode containing the TACE family, including documented embolization/infusion combinations.
- `alternate_therapeutic_liver_directed`: audited liver-directed therapeutic family not classified as TACE.
- `diagnostic_only_liver_contact`: encounter-constrained liver/abdominal angiography or examination-like procedure with no therapeutic family hit in that encounter episode.
- `mixed_or_unclassified`: unresolved composite or family conflict; never silently promoted to therapeutic.
- `non_liver_procedure`: timed non-liver procedure, both a competing local-care state and capture control.
- `end_of_local_observation`: no later qualifying encounter, admission or discharge timestamp under the administrative rule; not death or outside-care cessation.
- `observed_no_therapeutic_action`: no classified therapeutic action through day 365 while locally observable.

The primary estimand is the day-45-to-first therapeutic TACE transition and its cumulative incidence, separately for a strict local reassessment opportunity and no such opportunity. The reassessment flag uses only records strictly before the therapeutic episode and outside its encounter: a dated liver-relevant lab or liver/abdominal examination in the preceding 14 days, with 30-day and encounter-only sensitivities. It is a recorded opportunity construct, not adjudicated imaging or tumour reassessment.

The dynamic estimand starts at the first encounter-constrained diagnostic-only contact after day 45. For those entering this state, freeze predictors immediately before contact and estimate the probability/hazard of first therapeutic TACE over the following 30, 90 and 320 days, with alternate, mixed, non-liver and end-of-observation states competing. This avoids treating post-index diagnostic contact as a fixed day-45 covariate or conditioning on it while calling the contrast causal. Report both direct day-45 transitions and diagnostic-contact-mediated transitions.

## Predictors and information boundary

Trajectory availability A=1 requires at least one parseable numeric AFP in days -30 to -1 and days 7–45, plus at least one of albumin, total bilirubin, the observed coagulation-ratio assay or platelets in each window. Preserve partial patterns and missingness; do not complete-case the full cohort.

For each assay, use the last numeric pre-index value and first numeric early-post value. Retain log1p AFP, continuous changes, frozen direction indicators, assay identity, timing and measurement counts. Do not pool assays, infer units, construct ALBI/MELD, or apply unit-dependent thresholds: `labs` has no unit column.

The observation model uses only variables available by day 45: age, sex, index year, department, pre-index procedure/diagnosis history, encounter/contact intensity, assay availability, examination opportunities, primary diagnostic-only state, non-liver/unclassified counts and missingness. Fit observation/transport weights on fit data only; report overlap, positivity and truncation. Weighting is predictive standardization, not a causal claim.

Primary baseline history includes pre-index counts, assay summaries/missingness, encounter dates and day-45 capture variables. `examinations.start time` supplies dated opportunity only; `examination findings` and `examination diagnosis` are excluded from primary inference. `orders` is a dated opportunity/capture sensitivity, never completed treatment. No future lab, diagnostic contact, treatment episode or reassessment is a predictor for the day-45 model. Dynamic models may use only history strictly before their own diagnostic-contact landmark.

## Matched baseline and substantive alternative

All models share the same cohort, encounter-constrained ontology, temporal split, horizon, standardization target and patient-clustered uncertainty.

- **B0 transparent baseline:** regularized discrete-time cause-specific transition hazards from pre-index history and day-45 capture/process variables; no post-index numeric trajectory.
- **Bobs observation/pathway comparator:** B0 plus A, measurement pattern, diagnostic-only and observation-process variables, but no numeric lab values. It tests whether apparent gain is only who is measured, contacted or enters the diagnostic pathway.
- **B2 trajectory model:** Bobs plus AFP/hepatic values and changes, with both A=1 and training-only full-index standardization. This is the primary incremental comparison.
- **M3/dynamic state-space alternative:** a sparse low-rank irregular-time model with separate latent AFP activity and hepatic-function trajectories, assay-specific observation models, encounter-constrained diagnostic transition intensity and post-diagnostic therapeutic hazards. It is evaluated on the same transition targets and exposes asynchronous shape, biomarker discordance and observation intensity that paired B2 summaries lose. It must not smooth a landmark state using future observations.

The principal scientific comparisons are B2 versus Bobs, full-index versus paired-observed estimates, direct versus diagnostic-mediated transitions, primary episode identity versus the parent patient-time sensitivity, and M3 versus B2. B0/Bobs/B2 are selected for auditability and operating-boundary interpretation; M3 is retained because the dynamic irregular-time mechanism is the scientific uncertainty, not because complexity earns merit. Defer a transformer because additional sequence capacity cannot resolve encounter identity or unvalidated procedure intent. Defer causal treatment-effect models, radiology/image/text response models and external validation because eligibility, intent, response adjudication, death/outside-care linkage, images and an external cohort are unavailable.

## Falsification and interpretation

Supportive evidence requires:

- B2 versus Bobs achieves the >=10-point locked-test alert reduction at 90% sensitivity for the primary reassessment-anchored therapeutic TACE outcome, after valid full-index standardization, with a bootstrap interval excluding zero.
- On the dynamic post-diagnostic landmark, trajectory gain versus its observation-only comparator is at least 5 points with interval excluding zero.
- Primary versus parent 24-hour episode identity changes the gain by no more than 5 points and does not reverse the pathway conclusion.
- Gain is not comparable for diagnostic-only contact, non-liver capture control or pre-index placebo; results are stable under 14/30-day and encounter-only reassessment definitions, continued-observation and last-contact sensitivities.
- Event support, missing-time rates, mixed/unclassified rates, positivity, calibration and threshold uncertainty are acceptable. If M3 is attempted, it converges and improves the same locked target by >=5 points or mean log score by >=0.02 with calibrated uncertainty.

Adverse evidence includes no or reversed trajectory gain; Bobs explaining nearly all B2 gain; gain confined to high-contact or disappearance strata; similar gain for non-liver, diagnostic-only or placebo controls; a materially changed result under encounter-constrained versus patient-time grouping; cross-encounter merging changing pathway labels; poor overlap/calibration; or M3 nonconvergence. This weakens the disease-specific recorded-care interpretation and supports a capture or coding-pathway explanation.

Inconclusive evidence includes too few therapeutic or diagnostic transitions, high missing procedure times, unresolved composites, inadequate one-year support, calendar-split failure, positivity failure, bootstrap intervals spanning margins, or dynamic landmark support too sparse for stable estimates. Do not relax rules after seeing the locked outcome.

A supportive computation would justify expert adjudication of procedure indication/bundles, radiology response, treatment intent and eligibility, followed by prospective surveillance evaluation and independent validation. It would not justify changing treatment. The computation can establish reproducible local recorded-care stratification and robustness to episode identity; it cannot establish recurrence, viable tumour, radiologic response, treatment benefit/failure, survival, death, outside care, appropriateness, causal effect, clinical utility or external validity.
