# Episode 18 branch: a falsifiable temporal boundary test for ICU-discharge instability

Status: design-only branch proposal. No cohort scan, row-count estimate, feature materialization, fitted model, Harbor execution, or clinical result was run.

Scientific parent: [prior hypothesis]. This is a substantive child in estimand and interpretation, not a mechanical compiler repair.

## Decision and unresolved question

The parent asks whether persistent multi-domain physiologic abnormality in the final 24 hours before an alive ICU exit is associated with first ICU readmission or hospital death within 48 hours. The strongest rival is not simply “the model is confounded.” It is more specific:

> The final-24-hour signal may be a discharge-selection/residual-severity signature: patients who are already severe, difficult to discharge, densely observed, or selected for a particular transition may carry the same risk whether the measurement is made 48–24 hours or 24–0 hours before ICU exit.

The closest inspected evidence supports the opening but does not resolve this rival. Timmers et al. report that 48-hour ICU readmission is clinically consequential and that respiratory decompensation, severity and ventilation are relevant correlates, while predictors remain scarce [K1]. Rosa et al. found that discharge-day SWIFT, SOFA and TISS-28 had only moderate and similar accuracy for a 48-hour composite of unplanned ICU readmission or unexpected death [K2]. Kramer et al. show why a patient-level readmission association must not be interpreted as a unit-quality or discharge-policy effect: patient readmission was associated with worse outcomes, but unit-level differences disappeared after case-mix adjustment [K3].

The unresolved, falsifiable question is therefore:

> Conditional on reaching an observed alive index ICU exit, and using the same subjects and pre-exit adjustment information, is the final 24-hour multi-domain instability contrast more specific for the next 48-hour first-event risk than the prespecified earlier [t_d−48 h,t_d−24 h) contrast, after separately auditing observation density and post-landmark destination?

Primary hypothesis: in adults with adequate observations in both windows, a persistent abnormality in at least two prespecified physiologic domains during the final window has a larger standardized 48-hour absolute risk difference for first ICU readmission and/or hospital death than the same fixed-summary contrast measured in the earlier window. The late-versus-early difference must be directionally accompanied by within-stay transitions (late-onset/persistent patterns higher than resolving patterns) and must not be explained by one destination family or a documentation-density gradient.

This is an association conditional on observed ICU exit and exposure availability. It is not a causal effect of discharge timing, discharge delay, monitoring, oxygen, treatment limitation, ward destination, or any intervention.

## What would change understanding

A late-specific, replicated contrast would support a clinically useful boundary claim: the information nearest to ICU exit contains a time-local instability phenotype beyond the earlier severity state, worth prospective discharge-readiness or post-ICU monitoring validation. It would not establish that delaying discharge improves outcomes.

A similar late and early contrast, or attenuation after pre-window severity/density standardization, would favor residual severity or discharge selection. A destination-specific result would favor transition context rather than a general physiologic phenotype. A signal confined to oxygen-support flags would be compatible with chronic support, prescribing practice or documentation rather than hypoxemia. A temporal model gain without a reproducible late-versus-early exposure contrast would be predictive utility only.

The smallest consequential check is the paired temporal-boundary comparison. It can justify continuing to clinical adjudication/prospective validation, revising the phenotype toward destination/support, or abandoning the “time-specific instability” interpretation. It cannot identify an unmeasured discharge rationale, treatment limitation, chronic oxygen need, functional status, work of breathing, clinician intent or mechanism from this archive.

## Population, landmark and time boundaries

The unit is one hospital admission.

1. Join patients.csv.gz to icustays.csv.gz on subject_id, and admissions.csv.gz on (subject_id,hadm_id).
2. Keep patients.anchor_age >= 18; report patients.gender.
3. Require icustays.intime < icustays.outtime. For each (subject_id,hadm_id), retain the earliest valid ICU stay by icustays.intime; this makes the index ICU stay deterministic and avoids counting later ICU stays as independent index discharges.
4. Define the ICU-exit landmark t_d = icustays.outtime.
5. Retain only an alive ICU exit: admissions.deathtime is null or strictly later than t_d. A death exactly at t_d is not an eligible alive exit.
6. Do not exclude by ultimate discharge location. Preserve ward, intermediate/step-down, hospice/palliative, unknown and all other admissions.discharge_location values for descriptive audit.

The index ICU-exit clock ends at t_d; it is the deterministic landmark after conditioning on observed alive ICU exit, not a post-landmark competing event. The outcome clocks start at t_d:

- R: first later ICU readmission, the earliest later icustays.intime for the same (subject_id,hadm_id) with t_d <= intime < t_d+48h; exclude the index stay_id.
- D: hospital death, admissions.deathtime with t_d <= deathtime < t_d+48h.
- A: alive hospital discharge, admissions.dischtime in the same interval, only if it precedes R and D.
- E: no first event by t_d+48h while the admission remains in the same-hospital risk set.

These are first-event competing clocks. If R and D have exactly the same timestamp, assign D first, emit a data-integrity flag, and do not count later death after a readmission as a first-event death. admissions.hospital_expire_flag is a consistency check only; it cannot replace deathtime. Report the composite R or D only as a secondary summary. Do not use post-landmark values, transfer destination, later notes, or post-exit treatment in exposure or primary adjustment.

## Exposure windows and temporal boundary estimand

Use the parent’s fixed dictionary, thresholds and availability gate in both windows. Define:

- earlier window W_E = [t_d−48h,t_d−24h);
- late window W_L = [t_d−24h,t_d).

Each window has four half-open 6-hour bins. A primary core record must have charttime < t_d and, when storetime is non-null, storetime <= t_d; missing storetime is fail-closed for the primary exposure. For W_E, require the analogous record to be available by the end of that earlier bin/window and never use a record first stored after its relevant window end. Values at or after t_d are never predictors. Report a charttime-only sensitivity, labelled documentation-lag sensitivity, rather than silently mixing rules.

Core numeric channels and fixed transformations:

- 220045 Heart Rate, bpm;
- 220052 Arterial Blood Pressure mean, mmHg;
- 220210 Respiratory Rate, insp/min;
- 220277 O2 saturation pulseoxymetry, %;
- 223761 Temperature Fahrenheit, °F, converted to Celsius;
- 223762 Temperature Celsius, °C;
- 223834 O2 Flow, L/min;
- 223835 Inspired O2 Fraction, dictionary unitname None, numeric;
- 226732 O2 Delivery Device(s), text, descriptive sensitivity only and never coerced to numeric.

The two temperature itemids are collapsed to one Celsius channel; a bin fails closed if both are present but conflict after conversion beyond a prespecified implementation tolerance. Within each 6-hour bin use median HR/MAP/RR/SpO2/temperature and maximum O2 flow/FiO2; preserve valid-record counts and a missingness mask. Numeric values must be finite and unit-compatible. An item is not accepted by label alone: verify d_items label, abbreviation, linksto, category, unitname, param_type, lownormalvalue and highnormalvalue, and fail closed on missing or colliding dictionary matches.

Fixed domain flags, applied identically in W_E and W_L, are:

- respiratory/oxygen: SpO2 <92%, O2 flow >0 L/min, or FiO2 >0.21;
- hemodynamic: MAP <65 mmHg, HR >100 or <50 bpm;
- ventilatory pattern: RR >24 or <8/min;
- temperature: Celsius <36.0 or >38.3.

A domain is persistent within a window when flagged in at least two of four bins. Window exposure states are stable/no persistent domain, exactly one persistent domain, at least two persistent domains, or unknown/insufficient. The primary within-window contrast is at least two persistent domains versus stable. A patient is exposure-known for a window only if at least three of its four bins contain a valid primary core observation. Missing is unknown, not normal. The all-eligible flow retains unknown/insufficient subjects; the primary exposure-known estimand is explicitly restricted and the unknown fraction is reported.

The paired boundary cohort is the subset of eligible alive exits exposure-known in both W_E and W_L. It is fixed before outcomes are read. The primary boundary estimand is:

Δ_RD,k = RD_k(W_L, at-least-two versus stable) − RD_k(W_E, at-least-two versus stable)

for k in {R,D} at 48 hours, with each RD standardized under the same pre-t_d covariate distribution and the same competing-event definition. Report the two risk differences and their paired subject-bootstrap interval; do not infer a treatment effect from Δ_RD,k.

For trajectory transitions, first derive a fixed eight-bin domain history over W_E then W_L. Report a prespecified transition table for each domain-count state:

- stable→stable (no persistent domain in either window);
- stable→persistent (no persistent earlier, at least one persistent late);
- persistent→persistent;
- persistent→stable;
- other/unknown (including inadequate observation or non-identifiable transition).

Do not outcome-selectively merge these cells. Within W_L, also report the fixed descriptive classes stable, persistent across observed bins, newly abnormal in only the final two bins, resolving in only the first two bins, intermittent in two non-adjacent bins, and unknown when required. A late-specific phenotype predicts higher risk in stable→persistent and persistent/newly abnormal classes than in stable/resolving classes; a severity-selection explanation predicts similar risk in persistent→persistent and late-only states after earlier-state standardization, or a comparable W_E contrast.

## Matched fixed-summary baseline and same-question temporal alternative

The methods are selected to answer one scientific question: whether late ordering, persistence and transition state add time-local information beyond a fixed summary. They are not a contest for a small predictive gain.

Baseline B is an auditable fixed-summary cause-specific discrete-time logistic hazard model. On each paired subject it uses the same outcome clocks, W_E or W_L, six-hour post-t_d outcome intervals, deterministic patient-level split and static/pre-window covariates. Its predictors are:

- window exposure state, domain count and per-domain flags;
- window median/minimum/maximum/range and flagged-bin counts, with no bin order;
- valid-record counts, masks and total/core observation density;
- age, gender, admission type, ICU duration/timing;
- the fixed pre-W_L laboratory severity summaries and missingness;
- for the paired early analysis, the corresponding summaries computed in W_E under the same rules.

Fit separate cause-specific hazards for R, D and A; standardize predicted first-event CIFs to 48 hours, retaining alive discharge as a competing event. B is the primary scientific estimator because each input and coefficient can be audited and because it makes the late-versus-early comparison transparent.

Temporal alternative T is a one-layer GRU competing-risk model on exactly the same paired subjects, windows, outcomes, static covariates and pre-window severity. Each window is represented by 12 chronological two-hour bins with the seven numeric channels (temperature collapsed), per-channel missingness masks, elapsed-time/bin index, core and total observation counts, and fixed domain flags. Static covariates enter a static head. T outputs three cause-specific hazards for eight six-hour post-landmark intervals. It sees neither post-t_d observations, immediate destination nor any outcome-derived feature.

Use SHA-256 subject_id split 70/15/15; all admissions of a subject stay in one partition. Fit normalization and density cut points on training data only. Use seeds {17,29,43}, hidden size 32, dropout 0, Adam learning rate 1e-3, batch size 128, maximum 50 epochs, and early stopping after five validation epochs without improvement in mean cause-specific Brier score. Report every seed and the prespecified aggregate. The order-permuted control shuffles chronological bin order within subject while retaining values, masks and counts, using the same split and training protocol.

Held-out evaluation must include:

- cause-specific Brier score by 6-hour interval and at 48 hours for R, D and A;
- calibration plots/tables for 48-hour first-event CIFs with calibration slope/intercept or observed-versus-predicted risk by prespecified bins;
- AUROC/AUPRC only as secondary discrimination summaries;
- bootstrap uncertainty by resampling subjects within the held-out test split, retaining all repeated admissions of a subject together;
- standardized held-out predictions for stable, persistent, stable→persistent, persistent→persistent and persistent→stable classes;
- comparison of chronological T with order-permuted T.

A temporal improvement is scientifically supportive only if it is stable across seeds, improves held-out calibration/Brier in a clinically interpretable manner, and changes the prespecified transition contrasts or late-versus-early comparison. Similar chronological and order-permuted performance means the data do not support an ordering claim. A small discrimination improvement with unchanged transition contrasts is predictive utility, not evidence for a time-specific phenotype. Saliency is descriptive and not a mechanism.

Alternatives not selected: a transformer is deferred because the proposed question is a small, auditable boundary test and extra capacity would not distinguish a new scientific rival; revisit only if a bounded GRU/ordering diagnostic shows reproducible unresolved chronology and the solver envelope supports it. A causal treatment or discharge-policy model is deferred because no validated intervention, treatment intent, discharge rationale or goals-of-care field is available. A mechanistic latent physiologic model is deferred because the archive has no validated work-of-breathing, functional status, total-body fluid state, measured GFR or adjudicated treatment limitation. Evidence to revisit these alternatives is prospective clinical adjudication, a validated structured goals-of-care source, richer physiologic measurements, or an external cohort—not a small AUROC gain.

## Falsification and interpretation

Supportive evidence requires:

- in the paired cohort, Δ_RD,R and/or Δ_RD,D is positive with a 95% interval excluding zero, while the component CIFs and alive-discharge competing incidence are shown;
- the late contrast is larger than the earlier contrast under the same subjects and pre-window severity/density standardization;
- stable→persistent and persistent/newly abnormal classes are directionally higher risk than stable/resolving classes with adequate support;
- the direction is not confined to one adequately sized immediate-destination or density stratum;
- chronological T has stable held-out calibration/Brier behavior and reveals a reproducible transition/ordering contrast beyond B and the order-permuted control.

This supports a reproducible, time-local prognostic association compatible with a residual instability phenotype. It does not support causality, a safer discharge rule, or a mechanism.

Adverse evidence includes a late contrast no larger than the earlier contrast; attenuation after pre-window severity or density audit; a similar documentation-density gradient; destination-only concentration; signal confined to O2 flow/FiO2; reversal between charttime-only and conservative availability; comparable chronological and order-permuted T; or a T gain without stable transition contrasts. These favor residual severity, discharge selection, support meaning, ascertainment or mere predictive utility.

Inconclusive evidence includes a failed dictionary/unit gate, high unknown fraction, competing-clock inconsistency, fewer than 100 subjects or 20 events in a required stratum, sparse paired transitions, intervals spanning clinically important benefit and harm, or unstable seed/calibration results. Do not redefine thresholds, merge destinations, change the earlier window, or relabel unknowns after seeing outcomes. An imprecise null is not decisive refutation.
