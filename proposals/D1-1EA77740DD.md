# Research Proposal: Hemodynamic Variability Predicts AKI in Septic Shock

## Unresolved Question

**Current Knowledge Gap**: Clinical sepsis management emphasizes maintaining blood pressure (BP) above threshold targets. However, a critical unresolved question remains:

> **Does hemodynamic variability (measured as the coefficient of variation of SBP) during hypotension episodes predict acute kidney injury (AKI), and is this relationship independent of duration, minimum BP, and recovery rate?**

**The Gap**: Existing research focuses on:
- **Minimum BP** as a static threshold metric [K2]
- **Hypotension duration** as a cumulative exposure metric [K1]
- **Recovery rate** as a temporal trajectory metric (prior candidate)
- **Cumulative time below threshold** as a time-weighted metric [K3]

These approaches miss a critical dimension: **the stability of hemodynamics during hypotension episodes**. Two patients with identical hypotension duration, minimum BP, and recovery rate may have vastly different outcomes if one exhibits large BP swings (high variability) while the other maintains stable BP during the episode.

## Clinical Importance

### 1. Risk Stratification Beyond Static Metrics
A "variability" metric could:
- Identify patients at high risk despite meeting BP targets and having adequate recovery
- Predict AKI in patients with stable minimum BP but large BP swings
- Enable earlier intervention before irreversible organ injury

### 2. Treatment Optimization
Understanding the role of variability could:
- Inform whether aggressive BP control that creates large swings is beneficial or harmful
- Guide vasopressor titration strategies (prioritize stability vs. maintaining pressure)
- Identify patients who benefit from adjunctive therapies to reduce BP variability

### 3. Mechanistic Insight
This question addresses a fundamental physiological question: **Is AKI risk driven by cumulative exposure, recovery dynamics, or hemodynamic instability?** The answer has implications for:
- Pathophysiology of ischemia-reperfusion injury
- Optimal target hemodynamics in sepsis
- Interpretation of clinical trial endpoints

## Hypothesis

**Primary Hypothesis**: Among patients with septic shock, the *hemodynamic variability* (coefficient of variation of SBP) during hypotension episodes predicts subsequent AKI risk independently of:
- Total hypotension duration
- Minimum BP achieved
- Vasopressor exposure
- Rate of recovery

**Moderating Effect**: This relationship is strengthened in patients with:
- Lower baseline hemodynamic reserve (reduced cardiac output, elevated SVR)
- Higher vasopressor burden

**Expected Effect Size**: A 10% higher SBP coefficient of variation during hypotension episodes is associated with 1.3-1.8x increased AKI risk, after adjustment for minimum BP, duration, recovery rate, and vasopressor use.

## Methodological Alternatives

### Baseline Approach (Traditional)
- Logistic regression: AKI ~ hypotension duration + minimum SBP + vasopressor dose
- **What it tests**: Whether static summary metrics predict AKI
- **Limitation**: Cannot distinguish between cumulative exposure, recovery dynamics, or instability

### Alternative 1: Time-Weighted Burden Model
- Compute area-under-curve (AUC) of SBP deficit over hypotension episodes
- Model: AKI ~ AUC of SBP deficit + minimum SBP + vasopressor dose
- **What it reveals**: Whether total "pressure deficit" matters more than stability

### Alternative 2: Variability Model (Primary Analysis)
- Define variability as: Coefficient of Variation (CV) = std(SBP) / mean(SBP) during hypotension
- Model: AKI ~ CV + minimum SBP + duration + recovery rate + vasopressor dose
- **What it reveals**: Whether hemodynamic instability is an independent risk factor

### Alternative 3: Interaction Model
- Model: AKI ~ CV × vasopressor burden + minimum SBP + duration + recovery rate
- **What it reveals**: Whether vasopressors modify the relationship between variability and AKI

### Alternative 4: Machine Learning Baseline
- Gradient boosting or random forest with all temporal features
- **What it reveals**: Whether variability is a dominant feature compared to other temporal patterns

## Falsification Criteria

The hypothesis will be falsified if:
1. **Variability shows no independent association** with AKI after full adjustment (p > 0.05 for all coefficients)
2. **Minimum SBP fully mediates** the relationship between variability and AKI (indirect effect > 90% of total effect)
3. **No interaction** is observed between variability and vasopressor burden (interaction p > 0.1)
4. **No dose-response gradient** is observed (e.g., higher variability doesn't progressively increase risk)

## Expected Results and Interpretation

### Supportive Results (Hypothesis Supported)
- Variability is independently associated with AKI (OR ~1.3-1.8 per 10% higher CV)
- This association persists after adjusting for minimum SBP, duration, recovery rate, and vasopressors
- Vasopressor burden moderates the relationship (stronger in high-burden patients)
- **Clinical implication**: Aggressive BP control that creates large swings may be harmful; stability may be as important as maintaining targets

### Adverse Results (Hypothesis Refuted)
- Variability shows no independent association with AKI
- Minimum SBP fully explains the relationship
- No interaction with vasopressor burden
- **Clinical implication**: Current minimum-SBP targets are sufficient; variability is not an independent risk factor

### Inconclusive Results (Requires Further Study)
- Weak association (OR ~1.1-1.3) that loses significance with additional adjustment
- Heterogeneous effects across subgroups (e.g., by organ failure severity)
- **Clinical implication**: More refined stratification or mechanistic studies needed

## Novelty and Advance Over Existing Knowledge

### What This Adds
1. **New dimension**: Tests hemodynamic *stability* (variability) rather than just trajectory (rate) or cumulative exposure (duration)
2. **Temporal granularity**: Captures the pattern of BP fluctuations during hypotension, not just summary statistics
3. **Mechanistic insight**: Tests whether AKI risk is driven by instability (repeated ischemia-reperfusion) vs. cumulative exposure
4. **Treatment implications**: Informs whether aggressive BP control that creates large swings is beneficial or potentially harmful

### Limitations Acknowledged
- **Data limitations**: Vital sign recording frequency may vary; artifact handling required
- **Confounding**: Unmeasured treatment intensity, source of care, comorbidities
- **Causality**: Observational design cannot establish causation
- **External validity**: May not generalize to non-ICU settings or different populations
