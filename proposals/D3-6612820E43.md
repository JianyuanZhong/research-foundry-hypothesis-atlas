# D3-M3: Pathway-Activity Hypothesis — NF-κB/AP-1 Activation in RA Synovial Tissue

## Scientific Opening

Rheumatoid arthritis (RA) synovial tissue shows elevated expression of inflammatory genes (IL6, TNF, IL1B, NFKB1, REL, FOS, JUN) that drive joint destruction. However, the **pathway-level interpretation** of this signal is unresolved. Three non-equivalent explanations are compatible with the current evidence:

1. **Pathway-activation model**: The upregulation reflects genuine activation of the NF-κB and AP-1 transcriptional pathways in resident cells (FLS, macrophages). The pathway is constitutively active in RA synovium, and individual gene expression is a downstream consequence of pathway activity.

2. **Composition-driven model**: The apparent "pathway activation" is an artifact of changes in cell composition (e.g., increased macrophage infiltration, FLS proliferation) rather than a genuine change in pathway activity within individual cells.

3. **Stress-response model**: The upregulation reflects a non-specific stress response (e.g., hypoxia, nutrient deprivation, mechanical stress) that incidentally activates NF-κB/AP-1 reporter genes without representing a disease-specific pathway.

This distinction is clinically consequential: if the signal reflects genuine pathway activation, targeting the pathway (e.g., NF-κB inhibitors, JAK inhibitors) may be effective; if it is composition-driven, depleting the infiltrating cell type may suffice; if it is a stress response, addressing the underlying stressor is required.

## Hypothesis

**Hypothesis**: In RA synovial tissue, the upregulation of NF-κB and AP-1 pathway genes (NFKB1, REL, FOS, JUN, IL6, TNF, IL1B) reflects genuine pathway activation in resident fibroblast-like synoviocytes (FLS) and macrophages, rather than being driven primarily by changes in cell composition or a non-specific stress response.

**Strongest rival explanation**: The apparent pathway activation is composition-driven — the increase in pathway gene expression is explained by an increase in the proportion of inflammatory cell types (macrophages, activated FLS) rather than a change in pathway activity within individual cells.

## Clinical Importance

- If pathway activation is genuine: anti-inflammatory strategies targeting NF-κB or AP-1 (e.g., JAK inhibitors, NF-κB pathway inhibitors) may be effective in RA.
- If composition-driven: depleting infiltrating macrophages or modulating FLS proliferation may be sufficient.
- If stress-response: addressing the underlying stressor (e.g., hypoxia, mechanical stress) is required.

This distinction changes the next mechanistic experiment and could inform whether anti-inflammatory strategies should target pathway activity, cell composition, or the underlying stressor.

The D3-M3 expert seed asks: "Is a signal better explained by measured pathway activity, cell composition, or both?" We address this using the available single-cell and bulk transcriptomic datasets of RA synovial tissue:

- **Cell type proportions**: Proportion of macrophages, FLS, other cell types (from single-cell data).

### Outcome Variables

- **Pathway activity score**: Mean expression of pathway genes (NFKB1, REL, FOS, JUN, IL6, TNF, IL1B) per cell.
- **Composition score**: Proportion of inflammatory cell types (macrophages, activated FLS).

## Outcomes

1. **Pathway activation**: Significant upregulation of pathway genes in FLS and macrophages, independent of cell composition.
2. **Composition-driven**: Pathway gene expression is explained by cell composition (e.g., increased macrophage proportion).
3. **Stress response**: Pathway gene expression is correlated with stress markers (e.g., HIF1A, ATF4) rather than disease-specific markers.

## Baselines

1. **Simple baseline**: Mean expression of pathway genes per cell type, without adjustment for composition or donor.
2. **Composition-adjusted baseline**: Mean expression of pathway genes per cell type, adjusted for cell composition (e.g., macrophage proportion).
3. **Donor-aware baseline**: Mean expression of pathway genes per cell type, adjusted for donor ID.

## Analysis

### Method 1: Donor-Aware Mixed Model (Simple Baseline)

- **Model**: Linear mixed model with donor as a random effect.
- **Fixed effects**: Cell type (FLS, macrophage), condition (Mpos, Mneg, preLPS, preInhibitor, Inhibitor), cell composition (macrophage proportion).
- **Random effects**: Donor ID.
- **Outcome**: Pathway activity score.
- **Software**: R `lme4` or Python `statsmodels`.

### Method 2: XGBoost (Learned Alternative)

- **Model**: XGBoost classifier/regressor.
- **Features**: Pathway gene expression, cell composition, donor ID, condition.
- **Target**: Pathway activity score (continuous) or pathway activation status (binary).
- **Split**: Donor-aware split (train on donors 1-3, test on donor 4).
- **Evaluation**: R² (continuous) or AUC (binary), with 95% confidence intervals.
- **Software**: Python `xgboost`.

### Method 3: ODE Model (Mechanistic Alternative)

- **Model**: Ordinary differential equation model of NF-κB/AP-1 pathway activation.
- **Parameters**: Pathway activation rate, degradation rate, feedback strength.
- **Fitting**: Parameter estimation from time-series data (preLPS → LPS, preInhibitor → Inhibitor).
- **Evaluation**: Goodness of fit (R²), parameter uncertainty (95% confidence intervals).
- **Software**: Python `scipy` or R `deSolve`.

## Falsification Criteria

- **Pathway activation falsified**: If the donor-aware mixed model shows no significant upregulation of pathway genes after adjusting for cell composition and donor.
- **Composition-driven falsified**: If the donor-aware mixed model shows significant upregulation of pathway genes independent of cell composition.
- **Stress response falsified**: If the donor-aware mixed model shows no correlation between pathway gene expression and stress markers.

## What Supportive, Adverse, and Inconclusive Results Would Mean

- **Supportive**: Pathway gene expression is significantly upregulated in FLS and macrophages, independent of cell composition and donor. This supports the pathway-activation model and suggests that targeting NF-κB/AP-1 may be effective in RA.
- **Adverse**: Pathway gene expression is explained by cell composition (e.g., increased macrophage proportion) or stress markers. This supports the composition-driven or stress-response model and suggests that depleting infiltrating cells or addressing the underlying stressor may be more effective.
- **Inconclusive**: Pathway gene expression is not significantly different across conditions, or the effect is small and not reproducible across donors. This suggests that the hypothesis is not supported by the available data, or that the effect is donor-specific.
