# Research Proposal: Diuretic Initiation in Septic Patients with Fluid Overload

## Scientific Question

**Primary Hypothesis:** In septic patients with fluid overload, diuretic initiation yields greater clinical benefit when perfusion is stable compared to when ongoing hypoperfusion is present, even when fluid accumulation is present.

**Unresolved Question:** Does the physiological state of perfusion (stable vs. hypoperfused) modify the effect of diuretic treatment on clinical outcomes, beyond what is captured by fluid accumulation alone?

## Clinical Importance

1. **Current Limitations:** Clinical guidelines recommend diuretics for fluid overload in sepsis, but the optimal timing remains unclear. Standard practice often initiates diuretics once fluid overload is identified, without considering the patient's perfusion status.

2. **Risk of Harm:** Initiating diuretics in hypoperfused patients may precipitate acute kidney injury, worsen organ dysfunction, and increase mortality.

3. **Treatment Optimization:** Identifying patients who benefit from early diuretics vs. those who require fluid resuscitation first could save lives and reduce complications.

## Substantive Advance Over Existing Knowledge

**Closest Prior Work:** [Prognostic impact of early versus delayed loop diuretic administration in sepsis: a propensity score-matched analysis using the MIMIC-IV database](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11986544/)

**Limitation of Prior Work:** The referenced study focuses on early vs. delayed diuretic initiation but does not stratify by perfusion status. It treats fluid overload as a homogeneous phenotype.

**Substantive Advance:** This proposal refines the question by introducing **perfusion status** as a critical effect modifier. The hypothesis that diuretics benefit only patients with stable perfusion represents a mechanistic refinement: diuretics reduce preload, which is beneficial when the heart is not already failing due to hypoperfusion, but potentially harmful when the heart is compensating for inadequate perfusion.

## Hypothesis Refinement

**Original Seed Hypothesis:** "The effects of diuretic treatment differ between patients with stable perfusion and fluid accumulation and those with ongoing hypoperfusion."

**Refined Hypothesis:** After adjusting for baseline severity and confounders, septic patients with fluid overload and stable perfusion have significantly lower odds of 28-day mortality and renal replacement therapy (RRT) compared to those with fluid overload and ongoing hypoperfusion, when treated with diuretics versus continued fluid resuscitation.

**Quantifiable Prediction:** The odds ratio for mortality comparing diuretics vs. fluid resuscitation will differ by perfusion status (stable vs. hypoperfused), with a statistically significant interaction (p<0.05).

## Study Population

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- ICU patients aged 18+
- Diagnosed with sepsis or septic shock (ICD-10 codes A40-A47, R65.20, or diagnosis_icd_10 containing "sepsis" or "shock")
- Received at least one diuretic (loop or thiazide) during ICU stay
- Had at least one urine output measurement (outputevents or icu/outputevents)

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Active major bleeding (ICD-10 D65, D66, or diagnosis_icd_10 containing "bleeding")
- Pre-existing end-stage renal disease (ESRD) at ICU admission
- Patients without any fluid balance data (inputevents + outputevents)

**Temporal Boundaries:**
- Study period: MIMIC-IV ICU stays from 2018-01-01 to 2020-12-31
- Index time: First ICU day with confirmed sepsis diagnosis and diuretic administration
- Follow-up: 28 days from index time

**Key Variables for Analysis:**

**Perfusion Status Classification:**
- **Stable perfusion:** Urine output >0.5 mL/kg/hr for ≥6 hours, lactate <2 mmol/L, base deficit ≥ -5, SOFA score ≤ 8 for vasopressors
- **Ongoing hypoperfusion:** Urine output <0.5 mL/kg/hr for ≥6 hours, lactate ≥2 mmol/L, base deficit < -5, or SOFA score ≥ 9 for vasopressors

**Fluid Overload Definition:**
- Positive fluid balance (>500 mL over 24 hours)
- OR elevated central venous pressure (>8 mmHg if available)
- OR clinical signs of congestion (pulmonary edema on imaging)

**Treatment Assignment:**
- **Diuretics:** At least one diuretic administered in the 24 hours following index time
- **Fluid Resuscitation:** No diuretics in the 24 hours following index time

**Outcomes:**
- **Primary:** 28-day mortality
- **Secondary:**
  - Renal replacement therapy (RRT) initiation
  - Length of ICU stay
  - Length of hospital stay
  - ICU readmission within 30 days

## Baseline vs. Alternative Models

### Baseline Model: Treatment Effect Without Perfusion Stratification

**Model:** Logistic regression or propensity score-matched analysis comparing diuretics vs. fluid resuscitation.

**Equation:** logit(P(mortality)) = β₀ + β₁(diuretic) + β₂(confounders)

**Expected Output:** Overall odds ratio for mortality comparing diuretics vs. fluid resuscitation.

### Alternative Model: Perfusion-Stratified Treatment Effect

**Model:** Logistic regression with treatment-by-perfusion interaction.

**Equation:** logit(P(mortality)) = β₀ + β₁(diuretic) + β₂(perfusion_stable) + β₃(diuretic × perfusion_stable) + β₄(confounders)

**Expected Output:**
- Main effect of diuretics (β₁)
- Main effect of stable perfusion (β₂)
- **Interaction term (β₃):** Tests if treatment effect differs by perfusion status

### Information Revealed by Alternative

The alternative model reveals **effect modification by perfusion status**, which the baseline model cannot detect. This is clinically crucial because:
1. It identifies a subgroup (stable perfusion) that benefits from diuretics
2. It identifies a high-risk subgroup (hypoperfusion) that may be harmed by diuretics
3. It suggests personalized treatment recommendations based on physiological state

## Analysis Plan

### Step 1: Phenotype Construction

1. **Identify septic patients:** Match ICD-10 codes for sepsis/septic shock in hosp/d_icd_diagnoses
2. **Identify diuretic use:** Match drug_name in hosp/pharmacy to diuretic class
3. **Calculate fluid balance:** Sum inputevents and outputevents by 24-hour windows
4. **Classify perfusion status:** Based on chartevents vitals (lactate, urine output, SOFA score)
5. **Classify fluid overload:** Based on fluid balance and/or clinical markers

### Step 2: Propensity Score Matching

1. **Propensity model:** Multivariate logistic regression predicting diuretic use
   - Covariates: Age, sex, comorbidities, baseline severity, fluid balance, perfusion status
2. **Matching:** 1:1 nearest neighbor matching with caliper = 0.2 SD of logit
3. **Balance check:** Standardized mean differences < 0.1 for all covariates

### Step 3: Outcome Analysis

1. **Primary analysis:** Cox proportional hazards or logistic regression comparing outcomes between matched groups
2. **Interaction analysis:** Test for treatment-by-perfusion interaction
3. **Sensitivity analyses:**
   - Different fluid overload definitions
   - Different perfusion status thresholds
   - Time-varying treatment assignment
   - Subgroup analyses by sepsis severity

### Step 4: Falsification Criteria

**Supportive Evidence:**
- Significant negative interaction (p<0.05): Diuretics reduce mortality in stable perfusion but increase risk in hypoperfusion
- Hazard ratio for mortality in stable perfusion group < 1.0
- Hazard ratio for mortality in hypoperfusion group > 1.0

**Adverse Evidence:**
- No significant interaction (p>0.05): Treatment effect is similar across perfusion strata
- Hazard ratios are similar in both groups

**Inconclusive Evidence:**
- Interaction p-value between 0.01 and 0.10 (borderline)
- Small sample size in one stratum
- High confounding despite adjustment

## Limitations

1. **Unmeasured Confounding:** Despite propensity matching, unmeasured confounders may bias results
2. **Perfusion Measurement:** Perfusion status is inferred from limited vitals; not directly measured
3. **Treatment Adherence:** Diuretic dose and timing may vary; simple binary assignment may not capture nuance
4. **Selection Bias:** Patients with severe hypoperfusion may not receive diuretics, leading to healthy user bias
5. **Temporal Resolution:** 24-hour fluid balance windows may miss rapid changes

## Clinical Implications

If the hypothesis is supported:
1. **Treatment Guidelines:** Diuretics should be withheld in hypoperfused patients until perfusion improves
2. **Monitoring:** Perfusion status should be assessed before initiating diuretics in fluid-overloaded septic patients
3. **Risk Stratification:** Patients with fluid overload + hypoperfusion should be flagged for intensive monitoring
4. **Clinical Decision Support:** Real-time alerts for inappropriate diuretic initiation
