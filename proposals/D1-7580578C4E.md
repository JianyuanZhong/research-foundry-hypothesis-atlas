# Episode 7 domain-evolution child — laboratory-result process versus vital-observation process

Status: proposed child of assessed-valid `[prior hypothesis]`. No cohort count, fitted coefficient, prediction, metric, or clinical conclusion has been produced here. This document is the executable scientific design and evidence handoff.

## 1. Scientific opening and the changed claim

The parent’s combined process vector cannot answer the remaining clinically relevant question: is a transport-relevant process signal specific to laboratory-result recording, specific to vital observation, or merely a generic observation-density/documentation proxy? That distinction changes the next action. A lab-specific signal would justify checking analyte harmonization, result revision and laboratory workflow; a vital-specific signal would justify reviewing bedside-monitoring and vital-feed workflows; a joint signal that disappears under domain nulls would support retaining only state features; a signal that is unstable or selection-bound would justify abandoning process features until better evidence exists.

The inspected literature supports only the premises, not this eICU result. [K1] shows that sequential EHR representations can predict across medical centers, but does not separate physiologic state from recorded-process information. [K2] reports, from the abstract only, that missing-indicator information can be predictive while warning that shifts in missingness can impair transportability. [K3] shows why external discrimination, calibration, and data availability should be evaluated together, but does not identify which local observation process creates a transported signal. The three works therefore motivate, bound, and challenge the question; none supplies an answer for this cohort.

### Falsifiable hypothesis

Let T be the parent’s temporal patient-state model with no recording-process features. Let L, V, and B be respectively laboratory-result-process, vital-observation-process, and both process domains. For evaluation set e:

`G_L(e)=M(T+L,e)-M(T,e)`

`G_V(e)=M(T+V,e)-M(T,e)`

`G_B(e)=M(T+B,e)-M(T,e)`

where M is each prespecified performance metric. The primary transport contrasts are:

`X_L=G_L(external)-G_L(internal)` and `X_V=G_V(external)-G_V(internal)`.

The scientific hypothesis is that any non-negligible process contribution beyond T is domain-specific: at least one of L or V will have a held-out-hospital increment that is attenuated by its own T-state-matched null, while the other domain will not show the same pattern. The strongest rival is a generic workflow/documentation explanation: both domains behave similarly, neither domain is attenuated by its own state-matched null, or gains concentrate at EOL/landmark/coverage boundaries.

This is a descriptive transportability hypothesis. A surviving association does not establish that tests or bedside observations cause mortality, that a missing record means a missed observation, or that changing practice would improve outcomes.

## 2. Clinical advance and decision boundary

The advance over the parent is a domain-level attribution test with matched state, timing, hospital-held-out evaluation, and selection audits. It can change the next research decision:

- **Lab-specific support:** held-out L gain exceeds the prespecified negligible bound, exceeds its L-null contrast, is not explained by the +180-minute lab timing control, and is coherent in calibration/utility and S720/no-EOL audits. Continue with laboratory analyte/interface harmonization and clinician adjudication of result availability. Do not recommend changing testing.
- **Vital-specific support:** the analogous pattern occurs for V. Continue with bedside-monitoring/vital-feed review and timestamp validation. Do not interpret it as monitoring adequacy or a treatment effect.
- **Joint-only support:** B helps but neither single domain does, or the interaction is positive only in the combined model. Retain this as a descriptive joint observation signal; do not assign it to either workflow. A follow-up study with synchronized order/specimen/monitor feeds is required.
- **State explanation:** both gains attenuate toward zero under their domain-specific state-matched nulls and T retains calibration/utility. Defer or abandon process features; prioritize state harmonization.
- **Selection/workflow explanation:** gain survives the null but disappears at S720/no-EOL/IPW, is concentrated in one hospital, or lacks calibration/utility support. Stop process-feature interpretation and pursue data-quality/selection review.
- **Inconclusive:** intervals span the prespecified negligible region, viable hospitals fall below five, vital coverage collapses, or row/fold/null audits fail. Do not select a winner; repeat only after the identified data dependency is resolved.

No result is assumed. The above are prospective interpretation rules.

## 5. Leakage-safe features and the four domain models

All feature selection, clipping, imputation, analyte eligibility, and process-field eligibility are fit inside each outer training partition.

### Patient-state T

First construct the inherited value baseline V: age, gender, ethnicity, admissionweight, and the latest finite value in W0 for the 30 laboratory analytes selected by training-hospital patient coverage >=1%, plus its original result offset. Impute values with training-fold medians; no result text or discharge field is allowed.

Construct the inherited vital state:

- For each fixed candidate vital field with training-hospital patient coverage >=1%, retain finite values in W0.
- In fixed bins [0,120), [120,240), [240,360], calculate median and last finite value.
- Over W0 calculate first finite value, last finite value, last-minus-first change, and an OLS slope against observationoffset when at least two distinct offsets exist.
- There is no carry-forward. Fit robust clipping, if used, only at training 0.5th/99.5th percentiles; report a no-clipping sensitivity if clipping changes field inclusion. Median-impute values from training data without missingness indicators.
- Report row count, distinct-offset count, first/last offset, and observed-bin count as audits, but do not put these process audits into T.

T is V plus latest eligible vital values and these fixed vital level/change/trajectory summaries. It contains no lab-process or vital-process count/timing/presence feature. This is the parent’s temporal patient-state model and remains unchanged.

### Domain process vectors

For each selected analyte in L, include only recording-process summaries: finite result count, distinct result-offset count, first result offset, last result offset, observed-bin count, and presence. The result magnitude is not in L; it remains in T through the latest lab value.

For each eligible periodic and aperiodic vital field in V, include the analogous observation-process summaries, separately labeled by table and field: finite row count, distinct observation-offset count, first observation offset, last observation offset, observed-bin count, and presence. Include domain totals across L and V only as prespecified aggregates; do not collapse the two domains into a single primary process vector.

Define the four primary learned models on the same rows:

- **T:** patient state only.
- **TL = T+L:** lab-result process added.
- **TV = T+V:** vital-observation process added.
- **TB = T+L+V:** both domains added.

The primary scientific comparisons are TL-T, TV-T, and TB-T. The descriptive interaction contrast is `(TB-T)-(TL-T)-(TV-T)`, reported only with uncertainty and not interpreted as a biological interaction.

### Learners and model capacity

Use two explicit alternatives before any result is known:

1. **Simple V-Logit:** elastic-net logistic regression on V (age, gender, ethnicity, admissionweight, selected latest lab values and offsets), training-fold median imputation and standardization, `solver=saga`, `penalty=elasticnet`, `C=0.1`, `l1_ratio=0.1`, `max_iter=2000`, no class weighting, fixed seed. It is the interpretable value-only anchor for direction and calibration.
2. **Capacity-matched GBM:** `HistGradientBoostingClassifier` with `max_iter=300`, `learning_rate=0.05`, `max_leaf_nodes=15`, `min_samples_leaf=50`, `l2_regularization=1.0`, early stopping disabled, and one fixed seed. Fit the identical learner/preprocessing protocol separately for T, TL, TV, and TB (and V as a value-only GBM comparator). No model gets extra trees or tuning because it has more domains.

The scientific choice is the compact T/TL/TV/TB GBM comparison: it tests domain attribution while allowing nonlinear state/process interactions. V-Logit remains a low-cost robustness and interpretability anchor. This is not a search for the largest AUROC.

## 6. Domain-specific state-matched nulls and timing controls

All null assignments are deterministic, label-preserving, and performed separately within each hospital. For each outer fold, fit T on training hospitals and obtain out-of-fold T risk for training stays; use the fold-fitted T model for held-out stays. Form strata from T-risk quintiles with age, gender, ethnicity, and admissionweight bins as tie-breakers. Merge cells with fewer than 10 stays with the adjacent quintile. Assignment order is SHA-256(`"eicu-process-domain-null-v1\\0"+patientunitstayid`) and never uses Y or the process vector.

- **L-null:** permute each complete lab-process vector among stays within the T-state stratum, leaving T, vital process, hospital, and Y fixed. Fit/evaluate the TL null with the same fold protocol.
- **V-null:** independently permute each complete vital-process vector within the same type of T-state stratum, leaving T, lab process, hospital, and Y fixed. Fit/evaluate the TV null.
- **B-null:** apply the L and V assignments independently with separate salts to produce a joint null for TB. This is secondary because its interpretation depends on both domain marginals.
- **Timing controls:** for L-only, shift only lab process offsets by +180 minutes modulo W0 (`t+180` if `t<=180`, else `t-180`); for V-only, apply the same shift only to vital observation offsets, separately for periodic and aperiodic tables; for B, apply both. Preserve counts, presence, values, and T exactly. Recompute only first/last/observed-bin process summaries. The timing control tests coarse alignment, not causality.
- Retain the parent’s unrestricted process permutation as a secondary audit, clearly labeled weaker than the domain-specific T-state null.

The primary null contrast is `[G_d(real)-G_d(domain-null)]` for d in {L,V}; attenuation toward zero supports state-linked recorded information. A null that does not attenuate a real gain is evidence against a patient-state explanation, but still does not identify a workflow mechanism.

## 9. Interpretation and falsification rules

**Supportive for lab-specific transport:** external `G_L` has a 95% interval above zero and outside the AUROC negligible bound in the prespecified direction, the L-null attenuation is positive with interval excluding zero, the +180-minute L control removes or materially attenuates the gain, and no-EOL/S720/IPW results are not contradictory. Calibration or net benefit must not worsen beyond the prespecified negligible bounds. This supports a lab-result-process contribution, not a recommendation to test more.

**Supportive for vital-specific transport:** the same rule for `G_V`, V-null, and vital timing control. This supports a vital-observation-process contribution, not a claim about monitoring adequacy.

**Adverse to a domain interpretation:** the held-out domain gain’s interval is below -0.01 AUROC or -0.005 Brier, there is no calibration/utility support, or the gain survives its own state-matched null while concentrating in one hospital/documentation boundary. This favors a generic workflow or selection artifact, but does not prove which artifact.

**Inconclusive:** intervals span the negligible region; the domain ranking changes across folds; fewer than five hospitals or sparse EOL strata prevent stable cluster intervals; IPW effective sample size is unstable; vital coverage or field inclusion is materially collapsed; or any leakage, join, fold, or null audit fails. An imprecise null is not refutation.

If both single-domain gains are supported, report separate domain evidence only if the domain-specific null and timing results distinguish them; otherwise report a joint process finding. If neither survives, the next decision is to abandon process features and pursue state harmonization. If the results are mixed, preserve both favorable and adverse outputs and request clinical review rather than changing the endpoint, window, subgroups, or null after seeing results.

## 10. Alternatives, resource envelope, and raw-event deferral

The state summary plus capacity-matched GBM is selected because the scientific uncertainty is the lab-versus-vital process boundary, and its inputs are available with verified offsets and payload columns. V-Logit is retained because coefficients and calibration provide a transparent direction check. Both alternatives use the exact same cohort, folds, outcomes, uncertainty, and process controls.

A raw neural event model is deferred for a scientific reason, not because neural networks or GPUs are disallowed. It would introduce an additional uncertainty—irregular event-order representation and semantic encoding—before the domain question is resolved. The available tables do not provide order status, specimen collection semantics, monitor-feed provenance, interface-outage indicators, or clinician rationale; a raw sequence could model timestamp regularities without identifying their clinical meaning. It also complicates the matched capacity comparison and does not by itself resolve causality.

Revisit a compact GRU/temporal-convolution event model only if all of the following are observed and documented in a bounded readiness/future-study check: (a) at least 20,000 eligible S6 stays remain across at least five hospitals after exact joins; (b) at least 20% of eligible stays have three or more distinct vital timestamps in at least one fixed payload field and the field-level timing audit is not collapsed; (c) on a held-out readiness split, time-order shuffling that preserves event counts changes the T-state prediction by at least 0.01 AUROC in at least five eligible fields, showing that fixed summaries may discard substantive order information; and (d) the compact T/TP fits are otherwise executable and the additional model can use the same folds, outcome, cluster bootstrap, domain nulls, and an explicitly approved resource budget. If any condition fails, retain the summary design and do not add a neural model merely for predictive complexity.

Discovery and solver resources remain separate. The inherited bounded discovery check is a read-only vital-coverage scan using approximately 8 CPUs and 32 GiB; it is feasibility evidence only and is not a study result. The future solver envelope is at most 16 CPUs, 256 GiB memory, and 8 hours for extraction, five folds, model fits, null/timing controls, sensitivity analyses, and 2,000-cluster bootstrap. The proposed tabular fits require no GPU. A GPU may be requested only for the separately approved neural revisit after the conditions above; GPU availability is not an assumption of this proposal.

## 11. Causal and unavailable-evidence limits

eICU does not supply a validated death time for this label, order status, specimen collection time, bedside-monitor feed provenance, interface-outage indicator, clinician rationale, or an adjudicated EOL decision time. The process vectors therefore represent recorded-result/recorded-observation patterns, not testing orders, monitoring intensity, clinician behavior, or missing care. Hospital-held-out transport is not prospective external validation beyond this snapshot. No association surviving adjustment or nulling identifies a mechanism. A clinical recommendation would require synchronized order/specimen/monitor/interface data, expert adjudication, independent replication, and a prospective silent-impact study with patient-centered outcomes.
