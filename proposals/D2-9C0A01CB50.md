# MerTK/CD206pos macrophage control of the FLS effector program: causal brake vs state marker (episode 11)

Domain: therapeutic_targets (D2). Rubric: life-science-stage-aware-v1. Seed: D2-T2 (repaired/evolved).
Operation: evolve. Parent: [prior hypothesis] (valid). Key works: [K1],[K2],[K3].

## Unresolved question
Does the MerTK/CD206pos (MRC1+) synovial macrophage state **actively brake** the pathogenic
fibroblast-like synoviocyte (FLS) effector program, or does the pos-vs-neg effector difference
merely reflect macrophage **composition/state** and FLS donor identity? The prior branch tested a
within-donor pos-vs-neg effector-score difference; that contrast alone cannot separate a
MerTK-signalling brake from a macrophage-load/admixture or a state-marker explanation.

## Clinical importance
Roughly a third of RA patients fail to sustain remission. If the MerTK+/CD206+ state is a genuine
causal brake on matrix-degrading/inflammatory FLS output, MerTK **agonism** (resolution-restoring)
is the therapeutic direction. If instead it is a marker that can co-exist with pro-arthritogenic
function [K2], then simply enriching or blocking MerTK could lower inflammation without restoring
resolution [K3] — and could be harmful. The decision hinges on direction and dose-dependence.

## Leading claim vs strongest rival
- Leading claim (causal brake): effector output falls monotonically as the MerTK/CD206pos fraction
  rises, and rises when MerTK is inhibited.
- Rival (composition/state marker): the pos-vs-neg difference disappears once total macrophage
  content and a resident-like (FOLR2/TREM2) vs SPP1+ state are modelled; titration is flat; the
  effect tracks FLS donor identity. [K2] shows MerTK+CD206+ cells acquiring pro-arthritogenic
  (C1q/SARM1) function; [K1] shows the subset associated with remission — supporting measurement
  vs causal-brake are different claims.

## Baseline vs substantive alternative (method exploration)
- **Baseline**: interpretable gene-set **effector score** = mean z-score(MMP1/3/9/13, IL6, CXCL8/9/10, CCL2/5)
  per sample; within-FLS-donor paired Mpos vs Mneg difference.
- **Substantive alternative (mechanistic dosimetry)**: regression `effector_score ~ MerTKpos_fraction +
  total_macrophage_content + FLS_donor`, plus a macrophage-state covariate (FOLR2/TREM2 resident-like vs
  SPP1+), and an explicit **monotonicity test** across the 1:3 vs 3:1 titration wells. This separates a
  dose-dependent MerTK brake from a composition/load artifact — information the plain pos-vs-neg score loses.
- **Learned/neural model**: not justified — a targeted 445-gene panel with n=33 samples would not support a
  generalisable learned model, and there is no independent test set; a mechanistic covariate/dosimetry model
  is the scientifically useful alternative here. Documented reason for non-use.
- Unit of inference = FLS donor; macrophage donor nested. Exact paired permutation/sign test (not ordinary t-test).

## Estimand, outcomes, falsification
Primary estimand: within-FLS-donor paired difference in effector score (pos − neg), and its monotone dependence on MerTKpos fraction.
- **Supportive**: consistent within-donor pos<neg; monotone decrease with MerTKpos fraction; MerTK-inhibitor raises effector score.
- **Adverse**: no within-donor difference; flat/non-monotone titration; effect fully explained by macrophage content/SPP1+ state or FLS donor.
- **Inconclusive**: n=1 per titration arm; absent ligand genes; donor imbalance; cross-sectional anchor only → cannot separate; needs targeted ligand assay/wet-lab perturbation.
