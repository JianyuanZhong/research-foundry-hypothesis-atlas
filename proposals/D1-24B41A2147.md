# HCC repeat-TACE P-beyond-B validation with an operational assay-completion to downstream-recorded-care bridge

## Targeted successor and scientific question

This is an independent, targeted successor of `[prior hypothesis]` and `[prior hypothesis]`. It preserves the Lead's frozen adult HCC repeat-TACE prognostic design: the selected population and event construction, the B/P/event/Y clocks, annual forward validation, nested leakage-safe models, assay-separated missing-Y identification bounds, and the strictly noncausal interpretation.

The frozen primary question remains:

> Among selected repeat-TACE HCC episodes with a remote baseline assay B and a contemporaneous pre-repeat assay P, does P improve short-horizon prediction of the subsequent same-assay assay Y beyond B and the locked pre-event core?

The targeted operational question is:

> Does the observed post-event assay state (Y observed versus missing), together with the outcome-blind event-to-72-hour ascertainment opportunity, map to a prespecified pattern of downstream **recorded** care in the next fixed 30 days, separately for albumin and total bilirubin and without changing the primary prognostic estimand?

This is an operational bridge, not a claim that assay completion caused care, that recorded care was clinically appropriate, or that either assay improves patient outcomes. The HCC data can show only that records of encounters, tests, orders, medications, procedures, or documents occur after the event. They cannot establish completed care, treatment administration, intent, treatment response, toxicity, survival, hepatic failure, imaging response, external-care capture, utility, actionability, or benefit.

## What is supported versus untested

The parent candidates support a narrow feasibility statement: the selected repeat-TACE reconstruction and assay-specific observation process can be audited, while later outcome ascertainment is sparse and differential. They do not establish MAR, robust incremental prediction, or clinical usefulness. The new untested claim is deliberately weaker and falsifiable: if selective post-event assay recording is operationally connected to subsequent recorded care, Y-observed and Y-missing episodes, or their pre-Y opportunity strata, should exhibit different downstream record profiles under a fixed, reproducible window. A null or unstable pattern is equally informative: it would weaken the proposed operational bridge, though it would not prove equivalent clinical care or absence of unrecorded care.

No new HCC empirical result is claimed here. The complete reconstruction, models, bounds, and downstream audit remain compiler jobs.

## Operational bridge: prospective completion trigger and downstream recorded-care audit

### Unit and timing

The unit is one selected repeat-TACE event × one assay among all `BP_eligible` rows, with one row per patient-assay-event after the inherited duplicate and deterministic tie handling. The assay-specific exposure labels are `Y_observed` and `Y_missing`; the pre-Y opportunity category is attached without using Y. The audit begins strictly after the Y window, so downstream care cannot define or leak into Y:

* `care_start = event_time + 72 hours`, open at the boundary;
* `care_end = event_time + 30 calendar days`, closed at the boundary;
* downstream timestamp windows are `(care_start, care_end]`.

The 72-hour separation is intentional: it excludes the entire Y selection window and prevents a same-window record from being treated as downstream care. Event assignment, B/P eligibility, Y selection, model fitting, and missing-Y bounds are never changed by this audit.

Call the prospective operational rule `completion_trigger` only as a protocol label: at `care_start`, an episode with `Y_missing` would be flagged for prospective assay-completion review, while an episode with `Y_observed` would be recorded as assay-observed. The retrospective HCC files did not implement this trigger, do not contain a reason for noncompletion, and cannot identify whether a missing assay was clinically indicated. Therefore `completion_trigger` is not an intervention, adherence measure, or causal exposure.

### Downstream recorded-care outcomes

For every BP row, scan all linked rows after the event and create source-specific binary outcomes, first valid timestamp, and row counts. Counts are deduplicated by source row or by the relevant composite existence key; they are not patient-level rates unless explicitly labeled.

1. `D_encounter`: any **additional** encounter with valid `encounter time` in `(care_start, care_end]`; record first time and count of distinct (`patient master index`,`encounter number`) keys. The event encounter is excluded.
2. `D_lab`: any linked lab row with valid `Test time` in the window; record first time and count. Also report assay-specific repeat-lab flags for exact `Test == Albumin` and exact `Test == Total bilirubin` separately, with no pooling and no requirement for a finite result for the mere fact of a recorded lab row. These are recording outcomes, not adequate monitoring or biological response.
3. `D_exam`: any examination row with valid `Start Time` in the window; record first time and count. Do not use narrative result fields.
4. `D_order`: any non-drug order row with valid `Order Time` **or**, if absent, valid `Start Time` in the window; record the chosen first timestamp under a fixed precedence (`Order Time` before `Start Time`) and count. Orders are not completion or administration.
5. `D_medication`: any medication row with valid `Start time` in the window; record first time and count. This is a recorded medication entry, not administration, adherence, indication, or response.
6. `D_procedure`: any procedure row with valid `Start Time` in the window; record first time and count, with a separate descriptive flag for exact TACE-like text only if the inherited procedure parser recognizes it. A procedure row is not proof of intent, delivery, or benefit.
7. `D_document`: nonempty clinical-document row linked to an additional encounter whose valid `Visit Time` is in the window; record the linked encounter time and count of distinct linked encounters. The document's own row has no time, so this is an encounter-timed documentation indicator, not a document timestamp.
8. `D_any_recorded_care` is the union of the seven source-specific flags. Report its first source/time with a deterministic source precedence, but retain the component flags; do not treat the union as a clinical endpoint.

Do not use downstream rows to fill Y, B, P, or model covariates. Do not text-mine examinations or documents. Do not infer a negative care result from absence of a row: every outcome is named `recorded_*`, and missing source timestamps or administrative truncation are handled as incomplete, not zero.

## Prespecified falsification and interpretation rules

The compiler/verifier must execute positive, adverse, and inconclusive synthetic branches in addition to the inherited clock, bounds, and missingness tests.

* **Operationally concordant, not clinically supportive:** Y-observed or a pre-Y opportunity stratum has a reproducibly different downstream recorded-care profile, with adequate denominators, complete source windows, and consistent component direction. Label this `recording-pattern-difference`; it may motivate a prospective assay-completion workflow, but it does not establish assay benefit, need, causality, or completed care.
* **Operational bridge not supported:** component recorded-care fractions are similar within prespecified uncertainty, or directions reverse across sources/eras/assays. Label `no_reproducible_recording_difference`; this weakens the operational bridge but does not establish equivalent clinical care because unrecorded/external care is unavailable.
* **Selection-sensitive:** favorable primary complete-case prediction or a favorable recording contrast coexists with a global missing-Y envelope crossing zero, large observed-versus-missing gradients, materially different albumin and bilirubin observation, sparse cells, or strong calendar/arm heterogeneity. Label `selection-sensitive`; never upgrade to clinical relevance or benefit.
* **Adverse primary prognostic evidence:** a nonpositive adequately supported primary gain, repeated M1 degradation, or a global bound wholly nonpositive remains `adverse` for incremental prognostic prediction regardless of a downstream-care pattern. Downstream records cannot rescue the primary endpoint.
* **Unsupported/inconclusive:** failed BP or arm gates; incomplete administrative windows; out-of-range future Y; empty/degenerate training range; nonfinite predictions; fewer than five-cell reporting limits; nonestimable bootstrap; low patient-cluster support; assay disagreement; or dependence on full-source rather than training-range bounds. A favorable 2022 local audit cannot generalize to later blocks without support.

Precedence is fail-closed: unsupported/inconclusive status prevents supportive language; selection-sensitive status prevents a robust positive interpretation; adverse primary status remains adverse even if a care component is favorable. A downstream recorded-care difference is never called an outcome, clinical utility, actionability, quality, safety, or benefit signal.

## Computational versus clinical claims

Computationally checkable claims are source hashes and headers, duplicate-safe joins, frozen denominator/event reconstruction, exact clocks, Y observation and opportunity strata, source-specific downstream windows, first-time/count arithmetic, administrative completeness flags, assay separation, fixed denominators, deterministic bounds, model predictions, and bootstrap bookkeeping. Clinical adjudication or another prospective/external study is required for assay units and reference ranges; indication, intent, administration and completion; disease severity, tumor burden, imaging response, toxicity, mortality, hepatic failure; external-care capture; reasons for missing assays; patient-important outcomes; causal effects; utility; policy; and benefit.

The substantive advance is therefore narrow but operationally consequential: it links differential post-event assay recording to a prespecified, temporally separated audit of what care is recorded next, while preserving the frozen prognostic estimand and making every favorable interpretation fail closed at the boundary between recorded workflow and clinical benefit. No new empirical HCC finding is asserted.
