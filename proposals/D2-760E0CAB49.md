# Provisional hypothesis: macrophage–fibroblast context modifies the intervention-associated fibroblast transcript response

Seed: D2-T1
Status: provisional and evidence-bound; no inferential result has been computed.

## Proposed relationship

Among rheumatoid-arthritis fibroblast-like synoviocytes (FLS) exposed to the interventions represented in the available E-MTAB-8316 assay, the direction and magnitude of the intervention-associated transcript response will depend on macrophage context (monocyte/macrophage phenotype or coculture condition), rather than being a context-independent FLS response. Operationally, this is a reproducible intervention-by-macrophage-context interaction after accounting for donor pairing and repeated context measurements.

This is a hypothesis about an intervention-associated expression pattern. It is not a claim that any drug acts directly on a specific target, that the expression change is causal, or that a clinical outcome has been observed.

## Why it matters

Target prioritization is consequential when an intervention appears effective in one inflammatory microenvironment but ineffective or potentially harmful in another. A context-dependent response would justify prioritizing targets whose activity is contingent on the local immune state and would warn against treating an apparent drug effect as universally applicable. It would also distinguish a context-specific intervention response from a donor-specific or assay-specific pattern.

## What is currently supported, and what remains unresolved

The available evidence supports only that the configured therapeutic-targets collection includes an E-MTAB-8316 assay with FLS, macrophage/coculture-related conditions, and intervention-associated expression measurements. It does not establish that the interaction is real, reproducible, clinically meaningful, or mechanistic. The exact assay registry, condition labels, donor pairing structure, gene-level availability, and required normalization/quality-control decisions still need to be verified against the source files and catalog. No computation has been run in this provisional draft.

## Falsifiable test

Compare a donor-aware additive model (intervention effect plus macrophage-context effect, with donor/technical factors) against a model adding their interaction, using the same E-MTAB-8316 population and prespecified condition contrasts. The primary outcome is the interaction estimate for intervention-associated transcript changes; secondary outcomes are direction reversals and robustness across donors/replicates. Support would require a reproducible interaction with uncertainty that excludes the prespecified null region and consistency under the planned sensitivity checks. An adverse result would be a stable null or context-independent effect, or evidence that apparent interaction is driven by donor, batch, or condition misclassification. An inconclusive result would be insufficient paired donors, unstable estimates, or failure of assay/design verification.

The test cannot establish direct target action, mechanism, or clinical benefit. Those require additional perturbation, target-specific validation, clinical outcomes, or expert adjudication.

## Method comparison

A simple donor-aware additive baseline tests whether intervention and macrophage context have independent associations. The substantive alternative adds an interaction term and, if feasible, a hierarchical/donor-aware model to borrow strength across donors and quantify uncertainty. The alternative can reveal whether the intervention response changes sign or magnitude by context, which the additive baseline discards. Selection of the alternative is justified by the scientific uncertainty, not by predictive complexity. A mechanistic or perturbation-based alternative is deferred because the available data do not yet establish target-specific perturbation or causal mediation.
