# Hospital template or bedside concern? Decomposing laboratory-observation transport in eICU

## Status, parent, and scientific deliverable

This is a substantive child of [prior hypothesis], not an executed result. It preserves the parent's audited eICU snapshot, 24-hour landmark cohort, 14-analyte panel, six source tables, ten whole-hospital folds, next-seven-day in-hospital death outcome, and matched transparent/learned model comparison. The revision changes the attribution experiment, not the population or clinical estimand.

The future solver must newly fit: (1) the parent's value-only and observation-aware elastic-net pair and matched GRU/GRU-D-style pair; (2) an outcome-blind, cross-fitted observation-hazard model that separates measurement expected from recorded physiology/measured severity from a case-mix-standardized hospital template and a patient-specific residual; and (3) factorial, value-preserving perturbations that separately alter the hospital template and the patient residual. Completion requires frozen cohort/split manifests, all four fitted mortality models, the observation model, outer-fold predictions, process-decomposition files, artifact-control files, paired uncertainty, and a machine-readable claim-to-result table. A positive result is not required.

## Opening, evidence, and revised hypothesis

The strongest existing evidence supports only three narrower claims. Laboratory presence, frequency, and timing can carry outcome-associated healthcare-process information distinct from laboratory values [K1]. Mortality discrimination and calibration vary when models are transported among eICU hospitals [K2]. GRU-D can learn from values, masks, and elapsed times and improved mortality prediction in the authors' MIMIC-III and PhysioNet experiments [K3]. None establishes that hospital testing policy causes transport failure, or even that a hospital-level process rather than severity, concern, disposition, or interface conventions explains a process-aware model's transport gap.

Untested hypothesis: Among adults still in the ICU and hospital at hour 24, any excess whole-hospital transport penalty of a first-day laboratory-observation-aware model will be concentrated in the case-mix-standardized hospital component of measurement timing/frequency, rather than being fully explained by recorded physiology, measured APACHE severity, or the within-hospital patient-specific component compatible with clinician concern. If so, excess transport loss should rise with residual hospital-template divergence and improve when the target template is normalized to the source template while each patient's within-site process rank and all observed values are fixed. It must also survive timestamp/unit/deduplication controls and not reduce merely to predicting discharge or documented end-of-life care.

This hypothesis is about predictive reliance and transport, not the causal effect of ordering, withholding, or changing a test. “Hospital template” and “concern-compatible residual” are operational labels, not identified policy and intent.

The clinical advance would be a deployment-relevant diagnosis: whether an apparently useful day-1 ICU mortality model is fragile chiefly because it uses site-level observation conventions, or whether it uses patient-level concern/severity information that remains useful across sites. Those answers imply different next actions—process-invariant modeling/site recalibration, better physiologic measurement, or abandonment of a testing-policy interpretation—but no result authorizes changing care.

## Why the parent needs this child

The parent grouped all mask/count/gap signal as “observation process.” Generic shuffling, APACHE adjustment, site classification, and divergence correlation can show reliance but cannot localize it: hospital case mix changes observation distributions, patient-specific concern can coexist with site templates, and documentation conventions can masquerade as both. The child prespecifies a two-level decomposition and asks the rival explanations to make different observable predictions on the same patients, outcomes, models, and splits.

## Shared representations and matched outcome models

Construct twelve two-hour bins. After canonical-unit conversion and prespecified physiologic-range QC, retain the last value available in each bin. Fit scaling, winsorization, median initialization, vocabulary, and all imputers on outer-training hospitals only. Before first observation use a training-hospital median; afterward carry the last value forward. Value summaries are first/last/min/max/slope and imputed trajectories. Observation channels are analyte-by-bin masks/counts, first/last time, elapsed time, repeated-test indicators, and 15-minute co-documentation batch sizes. Revision delay and interface strings never enter primary mortality models.

Fit on identical outer splits:

- B-V: elastic-net logistic regression on age, gender, ethnicity, ICU type/source/stay type, training-only apacheadmissiondx vocabulary, and value summaries, with no explicit process indicator.
- B-VO: identical outcome, rows, value block, tuning, and regularization grid plus masks/counts/gaps/batch summaries.
- L-V: one-layer GRU over the twelve imputed value bins plus the common covariates, without masks or gaps.
- L-VO: parameter-matched (within 10%) GRU-D-style model using value, mask, count, and elapsed-time channels plus common covariates.

The elastic-net pair is the simple, auditable baseline. The learned pair is substantive because it can represent ordered value–recency interactions and repeated-test responses that static summaries lose. Report coefficients, decay terms, mask/gap ablations, five fixed seeds, and parameter counts. A boosted static model is deferred because flexibility alone does not separate site template from concern. A transformer is deferred because twelve bins provide no prespecified long-range question. Revisit either only if both baseline and GRU show reproducible underfit on training-hospital validation.

## New outcome-blind observation decomposition

This is the scientific core of the child. It is fitted inside each outer fold without held-out-hospital outcomes. The simple decomposition may stratify on a cross-fitted value-only prognostic score learned only in source hospitals; the learned measurement-hazard model itself uses no mortality labels.

### Simple stratified decomposition

For each analyte/bin mask, count, and gap feature, estimate its conditional distribution within outer-training data using value-only risk decile, age band, ICU type, broad admission diagnosis, and prior-bin observed values. In the APACHE-linked sensitivity add non-laboratory APS components, then add IVa acute physiology/APACHE scores in a separate full-score analysis. Apply the fitted expectation to internal and target stays.

Define the case-mix-expected component as that fitted expectation. Define a patient's process residual as observed minus expected. Within a target hospital, decompose the residual into (a) a leave-one-person-out hospital mean/conditional quantile template and (b) the patient's deviation from that template. Standardize hospital templates to the pooled source distribution of the conditioning strata with trimmed weights; flag target hospitals with effective sample size below 50% of their observed count or more than 10% outside training support rather than extrapolating.

Neither component is causally pure. The hospital component can retain unmeasured case mix; the patient component can contain severity, concern, protocol response, or error.

### Mechanistic learned alternative: measurement-hazard model

Fit a low-rank discrete-time observation model to the same 14×12 masks. For patient i, hospital h, analyte a, and bin b, model the probability of measurement from past-only values/abnormality, common covariates, elapsed time, and a shrinkage-regularized hospital×analyte×bin embedding. Fit by binary cross-entropy on outer-training hospitals. For a held-out hospital, freeze shared parameters and estimate only its site embedding from that hospital's unlabeled first-day masks/values; never use outcomes. Repeat with non-laboratory APACHE components in the linked subset. Publish held-out mask log loss/calibration, embedding rank/penalty, and shared-versus-site deviance.

This model can distinguish a shared physiologic/concern-responsive measurement hazard from a reproducible site departure while borrowing strength across sparse analyte/bin cells; the simple stratified decomposition cannot. It still cannot identify order intent or policy. Concordance between the simple and learned decompositions is required for a strong attribution; disagreement is inconclusive, not a reason to select the favorable method.

## Factorial perturbations and rival predictions

Every perturbation is outcome-blind, uses 100 fixed seeds, holds the dense imputed values and common covariates fixed, and is fed to each frozen observation-aware model. These are model-reliance diagnostics, not interventions on care.

1. Patient-residual shuffle (concern-compatible axis): Within target hospital, value-only risk decile, ICU type, broad diagnosis, APACHE quartile when available, and EOL stratum, permute only patient deviations while preserving the standardized hospital template. The paired increase in log loss and absolute logit change quantify reliance on patient-specific observation information beyond measured matching variables.
2. Hospital-template normalization (site axis): Preserve each patient's within-hospital residual rank and all values, but map the hospital template/conditional distribution to the pooled source template using fixed randomized quantile coupling. For the learned hazard model, replace only the held-out site embedding by the pooled-source embedding while retaining shared patient-state terms and fixed uniforms. Improvement in external log loss is N_c = LL(normalized VO_c) - LL(raw VO_c); negative is recovery. This analysis is transductive because it uses unlabeled target observation distributions and is not a zero-shot deployment estimate.
3. Factorial combination: Apply both operations. Nonadditivity on the log-loss and logit scales tests whether concern-compatible deviations have site-dependent meaning. Report all four cells (raw, patient shuffle, template normalization, both), not only a favorable contrast.
4. Generic deletion control: Thin 10% and 30% of observed cells MCAR with identical masks across model pairs, then reconstruct values/process channels. This distinguishes generic information loss from template-shaped sensitivity.
5. Template-shaped deletion control: Thin only denser target analyte/bins to pooled-source rates within value-risk strata; never invent unobserved values. Compare selective VO-versus-V degradation with generic deletion.

Additional diagnostics: correlate D_c with Jensen–Shannon divergence of both raw and case-mix-standardized templates; classify hospital from raw and severity-residualized process signatures with outer-hospital logic; and test whether value-only features reconstruct original masks. If held-out mask AUC exceeds 0.60, label value-only/process separation compromised and repeat with posterior-draw imputation; unresolved leakage makes attribution inconclusive.

## Four strongest rivals and adjudication logic

### Stable physiology and measured severity

Prediction: value-only models transport similarly to observation-aware models, D_c is near or below zero, or excess disappears after conditional residualization/APACHE restriction. Check the full cohort, the 72,552 IVa-linked stays, the 79,013 APS-component stays, non-laboratory components first, full scores second, and within-severity strata. Persistence bounds these measured explanations only; adjustment cannot prove a mechanism.

### Clinician concern

Prediction: patient-residual shuffling harms internal and external prediction similarly, while template normalization provides little external recovery and standardized template divergence does not track D_c. A value-triggered-repeat analysis—repeat measurement within six hours modeled from the preceding observed value and elapsed time—tests whether signal is concentrated in patient-responsive testing rather than routine site timing. This is “concern-compatible,” because orders/reasons, staffing, bedside assessments, and complete treatments are unavailable.

### Discharge and end-of-life selection

Prediction: observation channels predict alive ICU/hospital discharge as strongly as death, multinomial effects are dominated by transition timing, or death contrasts attenuate after excluding documented EOL by the landmark. Report broad (any save/discussion offset at or before 1,440) and 0–1,440 EOL exclusions. The audit found only 461 day-1 documented EOL stays, so absence cannot adjudicate goals of care. Never condition the primary analysis on future hospitalization.

### Interface and timestamp artifact

Rebuild all process channels under prespecified variants: conservative availability; result-offset-only; exact stay/name/offset/value duplicate collapse; one event per analyte per 15-minute batch; and restriction to clinically harmonized system-unit/analyte combinations represented in at least 90% of training hospitals. Construct audit-only hospital signatures from revision delay, revised-before-result fraction, raw-to-deduplicated ratio, and system/interface mismatch. If D_c or normalization recovery disappears under robust encodings or tracks artifact-signature divergence more strongly than physiological-residual template divergence, the site-policy interpretation is adverse.

Feasibility is measured: the preserved panel has 1,572,519 numeric rows available by the landmark, 28,762 apparently in-window rows revised after it, and 5,069 duplicate stay/name/time/value rows. A child audit found hospital delayed-row rates 0–4.46%, system/interface mismatch rates 0.01–52.1%, and an extreme revised-before-result convention at one site. These motivate the negative controls; they are not outcome results.

## Falsification and interpretation

Supportive of site-specific observation-pattern reliance: both co-primary learned-model intervals show source-internal gain (G_L^I < 0) and excess transport loss (D_L > 0); the baseline pair agrees in direction; D increases with case-mix-standardized residual-template divergence; template normalization selectively improves external VO log loss (N < 0) more than generic deletion or patient-residual shuffling; and the pattern persists in the APACHE-linked and at least two artifact-robust encodings. This supports model reliance on a site-varying observation pattern, not causal testing policy.

Supportive of shared concern/severity instead: observation-aware gains are stable across hospitals (D <= 0 with a reasonably narrow interval), patient-residual shuffling removes the gain, template normalization does not improve external loss, or APACHE/value conditionalization removes the excess. This is adverse to the child hypothesis but clinically informative.

Supportive of documentation artifact instead: excess and normalization effects disappear after conservative timing/deduplication/common-unit reconstruction or are concentrated in hospitals with extreme audit signatures. This falsifies a testing-practice interpretation while identifying a data-engineering hazard.

Supportive of disposition/EOL selection instead: effects are stronger for alive discharge states than death or materially attenuate after EOL exclusion. This weakens mortality-specific interpretation but does not establish why testing changed.

Inconclusive: hospital-clustered intervals cross clinically relevant directions; standardization overlap fails; simple and learned decompositions disagree; results depend on one hospital, model seed, imputation leakage, or one interface definition; or only an imprecise APACHE subset is available. An imprecise null is not invariance.

Abandon site-policy attribution after precise adverse results across both model classes and artifact encodings. Continue to process-invariant mitigation only after supportive, artifact-resistant results. Revise toward clinician-state modeling if patient residuals, not site templates, carry reproducible signal.
