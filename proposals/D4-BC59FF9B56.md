# Direction-A incremental-information experiment: baseline cystatin-C discordance beyond a flexible creatinine comparator

## Lineage, unresolved question, and clinical importance

This proposal evolves `[prior hypothesis]` without changing its UK Biobank snapshot, source joins, population, timing, equations, threshold direction, or selected-attender interpretation. The parent execution found a reproducible-looking Direction-A threshold association among selected first-repeat attenders, but its fixed main-effects adjustment cannot answer whether cystatin-C discordance adds information beyond a sufficiently flexible model of baseline creatinine-derived kidney function, age, sex, and follow-up time. A positive adjusted coefficient or in-sample fit improvement would not establish incremental out-of-sample information.

The falsifiable question is: **among baseline eGFRcr>=60 participants who return for a first qualifying repeat assessment 2–8 years later, does baseline cystatin-C discordance (primary binary indicator eGFRcys_0<60, with a prespecified continuous-discordance sensitivity analysis) improve repeated participant-level cross-fitted prediction of repeat eGFRcr_1<60 beyond a prospectively frozen flexible comparator using only baseline eGFRcr_0, age, sex, and elapsed years?**

This matters because the clinical decision is whether a cystatin-C measurement can identify additional people who will cross the creatinine-derived q=60 threshold when the usual baseline creatinine-derived estimate and basic demographics already support a flexible risk estimate. The substantive advance is an honest incremental-information test with out-of-sample calibration, absolute-risk capacity, and stability gates, rather than another adjusted association. It remains a protocol-biomarker threshold experiment, not a CKD diagnosis, measured-GFR validation, causal intervention, or utility study.

The parent aggregate result is retained as context, not silently re-estimated: Direction A selected cohort `n=16,372`, cells `(E0Y0,E0Y1,E1Y0,E1Y1)=(15,769,255,294,54)`, and fixed-model standardized RD `0.0201975923` with Bonferroni simultaneous 95% interval `[0.0087166801,0.0329636773]` and Holm p `0.0009995002`. Those results support proceeding to the unresolved incremental question; they do not support the stronger claim that cystatin C improves prediction.

## Evidence boundary

The strongest available evidence is the parent's aggregate selected-attender Direction-A association under its fixed model and gates. The new claim tested here is narrower and stronger in a predictive sense: baseline cystatin-C discordance improves *repeated out-of-sample* discrimination, calibration, or clinically interpretable absolute-risk capacity beyond the flexible creatinine comparator. Failure to improve these quantities falsifies the incremental-information claim even if an in-sample coefficient is nonzero. Support still does not establish CKD, chronicity, renal specificity, measured GFR, treatment/referral benefit, clinical utility, benefit from testing, risk among nonattenders, or transportability.

Attendance is a selection variable. All estimands are conditional on `S_A`, the selected qualifying first-repeat-attender cohort. Attendance is not a predictor, weight, imputation variable, transport mechanism, or causal intervention. No result is reported for baseline nonattenders.

## Prospectively frozen nested prediction models

The estimand is predictive and conditional on `S_A`. Every model is fit inside training data only. Every reported prediction for a participant is obtained without using that participant's outcome in training.

### Comparator `M0`: flexible baseline-only risk model

Predictors are exactly `eGFRcr_0`, baseline age `21003-0.0`, sex `31-0.0`, and elapsed years `t=(date_1-date_0)/365.25`; no cystatin-C field, repeat assay, attendance indicator, or post-outcome variable enters `M0`. The fixed learner library is:

1. intercept-only logistic model;
2. logistic regression with sex and linear main effects of eGFRcr_0, age, and `t`;
3. logistic regression with prespecified natural cubic spline bases (4 internal knots at the training-fold 5th, 35th, 65th, and 95th percentiles, with boundary knots at the training-fold minimum and maximum) for each of eGFRcr_0, age, and `t`, plus sex and no interactions;
4. a fixed-depth gradient-boosted logistic learner with 200 trees, learning rate 0.03, maximum depth 2, minimum leaf size 50, subsampling 0.8, and no feature subsampling;
5. a fixed random forest with 500 trees, minimum leaf size 100, square-root feature selection, and seed fixed from the outer repeat/fold only.

The library and hyperparameters are frozen before looking at outcomes in held-out folds. Within each outer training partition, choose convex nonnegative ensemble weights summing to one by an inner 5-fold participant-level cross-validated log-loss minimization, with a deterministic tie-break toward the simpler learner order above. No outcome-derived feature selection, tuning, calibration, or threshold selection may use the outer assessment partition.

### Incremental model `M1`: comparator plus cystatin-C discordance

`M1` uses the identical learner library, preprocessing, inner-fold procedure, and hyperparameters, with one additional primary feature `E_A=1(eGFRcys_0<60)`. This is a nested information comparison because the only newly available input is baseline cystatin-C threshold discordance. A prespecified sensitivity model adds continuous `D=eGFRcys_0-eGFRcr_0` instead of `E_A`; it is secondary and does not replace the primary binary question. No interaction with exposure is added in the primary analysis. A separate exploratory interaction analysis is labelled exploratory and cannot rescue a failed primary gate.

For numerical safety, all probability predictions are clipped only for evaluating log loss to `[1e-6,1-1e-6]`; unclipped predictions are used for calibration and absolute-risk capacity, and any clipping frequency is reported. Missing values are not imputed: the exact parent complete-case `S_A` is the analysis population. Any model failure, nonfinite prediction, or unsupported extrapolation causes that outer fold/repeat to fail rather than being repaired by outcome-informed substitution.

## Repeated participant-level cross-fitting and estimands

Use `K=5` outer folds and `J=20` independently seeded repeats. Assign folds at participant level using a deterministic hash of private `eid` and the frozen repeat seed; never split rows from one participant across folds. All repeats use the same `S_A` and outcome but independent fold assignments. Save only aggregate fold/repeat diagnostics and paired aggregate predictions needed for analysis.

For each repeat, obtain paired out-of-fold predictions `p0_ij` and `p1_ij` for every participant. Primary estimands, averaged over repeats, are:

- cross-fitted log-loss improvement `DeltaLL = LL(M0)-LL(M1)`;
- Brier-score improvement `DeltaBrier = Brier(M0)-Brier(M1)`;
- discrimination change in cross-fitted AUROC and area under the precision-recall curve, reported descriptively because discrimination is not itself clinical utility;
- calibration intercept and slope for each model, calibration-in-the-large, and observed-versus-predicted risk by prespecified risk bins (0–1%, 1–2%, 2–3%, 3–5%, 5–10%, >10%, with pooling only by a frozen rule when a bin has fewer than 20 outcomes);
- paired absolute-risk separation `A = mean(p1-p0)` and paired mean absolute prediction change `mean(abs(p1-p0))`, explicitly interpreted as model prediction quantities, not counterfactual treatment effects;
- capacity at prespecified absolute-risk thresholds `q=0.03` and `q=0.05`: `C_m(q)=n^{-1} sum_i 1(p_mi>=q)`, capacity change `C_1(q)-C_0(q)`, and observed event risk among each selected set. The 3% threshold is primary because it is near the parent Direction-A standardized risk range; 5% is a clinically interpretable secondary stress test. Thresholds and all gates are frozen before execution.

For each participant, first average its 20 out-of-fold predictions within model, then compute participant-level summaries; do not treat the 20 predictions as 20 independent people. The primary evidence for incremental information requires improvement in `DeltaLL` and `DeltaBrier` with a two-sided 95% interval excluding zero, no material worsening of calibration, and reproducible direction in at least 18 of 20 repeats. A purely tiny statistically detectable improvement is not called clinically meaningful: report the full absolute-risk and capacity changes with intervals, and label the result statistically supportive but clinically negligible if it fails the prespecified practical margin of `DeltaBrier >= 0.001` or yields capacity change below 1 percentage point at both thresholds.

## Calibration, support, and multiplicity gates

A valid primary result requires all of the following: every outer repeat/fold has both outcome classes in training and assessment; all fits converge and produce finite probabilities; no fold has a learner-specific separation or rank failure that changes the selected library; at least 19/20 repeats complete for both models, with any missing repeat disclosed; the effective support for the incremental feature is adequate (both `E_A` arms represented in every outer assessment fold and at least 20 events in each arm in the pooled selected cohort); and the baseline feature ranges in assessment folds lie within training support or the fold is flagged and the corresponding estimand is not promoted.

For calibration, require pooled cross-fitted calibration slope for `M1` in `[0.8,1.2]` and calibration intercept in `[-0.02,0.02]`, with bootstrap intervals reported; `M1` must not worsen absolute calibration error by more than 0.01 versus `M0`. These are support gates, not claims that UKB calibration transports. Report fold-level calibration and prediction-range diagnostics so a pooled result cannot conceal a failed repeat.

The primary multiplicity family contains the two nested predictive improvements (`DeltaLL`, `DeltaBrier`) and the two capacity thresholds. Control family-wise error at 0.05 using Holm across these four prespecified tests. AUROC/AUPRC, continuous-discordance, interaction, and alternative horizons are secondary and clearly labelled. The primary incremental claim requires positive point estimates, Holm rejection for at least one of `DeltaLL` or `DeltaBrier`, no adverse calibration gate, and reproducibility/support gates. If only capacity changes reject, report that as capacity evidence without claiming general predictive improvement.

Use participant-level paired nonparametric bootstrap with at least 2,000 successful resamples for confidence intervals of all primary metrics, resampling participants as units and retaining each participant's complete set of repeat predictions. For stricter uncertainty, repeat the entire 5-fold/20-repeat fitting procedure in at least 500 bootstrap samples if computationally feasible; otherwise mark the paired-prediction interval as conditional on the frozen cross-fitting fits and do not overstate it. Use percentile intervals for descriptive metrics and a simultaneous max-|t| bootstrap interval for the four primary tests. A bootstrap failure, instability, or sensitivity interval crossing the practical margin is adverse or inconclusive, not a reason to select a favourable seed.

## Falsification and interpretation

Supportive evidence requires a positive, repeat-reproducible out-of-sample improvement for the primary binary discordance addition, acceptable calibration and support, Holm-adjusted inference, and a clinically interpretable nontrivial absolute-risk/capacity change. This would justify the limited conclusion that cystatin-C discordance contains incremental predictive information for the selected protocol outcome in this UKB selected-attender cohort, motivating external validation with adjudicated kidney outcomes.

Adverse evidence includes negative `DeltaLL`/`DeltaBrier`, worsening calibration, capacity change in the wrong direction, a positive result only in-sample, improvement confined to one or two repeats, loss of support, failure of nested fitting, or a strong parent association disappearing under `M0`. This would falsify the claim that the parent association demonstrates useful incremental information beyond a flexible creatinine comparator. A result with a nominal improvement but failed gates is not supportive.

Inconclusive evidence includes wide intervals spanning zero and the practical margin, fewer than 19 completed repeats, missing outcome arms, unstable fold results, or inability to certify the equation/field semantics. It should lead to a larger or externally validated study, not a claim of no biological signal.

Even a fully supportive result cannot establish CKD chronicity (no required repeated 3-month confirmation), measured GFR, assay traceability, renal specificity, causal effects of ordering cystatin C, clinical utility, treatment benefit, or performance in baseline nonattenders. Those claims require clinically adjudicated longitudinal outcomes, assay and laboratory metadata, decision-analytic or prospective implementation studies, and validation in an independent population. Computationally checkable claims are source integrity, joins, filter counts, equation arithmetic, fold separation, fitting, cross-fitted metrics, calibration, support, bootstrap, multiplicity, and aggregate-only privacy compliance.
