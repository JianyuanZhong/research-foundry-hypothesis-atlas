# Temporal Feedback Structure of Macrophage-FLS Inflammatory Signaling in RA Synovial Tissue

## Scientific Opening

In rheumatoid arthritis (RA), synovial tissue exhibits a self-reinforcing inflammatory loop between macrophages and fibroblast-like synoviocytes (FLS) [K1]. The crosstalk is well documented: FLS secrete IL-34, CCL2, and CXCL12 that recruit and polarize macrophages toward the M1 phenotype, while M1 macrophage-derived TNF-α, IL-6, and IL-1β further activate FLS [K1]. However, the **temporal ordering** of this feedback is unresolved. Three non-equivalent explanations are compatible with the current evidence:

1. **Unidirectional macrophage-driven model**: Macrophage activation precedes and drives the FLS inflammatory response. FLS are passive recipients of macrophage-derived signals.
2. **Bidirectional feedback loop**: Macrophage and FLS activation are mutually reinforcing with no clear temporal precedence. Both cell types can initiate the loop.
3. **Composition-driven model**: The apparent "feedback" is an artifact of changes in cell composition (e.g., increased macrophage infiltration) rather than a genuine dynamic interaction between cell types.

This distinction is clinically consequential: if the loop is bidirectional with phase-dependent precedence, therapeutic interventions must target both cell types at different disease stages; if unidirectional, a single-cell-type intervention may suffice.

## Hypothesis

In RA synovial tissue, the temporal ordering of macrophage-FLS inflammatory signaling is **bidirectional rather than unidirectional**, with macrophage activation preceding FLS activation in the early inflammatory phase and FLS activation preceding macrophage activation in the established inflammatory phase.

## Evidence Base

The hypothesis is supported by:
- The documented crosstalk between macrophages and FLS in RA synovial tissue [K1]
- The availability of single-cell RNA-seq data from RA synovial tissue (E-MTAB-8873, GSE95588) and paired bulk RNA-seq data (E-MTAB-8316, E-MTAB-8322) that can be used to test the temporal ordering
- The methodological framework of donor-aware mixed models that can account for donor-specific variation

## Competing Explanations

1. **Unidirectional macrophage-driven model**: Macrophage activation precedes and drives the FLS inflammatory response. Observable prediction: macrophage activation markers (CD68, CD163) will show a consistent temporal lead over FLS activation markers (IL6, TNF, IL1B) across all disease phases.
2. **Bidirectional feedback loop**: Macrophage and FLS activation are mutually reinforcing with phase-dependent precedence. Observable prediction: the temporal ordering will differ between early and established inflammatory phases, with macrophage activation leading in the early phase and FLS activation leading in the established phase.
3. **Composition-driven model**: The apparent feedback is an artifact of changes in cell composition. Observable prediction: the temporal ordering will be explained by changes in the proportion of macrophages and FLS rather than by changes in the transcriptional state of individual cells.
4. **Donor-specific model**: The temporal ordering varies across donors. Observable prediction: the interaction coefficient (β_3) will vary significantly across donors, with some donors showing unidirectional and others showing bidirectional patterns.

## Falsifiable Test

A donor-aware mixed model on the Mpos vs Mneg contrast will test whether the temporal ordering of macrophage and FLS activation is bidirectional. The model will estimate the interaction coefficient (β_3) between macrophage activation and FLS activation, with donor as a random effect. A significant positive β_3 would support the bidirectional model; a non-significant β_3 would support the unidirectional model.

## Method Alternatives

1. **Donor-aware mixed model** (primary): Estimates the interaction coefficient (β_3) with donor as a random effect. Advantage: accounts for donor-specific variation. Disadvantage: requires a sufficient number of donors.
2. **XGBoost** (alternative): A machine learning approach that can capture non-linear interactions. Advantage: can handle high-dimensional data. Disadvantage: less interpretable than the mixed model.
3. **ODE model** (alternative): A mechanistic model that can capture the temporal dynamics. Advantage: can provide a mechanistic interpretation. Disadvantage: requires more assumptions about the underlying dynamics.

## Clinical Importance

Resolving the temporal ordering of the macrophage-FLS feedback loop is clinically consequential: if the loop is bidirectional with phase-dependent precedence, therapeutic interventions must target both cell types at different disease stages; if unidirectional, a single-cell-type intervention may suffice. This distinction directly informs treatment strategy for RA.

## Limitations

- The available data can test whether the feedback is bidirectional with phase-dependent precedence, but cannot establish causality or determine the molecular mechanisms underlying the temporal ordering.
- The donor-aware mixed model requires a sufficient number of donors to estimate the random effect.
- The XGBoost and ODE alternatives are less interpretable than the mixed model.
