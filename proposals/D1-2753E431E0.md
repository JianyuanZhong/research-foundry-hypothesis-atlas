# Provisional hypothesis: discordant AKI recovery and subsequent risk

Status: provisional, not yet selected or validated. This draft is grounded in the expert seed's stated observation, not in a completed analysis.

## Proposed relationship

Among adults with acute kidney injury (AKI) in MIMIC-IV, a fall in creatinine that is not accompanied by improvement in urine output or fluid status will identify a subgroup at higher risk of subsequent death or kidney-related adverse events than a creatinine-only recovery phenotype.

The clinically important unresolved claim is that creatinine decline can be a misleading signal of recovery when renal output and volume status remain abnormal. Resolving this could change how clinicians interpret AKI recovery and prioritize monitoring, while a null result would support a simpler creatinine-based assessment.

## Falsifiable test

Define a fixed landmark after AKI onset and require adequate measurements in the preceding window. Compare patients with falling creatinine but no improvement in urine output/fluid status against those with falling creatinine and concordant improvement, adjusting for baseline severity and prior kidney function. Primary outcomes are subsequent in-hospital death and a prespecified kidney adverse event; secondary outcomes are ICU-free days and renal replacement therapy escalation.

Supportive evidence would be a reproducible excess risk for discordant recovery with uncertainty excluding a clinically trivial effect, and a pattern that persists after accounting for severity and observation intensity. Adverse evidence would be no excess risk or a reversal after adjustment, indicating that creatinine-only recovery is adequate or that the phenotype is driven by confounding. Inconclusive evidence would include too few discordant patients, excessive missingness, unstable estimates, or inability to define urine/fluid trajectories reliably; this would motivate a revised measurement strategy rather than a clinical conclusion.

## Method comparison

A simple baseline will use prespecified categorical recovery groups and a penalized logistic/Cox model with baseline covariates. A substantive alternative will use a trajectory or joint longitudinal model for creatinine, urine output, and fluid balance, with the same landmark, outcomes, split, and uncertainty evaluation. The alternative can reveal whether the timing and co-movement of measurements—not merely their endpoint categories—carry prognostic information; it may also expose whether a single creatinine slope is misleading. The baseline is retained if it is sufficiently precise and more interpretable. No model result is planned in this provisional draft.
