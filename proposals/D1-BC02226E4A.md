# Episode 49 fallback: does lactate trajectory change a fixed-capacity 24-hour high-risk review list?

## Unresolved question, clinical importance, and advance

At the 24-hour landmark, a clinician already has the late lactate value. The selected parent established that adding the continuous trajectory from an earlier lactate to a leakage-controlled late-value model changed leave-one-hospital-out AUROC by only +0.00479 (95% copied-hospital refit-bootstrap interval -0.00849 to +0.02286), with small favorable point changes in Brier score and log loss, acceptable calibration, and positive AUROC differences in all 33 site deletions. That is the strongest claim supported by the available EICU evidence: this specific trajectory model produced a small, site-stable point improvement, while the data did not precisely exclude the parent's +0.02 global-AUROC target.

Global AUROC averages performance over all possible thresholds and does not answer whether that small shift changes the people selected when review capacity is fixed. The unresolved claim is therefore:

**Primary falsifiable hypothesis.** In the parent's exact age-inclusive, 33-hospital EICU 24-hour landmark population, replacing the current-value model M0 with the continuous-trajectory model M1 will increase the pooled sensitivity for subsequent hospital death by at least 2 absolute percentage points when both models must flag exactly the same hospital-stratified top 20% of people for hypothetical additional clinical review. Support also requires no adverse proper-score or calibration signal under the rules below.

Because there are 507 deaths, a +0.02 sensitivity difference corresponds to approximately 10 additional eventual deaths placed in the fixed-capacity review set in the observed cohort. The 20% capacity is a prespecified operational research scenario, not a validated treatment threshold, MCID, existing protocol, or claim that reviewing 20% is optimal. The clinical advance over the parent is an interpretable resource-constrained risk-ranking quantity: whether retaining the earlier lactate materially changes whom a fixed review process would prioritize. A positive result could justify prospective validation of a trajectory-informed review tool; it would not establish that any review or treatment improves outcomes.

The Episode 48 binary nonclearance branch cannot answer this question. Conditioning a thresholded nonclearance exposure on the late value generated near-deterministic propensity scores (0.000141-0.992760 with 177 violations), so its -0.92-point association was correctly labeled inconclusive and nonestimable. This successor uses continuous prediction scores and matched review capacity; it estimates no binary exposure effect and requires no exposure propensity model.

The inspected full-text XML of Baysan et al. (Crit Care Explor 2022; DOI 10.1097/CCE.0000000000000750; PMCID PMC9444407; frozen source [source checksum], [source checksum]) reports a 4,440-person MIMIC validation in which adding 24-hour lactate to recalibrated APACHE IV increased C-statistic from 0.62 to 0.64 and the model containing admission plus 24-hour lactate had the smallest AIC. This supports modest prognostic contribution of serial lactate values, not trajectory beyond the late value at a fixed alert burden. The inspected full-text XML of Varga et al. (Diagnostics 2026; DOI 10.3390/diagnostics16101522; PMCID PMC13205222; frozen source [source checksum], [source checksum]) describes an exploratory single-center 48-hour landmark cohort of 126 patients and reports persistent late lactate as informative while lactate clearance was not; the authors explicitly did not present a validated individual-level calculator. Neither study resolves this EICU operating-point hypothesis.

## Models, predictions, and fixed-capacity estimand

Reproduce the parent's cross-hospital models exactly. A sanity baseline assigns each held-out person the training-fold mortality prevalence. M0 contains age and age squared; Female and Male indicators with Other/Unknown omitted; t0/1440 and its square; baseline collection lag `(t0-B_offset)/360`; late-measurement age `(L24-F_offset)/720`; ln(F) and its square. M1 adds T and T squared. X is not modeled.

Within each leave-one-hospital-out fold, winsorize every continuous design column at training-fold 1st and 99th percentiles and standardize using the training-fold mean and population SD; apply constants unchanged to held-out rows. Fit unweighted L2 logistic regression with intercept, C=1.0, lbfgs, max_iter=10000, tolerance=1e-8, and no class weights. Hospital identity and outcomes from the held-out hospital are never predictors or preprocessing inputs. Require one finite unrecalibrated M0 and M1 probability per person.

For each held-out hospital h with n_h people, set q_h=max(1,ceil(0.20*n_h)). Separately for M0 and M1, flag the q_h largest predicted probabilities. Resolve exact score ties by ascending numeric `patientunitstayid`; outcome is never used for ranking. Both models therefore flag exactly Q=sum_h q_h people and exactly q_h in each hospital.

Primary metrics are:
- Sensitivity_m = sum(Y_i A_im)/sum(Y_i), where A_im is the model-specific flag.
- PPV_m = sum(Y_i A_im)/Q.
- DeltaSensitivity=Sensitivity_M1-Sensitivity_M0. Positive favors trajectory.
- Net additional captured deaths D=sum_i Y_i(A_iM1-A_iM0). With fixed Q, DeltaSensitivity=D/507 in the observed cohort and DeltaPPV=D/Q; report all three.
- The number entering and leaving the list, outcome counts among entrants and leavers, and overlap/Jaccard of M0 and M1 lists are required descriptive outputs.

The materiality margin is DeltaSensitivity >= +0.02. This is a transparent cohort-scale design target corresponding to about 10 additional deaths captured in the observed data, not a universal clinical MCID.

Prespecified guards reproduce pooled M1 calibration-in-the-large and slope and proper scores. Positive DeltaBrier=Brier(M0)-Brier(M1) favors M1. Positive DeltaLogLoss is defined analogously. Report pooled AUROC only as inherited secondary context; it cannot override the primary label.

## Uncertainty, gates, and falsification

Use seed 20260949 and 2,000 hierarchical copied-hospital refit replicates. Sample the 33 original hospitals with replacement. Each sampled hospital copy carries all its sampled people; within each selected original hospital, sample people with replacement to preserve person-level uncertainty, assigning unique copy IDs while retaining original hospital grouping. All copies of an original hospital are held out together; other sampled hospital copies enter training with multiplicity. Repeat fold preprocessing and refit M0/M1. In each held-out original hospital copy, recompute q_h from its resampled size and rank copied people by score with deterministic tie-breaking on (original patientunitstayid, within-replicate copy index). Compute replicate DeltaSensitivity and proper-score differences using multiplicities. The percentile 2.5th and 97.5th percentiles among finite replicates form the 95% intervals. Require at least 95% finite, converged replicates and report the exact denominator.

Also compute 33 fixed-original-prediction leave-one-hospital-out deletions of DeltaSensitivity, and site-level changes in captured deaths at the site's fixed q_h. These are stability diagnostics, not independent trials.

Computability gates require exact source hashes and headers; exact 1,506/1,229/33 cohort; >=25 hospitals, >=800 people, >=200 deaths and survivors; every original M0/M1 fit converged with finite probabilities; no site >15% of people; >=95% finite bootstrap refits; and >=80% finite deletions. M1 calibration guards require slope 0.80-1.20 and absolute calibration-in-the-large <=0.05. If any gate fails, the result is **inconclusive/noncomputable** and no inferential operating-point label is permitted.

Classification is hierarchical:
- **Supportive:** all gates and calibration guards pass; the 95% lower bound for DeltaSensitivity is >=+0.02; and the 95% lower bound for DeltaBrier is >=0. This supports a material resource-neutral improvement for this model and population.
- **Adverse to the material-gain hypothesis:** gates pass and the upper bound for DeltaSensitivity is <+0.02. This rules out the prespecified gain for this procedure/population; it does not prove serial lactate useless.
- **Opposite:** gates pass and the upper bound for DeltaSensitivity is <0, or M1 fails calibration while M0 passes, or the upper bound for DeltaBrier is <0. This indicates worse operating-point/proper-score behavior, not patient harm.
- **Inconclusive:** gates pass but the interval includes +0.02, or sensitivity appears supportive while proper-score uncertainty crosses zero. Report the precise reason and do not promote secondary metrics.
- A positive point estimate with an interval crossing +0.02 is not supportive. An interval excluding zero but with upper bound below +0.02 is adverse to materiality, even though a smaller gain exists.
