# Does a laboratory-observation increment survive documentation adjustment and transport across surveillance regimes?

Status: Episode-30 substantive successor to `[prior hypothesis]`. This is an observational prognostic and measurement-process experiment. It is not a causal test-ordering study, a hospital-quality ranking, evidence that an alert improves survival, or evidence that more surveillance benefits patients.

## Unresolved question and substantive advance

At ICU-relative minute 360, does patient-level laboratory-observation history add transportable information about a later *recorded ICU death* after adjustment for recorded physiologic state, vital-observation intensity, and broad nursing/respiratory documentation intensity? More strongly, does that increment remain useful when the target hospital's outcome-free surveillance regime differs from the hospitals used to fit the model?

This is clinically consequential because a laboratory-process signal can be interpreted in two opposing ways. It may identify a patient whose evolving state is not represented by the available vital summaries, making it a candidate for retrospective review prioritization. Or it may mainly identify local testing and charting practice, delayed entry, interface conventions, or residual illness severity. The latter would make a laboratory-based review rule much less credible as a transportable monitoring strategy. Equal-review-burden comparisons make the question more decision-relevant than a small pooled discrimination difference, while remaining explicitly retrospective.

The strongest available evidence is outcome-blind feasibility and source-schema evidence, including the parent proposal's reported audit of eligible adult first stays, recorded deaths, hospitals, and revision-aware laboratory rows. No fitted increment, regime transport result, physiologic interpretation, clinician-intent interpretation, or clinical-benefit result is established. The expert eICU-02 seed raises dependence of prediction on testing practice and eICU-08 raises reduced-testing/deterioration concerns; both are untested hypotheses, not evidence. The research-ambition files were read for their documented methods and availability limits. They motivate a bounded sequence comparison but do not prescribe this topic or constitute a reproduction target; the cancer demonstration's main paper and complete STAR Methods remain unavailable and are not claimed as inspected.

The substantive advance over the parent is a prespecified, outcome-free test of transport across hospital surveillance regimes. A random hospital split can average over workflow environments. Here, each hospital receives a regime fingerprint derived only from [0,360]-minute input availability/timing, with no outcome, discharge field, post-landmark row, or hospital outcome statistic. Models are fit with whole hospitals held out, and a leave-one-regime-out analysis asks whether the laboratory increment survives transport into a surveillance environment not represented in training. Regime labels are stress-test strata, not patient predictors and not causal descriptions of hospital policy.

## Falsifiable hypothesis and actual deliverable

Primary hypothesis: among adults reaching minute 360, adding laboratory-observation process features to a transparent model containing admission context, laboratory/vital recorded state, vital-observation intensity, and aggregate nursing/respiratory documentation intensity improves held-out-hospital macro-hospital integrated competing-risk Brier score for recorded ICU death through minute 1,800. At the same prespecified number of reviews within each evaluation hospital, it captures more recorded ICU deaths or has higher recorded-death PPV than the documentation-adjusted baseline. This improvement will remain directionally consistent, with no material calibration harm, when training excludes the target hospital's outcome-free surveillance regime.

The solver must newly construct and freeze the cohort, outcome-blind lab codebook, documentation/process dictionary, six-bin matrices, outcome labels, hospital fingerprints/regime assignments, five random whole-hospital folds and leave-one-regime-out folds; fit the nested transparent models and same-input temporal alternative; produce held-out predictions and hospital/regime-clustered uncertainty; calculate competing-risk calibration/Brier/CIF and exact equal-burden metrics; run the locked process, site, revision, regime and temporal falsifications; and provide a conclusion trace mapping every claim to an output, uncertainty interval and support gate. No fitted result is claimed in this proposal.

The primary patient-level estimand is predictive, not causal: the paired held-out difference in integrated competing-risk Brier score over (360,1800] for BpD versus Bd, summarized first as the macro-hospital mean and then by surveillance regime. A supportive regime result requires that the laboratory increment not be confined to the random split average; it must remain favorable in a prespecified majority of supported target regimes and in the leave-one-regime-out analysis, with uncertainty reported rather than selected after inspecting results.

## Population, temporal boundaries and outcome

- require numeric `patientunitstayid` and `unitdischargeoffset > 360`.

The landmark is ICU-relative minute 360. The predictor window is inclusive `[0,360]` minutes. Lab information is available at the landmark only if numeric `labresult`, numeric `labresultoffset` in `[0,360]`, and `labresultrevisedoffset <= 360` or null. Vital, nursing and respiratory rows use their event/observation offsets in `[0,360]`; entry offsets are used only to construct pre-landmark entry-lag process features and are audited for invalid values. The first selected-row discharge event after the landmark and no later than minute 1,800 defines the outcome:

- event of interest: normalized `unitdischargestatus = Expired` and `unitdischargeoffset in (360,1800]`;
- competing event: normalized `unitdischargestatus = Alive` and `unitdischargeoffset in (360,1800]`;
- administrative censoring: neither event through minute 1,800.

Death after alive ICU discharge, hospital mortality, latent death, and post-ICU events are outside the estimand. Create 24 one-hour post-landmark bins covering `(360,1800]` and fit cause-specific discrete-time hazards, deriving cumulative incidence for recorded ICU death with alive ICU discharge competing. All status normalization, duplicate stays, negative or impossible offsets, missing outcomes and unexpected status values are audited and retained in a flow/invalid-row report; none is silently remapped.

## Outcome-blind features and surveillance regimes

Freeze all field maps, valid ranges, duplicate/revision handling, missing-value rules, scaling and imputation from development hospitals only. Candidate labs are the prespecified common panel (potassium, sodium, hemoglobin, hematocrit, glucose, creatinine, BUN, calcium, WBC, platelets, bicarbonate, FiO2, PaO2, PaCO2, pH, anion gap, HCO3, oxygen saturation, magnesium and base excess), retained only when observed name/system/interface support and numeric validity pass. `lab` has no separate unit column; `labresulttext` cannot establish a unit. Unsupported or incompatible analytes are excluded or reported in separate supported strata.

For each episode, construct:

- `Ls`: first, last, median, range and robust slope for supported lab analytes plus explicit availability masks;
- `Lp`: valid lab row count, distinct analytes, distinct result times, first/last result time, recency, span, inter-result-gap summaries, analyte-specific counts/recency and revision-known status;
- `Vs`: predeclared summaries and masks from the two vital tables;
- `Vd`: periodic/aperiodic row counts, distinct observation times, last time, span and six one-hour coverage;
- `D`: separately and jointly for nursing and respiratory tables, row count, distinct event times, first/last event time, span, six-bin coverage and audited entry-lag summaries. Do not use chart values, labels or category names in primary `D); those would mix documentation intensity with physiology or treatment.

The outcome-free hospital surveillance fingerprint is defined within each training fold. For every eligible episode, use only the six-hour input vectors (`Lp` count/timing, `Vd` timing/coverage, `D` count/timing/coverage) listed above. Apply development-frozen rank/robust scaling, then calculate six prespecified standardized components: lab-row/result-time intensity, vital-time intensity, nursing-row/time intensity, respiratory-row/time intensity, combined documentation coverage, and entry-lag burden. The hospital fingerprint is the component-wise median across that hospital's eligible episodes and an equal-weight mean composite after development-hospital standardization. Hospital scores are assigned low/middle/high regime using development-hospital tertile cut-points; a target hospital is assigned using its unlabeled [0,360] input rows and the training-fold transformations. No outcome, `unitdischargeoffset`, `unitdischargestatus`, post-landmark row, hospital mortality field or hospital descriptor enters this fingerprint. Require at least 20 eligible episodes per hospital; report the number of hospitals and episodes per regime.

This is a reproducible surveillance-environment stress test, not proof of a hospital policy. Because process intensity can also reflect case mix, report a sensitivity fingerprint using residualized process components after development-fitted adjustment for admission context A and recorded landmark state Vs/Ls; if residualization is unstable, label it unavailable rather than changing the primary regime definition. A stricter sensitivity uses the first 20 eligible `patientunitstayid`-ordered episodes for fingerprinting and evaluates the remainder.

## Matched transparent baseline and substantive alternative

Admission context A is `gender`, `ethnicity`, `admissionweight`, `hospitaladmitoffset`, `hospitaladmitsource` and `unitstaytype`. No hospital ID, ward ID, hospital descriptor, discharge field or future row enters a patient predictor. Fit pooled elastic-net cause-specific discrete-time logistic hazards with identical episodes, preprocessing, 24 labels, grouped folds and hyperparameter selection:

- Bv = A + Ls + Vs;
- BvV = Bv + Vd;
- Bd = BvV + D;
- BpV = BvV + Lp;
- BpD = Bd + Lp.

The primary increment is BpD minus Bd. BpV minus BvV is the intermediate increment before broad documentation adjustment. Coefficients are descriptive; claims use held-out predictions, calibration and hospital-clustered uncertainty.

The substantive learned alternative uses exactly the same eligible episodes, labels, folds, feature codebooks and channel contents. In six fixed bins `[0,60),...,[300,360)`, T0 receives A plus binwise Ls/Vs masks, Vd and D channels; T1 receives exactly T0 plus binwise Lp channels. Use one-layer GRUs with hidden size 32 and dropout 0.1, development-only early stopping, two competing-risk heads and no hospital ID. T1 versus T0 is the learned analogue of BpD versus Bd: it can reveal whether ordered persistence, co-movement and event timing contain information lost when the transparent baseline compresses the six-hour record. A parameter-count-matched non-temporal control with the same channels and order removed, plus six-bin order permutation, is mandatory before calling any gain temporal. This alternative is scientifically informative, not selected for a small predictive gain.

The transparent models are primary because the nested process contrast is auditable and CPU-suitable. The GRU is retained because temporal ordering can distinguish a late testing burst or documentation lag from the same total count, a distinction the baseline loses. A point-process/state-space model is deferred: its latent missingness assumptions would add an unverified mechanism before the immediate patient-linked-versus-workflow question is identified. Value-rich charting is deferred because values/labels may be physiology or treatment proxies and are not validated review events. Revisit either branch if a validated order/review field, expert analyte/unit mapping, or sufficient support becomes available.

## Whole-hospital evaluation, uncertainty and equal review burden

Use five grouped outer folds, each holding out approximately 20% of hospitals once; keep all stays with the same `uniquepid` together and never split a hospital across training and evaluation. Freeze transformations, codebooks, regime cut-points, model tuning and policy definitions in development hospitals. The primary output is the per-hospital paired BpD-Bd integrated Brier difference, macro-hospital mean and 95% hospital-clustered bootstrap or hierarchical interval (at least 1,000 resamples if feasible). Report time-specific Brier, recorded-death CIF at 1,800, calibration, fold direction, every hospital's estimate and pooled values only as secondary summaries.

The transport output is identical metrics in each supported target regime and leave-one-regime-out: fit on all development hospitals excluding one outcome-free regime, predict every eligible hospital in that regime, and compare with a model trained on the corresponding random-fold development set. A regime is supported only with at least 10 hospitals and 200 eligible evaluation episodes, at least 20 episodes per hospital, and at least 20 recorded deaths across the supported target cells; otherwise report it as exploratory/inconclusive. If fewer than three regimes pass, the regime analysis cannot support a three-regime transport claim.

For each held-out hospital h and q in `{0.01, 0.02, 0.05, 0.10}`, define Bd's landmark recorded-death cumulative-incidence policy as risk at least q. Let `k(h,q)` be its alert count. Rank the same episodes by BpD, BpV, T1 and controls and choose exactly the top k with deterministic ties; no outcome is used. If k=0, no PPV/capture assignment exists. Report review fraction, recorded-death capture, recorded-death PPV, calibration among reviewed/non-reviewed episodes and incremental membership. Report macro-hospital paired differences overall and by target regime. This is a label-relative retrospective review comparison, not net clinical benefit.

Implementation-oriented interpretation additionally requires at least 20 evaluation hospitals, at least 10 alerts per evaluation hospital on average, at least 100 alerted episodes and at least 20 recorded deaths in the reported q cell. Unsupported q cells remain in the audit but cannot be used to claim review-burden value.

## Support gates and falsification criteria

Before fitting, emit a frozen data dictionary, cohort flow, invalid-row audit, outcome-blind codebook, feature-support manifest, fold/regime manifest and timestamp/leakage audit. Primary interpretation requires at least 1,000 recorded deaths; 50 hospitals with at least 20 eligible episodes; all primary models converged with finite predictions; every primary field meeting development-frozen overall and per-hospital support rules; nontrivial Lp overlap across Bd-risk, vital-coverage and D-coverage strata; and all joins, first-stay, revision-known, outcome and hospital-leakage audits passing. A failed gate is a support result, not a null scientific result.

Lock these falsifications before reading held-out outcomes:

1. Permute Lp within held-out hospital, stratified by Bd-risk quintile, vital-coverage and D-coverage, holding Ls/Vs/Vd/D fixed. A patient-linked laboratory increment should attenuate.
2. Compare BpV-BvV with BpD-Bd. Disappearance only after D adjustment supports a broader documentation-workflow explanation, not absence of physiology.
3. Fit documentation-only, vital-intensity-only and hospital-descriptor-only diagnostics. Reproduction by descriptors, one site/interface or one analyte family weakens general transport.
4. Repeat with common analytes and revision-known-only rows. Loss under defensible unit/interface/revision rules prevents a robust analyte interpretation.
5. Repeat using outcome-free within-hospital percentile normalization of Lp and D, requiring adequate calibration candidates. Attenuation indicates dependence on local workflow scale.
6. Compare random hospital holdout with leave-one-regime-out. A random-fold gain that reverses in a supported regime is a transport failure, not a generalizable increment.
7. Permute regime labels across hospitals and rerun the leave-regime-out assignment. If the apparent regime contrast survives arbitrary labels, the regime conclusion is not credible.
8. For T0/T1, permute six-bin order and compare with the parameter-count-matched non-temporal control. A temporal claim requires order-sensitive, matched-control-supported improvement.
9. Recompute all policy results with identical per-hospital `k(h,q)`, report leave-one-hospital-out influence and fold direction. A pooled gain driven by one hospital is not transport evidence.
10. Shift or permute entry-lag features within documentation strata while retaining counts. A result driven by arbitrary entry metadata is workflow-sensitive.

## Interpretation and evidence limits

Supportive computation requires passed gates, favorable macro-hospital BpD-Bd Brier contrast with uncertainty excluding no improvement, consistent direction in a prespecified majority of folds and supported regimes, no material calibration harm, and an equal-burden capture or PPV improvement. The increment should attenuate under Lp permutation, not be reproduced by site/documentation-only diagnostics, and remain directionally credible in leave-one-regime-out transport. T1 supports a temporal extension only after the order and matched-control tests. The conclusion remains limited to retrospective prediction of recorded ICU death and a recorded-death review proxy.

Adverse evidence includes a null/reversed increment, calibration harm, no equal-burden improvement, persistence under process permutation, descriptor/site reproduction, concentration in one regime or analyte/interface, or random-fold success with leave-regime-out failure. These results favor workflow, missing physiology, case-mix or interface explanations and argue against treating laboratory history as a transportable patient-linked signal.

Inconclusive evidence includes failed overlap/regime support, sparse policy cells, unstable folds, nonconvergence, leakage or finite-prediction failure, wide intervals spanning clinically important favorable and adverse values, unstable regime assignment, or unsupported T1. Inconclusive is neither confirmation nor refutation.

The snapshot has no validated lab order time, clinician reason for ordering, human review/acknowledgement, treatment response, independent physiologic adjudication, bedside waveform, prospective alert outcome, patient-centered harm/benefit endpoint or reliable causal intervention assignment. Public narrative sections are removed; retained structured fields do not establish intent or meaning. Chart entry/event offsets are documentation timestamps, not clinician-action timestamps. `vitalPeriodic` is a summary, and `lab` has no separate unit column. Therefore even supportive results cannot establish causal benefit, clinician actionability, physiologic truth, hospital quality, or superiority of a monitoring program. Those claims require expert analyte/unit and clinical adjudication, external validation, prospective silent-alert testing, and an impact or stepped-wedge study measuring responses, harms, review burden and patient-centered outcomes.
