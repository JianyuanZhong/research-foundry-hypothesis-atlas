## Unresolved question, strongest support, and clinical advance

When an adult leaves the ICU, does the ordered trajectory of physiologic recovery during the preceding 24 hours predict a failed transition beyond the most recent state and the fact that clinicians measured the patient? This matters because post-ICU ward surveillance and ICU-bed review must distinguish durable stability from persistent or oscillating respiratory/hemodynamic abnormality.

The strongest available support is plausibility, not an established result: residual oxygen need and fluctuating vital signs are recognizable warning signs, and this MIMIC snapshot contains repeated dated structured observations. The seed and inspected references do not establish an effect size, plannedness of ICU returns, or benefit from changing discharge policy. The falsifiable hypothesis is:

Among adults discharged alive from an index ICU stay, a landmark-safe, ordered and irregular final-day sequence of vital signs and respiratory support will improve locked patient-held-out 48-hour cause-specific risk estimation for first subsequent ICU admission, while preserving death calibration, compared with a prespecified last-state-plus-observation-process baseline.

A full-sequence gain over both the baseline and a process-only null, followed by loss under value/order permutation with masks preserved, would support incremental prognostic information in measured physiologic trajectories. It would not establish causality, unplanned return, or benefit from delaying discharge or adding monitoring. The advance over a last-value score is a same-cohort separation of current state, persistence/recovery/order, and selective charting.

## Baseline, alternative and analysis

Fit an interpretable regularized pooled-logistic discrete-time cause-specific hazard model. For each outcome bin include interval indicators, baseline covariates, last valid values, support, recency, counts and missingness, with only prespecified SpO2-by-FiO2 and support-by-device interactions. Convert hazards to cause-specific cumulative incidence functions.

Fit a process-only null with the same non-measurement covariates and masks, counts, gaps and timing, but no physiologic values. Fit a one-layer 64-unit GRU-D-style encoder with dropout 0.1 over the four ordered bins, using physiologic values, support, mask, count and delta-time channels plus the same baseline covariates, with two cause-specific hazard heads and competing-risk negative log likelihood. Early-stop on validation loss only. The GRU-D alternative is substantive because it can represent persistence, recovery, oscillation and nonlinear interactions that last-state summaries lose; its scientific value is tested only if it beats both the baseline and process-only null.

Use deterministic patient-level 70/15/15 subject splits with five fixed hash seeds. All transformations, device mapping, imputation and standardization are training-only. Keep split manifests by internal IDs. Primary test comparison is paired 48-hour cause-specific Brier score and CIF calibration for ICU admission and death; a prespecified interpretation benchmark is at least 0.005 absolute ICU-admission Brier reduction without material death-calibration degradation, not proof of utility. Secondary outputs are seven-day CIF calibration, integrated Brier, AUROC/AUPRC, calibration intercept/slope and decision-curve net benefit at thresholds frozen before test evaluation. Use 2,000 subject-level paired bootstrap replicates and report event counts, intervals and all seed results.

## Falsification and interpretation

Required ablations are last-bin-only, order permutation, value permutation preserving masks/counts, removal of mask/delta channels, and the post-landmark destination audit. Label permutation within outcome-rate strata must eliminate apparent discrimination. A pre-outtime timestamp audit must pass.

Supportive results require reproducible held-out ICU-admission improvement over the baseline and process-only null, acceptable death calibration, and performance loss under value/order permutation. They support prognostic information in recorded final-day trajectories and justify prospective monitoring-risk validation.

Adverse results are no incremental improvement, calibration degradation, instability across subject splits, no loss under value/order permutation, or label-permutation performance above chance. These refute the specified incremental ordered-trajectory claim in this snapshot, not the broader possibility that residual instability exists.

Inconclusive results include too few events, broad intervals crossing no effect, high missingness, unresolved death timing, poor transition linkage, convergence failure or collapse in a robustness split. These mean this dataset cannot resolve the claim.

Automatically checkable claims are cohort flow, joins, timestamp exclusion, one-row-per-index-stay construction, first-event ordering, model fitting, locked metrics, calibration, uncertainty, and ablations. Clinical adjudication is still required for planned versus unplanned ICU return, whether procedure intervals reflect actual support, and whether treatment limitations or clinician intent affected discharge. The snapshot lacks reliable goals-of-care, functional-readiness, bedside-assessment, raw-waveform and MIMIC-CXR image evidence. External/prospective data and clinical review are required before deployment or causal interpretation.
