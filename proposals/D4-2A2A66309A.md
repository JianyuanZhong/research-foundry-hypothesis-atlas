# Baseline creatinine–cystatin-C discordance for fixed-horizon treated kidney failure: a coverage-gated experiment

## Lineage and substantive change

This is a substantive Evolution child of assessed-valid parent `[prior hypothesis]`. The parent executed a continuous discordance analysis, but its population was selected by future repeat attendance, its comparator used the future-known elapsed repeat interval, repeat cystatin-C completeness was required, and its outcome was a repeat creatinine-eGFR threshold. This child removes all four defects at the design level. It defines a baseline-landmark prediction experiment in which every predictor is available at the baseline assessment and the outcome is ascertained after baseline without conditioning on a later visit.

The child is deliberately **coverage-gated**. The local rectangular snapshot contains candidate hospital diagnosis, procedure, and death arrays, but its metadata does not certify their labels, coding dictionaries, code/date positional relationships, extraction date, or nation-specific linked-record end dates. Consequently, this proposal specifies an executable experiment and an explicit activation gate; it does not claim that the fixed-horizon endpoint is currently certified or that the experiment has been executed.

## Clinical question and claims

The consequential question is whether a single baseline continuous measure of creatinine–cystatin-C discordance improves prediction of a subsequently treated, severe kidney outcome over a fixed horizon among adults without advanced baseline creatinine-defined kidney dysfunction, compared with a strong creatinine-only baseline model. A positive result could motivate independent validation of whether discordance identifies people needing earlier kidney-risk review; it would not establish benefit from ordering cystatin-C, causality, clinical utility, or deployment readiness.

Evidence already supported by the parent is narrower: in a selected first-repeat-attender cohort, `Z = log(eGFRcys_0/eGFRcr_0)` had reproducible held-out log-loss improvement beyond its repeat-attendance comparator, while its fixed-capacity concentration result was inconclusive. That same-snapshot result is internal evidence only and is not evidence for this new endpoint or for baseline deployment.

The new, untested hypothesis is: among baseline-eligible participants with complete baseline predictors and no prevalent certified treated kidney failure, adding exactly one frozen continuous feature, `Z = log(eGFRcys_0/eGFRcr_0)`, to a creatinine-only baseline model improves fixed-horizon risk prediction and clinically relevant event concentration without degrading calibration. The hypothesis is falsified if the extension fails the prespecified information and capacity gates, materially worsens calibration, or shows instability across prespecified resamples or geographic linkage strata after the endpoint gate passes.

## Population and landmark

The landmark is the baseline assessment date in `53-0.0`. Include participants with positive finite creatinine and cystatin-C values, valid baseline age and sex, a nonmissing baseline date, and race-free 2021 CKD-EPI creatinine eGFR `eGFRcr_0 >= 60 mL/min/1.73m2`, retaining the parent's target range until the Lead approves a different clinical population. Do not require any repeat assessment, repeat biomarker, online follow-up, or future elapsed interval. Exclude participants with a certified prevalent endpoint on or before the landmark, including same-day records. If prior-event ascertainment is incomplete, the experiment remains inactive rather than silently treating missing history as absence.

Compute race-free 2021 CKD-EPI creatinine eGFR from creatinine in µmol/L as `Scr=creatinine/88.4`, `k=0.9` and `alpha=-0.302` for sex code 1, `k=0.7` and `alpha=-0.241` for sex code 0, then `eGFRcr=142*min(Scr/k,1)^alpha*max(Scr/k,1)^(-1.200)*0.9938^age*(1.0 if sex=1 else 1.012)`. Compute 2012 cystatin-C-only eGFR as `eGFRcys=133*min(Scys/0.8,1)^(-0.499)*max(Scys/0.8,1)^(-1.328)*0.996^age*(1.0 if sex=1 else 0.932)`, provisionally interpreting cystatin C as mg/L; assay units must be certified before activation. The predictors are baseline age, sex, baseline eGFRcr, and their frozen flexible baseline terms in the comparator; the extension is exactly one training-standardized continuous variable `Z = log(eGFRcys_0/eGFRcr_0)`. No hospital, death, repeat-attendance, post-baseline, or future-known variable may enter either model. Cystatin-C is used for `Z` only, not as an additional standalone predictor or a second extension.

## Endpoint, time, and censoring

The preferred primary endpoint is a certified treated-kidney-failure/transplant composite, defined as the first post-landmark date of any of:

1. a clinically certified dialysis-dependence diagnosis (candidate ICD-10 `Z99.2`);
2. a clinically certified kidney-transplant-status diagnosis (candidate ICD-10 `Z94.0`); or
3. a clinically certified kidney-transplant procedure (candidate OPCS-4 `M01` family).

These code candidates are deliberately provisional. `N18.5/N18.6`, `Z49.1/Z49.2`, and `X40/X41/X42` must be retained as separate exploratory groups until clinical adjudication establishes whether they represent advanced CKD, acute or maintenance dialysis, or another procedure context. Broad incident CKD is not the primary endpoint: hospital and death linkage alone cannot certify the onset or chronicity of CKD, which may be diagnosed in primary care or laboratory trajectories absent from this snapshot. Kidney-failure death may be a secondary endpoint only if cause-of-death fields and their coding dictionary are certified.

Define a fixed horizon `H` only after a release/coverage manifest supplies an administratively valid common end date for the relevant hospital, procedure, and death sources in every included nation. Test the ordered candidate horizons 10, 8, then 5 years and activate the first horizon for which all endpoint sources and included participants have certified coverage; this choice uses coverage metadata only, before event counting or modeling. If none passes, the experiment remains inactive. Ten years after `53-0.0` is the scientific target, but it is not activated merely because observed dates extend that far. If the certified common end date is `C`, a participant is horizon-complete only when `baseline + H <= C` under the applicable source coverage rules. Do not infer censoring from the maximum observed event date.

Follow each participant from the landmark. A qualifying composite event on a date strictly after baseline and no later than baseline plus `H` is the primary event. Any qualifying code on or before baseline, including the same day, is prevalent and excluded. If death occurs before a qualifying kidney event, classify it as a competing nonrenal death, not as an ordinary non-event. If kidney event and death share a date, apply a frozen hierarchy after clinical review; absent such adjudication, report the tie as unresolved and do not activate the primary analysis. Participants alive and event-free at the certified horizon are event-free. Participants whose source coverage ends before the horizon are administratively right-censored and must not be labelled event-free.

The primary estimand is the absolute difference between models in predicted cumulative incidence of the treated-kidney-failure composite by `H`, with nonrenal death treated as a competing state. Because activation requires certified complete follow-up through `H`, each participant is assigned exactly one three-state outcome: kidney failure first, nonrenal death first, or alive/event-free at `H`. Kidney failure followed by later death remains a kidney-failure outcome; death strictly before kidney failure is the competing-death outcome. The primary model directly estimates all three fixed-horizon probabilities. Cause-specific and Fine–Gray survival analyses are sensitivity analyses and cannot replace this primary fixed-horizon estimand. A binary analysis that treats competing deaths or incomplete follow-up as event-free is prohibited. Country-specific linkage availability and administrative censoring must be reported in aggregate; if source coverage cannot be tied to participants or a conservative common complete horizon, the study remains gated.

4. A certified method for identifying all prevalent endpoint records before or on `53-0.0`, including records in every available array position and all relevant record sources.
5. A frozen rule for same-day diagnosis/procedure/death ties and a verified event-date precision.
6. A participant-level or otherwise valid aggregate linkage/coverage indicator sufficient to define who has complete follow-up through `H`; if unavailable, use only a conservative common horizon certified for the entire analytic population.
7. Aggregate-only feasibility thresholds: enough at-risk participants and endpoint events for two prespecified models, competing-risk estimation, calibration, uncertainty intervals, and the capacity analysis. The threshold must be set before inspecting model results; event counts alone cannot certify semantic validity.
8. Privacy review confirming that no participant rows, identifiers, event dates, individual predictions, fold membership, or membership flags leave the workspace.

If any gate fails, publish a gated feasibility result only. The study then supports the claim that the local snapshot contains physically present candidate fields and an explicitly specified next experiment, not that it measures treated kidney failure or supports deployment.

## Models and analysis after activation

The comparator `M0` is multinomial logistic regression for the three fixed-horizon states, with an unpenalized intercept, sex, and separate natural-cubic-spline bases for baseline eGFRcr and age. For each continuous predictor, use three interior knots at its training-fold 25th, 50th, and 75th percentiles, training-fold minimum/maximum boundary knots, a centered constraint, and training-only column standardization; degenerate knots are an execution failure. Fit L2-penalized multinomial logistic regression with `C=1`, no class weighting, tolerance `1e-10`, maximum 2,000 iterations, and a solver that leaves the intercept unpenalized. `MZ` is exactly `M0 +` one training-standardized linear `Z`; no other variable, interaction, tuning branch, or biomarker is permitted. Use ten frozen seeds, each defining an outcome-stratified five-fold split, yielding paired held-out three-state probabilities from 100 fits; every preprocessing operation is trained inside each fold. Report convergence, finite prediction coverage, class counts by fold, and the frozen seeds. Failure to place every state in every training fold or to obtain converged finite probabilities is a computational gate failure.

Primary predictive comparison uses paired held-out multiclass log loss, defined as the mean negative log probability assigned to the observed three-state outcome, and the kidney-event Brier score at `H`, `(I[state=kidney]-p_kidney)^2`. `Delta_logloss = logloss(MZ)-logloss(M0)` and `Delta_Brier = Brier(MZ)-Brier(M0)`, so negative values favor `MZ`; log loss is the gating information metric and Brier score is a concordant secondary metric. Obtain two-sided 95% percentile intervals from 4,000 participant-clustered bootstrap resamples with a frozen seed, carrying each participant's ten paired held-out probability vectors together; this conditional analysis does not include refitting/split uncertainty and must be labelled accordingly. Report kidney-event calibration-in-the-large, calibration slope, and observed versus predicted cumulative incidence at `H`, plus competing-death calibration. Discrimination is descriptive and cannot override calibration or clinical-capacity gates.

At a single frozen capacity of 10% of eligible baseline participants, rank by held-out predicted kidney-event probability separately within each fold; use stable ties by row order and floor-plus-largest-remainder fold quotas so each model selects exactly `round(0.10*N)` positions per repeat. Compare the number of primary kidney events captured by `MZ` and `M0`, averaging the paired difference across ten repeats; report absolute event capture, selected kidney-event risk, and competing-death composition. Before execution, a nephrology/decision review must specify a nonzero minimum worthwhile increase `K` in captured kidney events per `round(0.10*N)` positions and justify it from follow-up burden, consequences, and horizon incidence. If no such clinical threshold is approved, execute descriptive capacity analysis but supportive classification is unavailable. This is a capacity comparison, not a claim of clinical utility, and no threshold is inherited from the parent's repeat-attendance outcome.

## Decision and falsification criteria

Support requires all of: (a) the 95% interval for `Delta_logloss` lies below zero and `MZ` has lower log loss in at least 8 of 10 repeats; (b) the 95% interval for the mean 10%-capacity event-count difference lies above zero, its point estimate is at least the preapproved `K`, and the difference is positive in at least 8 of 10 repeats; (c) the 95% interval for `Delta_Brier` does not indicate material worsening and no clinically approved calibration-degradation margin is crossed for kidney-event calibration-in-the-large or slope; (d) direction is retained in every sufficiently powered linkage nation and leave-one-nation-out analysis; and (e) all activation, computation, and privacy checks pass. If calibration margins or `K` are not clinically approved before model outputs are inspected, supportive classification is unavailable. A supportive result remains same-snapshot internal validation and requires external/prospective validation before any deployment claim.

Adverse classification requires endpoint certification and all computational checks, and is triggered if the 95% interval for `Delta_logloss` lies above zero, a clinically material calibration-degradation margin is crossed, or the 95% interval for the capacity difference lies below zero with harmful direction in at least 8 of 10 repeats. Inconclusive classification applies after activation when information and capacity disagree, intervals include both meaningful benefit and harm, `K` or calibration margins were not approved, event counts are inadequate, or heterogeneity prevents a stable interpretation. If endpoint or horizon certification fails, the correct conclusion is **inactive/gated**, not adverse or inconclusive biological evidence.

Falsification checks include: permuting `Z` within each training fold and refitting through the identical pipeline; verifying by an explicit feature-name allowlist that no outcome code/date, later instance, coverage end, or elapsed follow-up variable enters predictors; rerunning with each candidate code subgroup separated; excluding ambiguous acute-dialysis procedures; leave-one-nation-out analyses; the next shorter certified horizon; and a prespecified nonrenal negative-control hospitalization endpoint selected and clinically justified before model fitting. A materially positive result that disappears under code adjudication, conservative coverage restriction, or leave-one-nation-out analysis, or an apparent increment of similar magnitude for permuted `Z` or the negative control, falsifies specificity of the claimed discordance increment. Alternative censoring weights are permitted only if an activated design retains administratively censored participants rather than the primary common-complete-horizon population.

## Evidence boundaries and clinical advance

The substantive advance is not a claim that cystatin-C is clinically useful. It is a repair of the parent’s central design flaw: the proposed predictor is available at baseline, the horizon is fixed before modeling, follow-up does not depend on repeat attendance, and the endpoint/censoring semantics are made explicit. It also redirects the question from a repeat laboratory threshold to a consequential outcome that could inform kidney-risk review if independently certified.

Current local evidence is insufficient to activate the endpoint. The source physically contains the named field families, but the local metadata explicitly lacks field labels, coding dictionaries, code/date declarations, release cutoffs, and nation-specific coverage certification. Published UK Biobank research demonstrates that ESKD outcomes have been constructed in other analyses, but those papers do not certify that this rectangular snapshot contains the same code algorithm, linkage cutoff, or field semantics. The frozen Europe PMC sources `[source checksum]` and `[source checksum]` are contextual references, not substitutes for local endpoint adjudication; the 2026 paper's main text and supplementary availability also do not establish this snapshot's coverage.
