# Nighttime ICU discharge and residual instability

Status: Episode 28 successor to `[prior hypothesis]` and `[prior hypothesis]`. This is an observational discharge-timing and risk-stratification experiment. It is not a causal test of delaying discharge, a hospital-quality ranking, or evidence that changing discharge time improves survival. No model has been fitted.

## Unresolved question and clinical importance

The expert seed asks whether excess risk associated with nighttime ICU discharge is concentrated among patients with residual instability. The parents establish a feasible, revision-aware eICU framework for patient-linked laboratory/vital processes, but they do not address a discharge transition or the clinically different question of whether discharge timing interacts with pre-discharge stability.

The strongest available evidence here is an outcome-blind source/schema audit, not a clinical result. In the local eICU snapshot, an audit found 135,617 adult first-valid ICU stays lasting beyond 360 minutes across 208 hospitals. It found 128,190 ICU-alive discharges, of which 83,593 had a clock time in the prespecified night window below; 4,736 later had recorded hospital death. Respiratory rows and continuous-ish vital summaries are available, but these counts do not establish a nighttime effect or true physiologic stability.

The unresolved claim is:

> Among patients discharged alive from the ICU, is the association between nighttime discharge and subsequent recorded in-hospital death concentrated in those with a reproducible residual-instability phenotype immediately before discharge, after accounting for recorded illness state and hospital transport?

This matters because a positive, stable interaction could identify a subgroup in which discharge review or handoff safeguards deserve prospective testing. A null interaction would argue against treating nighttime discharge as a generic risk marker. Either result would be more useful than an unqualified “night is unsafe” association, while remaining honest about the unavailable staffing, bed-capacity, handoff, order, and clinical-adjudication evidence.

## Falsifiable hypothesis and estimand

Define night from `patient.unitdischargetime24` as 19:00--06:59 inclusive and day as 07:00--18:59. The primary exposure is night versus day among ICU-alive discharges with a parseable clock time. Clock time is not a random treatment; it is a recorded discharge-time exposure.

Define the primary residual-instability phenotype without using outcomes or discharge time: in the last 120 minutes before ICU discharge, require at least two valid observations from the prioritized pressure/heart-rate/oxygenation panel and flag instability if at least one of the following holds in both the 120--60 and 60--0 minute halves, or if the robust median over the interval is abnormal: selected MAP <65 mmHg, heart rate >110/min, SpO2 <92%, or respiration >24/min. MAP uses last-valid systemic mean from `vitalPeriodic` and otherwise noninvasive mean from `vitalAperiodic`; absent observations are unknown, never reassuring. Thresholds are analytic labels, not treatment targets. The primary analysis also retains a continuous standardized state score and separate missingness/source indicators rather than converting missingness to stability.

The primary outcome starts at ICU discharge and uses hospital-relative offsets. Among `unitdischargestatus == Alive` episodes with valid `hospitaldischargeoffset > unitdischargeoffset` and a parseable `hospitaldischargestatus`, estimate the 48-hour cumulative incidence of recorded hospital death (`hospitaldischargestatus == Expired`) with recorded alive hospital discharge as the competing event, administratively censored at 48 hours. A secondary estimand follows the same competing risks until recorded hospital discharge. Patients with missing or impossible disposition timing are audited and excluded from the primary estimand rather than silently recoded. Death after hospital discharge, latent death, and post-hospital outcomes are outside scope.

The prespecified interaction is the standardized difference in 48-hour death CIF for night versus day within stable and unstable strata, and the contrast of those two differences. The primary transport estimand is the macro-hospital mean held-out difference in calibrated death-CIF/Brier performance and the held-out standardized risk interaction; patient-weighted estimates and complete-hospitalization follow-up are secondary. A clinically meaningful interaction threshold must be frozen by the solver before test predictions, based on uncertainty and decision context; this discovery proposal does not invent a result or threshold.

## Comparable baseline and substantive alternatives

The transparent baseline `B_state` is pooled elastic-net cause-specific discrete-time logistic regression over 48 one-hour post-discharge bins. It uses admission context, the pre-discharge vital state/process and missingness/source indicators, a prespecified night indicator, and discharge-time administrative context; it has no night-by-instability product. Scaling, imputation, regularization and calibration are learned within development hospitals only. It produces death and alive-discharge cause-specific hazards and CIFs.

The primary substantive alternative `M_interaction` uses the identical rows, predictors, folds and outcome, adds only the prespecified residual-instability indicator, continuous state score, and night-by-instability interaction. This model can reveal effect modification that an additive baseline loses: whether timing is clinically relevant specifically when physiologic recovery remains incomplete. Report interaction coefficients, standardized CIF contrasts, calibration, and uncertainty—not merely a small discrimination gain. A parameter-matched additive model without the interaction is the required control.

A secondary learned alternative `T_recent` is a small two-layer GRU or temporal convolution over six 20-minute bins in the same 120-minute pre-discharge interval, with separate death/discharge hazard heads and the same discharge-time exposure and admission variables. It may reveal persistence, recovery trajectory, and nonlinear co-movement that the robust median/half-window interaction loses. It is retained as scientific evidence only if its held-out calibration/Brier improvement is uncertainty-compatible, its temporal order ablation attenuates the signal, and its attribution/trajectory pattern agrees with `M_interaction`. Fit a parameter-matched non-temporal MLP as control. Do not interpret the learned representation as latent physiology.

A mechanistic respiratory liberation model is deferred: although `respiratoryCare` and charting exist, support termination semantics, ventilator settings, oxygen delivery, and clinical readiness are incomplete; the bounded audit showed only 157 nonzero `ventendoffset` rows among the eligible respiratory rows and cannot support a primary liberation estimand. Revisit if a source-system audit establishes reliable support episodes and complete liberation timestamps.

## Splits, uncertainty, and evaluation

Use five outer folds holding out approximately 20% of hospitals; keep all stays for each `uniquepid` together. Within each development fold, use a 75/25 hospital-stratified fit/validation split. Freeze night window, instability thresholds, missingness rules, lab codebook, preprocessing, hyperparameters, calibration, and any alert threshold in the fit subset. Never use hospital ID as a predictor.

Primary uncertainty is at least 1,000 hospital-cluster bootstrap resamples of held-out predictions, or a prespecified hierarchical hospital model if bootstrap is unstable. Report macro-hospital and patient-weighted results, hospital/episode/death/discharge/censor counts, and stable/unstable/unknown counts per fold. Primary metrics are held-out 48-hour death-CIF calibration, integrated competing-risk Brier score, and standardized night-minus-day risk differences within instability strata; discrimination and full-hospitalization analyses are secondary. Report the parallel alive-discharge CIF and complete-case/unknown-state strata.

Require support gates before interpreting the interaction: at least 100 eligible hospitals, 50 hospitals with post-ICU death events, 1,000 total post-ICU death events or, if the 48-hour endpoint is sparser, an explicitly predeclared lower event threshold with wider uncertainty; at least 1,000 competing alive hospital discharges; at least 80% of held-out hospitals with both day/night overlap and stable/unstable overlap; and non-sparse instability strata. If a cell has fewer than 20 hospitals, 200 episodes, 20 deaths or 20 competing discharges, mark its contrast unsupported. A failed gate makes the clinical interaction claim inconclusive, not a reason to relax the population or endpoint.

## Falsification and interpretation

Save all predeclared outputs:

1. Within-hospital, baseline-state-stratified permutation of night/day labels, preserving hospital and discharge-state distributions but breaking any timing association.
2. A negative-control timing outcome: alive ICU discharges followed by a routine recorded alive hospital discharge within 48 hours, analyzed with the same state adjustment; a similar night interaction would suggest disposition/workflow rather than death-specific risk.
3. Compare night-by-instability with an additive instability model, a night-only model, stable-only and unstable-only fits, and a matched discharge-process interaction such as night-by-documentation density.
4. Use the death CIF versus alive-discharge CIF contrast; an equal or larger discharge increment is adverse to a death-specific interpretation.
5. Mask the last 20 minutes and repeat with 60-, 120-, and 240-minute pre-discharge windows; a signal appearing only at the immediate boundary is a likely documentation/decision artifact.
6. Repeat with vital-source-specific pressure, strict two-half persistence, continuous-score and complete-case definitions.
7. Restrict/challenge by `carePlanEOL` documentation, treatment context, respiratory support, admission source, and hospital descriptors; sparse documentation is unknown.
8. Repeat on the biochemical secondary branch with revision-safe rows and common analytes; any gain requiring post-discharge or post-outcome rows is leakage.
9. For `T_recent`, perform temporal-order shuffle, parameter-matched non-temporal control, and input-label/domain shuffles.
10. Audit impossible offsets, duplicate first stays, clock parsing, ICU/hospital discharge ordering, missing statuses and all exclusions before model fitting.

Supportive evidence requires support gates, a reproducible positive night-by-instability interaction with uncertainty compatible with a prespecified meaningful death-CIF difference, no material calibration harm, attenuation under label permutation and boundary masking, smaller/null alive-discharge increment, and stability across hospitals and state definitions. This supports only a transportable recorded-risk stratification signal.

Adverse evidence is a null/adverse interaction, unchanged result after night-label permutation, a similar or larger alive-discharge effect, dependence on one hospital/interface, concentration only in EOL/treatment/documentation strata, or a gain that vanishes under source/missingness checks. It rejects the proposed death-specific residual-instability interpretation even if night discharge remains associated with outcomes.

Inconclusive evidence includes failed event/overlap gates, uncertainty spanning zero and the frozen meaningful effect, excessive unknown state, unstable folds, unreliable discharge clock/status, or a learned model gain without temporal/falsification-consistent pattern. These outcomes do not establish safety or harm.

## Evidence limits and dispositions

eICU has recorded discharge clocks and statuses, not bed-capacity, staffing, handoff quality, orders, collection times, complete goals-of-care documentation, true physiology, or complete mortality. Public narrative sections are removed; structured fields cannot adjudicate residual instability, clinician intent, or discharge readiness. Hospital death after ICU discharge is a recorded outcome and not proof that nighttime discharge caused harm. Clinical adjudication, source-system timestamps, external health-system validation, and a prospective silent-mode/impact or randomized study are required for causal safety or benefit claims.

Demonstration dispositions: the inspected Delphi/natural-history materials support considering a bounded temporal alternative, but eICU’s short ICU-relative horizon and missing lifetime disease trajectory do not support reproduction; the inspected ALADYNOULLI materials require genetic inputs unavailable here, and its latent EHR-only adaptation is deferred for this discharge estimand; the Oncoformer supplement shows multimodal learning but the main article/full STAR Methods remain unavailable and local images are absent, so no reproduction is claimed and a lab-only adaptation is not selected because it overlaps the parent process line.
