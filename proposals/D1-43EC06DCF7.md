# Provisional hypothesis: reduced laboratory monitoring during persistent instability

## Status

This is a provisional, data-grounded adaptation of expert seed `[starting question]` (`[prior hypothesis]`). It is not a completed study and no effect has been estimated.

## Proposed relationship

Among adult eICU stays that remain hemodynamically or physiologically unstable, an abrupt reduction in the rate of laboratory testing over a prespecified 6-hour window is associated with a higher probability of deterioration in the following 6 hours, compared with continued testing at a similar rate, after adjustment for the immediately preceding clinical state and hospital.

The primary estimand is a descriptive/adjusted association, not a causal effect of withholding tests. The unresolved claim is whether the association reflects information loss during an unstable period rather than current severity, discharge/transfer, end-of-life care, or observation practice.

## Proposed experiment

Use one index window per stay. Restrict to adult stays with a valid `unitdischargeoffset`, at least one prior 6-hour laboratory window, and at least one subsequent 6-hour observation before discharge/transfer. Exclude windows after an EOL plan and windows immediately before discharge/transfer in a prespecified sensitivity analysis.

Exposure: count distinct laboratory tests and, secondarily, distinct `labname` values in the preceding 6 hours, and compare with the prior 6 hours. Define an abrupt reduction by a prespecified contrast such as a decrease of at least 50% or at least two tests, chosen after one descriptive audit and frozen before outcome modeling. A zero count is missing observation, not proof that testing stopped.

Outcome: a binary deterioration event in the next 6 hours, defined prospectively from available vitals and laboratory values, such as new/worsening hypotension, tachycardia, hypoxemia, or a prespecified abnormal laboratory trajectory. Thresholds must be fixed after the audit and reported. Secondary outcomes are ICU/hospital mortality from `apachePatientResult.csv.gz` and discharge status from `patient.csv.gz`.

Analysis: compare abrupt reduction with continued testing using an adjusted logistic baseline of prior physiology, laboratory values, time since admission, hospital, and available severity measures. Use a hierarchical or hospital-held-out analysis and report uncertainty intervals and calibration.

Falsification:
- Supportive: adjusted association persists across hospitals and after excluding EOL/discharge windows, with uncertainty excluding a clinically negligible association.
- Adverse: association disappears after adjustment for current state, is confined to EOL/discharge windows, or reverses across hospitals.
- Inconclusive: sparse testing, wide intervals, or unstable outcome definitions make the result non-informative.

## Missing evidence and limitations

The data do not establish whether a test was unavailable, ordered but not recorded, affected by interface downtime, or intentionally reduced. There is no direct bedside decision, staffing, bed-capacity, or treatment-limitation adjudication. Mortality and discharge fields do not validate deterioration. Exact thresholds, row-level missingness, and event rates remain to be audited.

## Method alternatives

The simple baseline is a prespecified adjusted logistic model using counts and prior physiology. A substantive alternative is a time-aware survival/sequence model using ordered laboratory and vital observations, retaining timing, test identity, and evolving state. It is useful only if it improves discrimination/calibration for the same estimand and reveals trajectory-specific signal; a small predictive gain alone is not an advance. CPU is appropriate for the baseline and likely sufficient for a bounded sequence model.

## Deliverable

The future solver must construct the frozen cohort, estimate the adjusted association and uncertainty, produce hospital-held-out performance/calibration outputs, and report supportive, adverse, or inconclusive results. It must not report a causal treatment recommendation from this observational design.
