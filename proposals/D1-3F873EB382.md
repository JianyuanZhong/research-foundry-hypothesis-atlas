## What is known and what remains unresolved

The strongest evidence available for this proposal supports a plausible but unproven mechanism: fluid overload and venous congestion may contribute to kidney dysfunction, and diuretics are one possible decongestive intervention. The inspected open-access 2026 narrative review by Orieux et al. (DOI 10.1016/j.aicoj.2026.100083; frozen source [source checksum]) explicitly characterizes renal congestion as hypothesis-generating, states that interventional evidence is lacking, and notes that the clinical utility of targeting congestion remains unestablished. That supports equipoise and the importance of testing the question; it does not support a benefit, a safety claim, or a treatment recommendation.

The unresolved claim is narrower and computable: in a precisely defined post-shock, fluid-positive, ventilated recorded phenotype, is an early recorded loop-input policy associated with a clinically consequential day-7 recorded outcome and acceptable recorded harms? The substantive advance is an observation-aware, precedence-fixed target-trial emulation that separates a decision-time recorded-input estimand from the unavailable truth of medication receipt, and tests benefit and harm jointly with explicit inconclusive branches.

The three demonstration files in references/research-ambition/README.md are treated as demonstrations of ambition and rigor, not as evidence for this clinical claim. ## Falsification and branch rules

Automation must invalidate directional conclusions if participant split, source hashes, archive members, joins, earliest-broad selection, no-reopening, V0 uniqueness, C0 timing, equal cloning, as-of covariate availability, bootstrap rebuild, or conclusion-to-output linkage fails.

Use negative-control timing checks: a pre-t0 loop-input pseudo-window that cannot be caused by the assigned A/B policy; a post-C0 recorded-input window, reported descriptively and never adjusted for; and a non-loop input class with the same source and interval structure. These are falsification diagnostics, not proof of no confounding.

Supportive branch: the H120 identification interval and uncertainty support benefit, every prespecified harm remains below its margin with FWER control, overlap/balance and administration-concordance gates pass, negative controls are null within prespecified tolerances, and early/late estimates do not materially reverse. This supports only an association/compatibility result for the recorded-input policy in the MIMIC discovery population; it does not establish receipt, causal benefit, renal safety, or a clinical recommendation.

Adverse branch: the benefit is absent or unfavorable, or any harm exceeds its margin with the required uncertainty control. This argues against treating the recorded policy as promising in this dataset and motivates a better-measured or randomized study; it does not prove diuretics are harmful in all patients.

Inconclusive branch: bounds cross the benefit/harm margins; concordance or endpoint observation is inadequate; positivity fails; the primary and administration-aware analyses materially disagree; the negative controls fail; or epoch/era estimates reverse. Report unresolved uncertainty and do not select a treatment direction. A computationally infeasible or population-invalid run is a design failure, not an adverse clinical finding.

## Evidence limits

MIMIC timestamps are subject-specific shifted timestamps; within-subject intervals are usable, but cross-subject calendar alignment is invalid. The configured source lacks raw waveforms and imaging; radiology text is present but images are not. The local text-extraction support is an unvalidated lexical-context detector for cirrhosis, not a general diagnostic system. The archive cannot establish actual medication receipt, dose delivered, clinician intent, true congestion, true fluid/urine balance, true ventilation status, KDIGO-adjudicated AKI, complete outside-system outcomes, exchangeability, causal effect, transportability, or actionability. Those claims require chart adjudication, linked medication and post-discharge data, external replication, and preferably a prospective randomized trial.
