# Episode 13: can a lower SpO2 band improve the acidemic oxygenation-category tradeoff?

## Unresolved question, clinical importance, and substantive advance

For invasively ventilated adults who are acidemic, does a released monitor SpO2 of 94–95%, rather than 96–97%, map to meaningfully fewer simultaneously observed PaO2 values in a liberal oxygenation category without materially increasing recorded severe hypoxemia?

This is a clinically consequential tradeoff, not a search for another favorable oxygenation threshold. Oxygen targets remain unsettled. The inspected OXY-BREATHES full-text review (Nguyen et al., Critical Care Medicine 2026; DOI 10.1097/CCM.0000000000007031; PMCID PMC13134654) defined conservative targets as SpO2 88–94% or PaO2 <=80 mm Hg and liberal targets as SpO2 >94% or PaO2 >=90 mm Hg, among other criteria. Across nine randomized trials and 20,447 mechanically ventilated adults it found no substantial overall 90-day mortality difference (RR 1.01, 95% CI 0.94–1.09), with condition-specific uncertainty. That establishes the consequence and uncertainty of oxygen-target selection, but it does not identify a safe target for acidemic patients.

The inspected full-text Delgado study (Antioxidants 2026; DOI 10.3390/antiox15020235; PMCID PMC12938712) reported a single-center retrospective cohort of 21,406 adults and 717,064 paired measurements. It found higher PaO2 in acidotic states at comparable SpO2 and generally physiologic PaO2 at SpO2 96–97%. It supports physiologic plausibility and a concurrent single-center mapping association; it does not establish an EICU target-band tradeoff, causality, or safety.

The selected episode-12 parent made PaO2 >=90 the primary category at SpO2 96–97. Its complete-source EICU result was a symmetric-overlap acidemic-minus-normal risk difference of +12.727 points, but the 95% interval was −11.613 to +31.897. The strongest claim supported is therefore only that the exact construction is feasible and its observed point estimate is compatible with a category-mapping difference; direction and a +10-point magnitude remain unresolved.

The substantive next question is different: within acidemic observations, does moving from the 96–97 band to 94–95 appear to trade fewer PaO2 >=90 observations for an acceptably small increase in PaO2 <60? This directly exposes the benefit–safety tension that the parent could not answer. It can prioritize or discourage a prospective pH-stratified target trial, but the retrospective comparison cannot estimate the effect of assigning either target.

**Co-primary unresolved hypothesis:** in the measured-overlap population below:

1. the high-minus-low-band risk difference for observed PaO2 >=90 mm Hg, (B=R_{96-97}(Y90)-R_{94-95}(Y90)), is at least +10 percentage points; and
2. the low-minus-high-band risk difference for observed PaO2 <60 mm Hg, (H=R_{94-95}(Y60)-R_{96-97}(Y60)), is less than +5 percentage points.

Both claims must be supported. The +10 benefit margin is a population-level category anchor inherited from the evidence-defined separation between PaO2 80 and 90, not a validated MCID. The +5 safety margin is a deliberately stringent population risk anchor, also not a validated MCID. PaO2 <60 is recorded severe hypoxemia, not proof of tissue hypoxia.

Reconcile exactly 198 observations, 55 cells, 170 stays, 170 people, and 11 hospitals (122, 181, 182, 183, 184, 420, 440, 443, 444, 458, 459): 85 lower-band and 113 higher-band observations.

For cell s with counts nLs and nHs, assign each lower-band row weight `nHs/(nLs+nHs)` and each higher-band row `nLs/(nLs+nHs)`; normalize separately by band. This symmetric overlap estimand describes the recorded acidemic observations with measured support for either band. It is not an intervention estimand.

Define `Y90=I(PaO2>=90)` and `Y60=I(PaO2<60)`. Compute weighted risks in each band, then:

- benefit component `B=R_high(Y90)-R_low(Y90)`;
- safety-cost component `H=R_low(Y60)-R_high(Y60)`.

Report all four risks, B, H, raw events/non-events, group weight sums, effective sample sizes, maximum normalized row weight, and hospital weight fractions. The intersection-union hypothesis requires both components, so a favorable B cannot rescue unresolved or adverse H.

## Full-source feasibility result and evidence boundary

The complete no-sampling audit reproduced the inherited 329 acidemic and 376 normal high-band observations and found 227 acidemic lower-band observations. The pH-severity overlap has 198 rows, 55 cells, 170 people, and 11 hospitals. There are 33 versus 14 raw Y90 events and 5 versus 7 raw Y60 events in higher versus lower bands. ESS is 94.77 higher and 75.31 lower; maximum site weight fraction is 40.15%.

Observed symmetric-overlap estimates are B=+16.273 points and H=+3.972 points. Every leave-site-out B remains positive (+10.952 to +18.757 points), while leave-site-out H ranges +1.858 to +5.226 points. The stricter 0.05-pH-cell sensitivity gives B=+17.392 and H=+6.241 points.

A bounded 1,000-replicate implementation audit (same copied-hospital/person algorithm and seed) produced 1,000/1,000 defined replicates: B 95% interval −13.228 to +29.695 points and H interval −11.800 to +10.932 points. This audit establishes computability but is not substituted for the frozen 5,000-replicate execution.

The only justified reference classification is **inconclusive for both material benefit and safety**: B's interval includes +10 and zero; H's upper bound exceeds +5; Y60 is sparse; and the tighter-pH sensitivity has H above +5 at the point-estimate level. The data do not support “lower is safer,” “lower is harmful,” “no difference,” assignment of either oxygen target, or an acidemia-specific treatment effect.

## Falsification and interpretation

**Supportive for the complete tradeoff hypothesis** requires B's lower 95% bound >=+10 points, H's upper 95% bound <+5 points, and all diagnostics to pass. The strongest allowed conclusion is: “Within this retrospective measured-overlap construction, acidemic observations at released SpO2 94–95 had at least a 10-point lower probability of simultaneous PaO2 >=90 than observations at SpO2 96–97, while the increase in recorded PaO2 <60 was bounded below five points.” This supports prospective synchronized study of a pH-stratified target, not changing care.

**Adverse to the benefit component** means B's upper bound <+10. If it is also <0, the lower band maps in the opposite direction. **Adverse to the safety component** means H's lower bound >=+5; if the interval is wholly above zero but crosses +5, there is evidence of increased recorded hypoxemia but unresolved material magnitude. Either adverse component falsifies the joint hypothesis.

**Inconclusive** includes B's interval containing +10, H's interval containing +5, mandatory diagnostic failure, or required sensitivity conflict. A supported B with inconclusive H means possible reduction in liberal-category mapping with unresolved severe-hypoxemia cost. A supported H with inconclusive B means safety may be bounded but benefit remains unresolved. Neither permits promoting PaO2 >120, a restricted mean, or a model-based baseline.

Verifier fixtures:

- B +15 (95% CI +11 to +20), H +2 (−1 to +4), diagnostics pass: accept only limited support for the concurrent dual-category mapping.
- B +16 (−13 to +30), H +4 (−12 to +11): inconclusive for both; reject support, adversity, “safe,” and “no difference.”
- B +4 (−2 to +8), H +1 (−3 to +4): adverse to the +10 benefit margin despite compatible safety.
- B +14 (+11 to +19), H +8 (+6 to +12): adverse safety; reject joint support.
- B +14 (+11 to +19), H +4 (−2 to +9): benefit supported, safety inconclusive, joint inconclusive.
- Any correct computation paired with a recommendation to lower SpO2/FiO2, a causal treatment effect, tissue oxygenation, toxicity, mortality benefit, device validity, or proof of safe care must fail verification.

## Unavailable evidence, adjudication, and verifier boundary

Structured notes do not repair those missing facts.

Clinical adjudication is required for specimen identity, ventilation status, and implausible values. Prospective synchronized measurements are required to validate the mapping. External validation is required for transportability. A randomized target-assignment study with patient-centered outcomes is required to establish whether 94–95 is safer or beneficial for acidemic patients.
