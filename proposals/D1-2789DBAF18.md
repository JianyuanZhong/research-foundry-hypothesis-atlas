# Proposal: early post-TACE biomarker recovery and recorded retreatment

## Scientific question and falsifiable hypothesis

Hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) care must balance two decisions after transarterial chemoembolization (TACE): whether apparent treatment response warrants routine surveillance, and whether a patient should be reassessed early for additional liver-directed treatment. AFP is clinically used but its post-treatment kinetics are heterogeneous, while hepatic-function markers can worsen after TACE. The unresolved question is whether the joint early trajectory—tumor-marker change together with hepatic-function recovery—identifies subsequent treatment escalation better than a pre-TACE snapshot.

Primary hypothesis, stated before fitting:

> In adults with a structured HCC diagnosis and a first recorded TACE-like procedure, among patients with numeric pre-TACE and early post-TACE AFP, a prespecified early-response phenotype (at least a 50% fall in log1p AFP from the last value in days -30 to -1 to the first value in days 7–45, with no deterioration in at least two of albumin, total bilirubin, INR, and platelet count over the same windows) is associated with lower odds of a recorded repeat TACE-like procedure from days 46–365, conditional on baseline disease burden, prior treatment history, demographics, and pre-TACE laboratory values.

The estimand is prognostic, not causal: an adjusted association between an information-available-at-day-45 phenotype and a subsequently recorded treatment action. The study will not call repeat TACE “recurrence,” nor infer that changing the phenotype or surveillance causes benefit.

The strongest claim currently supported by the inspected public evidence is only that AFP, imaging anchors, and hepatic-function measures are clinically used but heterogeneous and incomplete for residual disease or recovery; the reviewed literature explicitly emphasizes assay variation, lead-lag behavior, leakage, spectrum bias, and lack of prospective validation (frozen source [source checksum], PMC12575369, read for the relevant introduction/limitations passages). It does not establish this HCC-center association. The experiment tests whether this paired trajectory adds reproducible prognostic information in this local longitudinal record.

## Population and temporal design

Use one index per patient: the earliest recorded procedure satisfying the TACE-like rule below, with an index procedure start time. Require:

- age >=18 and nonmissing sex at the index encounter, from encounters;
- a diagnosis name containing hepatocellular carcinoma in diagnoses on an encounter in the 180 days before through 7 days after index (the exact text match is a reproducible structured-label rule, not NLP);
- an index date on or before 2025-01-01, so a nominal 365-day observation window ends by the local 2026-01-01 snapshot;
- an index procedure with a usable start time, joined to its encounter by (Patient Master Index, Encounter Number).

TACE-like procedure names are selected before outcome inspection as names matching TACE, transarterial chemoembolization, or both hepatic artery and embolization in procedures. The implementation must retain the matched original name and count same-day duplicate rows as one procedure episode when they are within 24 hours for the same patient. A sensitivity definition will use only names containing the literal TACE or transarterial chemoembolization.

The landmark is day 45 after index. Exclude laboratory rows after day 45 from all prediction features. Define the primary outcome as any later TACE-like episode whose start is >45 and <=365 days after index. A secondary outcome is any later liver-directed procedure (name containing resection, ablation, radiofrequency, microwave, or the locally observed TACE rule) in the same window. There is no death field, complete claims capture, or reliable radiologic response adjudication; do not substitute those outcomes.

Primary analysis population: patients with at least one parseable numeric AFP in both the pre-window (-30,-1 days) and early post-window (7,45 days). Use the last pre-window AFP and first post-window AFP, preserving the qualitative-result inequality flag. An all-eligible secondary analysis includes patients with missingness indicators and does not silently code absent testing as biologic response. If post-treatment testing is too sparse for the complete-pair estimand, report that as a feasibility limitation rather than redefining the hypothesis after seeing results.

## Features and preprocessing

The baseline uses only information available before the landmark:

1. age, sex, index year, and index procedure name family;
2. prior 180-day counts/indicators for structured diagnoses containing cirrhosis, portal vein, intrahepatic metastasis, lung metastasis, bone metastasis, and ascites;
3. prior liver-directed procedure count and prior TACE/resection/ablation indicator;
4. last pre-index-window values and missingness for AFP, albumin, total bilirubin, INR, and platelet count.

The principal trajectory phenotype uses log1p AFP change and marker-specific changes in the five exact assays. Because the source has no assay-unit column, do not compute ALBI, MELD, or cross-assay ratios. Standardize each assay using training-set medians and IQRs only; retain within-assay values and missingness. Parse numeric quantitative results; represent qualitative-result inequalities as censored/flagged observations or, if the implementation uses capped values, run a sensitivity analysis at the assay's observed censoring boundary. Never use a value obtained after day 45.

To reduce encounter duplication, deduplicate exact patient/encounter/assay/timestamp/value rows and retain the row count as a quality flag. Verify that every joined row has a valid patient/encounter identity and that no index/outcome procedure appears in the feature window. Use no names, identity-card numbers, phone numbers, or other direct identifiers even though they are present in the encounters source.

## Baseline versus substantive alternative

The simple baseline is a regularized logistic regression for the binary day-46-to-365 repeat-TACE outcome. It estimates a transparent adjusted association and gives a calibrated benchmark; its deliberately limited representation is a single pre-index snapshot plus coarse history.

The substantive alternative is a prespecified joint longitudinal-state/discrete-time hazard model. It fits two latent, patient-specific trajectories over the -30-to-45-day window: a tumor-activity proxy informed primarily by log AFP, and a hepatic-function proxy informed jointly by albumin, bilirubin, INR, and platelets. Random intercepts and slopes accommodate irregular timing, while a discrete-time logistic hazard from day 46 onward relates the estimated states, their changes, missingness/measurement timing, baseline covariates, and treatment family to repeat TACE. This model is not claimed to recover true tumor burden or true liver reserve; the latent states are explicitly proxies.

The alternative could reveal whether direction and timing of recovery, rather than only the last value, are associated with later treatment action, and whether AFP improvement has a different meaning when hepatic markers deteriorate. The baseline cannot represent this asynchronous pattern. A learned event-sequence transformer was considered but deferred: the complete paired AFP subset is approximately 1,456 patients in the bounded profile, while images, validated labels, external cohort, and genetic/proteomic modalities are unavailable. It could be revisited if a larger validated outcome or external test set becomes available. This deferral is about the present scientific information and validation risk, not a ban on neural networks or GPUs.

## Split, estimand, and uncertainty

Use a temporal patient-level split with complete 365-day ascertainment: index years 2010–2022 for fitting, 2023 for tuning/threshold selection, and 2024 through 2025-01-01 as a locked test period. Because each patient contributes only one index, patient leakage is still checked explicitly. Report a grouped/random split sensitivity only as a stability check, never as the primary result.

The primary contrast is the adjusted odds ratio for the prespecified early-response phenotype, with a 95% confidence interval from patient bootstrap or robust sandwich uncertainty. The model comparison reports locked-test AUROC, PR-AUC, Brier score, calibration intercept/slope, and integrated log score. The scientific incremental test is the alternative's out-of-sample log-score/calibration improvement over the baseline, with a bootstrap interval; predictive metrics are supporting evidence, not a claim of clinical utility. A validation-only decision curve may be shown at a threshold chosen without test labels, but no treatment policy is recommended.

Report complete-pair availability, assay missingness, censoring, index-year distribution, procedure-name mix, and outcome prevalence before model results. Repeat analyses with (a) TACE-only literal naming, (b) 90-day and 180-day outcome windows, (c) no narrative features, and (d) inverse-probability weighting or a missingness-stratified analysis for post-treatment testing, with the missingness model itself trained without future data.

## Falsification and interpretation

Supportive evidence requires all of the following on the locked test data: the response phenotype's prespecified direction has an adjusted interval excluding the null and remains directionally consistent under the literal-name sensitivity; the joint model improves log score/calibration over baseline with uncertainty excluding a prespecified negligible margin; and the effect is not explained solely by procedure-name mix, calendar year, or whether AFP was measured. This supports a local prognostic association that may justify prospective surveillance research, not a causal treatment recommendation or a recurrence diagnosis.

Adverse evidence is a null or reversed phenotype association, deterioration of calibration, instability across naming/window/missingness sensitivities, or an apparent gain that disappears when timing and measurement presence are handled correctly. That would falsify the proposed early-response claim and argue against using this trajectory as a local escalation signal.

Inconclusive evidence includes too few paired measurements or events in the temporal test set, wide intervals crossing clinically meaningful effects, severe censoring/units ambiguity, or strong indication that repeat TACE reflects clinician access/eligibility rather than tumor response. In that case the correct conclusion is insufficient evidence, not confirmation or refutation.

Clinical adjudication is essential for radiologic response, Barcelona stage, treatment intent, true recurrence/progression, hepatic decompensation, death, and whether a repeat procedure was clinically appropriate. The local files cannot establish these. Additional data or expert review would be required for those claims, and an external institution or prospective cohort would be required for transportability and clinical utility.
