# Provisional repaired hypothesis: discordant HbA1c and weight trajectories before incident diabetes

## Proposed relationship

Among UK Biobank participants without diabetes or cancer at a second valid assessment-center visit, a similar rise in HbA1c between two visits is associated with higher subsequent incident-diabetes risk when accompanied by weight loss than when accompanied by weight gain or stability. This is a prognostic association, not a claim that weight loss causes diabetes or identifies occult cancer.

The clinical question is whether worsening glycemia has different follow-up meaning when the same biochemical change co-occurs with weight loss. The unresolved claim is that the joint trajectory adds information beyond HbA1c change and baseline severity. The strongest rival explanation is that weight loss reflects pre-existing illness, frailty, treatment, or observation/selection intensity; interval between visits and repeat-visit selection are additional rivals.

## Planned population, time origin, exposure, and outcome

Use adults with two valid assessment dates and paired HbA1c, glucose, weight, and BMI. Exclude prevalent diabetes/cancer before or at the second visit, using available diagnosis fields and dates. Set time origin at the second valid assessment date. Follow up after that origin; censor at death, loss of follow-up, or end of registry follow-up. Fix a short and longer window after an event-support audit and before modeling.

Exposure is the direction/magnitude of weight change at comparable HbA1c rise, with continuous and prespecified categorical versions. Primary outcome is incident diabetes from available diagnosis fields and dates; sensitivity analysis should distinguish inpatient ascertainment from broader ascertainment. Cancer and death are secondary only if event support is adequate.

## Baseline, alternative, and falsification

Baseline: discrete-time survival or Cox model with HbA1c change, weight-change direction/magnitude, interval, age, sex, baseline BMI, smoking, and available treatment/medication indicators, including an HbA1c-by-weight interaction. Use a participant-level train/validation/test split and repeated uncertainty estimation.

Substantive alternative: a joint repeated-measurement trajectory model for HbA1c and weight, estimating slope/curvature and their joint latent state, then relating that state to incident diabetes. It can reveal persistence, timing, and curvature lost by a single between-visit difference. It is deferred if repeated measurements are sparse, events are inadequate, or the model is not identifiable. CPU is adequate for the baseline and likely for a bounded trajectory analysis.

Support would require a positive weight-loss contrast after interval/treatment handling, uncertainty excluding a clinically negligible effect, and consistency in the trajectory analysis. Adverse results include no excess risk or disappearance after excluding early events/treatment or requiring adequate follow-up. Wide uncertainty, sparse overlap, inadequate events, or instability is inconclusive and should lead to revision or abandonment, not favorable subgroup selection. Clinical adjudication of diabetes onset, treatment intent, unintentional weight loss, and preclinical disease remains unavailable from these fields alone.
