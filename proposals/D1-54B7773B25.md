# Opportunity-calibrated assay content for the next recorded HCC transition

## Successor decision and scientific deliverable

This is a substantive child of `[prior hypothesis]`. I retain the parent’s semantics-independent, admission-anchored endpoint and its strict-prior assay-content estimand. The change is to make the residual observation-intensity concern an explicit, nested scientific test rather than only a covariate block, and to add a prespecified timing contrast that is more operationally consequential while remaining independent of unvalidated procedure meaning.

The scientific deliverable is a frozen, held-out decomposition of recorded-transition information:

1. an adult first-locally-recorded HCC-coded admission cohort with patient-disjoint random and later-calendar partitions;
2. the unchanged exhaustive seven-state admission-anchored terminal label `P0/D0/U0/R24/P1/D1/U1/N72`;
3. four nested, time-safe information sets that separate encounter opportunity, documentation/capture intensity, assay identity, and assay payload;
4. a transparent nested multinomial/discrete-time model and a matched irregular-time marked model, with the same target, split, preprocessing policy and locked predictions;
5. the primary strict-prior payload increment, `V-I), evaluated after the explicit opportunity and capture/process controls;
6. a secondary, all-admission timing contrast for early versus delayed/no recorded procedure, obtained by marginalizing the same seven-state predictions rather than conditioning on future care;
7. uncertainty, calibration, source-ablation and permutation falsifications; and
8. a claim-to-output table separating computable recorded-care information from claims requiring adjudication or another study.

Completion is the frozen cohort/feature/label audit, fitted models, locked probabilities, proper scores, timing contrasts, uncertainty intervals, falsification reports and interpretation table. Completion does not require a positive result.

## Evidence-supported and unresolved claims

The verified HCC snapshot supports that encounters contain native admission and discharge timestamps; labs contain assay, qualitative result, quantitative result, sample type and laboratory time; procedures contain a name, start/end time and source; and orders and medications contain process rows with native times. The source files can be joined by the pair (患者主索引, 就诊号). This supports an information question about locally recorded care.

It does not establish active HCC, incident disease, stage, tumor burden, liver reserve, resectability, indication, clinician intent, scheduling, consent, receipt, completion, response, toxicity, survival, benefit, causal effect, outside-hospital capture or patient utility. Procedure-table presence is not treatment receipt. Numeric lab values cannot be interpreted as physiology across assays because no separate unit/reference-range field is available. Diagnoses have no native diagnosis time; narrative clinical documents and pathology have no usable event-time field; vitals and transfers are identifier-only in this snapshot.

The strongest claim available before the experiment is therefore only that prior recorded assay content, source capture and later recorded procedures/discharge coexist in these files. The unresolved claim is:

> Among all eligible adults at their first locally recorded HCC-coded inpatient admission, do strictly pre-admission assay values/results and their within-assay trajectories improve calibrated prediction of the complete next recorded procedure/discharge transition beyond admission context, prior encounter opportunity, source-capture/process intensity and prior assay identity—and does that increment distinguish an early recorded procedure from a delayed or absent one?

The primary hypothesis is directional but falsifiable: on the locked later-calendar evaluation, `V` will have lower multiclass log loss and Brier score than `I` for the complete seven-state vector, with no material calibration harm. The improvement should remain after the explicit capture decomposition and should attenuate when prior assay values/results are permuted within assay and split while identity, timing and counts are preserved. A random-split gain alone is not confirmatory.

The secondary timing hypothesis is that the same `V-I` increment, if real, will produce a nonzero and directionally stable contrast in the marginal probability of `P0` (a generic procedure recorded in [0,24) before discharge) versus `P1` (first generic procedure recorded in [24,72)), early discharge and no procedure observed by 72 hours. This is a timing/coordination contrast, not a treatment-choice or causal pathway claim.

## Exact population, anchor and temporal rules

## Primary semantics-independent endpoint

For every eligible admission, inspect procedure rows with valid native 开始时间 in [t0, min(t0+72 hours, discharge time)].

- `P0`: the earliest established valid procedure is in [0,24) before discharge.
- `D0`: discharge occurs at or before 24 hours before any established valid early procedure.
- `U0`: missing/invalid procedure timing could change early procedure/discharge ordering or window assignment.
- `R24`: no established P0, D0 or U0.
- `P1`: among R24, the earliest established valid procedure is in [24,72) before discharge.
- `D1`: among R24, discharge occurs in [24,72) before any established later procedure.
- `U1`: among R24, missing/invalid timing could change later ordering or window assignment.
- `N72`: among R24, no established P1, D1 or U1 by 72 hours.

Use procedure-wins for exact procedure/discharge ties and rerun discharge-wins as a sensitivity. Midnight-start rows, before-admission procedures, after-discharge procedures, multiple earliest rows, exactly-24-hour and exactly-72-hour boundaries, and all missing/invalid times receive explicit counts. The endpoint is an exhaustive recorded transition and does not imply a clinically completed treatment.

The primary estimand is the paired held-out difference in log loss and multiclass Brier score for the complete seven-state probability vector. Report the R24-conditional distribution only as a secondary descriptive view; the primary estimand never conditions on reaching R24.

The secondary timing estimand is derived from the same all-admission predictions and labels:

- early generic procedure probability: Pr(P0);
- delayed generic procedure probability: Pr(P1);
- any established procedure by 72 hours: Pr(P0)+Pr(P1);
- early discharge before a procedure: Pr(D0)+Pr(D1);
- unresolved timing probability: Pr(U0)+Pr(U1).

Report `V-I` changes in these probabilities, calibration and decision-threshold curves at clinically neutral descriptive thresholds. Do not call these treatment recommendations, net clinical benefit or causal effects. No future procedure, discharge, order, medication or document row is a predictor.

## Nested information sets and capture decomposition

All information sets use the same admissions, endpoint, patient split, fit-only preprocessing and prediction target.

- `A`, admission context: age, sex, admitting department (就诊科室), calendar era, admission time-of-day, missingness indicators and strict-history availability.
- `O`, prior observation opportunity only: number of prior linked encounters, active encounter days, prior-history span, days since last encounter, number of distinct prior encounter start dates, left-truncation flag and no-prior-history flag. These use only encounters’ native 入院时间/出院时间 and contain no assay identity, values, results, names or process payload.
- `C`, capture/process intensity: `O` plus source-specific row counts, distinct linked-encounter counts, active-day counts, recency and invalid-time counts for prior labs, procedures, orders and medications. It also includes current-admission non-payload event opportunity in [t0,t0+12h): total valid rows, distinct observed source types and elapsed coverage, but no names, assay identities, values, results, doses, routes, frequencies or statuses. The current-admission part is used only in a prespecified t0+12-hour landmark sensitivity whose target begins after that landmark; it is never used to predict the admission-anchored primary endpoint.
- `I`, assay identity/occurrence: `C` plus prior assay identity (检验), assay-specific count, recency, span, missingness and number of distinct assays. It contains no qualitative result or quantitative value.
- `V`, assay payload: `I` plus prior assay-specific 定性结果 and 定量结果, with within-assay first/last values, change and slope only when at least two distinct valid native times exist, plus assay-specific timing and missingness. Scale and impute within assay using fit data only. Do not pool assays, invent units or create an unvalidated physiologic score.

The primary strict-prior content estimand is `V-I), which holds the explicit opportunity and capture/process controls and assay identity fixed. The decomposition `C-O` quantifies how much recorded source intensity adds beyond mere encounter opportunity; `I-C` quantifies assay-selection/occurrence information; and `V-I` quantifies payload information beyond both. These are descriptive nested information increments, not causal mediation effects or adjustment for a confounder. Report `I-O` and `V-O` as secondary nested contrasts and never reinterpret them as independent biological effects.

For the primary t0-anchored endpoint, `C` uses only strict-prior rows. The t0+12-hour landmark sensitivity is a separate design: predictors are A plus current [0,12h) lab and non-payload process/capture rows, and the outcome is the first terminal recorded state in [t0+12h,t0+72h), with explicit discharge and unresolved timing. No row after the landmark enters that sensitivity. This tests whether early process intensity can be a safe operational control when the prediction time is explicitly moved; it does not alter the primary estimand.

## Baseline and substantive learned alternative

The transparent baseline fits regularized sequential multinomial logistic models for the early P0/D0/U0/R24 distribution and the R24-conditional later P1/D1/U1/N72 distribution, then marginalizes to the complete seven-state vector. Fit A, O, C, I and V on identical partitions. Penalty, imputation, clipping, calibration and iteration limits are frozen after selection. An equivalent discrete-time cause-specific hazard implementation is allowed only if it emits the same joint probabilities and is scored identically.

The matched learned alternative `S` is a low-dimensional continuous-time marked event encoder using exactly the same eligible rows as the corresponding information set: source/table type, elapsed time from t0, event-time mask, payload mask, and assay identity/value only when allowed. It predicts the same seven-state target, with a supervised terminal-state negative log likelihood. `S_O`, `S_C`, `S_I` and `S_V` remove information in the same nested order. Fix latent dimension (suggested 64), decay/update family, regularization, optimizer, seed count and stopping rule before the locked evaluation; tune only on selection data.

The learned model is scientifically useful if it reveals order-sensitive irregular gaps, nonlinear content-by-capture interactions, timing-dependent missingness or prediction uncertainty that the first/last/count baseline discards. It is not retained for a small predictive gain: supportive evidence requires seed stability, calibration adequacy, the same direction of the `V-I` increment, and attenuation under value/time falsification. CPU is appropriate for chunked aggregation, multinomial fits, calibration and clustered bootstrap. A GPU is optional only for the event encoder after an allocated environment probe; no GPU claim is made from an unallocated shell.

## Evaluation, uncertainty and falsification

Report paired random-split and later-calendar locked log loss and Brier differences, calibration intercept/slope and reliability by state and time bin, observed-versus-predicted probabilities, the five secondary timing probabilities, and subgroup diagnostics by fit-defined opportunity/capture strata, calendar era and admitting department only when support gates pass. Use at least 1,000 patient-clustered paired bootstrap resamples, preserving all admissions from sampled patients. Report confidence intervals for score differences and the timing probability contrasts.

Pre-register these falsifications:

1. Permute prior assay values/results within assay, partition and fit strata, preserving assay identity, timestamps, counts and missingness. `V-I` should attenuate.
2. Permute assay identity while preserving prior payload/time counts. A payload increment that survives identity destruction is suspect.
3. Shuffle complete prior histories across patients within partition while preserving `O/C` summaries. Any remaining `V-I` gain indicates leakage or duplicate/split error.
4. Permute prior event times within patient and source/assay while preserving values and counts. Time/order gains should attenuate; encounter-opportunity counts may remain.
5. Reverse history order for S. Order-sensitive gains should attenuate while order-insensitive C and baseline count features remain stable.
6. Compare C against O. If C reproduces the incumbent apparent gain and V-I is null, classify the signal as documentation/capture information rather than assay payload.
7. Source-ablate prior procedures, orders and medications from C, one source at a time, and report whether the V-I increment is driven by one subsystem.
8. Repeat 30-, 180- and 730-day prior windows, tie precedence, midnight handling and optimistic/conservative unresolved-time bounds.
9. Run a forbidden-window sentinel using [t0,t0+12h) or [t0+12h,t0+24h) rows against an endpoint already begun in [t0,t0+24h). Any apparent gain is a leakage/label-timing failure and invalidates the affected result.
10. Verify no post-t0 primary features, no post-landmark sensitivity features, no discharge/procedure/order status from the outcome window, no narrative text and no patient overlap.

Supportive evidence requires the later-calendar `V-I` interval to favor V on at least one primary proper score without material calibration harm, with the other score not materially adverse, adequate state support, attenuation under value permutation, no history-shuffle or forbidden-window signal, and broadly stable results across supported capture strata and prior windows. A stable secondary `Pr(P0)` versus `Pr(P1)` contrast increases operational relevance but is not required to establish the primary information claim.

Adverse evidence is a null or worse later-calendar V-I result, calibration deterioration, reproduction by C, persistence after value permutation, strong time-permutation persistence, source-specific fragility, or a timing contrast that reverses under legitimate boundary rules. Inconclusive evidence includes insufficient states, more than 50% unresolved labels, sparse assays, wide patient-cluster intervals, poor calibration, failed learned-model convergence, large unresolved-time sensitivity, or no support for the secondary timing cells. A random-split improvement alone is not supportive of calendar transport.

## Compute, evidence limits and method choices

The future solver envelope is at most 16 CPUs, 262,144 MiB RAM, 8 allocated GPUs and 28,800 seconds. Expected but unverified workload is 1–4 hours for chunked aggregation of labs/orders, 1–4 hours for transparent fits and locked predictions, 2–8 hours CPU-first for the low-dimensional event encoder, and 1–4 hours for at least 1,000 patient-clustered bootstrap resamples. A GPU is optional for S only, after a bounded allocated probe of the cached `ehr-campaign-gpu:20260908` environment; an ordinary shell’s CUDA visibility is not evidence against GPU feasibility. These are planning estimates, not measured full-study runtimes.

The transparent model is retained because it makes the nested estimands, opportunity/capture decomposition, calibration and falsifications auditable. The irregular learned model is retained because it can reveal temporal ordering, nonlinear assay-by-capture interactions and uncertainty lost by summaries; it is deferred or reported as inconclusive if it cannot meet calibration/seed/support gates. A mechanistic ALBI/MELD-like branch is deferred because units, reference ranges, stage, tumor burden, pathology timing and expert assay mapping are unavailable. A full Delphi reproduction is not claimed: its UKB trajectory and external validation are unavailable here. A genetic longitudinal adaptation is not proposed because verified HCC genetics are absent. A multimodal Oncoformer reproduction is not proposed because local HCC images and the main article/full STAR Methods are unavailable; the accessible supplement is methodological context only.

Computed outputs can establish cohort/partition correctness, temporal safety, feature nesting, predictions, score differences, calibration, timing contrasts, uncertainty and falsification behavior. They cannot establish procedure meaning, intent, treatment receipt, benefit, biology, external transportability, clinical utility or patient safety. Expert review of a random procedure-name sample and linkage to imaging/pathology/oncology treatment records would be required to interpret a pathway semantically. A prospective or causal/implementation study would be required for treatment-effect or patient-benefit claims.

## Retain/defer decision

Retain this change as a meaningful child because it addresses the incumbent’s remaining clinical-value concern with two computable additions that do not require unsupported treatment semantics: (i) an explicit opportunity-to-capture decomposition tests whether the assay increment survives progressively stronger non-payload observation controls, and (ii) the same all-admission endpoint yields a timing contrast that can inform record-review/coordination prioritization without conditioning on a future care state. The primary hypothesis and admission-anchored endpoint remain unchanged for comparability.

Do not promote the timing probabilities into a treatment pathway claim. Defer a named procedure-family decision analysis unless expert adjudication establishes stable lexical meaning, adequate timing and inter-rater agreement. Revisit the learned model only if it adds stable calibrated information beyond V or if a new external validation question justifies its additional complexity. Revisit a mechanistic model only when units/reference ranges and clinically reviewed assay mappings are supplied.
