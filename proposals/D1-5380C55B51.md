TITLE
A temporally strict consequential-outcome check on the post-TACE timing hypothesis: does the day-5 bilirubin/INR signal enrich for adjudicated delayed major postoperative care escalation after HCC resection?

CLINICAL QUESTION, SUBSTANTIVE ADVANCE, AND CLAIM LADDER
The parent candidate asks whether acute bilirubin/INR perturbation after TACE modifies the association between the last-TACE-to-resection interval and a paired postoperative day-5 biochemical hepatic-dysfunction signal. Its principal clinical limitation is that this signal is not validated ISGLS post-hepatectomy liver failure (PHLF) and does not itself establish a consequential change in care. This child preserves that study and adds a deliberately narrow, falsifiable clinical-concordance experiment: among the same selected resection cohort, is the biochemical signal enriched for an adjudicated, newly initiated major postoperative care escalation that begins only after the immediate operative/PACU period?

The feasibility work rejects, rather than relabels, a broad “rescue” endpoint. Generic monitoring/ICU/order terms classified 604 of 747 earliest-clock resections and were dominated by telemetry, monitoring and pressure-sensor orders. A medication tier classified 49 patients and was dominated by terlipressin, for which the source does not encode routine versus rescue indication. Thoracentesis, paracentesis and lactulose are also too nonspecific. Transfers and vitals nominally exist but contain identifiers only, so they cannot reconstruct a dated ICU transfer or physiological deterioration. After restoring the parent’s last-TACE biological clock and excluding events before postoperative hour 24, only six of 824 strictly reconstructed selected patients had a new explicit structured candidate for invasive respiratory support, renal/liver support, critical-care transfer wording or reoperation; all six had an operation-encounter document, six had the acute modifier, five had paired bilirubin/INR, and two of those five had the biochemical event. This endpoint is therefore too sparse to replace or become co-primary with the biochemical endpoint and cannot support a timing-by-injury regression. The substantive advance is a high-specificity sentinel validation/falsification layer that prevents a biochemical association from being presented as clinically consequential unless it is concordant with independently adjudicated escalation.

Strongest claim currently supported by source evidence: the fixed day-28 ledger, last-TACE exposure clock, acute injury modifier and paired day-5 biochemical outcome are computable; broad postoperative order/medication composites are contaminated; and a delayed high-specificity structured escalation screen is rare. Routine bilirubin/INR are biologically relevant to PHLF assessment, but management is required for clinical grading, so neither the parent endpoint nor this unadjudicated screen is true ISGLS PHLF. The parent inspected the public review by Nishio et al. (2023; PMID 37927928, PMCID PMC10623981) and the observational preoperative-TACE literature cited there; this child relies on that evidence framing and does not claim to have inspected unavailable methods.

Unresolved claims tested:
1. Parent claim, unchanged: among selected patients undergoing recorded resection >28–180 days after their last qualifying TACE, acute post-TACE bilirubin/INR perturbation modifies the continuous association between interval and the paired postoperative biochemical signal.
2. New clinical-concordance claim: the paired day-5 biochemical signal is associated with a materially higher risk of adjudicated delayed major postoperative care escalation than absence of the signal. Hepatic contribution is a separate adjudicated attribute, not assumed from a treatment name.

The child does not test whether waiting causes recovery, whether an acute score should schedule surgery, whether an escalation was caused by liver failure, whether nonresected patients would benefit from surgery, or whether the biochemical event is validated ISGLS PHLF.

FIXED POPULATION, TWO CLOCKS, AND NONRESECTION
Unit is patient. Preserve the parent’s fixed eligibility ledger beginning at the earliest recorded qualifying TACE. Landmark t0 is exact TACE start time plus 28*24 elapsed hours. Include age >=18 in the index-TACE encounter, explicit HCC diagnosis in an encounter dated no later than t0, valid index-TACE time, and no qualifying resection or transplant through t0. Exclude intraoperative TACE, invalid timestamps, donor/transplant procedures and resection through t0.

Every eligible patient remains visible through earliest-TACE day 180: recorded resection on days >28–60, >60–90 or >90–180; no recorded resection; repeat TACE/other treatment; and presence/absence of later institutional observation. “No recorded resection” is not survival, continued waiting, resectability, outside-care absence or benefit.

For postoperative analyses, preserve the parent’s biological clock. Among ledger patients with a first qualifying resection after t0, choose the last qualifying recorded TACE strictly before that operation and include when last-TACE-to-operation time is >28–180 elapsed days. Earliest-TACE interval is retained as a ledger descriptor and sensitivity. The new feasibility implementation yielded 824 rather than the parent’s frozen 825; the one-patient discrepancy is an explicit execution gate, likely arising from exact duplicate/event-boundary handling, and must be resolved by unit-testing strict “before operation,” adult encounter linkage and duplicate collapse before inference. Counts must not be informally harmonized.

OUTCOMES AND EXACT TEMPORAL RULES
Parent primary outcome remains unchanged: maximum bilirubin on postoperative days [5,8) >=1.50 times latest bilirubin in preoperative [-7,0), AND maximum INR in [5,8) >= latest preoperative INR +0.20. Report continuous log bilirubin ratio and INR change. Call this a biochemical hepatic-dysfunction signal, not PHLF.

New secondary outcome is `adjudicated delayed major postoperative care escalation` within [24 hours, 8 days) after exact qualifying resection start. A structured candidate must also begin after recorded resection end when an end time is available. Qualifying classes are:
- new or restarted invasive ventilation/intubation after initial postoperative stabilization;
- new dialysis/CRRT;
- plasma exchange or artificial-liver support;
- documented transfer from lower-acuity postoperative care to ICU/critical care; or
- unplanned reoperation for postoperative hemorrhage.

For order-defined organ support, exclude a candidate when the same support class appears from day -7 through postoperative hour 24; this class-specific lookback avoids treating continuation of peri-anesthetic support as delayed rescue. An isolated device-supply order, cancelled order or planned routine ICU disposition is not an event without adjudication. Reintubation/restart after documented discontinuation qualifies. The all-cause escalation endpoint does not require hepatic attribution. Adjudicators separately classify hepatic dysfunction as major contributor, possible contributor, not contributor or indeterminate. Only the first category forms an exploratory `hepatic-attributed escalation` subset.

Explicit exclusions are telemetry, generic monitoring/“monitoring,” pressure transducers, PACU or planned ICU care, routine oxygen or uneventful extubation, fluids, albumin, antibiotics, analgesia, antiemetics, proton-pump inhibition, prophylaxis, lactulose, vasoactive medication names alone, thoracentesis and paracentesis. These cannot be rescued by renaming them.

ADJUDICATION
Two clinicians, including at least one hepatobiliary surgeon or intensivist, independently review candidate structured rows and operation-encounter documents while blinded to TACE dates/interval, acute-injury score and postoperative bilirubin/INR. They determine actual delivery, planned versus unplanned use, initial stabilization, lower-to-higher acuity transfer, event class, and hepatic contribution. Resolve disagreements by a third reviewer. Require kappa >=0.70, <=10% temporally/clinically indeterminate among screen positives, and screen positive predictive value >=0.80 for automated use. Use verification-sampling weights to estimate missed-event prevalence among screen negatives; require estimated sensitivity >=0.80 with a lower 95% bound >=0.60. Failure means the screen cannot define an automated endpoint; retain only manually adjudicated encounter-level findings and report inability to establish exact onset.

ANALYSIS AND ESTIMANDS
The parent continuous timing-by-acute-injury analysis, covariates, observation/unit/case-mix/intent gates, controls and bootstrap uncertainty remain unchanged.

The new estimand is the adjusted and unadjusted risk difference and risk ratio for adjudicated delayed major escalation comparing patients with versus without the paired biochemical signal among those with both ascertainable. Primary computation is verification-weighted because screen-negative adjudication is sampled. Report exact/binomial or bootstrap 95% intervals, the full 2x2 table, and missingness. Also compare each continuous biochemical component using rank-based distributions without choosing thresholds from escalation outcomes.

Because only six structured candidates were observed, do not fit a timing-by-injury interaction model to escalation and do not claim interval-specific escalation effects. If full adjudication unexpectedly yields >=20 exact-time events, a predeclared exploratory Firth logistic model may include only continuous last-TACE time, continuous acute score and their interaction; it remains exploratory and cannot supplant the powered biochemical analysis. With <20 events, report timing-band counts and exact intervals only.

Baselines are: biochemical-event prevalence without escalation information; escalation prevalence without biochemical information; the rejected broad-order composite shown only as a contamination benchmark; and each continuous bilirubin/INR component. Do not optimize lexical terms, the 24-hour boundary or biochemical thresholds against observed overlap.

FALSIFICATION, DECISION GATES, AND RESULT BRANCHES
Computational failure: fixed ledger or last clock is not reproduced; any escalation begins before hour 24, before operation end when known, or has a same-class continuation through hour 24; cancelled/planned care is counted; an undated document assigns onset; or postoperative data leak into exposure/covariates.

Supportive clinical-concordance result: adjudication gates pass; the biochemical signal has escalation risk ratio >=3 and absolute risk difference >=2 percentage points, with both 95% intervals excluding the null; bilirubin and INR continuous components are directionally coherent; and verification-weighted missed-event analysis does not reverse the association. This supports the biochemical signal as a marker enriched for consequential deterioration and strengthens, but does not clinically validate, any parent prognostic timing result.

Adverse result: adjudication-valid escalation is no more common with the biochemical signal, the upper 95% bound excludes RR 1.5 or RD 2 points, most candidates are planned/routine or not delivered, or continuous bilirubin and INR associations conflict. This weakens the clinical interpretation of the biochemical endpoint while leaving its arithmetic and the parent associational analysis intact.

Precise null: both RR and RD intervals exclude the prespecified material effects with all gates passing. This argues that the biochemical threshold is not a useful proxy for delayed major escalation in observed resected patients; it does not prove absence of milder PHLF.

Inconclusive is expected if <20 adjudicated events, intervals span both null and material enrichment, screen sensitivity/PPV or agreement gates fail, onset remains indeterminate, outcome observation is differential, or hepatic contribution is mostly indeterminate. The correct conclusion is that this snapshot cannot promote rescue/escalation to a comparative endpoint. A negative feasibility finding is not repaired by adding generic monitoring or vasoactive drugs.

INTERPRETATION LIMITS AND REQUIRED ADDITIONAL DATA
The source lacks timestamped bed transfers, ICU admission/discharge, ventilation settings, dialysis sessions, drug indication/dose trajectories, anesthesia/PACU records, blood loss/transfusion detail, organ-failure scores, mortality outside the institution, standardized ISGLS management grade and explicit hepatic attribution. Operation-encounter documents are undated narratives. Therefore this study cannot establish true ISGLS PHLF, exact onset without a structured dated row, causal hepatic etiology, severity grade or survival consequence. A prospective or linked multicenter study needs timestamped location/organ-support administrations, standardized postoperative management, complications and mortality before escalation can become a primary outcome or a scheduling rule can be evaluated.

A computational verifier can check hashes, joins, duplicate collapse, fixed ledger, two clocks, elapsed-time boundaries, class-specific lookback, candidate counts, sampling, biochemical arithmetic, weighted 2x2 estimates, uncertainty and whether the reported branch follows outputs. It cannot establish actual delivery, planned versus rescue intent, lower-acuity transfer, hepatic contribution, true PHLF, causal waiting benefit or resectability; these require clinical adjudication or additional data.
