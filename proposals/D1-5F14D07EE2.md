# Does a within-patient drop in ICU lab-draw intensity mark unmet monitoring need (failure to rescue) or recovery/practice?

## The unresolved question and why it matters

ICU patients are monitored by repeated laboratory draws, and clinicians decide how often to draw.
[K1] Agniel 2018 showed across 272 lab tests in 669,452 patients that the *presence and timing* of a
test order — not only its value — carries survival signal (order presence significant in 233/272
tests; timing beat the value in 118/174). Monitoring intensity therefore encodes a care process.
[K2] a 2026 surgical-ICU de-implementation programme reduced routine testing treating mortality/LOS
as safety counterbalances, and [K3] the WICH-ICU stepped-wedge cluster RCT is now measuring testing
intensity against organ-support endpoints. So the consequential question is live and unresolved:

> When a patient's own clinical-panel draw intensity falls while they remain physiologically
> unstable, does that fall mark **unmet monitoring need that precedes a rescue event** (organ-support
> escalation), or is it explained by **recovery** (confounding by indication), by **hospital practice
> culture**, or by **end-of-life/withdrawal** of care?

The parent candidate answered the weakest observable version of this — rare (≈1.4%) 24-h in-ICU
mortality, which is heavily end-of-life-confounded and power-poor. The **substantive advance** here
moves the estimand to a *rescue-sensitive* outcome (new organ-support escalation = failure to rescue)
in a *rescue-eligible* cohort, and uses a *within-patient* exposure contrast so that between-hospital
level differences cannot manufacture the signal.

## Leading claim and strongest rival

- **H1 (unmet need / monitoring deficit).** Among patients still physiologically deteriorating at the
  hour-24 landmark, a within-patient lowering of post- vs pre-landmark clinical-panel draw rate
  predicts higher odds of new organ-support escalation in (24,48]h.
- **R1 (confounding by indication / recovery).** The drop marks a *recovering* patient; the
  escalation association is confined to the improving stratum or is null.
- **R2 (practice).** The apparent association is a between-hospital practice fingerprint (drawing
  style), and vanishes under within-patient centering / leave-one-hospital-out transport.
- **R3 (end-of-life/withdrawal).** The drop tracks comfort-care transition; excluded by design.

These differ observably: H1 predicts the harmful association **concentrates in the not-improving
stratum**; R1 predicts the opposite (improving stratum only); R2 predicts hospital-level, not
within-patient, structure.

## Design (frozen)

**Population.** Adult eICU 2.0 first unit stays; age ≥ 16; ICU stay `unitdischargeoffset` ≥ 1440 min;
decisive `unitdischargestatus`; **rescue-eligible** at the hour-24 landmark = alive at 24 h, **no
active EOL order** (`carePlanEOL` cpleol at/before 24 h) and **not already on maximal organ support**
(no vasopressor and no invasive mechanical-ventilation treatment string before 24 h). Hospitals with
≥ 200 eligible stays retained for the multilevel / LOHO analysis.

**Temporal boundaries.** Landmark = hour 24. Exposure window = `(0,24]h` (pre) vs `(24,48]h` (post)
for the within-patient draw-rate ratio. Outcome ascertainment window = `(24,48]h`, with an
observability rule requiring ≥ 6 h of further in-unit observation or a documented escalation/death.

**Exposure.** Within-patient clinical-panel **log draw-rate ratio** `logR` = log((draws in `(24,48]`
/ 24 h + 0.5) / (draws in `(0,24]` / 24 h + 0.5)); clinical panel = `labname` in a pre-specified set
(creatinine, bicarbonate, pH, paO2, FiO2, lactate, WBC, hemoglobin/hematocrit, platelets, sodium,
BUN, glucose). Modeled continuously and as an RCS(3 df) main effect.

**Trajectory stratum.** `not_improving` vs `improving` at the landmark, from hour-24 physiology
(`apacheApsVar`, `vitalPeriodic`): worsening vs stable/improving MAP, and rising vs falling lactate
or creatinine across `(0,24]`. Used to convert R1 from an adjustment into a directional test.

**Outcome (failure to rescue).** New **organ-support escalation** in `(24,48]h` = first appearance of a
vasopressor treatment string (norepinephrine / phenylephrine / vasopressin / dopamine / dobutamine /
epinephrine) or a mechanical-ventilation string in `treatment.csv.gz`, absent before the landmark.

**Primary estimand.** `logR × trajectory_stratum` interaction inside a hospital random-intercept,
`logR` random-slope multilevel logistic model, adjusted for hour-24 APACHE-IVa severity and the
RCS(logR) main effect.

**Baseline vs substantive alternative (method comparison).**
- *Baseline:* fixed-effect logistic, hour-24 severity + linear `logR` (the simple, interpretable
  comparator; establishes whether structure beyond a linear dose-response is needed).
- *Alternative:* within-patient inhomogeneous marked point-process **draw model** yielding a
  physiology-adjusted *draw deficit*, compared to the baseline (and hospital-random-slope model) by
  ΔAUROC, calibration slope, O:E ratio, Brier score and net benefit, with leave-one-hospital-out
  (LOHO) transport. Information the alternative can reveal that the baseline loses: whether the
  signal is a within-patient deficit in expected draws given physiology (H1) or a between-hospital
  level/practice property (R2), which a single fixed-effect linear term cannot separate.

**Falsification criteria (pre-specified).**
- *Supportive of H1:* interaction OR > 1 excluding 1 **and** not-improving-stratum OR > 1 **and**
  improving-stratum OR CI includes 1 **and** the association survives the R3 restriction and
  within-patient centering.
- *Adverse to H1 (favours R1/R2):* not-improving-stratum OR ≤ 1, or an association confined to the
  improving stratum, or the effect disappearing under within-patient centering/LOHO.
- *Inconclusive:* wide CIs spanning 1, or hospital-level variance dominating the within-patient
  contrast. An imprecise null does not refute H1.

## Required data and honest limits

- **Required files (all available):** `patient`, `lab`, `vitalPeriodic`, `apacheApsVar`, `treatment`,
  `carePlanEOL`, `hospital` (see bindings). Join key is `patientunitstayid`; all offsets are MINUTES.
- **Essential adjudication / unavailable evidence:** eICU lacks order-set, staffing, and bedside
  decision data, so H1 vs a pure practice/measurement mechanism cannot be fully separated; that
  requires external clinical adjudication or another study. Vasopressor strings are bound at the
  string level (not dose-normalized). The outcome is a proxy for failure to rescue, not an adjudicated
  rescue event. Whether a draw-intensity drop is *clinically* a monitoring deficit remains a
  clinical-adjudication question beyond the computable test.
- **Key references** are abstract-only (see `work/refs/key-references.json`); claim scope is bounded to
  abstract-level evidence. No reference/solver solution was executed at discovery.

## What a solver must fit / produce, and how it is evaluated

Fit the multilevel interaction model and the point-process alternative on eICU; report the interaction
OR with CI, stratum-specific ORs, hospital-level variance, calibration/Brier/net-benefit and LOHO
transport. Completion is established by (i) the pre-specified supportive/adverse/inconclusive
classification, (ii) calibration and O:E reporting, and (iii) an explicit statement of which claims
are computationally checkable vs which need clinical adjudication.
