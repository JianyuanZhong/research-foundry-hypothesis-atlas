## Unresolved question and clinical importance
Multimorbidity risk models and staging tools almost universally treat disease burden as a **set** (conditions present/absent) or a **count**. Clinicians, however, reason about **sequence**: a patient who develops type 2 diabetes *then* hypertension is often managed differently from one who develops hypertension *then* diabetes. It is unknown whether the **order** of first diagnosis of two co-present cardiometabolic diseases carries information about subsequent disease accrual and multi-omic state, or whether order is merely a proxy for age at onset, care intensity, or the presence of both diseases. Resolving this decides whether sequence should enter multimorbidity risk stratification and whether "trajectory" is informative beyond cross-sectional burden.

## Leading claim vs strongest rivals, and where they differ
- **Leading claim (A):** given co-presence of diseases A and B, the order (A-first vs B-first) predicts downstream accrual of *new* conditions.
- **Rival B1 (ascertainment/care-intensity):** the first-diagnosed disease simply reflects earlier/more intense healthcare contact; order tracks ascertainment, not biology.
- **Rival B2 (timing/age):** A-first occurs earlier in life; downstream counts scale with elapsed time and age.
- **Rival B3 (null/co-presence suffices):** once both diagnoses and prior burden are fixed, order adds nothing.
- **Discriminating observation:** the co-presence-fixed **order effect Δ_order** = difference in downstream accrual (primary) between A-first and B-first, adjusted for age at first index date, sex, inter-diagnosis interval and **pre-index diagnosis count** (B1/B2), evaluated against a within-stratum permutation null. A survives only if Δ_order exceeds the null band; B3 is favoured if it collapses.

## Baseline vs substantive learned alternative (method comparison)
- **Baseline:** co-presence-only model (disease count, age, sex, prior burden) predicting downstream accrual — the current standard "set/count" view.
- **Learned alternative:** add a temporal-order feature (A-before-B indicator + inter-diagnosis interval), and a data-driven ordered-pair effect learned by penalised regression / gradient boosting under proper cross-validation, on a training half and evaluated on a held-out half. **What the alternative reveals that the baseline loses:** whether the pairwise ORDER structure (a directional sequence effect) adds held-out predictive information over unordered co-presence, and which ordered pairs deviate from exchangeability. **Recorded deferral:** if the order feature adds no held-out gain beyond age/sex/prior count, the sequence hypothesis is not supported (adverse) and the simple count view is adequate.
- **Secondary learned alternative (proteome):** whether order group separates Olink state beyond protein shared axes; explicitly cross-sectional, no temporal claim.

## Pre-specified supportive / adverse / inconclusive outcomes
- **Supportive:** Δ_order exceeds the 1,000-permutation within-stratum null band in a pre-registered majority of powered index pairs after adjustment, and the order feature improves held-out accrual prediction.
- **Adverse:** Δ_order is null/attenuated to within the band after adjusting for age/inter-diagnosis interval/prior count (order is explained by timing/ascertainment) — this refutes the claim and is a valuable negative.
- **Inconclusive:** insufficient powered index pairs, or the effect is entirely attributable to a care-intensity proxy not measurable here.
