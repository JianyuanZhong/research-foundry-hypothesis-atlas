## Unresolved question and advance

The parent asks whether prior-panel values add held-out recorded-risk information beyond the current panel, verified repeat spacing and measured repeat selection, and whether that increment is larger at the age at which the repeat panel is reviewed. The remaining validity question is temporal: would an age-aligned history-retention finding learned from an earlier repeat-assessment calendar era remain valid when assessment practice, participant mix, and ascertainment conditions move to a later era?

The pre-specified hypothesis is:

> After passing the assessment-date, biomarker, outcome and relevant ascertainment gates, an additive model fitted only in the early repeat-assessment era will retain a non-negligible, age-aligned prior-panel Brier improvement when transported without refitting to the late era. The older-versus-younger landmark-age contrast in that improvement will be directionally consistent with the early-era contrast after outcome-blind common-support and repeat-selection controls.

The primary clinical-scientific estimand is not a treatment effect. It is the late-era, no-refit, common-support contrast in prognostic information at the repeat-assessment decision time. For age group g, let

d_g,e = E_e,w[BS(I1)-BS(B0+L)]
and
d_g,l^tr = E_l,w[BS(I1^E)-BS((B0+L)^E)].

Here E denotes the early era, l the late era, superscript E means that the model, preprocessing, calibration and weights were fitted in early data and frozen, and w is defined without late outcomes. The primary temporal quantity is

H_l^tr = d_old,l^tr - d_young,l^tr,

where old is A_T1 >=65 and young is A_T1 <55. The transport drift is tau_H=H_l^tr-H_e, where H_e is computed from early outer-fold predictions. Negative d_g means prior values lower Brier loss; a negative H means the improvement is larger in older landmark-age participants. Report 55–64 descriptively, plus pooled, continuous-age and recruitment-age sensitivities.

This is a substantive successor because it separates temporal transport from ordinary random-split performance. A late-era loss of the history increment, an age-contrast reversal, or calibration/overlap failure would warn that the parent's conditional claim is era-specific. Consistency would strengthen only a conditional transportability claim within this UKB paired-observation support; it would not establish that repeat testing, monitoring, treatment or an age-specific policy benefits patients.

## Selection and temporal transport controls

Create the full flow from population anchor to T0-complete, observed valid T1, complete sixteen-cell pair, timing-eligible pair, early/late era, prior-record exclusion and endpoint categories. Report era-specific age, sex, T0/T1/L, panel missingness, repeat rates, outcome counts and standardized covariate differences.

Among T0-complete participants, define S=1 as a valid paired T1 and panel. Fit a regularized repeat-observation propensity in early data only, using T0 panel fields, recruitment age, sex and T0 calendar year. Apply it to late T0-complete participants. Use stabilized inverse-probability weights, truncated at the early 1st/99th percentiles, to give a late paired-cohort selection sensitivity toward the measured T0-complete population. Report predicted-propensity range, max weight, standardized differences and ESS. This is not a causal correction and does not remove unmeasured selection.

For the primary older-versus-younger contrast, fit an early-only age propensity for A_T1>=65 versus A_T1<55 using early training-only B0+L target/death risk predictions, L, T0 calendar year, sex and early repeat propensity. Use predeclared splines and overlap weights: 1-p for older and p for younger, normalized within group. Apply this frozen propensity model to late data. Multiply by the frozen repeat-selection weights. Use a second covariate-balancing weighting sensitivity on the same variables. Do not use late outcomes, late I1 predictions, threshold movement or age-group labels derived after model inspection to define weights.

Fail the transport estimate as inconclusive if either age group has fewer than 200 late eligible participants or 30 late target events; if predicted age or selection propensities lack usable support, with a prespecified required range [0.02,0.98]; if ESS is <200 or <50% of the corresponding unweighted group; if weights are unstable; or if era covariate overlap is materially absent. The conditional paired-cohort result may still be described, but no extrapolation is allowed.

## Models, split and no-refit procedure

Use one deterministic participant-level five-fold hash split on eid, published with its hash algorithm and manifest. The same assignment is used for all early fitting, preprocessing, tuning, calibration and falsification. No cross-fold duplicate is permitted.

The additive models retain the parent's decomposition:

- B0: current position-1 panel, 21022-0.0, 31-0.0 and T0 calendar year.
- B0+L: B0 plus verified L.
- I1: B0+L plus all eight position-0 panel fields, fit as a regularized additive multinomial probability model.
- A1: I1 plus centered A_T1 per decade interacted with prespecified prior-minus-current contrasts with shrinkage; it is a sensitivity for interpretable age modification, not the primary age-stratum definition.

In each early outer fold, fit and tune these models using training data only and generate early cross-fitted predictions. Select regularization and any calibration map by a fixed, training-only inner procedure. Then fit the final frozen versions on all eligible early data using only the predeclared settings. Apply the frozen transformations and final models to every eligible late participant. Late labels are used only after predictions and all weights are frozen to compute evaluation metrics.

The primary comparison on each participant is I1 versus B0+L, not I1 versus B0. This preserves the distinction between information in current values, repeat spacing and prior values. The primary late transported estimate uses the early-fitted predictions on the late set; it is not a refit comparison.

## Same-input nonlinear alternative

Fit a bounded gradient-boosted multinomial pair as a scientific alternative, not a leaderboard:

- N0 uses exactly the current position-1 panel, verified age, sex, T0 year and L inputs of B0+L.
- N1 adds exactly the eight literal position-0 panel inputs of I1.
- If A_T1 is included, it is included in both N0 and N1 and is derived only after the date gate. No extra biomarker semantics or unavailable modality is added.

Use a small predeclared depth/regularization grid and early-only training-fold stopping/calibration. Apply the selected frozen N0/N1 pair to late participants. Report the nonlinear late H and the same d_g, calibration, resolution and overlap diagnostics, but do not replace additive H_l^tr. N1 can reveal clinically meaningful age-by-history, threshold and current/prior-discordance regions that additive I1 averages away; it is informative only if the pattern is stable across early folds and reproduces in late held-out data with adequate calibration and support.

## Falsification and interpretation

Retain source and schema hashes, exact headers, row counts, key uniqueness, join coverage, overlap agreement, gate counts, date plausibility, T0<T1, interval and horizon checks, the fold manifest, no-cross-fold duplicate audit, post-T1 leakage scan, all model predictions and weight diagnostics.

Run the following predeclared falsifications:

- Permute endpoint labels within early folds and repeat-selection strata and within late evaluation strata; history increments and H should collapse toward null.
- Permute prior panels within age group, era, current-risk band, interval regime, missingness and selection strata while preserving current values, L and selection indicators.
- Deliberately break code/date position pairing and confirm the integrity gate detects it before accepting a result.
- Compare B0 versus B0+L versus I1 to identify gains explained by spacing rather than prior values.
- Compare raw and change representations only if timing and units pass; no annualized-change claim is allowed otherwise.
- Compare landmark-age with recruitment-age contrasts, unweighted with common-support and selection-weighted estimates, complete-case with prespecified fold-local missingness, and additive with nonlinear results.
- Check late calibration drift, resolution/reliability, interval-regime stability, risk-band stability and early-to-late covariate overlap.
- Run an era-label/permutation control for the transport statistic; a purported era interaction that is reproduced under permuted era labels is not credible.

Supportive evidence for temporal transport requires all relevant timing, biomarker and outcome gates to pass; at least 100 target events overall; adequate late age-group support; valid overlap and ESS; no integrity or leakage failure; and a late no-refit prior increment whose direction and practical magnitude are consistent with early results. Predeclare practical equivalence for transport drift as a 95% interval for tau_H contained within [-0.002, 0.002] Brier units, while also requiring calibration not to materially degrade and the age contrast not to reverse. Stronger support requires consistency under selection weighting, recruitment-age sensitivity, calibration/resolution checks and the nonlinear shape sensitivity.

Adverse evidence is a late increment that is null or opposite, an H reversal, a drift beyond the practical margin, a gain explained by B0+L spacing or calibration, late calibration deterioration, or disappearance after overlap/selection weighting. This weakens temporal robustness but does not prove that prior history has no value in another era or setting.

Inconclusive evidence includes failed date, biomarker, outcome or ascertainment gates; fewer than 100 target events; sparse age groups; poor era or age overlap/ESS; unstable weights; endpoint ambiguity; code/date pairing failure; major missingness/endpoint disagreement; or a late result requiring refitting or recalibration. Inconclusive is not evidence against the hypothesis.

Computationally checkable claims include source integrity, cohort flow, era assignment, predictions, proper-score arithmetic, calibration summaries, overlap/ESS, bootstrap intervals, permutation behavior and fold stability. Biomarker meaning, specimen ordering, disease onset, complete ascertainment, clinical action thresholds, causal testing effects, treatment benefit, optimal scheduling, biological mechanism and transport outside the measured paired UKB support require clinical adjudication, additional linkage or another study. Even a fully supportive result remains a conditional recorded-outcome prognostic-information finding among selected repeat-observation participants.

## Alternatives not selected and compute

The additive nested model is selected as primary because it directly tests the scientific uncertainty—whether the prior-value increment and landmark-age contrast survive a calendar-era shift—while preserving an auditable current-versus-spacing-versus-history decomposition. The same-input boosted pair is retained because clinically meaningful nonlinear age-by-history or discordance regions could be hidden by additive averaging; it is deferred from primary inference if early tuning is unstable, late calibration is materially worse, or a shape occurs in one fold only.

A reliability-aware latent persistent-burden model is deferred. Two nontechnical observations cannot identify assay error separately from biological change, and specimen timing/assay-quality metadata are not verified. A reverse late-to-early training direction is also deferred from the primary experiment because it would use the late era as a training source and would not answer the intended early-to-late deployment question; it may be revisited only as a separately labelled secondary direction if pre-fit support is adequate. Transformer, imaging, proteomic, genetic and mechanistic branches are deferred because the local catalog does not verify their required modalities or semantics. These are not evidence that the ideas are clinically unimportant.
