# Hospital transportability of measurement-process signals in early ICU risk prediction

## Scientific question, strongest evidence, and falsifiable hypothesis

The unresolved clinical question is whether laboratory measurement frequency, intervals, and missingness are transportable indicators of patient state or mainly encode a hospital's observation process. This matters because a risk model that appears accurate at its development hospital may be miscalibrated or unsafe when a receiving ICU orders tests differently; deciding whether to strip process features, recalibrate locally, or require site-specific validation is consequential.

The strongest inspected evidence supports only three bounded claims. Informative missingness can be predictive, but its mechanism may fail to transport [K1]. External and prospective evaluation is necessary for implementable EHR mortality models [K2]. Dated structured records can benefit from learned temporal representations when the relevant modalities and external cohorts are available [K3]. None establishes that eICU testing-process signals cause cross-hospital performance loss.

Hypothesis: in adult eICU stays with an auditable first-six-hour window, a process-aware model that adds laboratory measurement counts, inter-measurement intervals, and missingness indicators to fixed early physiology will have a larger loss in discrimination, calibration, and decision-curve net benefit on untouched hospitals than a matched physiology-only model, after prespecified case-mix standardization and target-hospital prevalence recalibration. The hypothesis is about predictive transportability and workflow dependence, not a causal claim that hospitals cause death.

The substantive advance is a hospital-held-out decomposition: it separates the transport contribution of patient physiology from observation process, case mix, outcome prevalence, and calibration. A null result is informative: it would weaken the claim that process features are the main reason for transport failure.

## What the solver must newly fit and estimate

The future solver must construct one patient-level feature table and newly fit both models on identical patient inclusion and hospital partitions.

Baseline: penalized logistic regression using only prespecified early physiology summaries: first/last/median/minimum/maximum and simple slope for the named vital fields, selected lab values with training-hospital-only imputation, plus age, sex/gender, admission source, and timing-safe admission severity. Missingness indicators and counts are excluded from this baseline. A gradient-boosted tabular sensitivity model may test nonlinearity but cannot replace the primary baseline.

Substantive alternative: a modest time-aware model using the same physiology values plus process features: per-lab measurement count, first-to-last interval, time since last measurement, and missingness indicators in the fixed 0--6 hour window. A small temporal convolution/LSTM/attention model is permitted if readiness shows adequate events and sites; otherwise use a hierarchical discrete-time logistic model with splines for time and hospital random effects. The learned alternative is scientifically useful because it can reveal whether irregular trajectories and observation process explain cross-site shifts that aggregate summaries erase. It is not selected for complexity or a small pooled score gain. Both models use the same patient-level hospital splits, preprocessing fitted only in training hospitals, and no post-six-hour treatment/discharge-derived variables.

Primary estimand: paired process-aware minus physiology-only performance difference on untouched target hospitals, summarized as the mean and distribution across targets. Report AUROC, AUPRC, Brier score, calibration intercept and slope, and decision-curve net benefit over a clinically reviewed risk range. Use hospital-cluster bootstrap intervals and a target-hospital prediction interval; do not rely on a pooled patient-level interval.

Decomposition: (1) compare within-hospital random splits with hospital-held-out splits; (2) ablate process features from the same fitted alternative; (3) recalibrate only the target-hospital intercept using a prespecified calibration subset or report recalibration as unavailable; (4) standardize or reweight target predictions to the training case-mix distribution using age, sex, admission source, timing-safe severity, and hospital descriptors; and (5) report site-specific process-feature distributions. These comparisons distinguish workflow dependence from case mix and prevalence shift without treating hospital preference as an instrument.

## Falsification and interpretation

The workflow explanation is weakened if process-aware degradation is equal to or smaller than baseline degradation, if the difference appears equally in within-hospital random splits, if standardization/recalibration removes it without site-specific process shifts, or if it is driven by unstable lab-name mapping or endpoint coding. A negative-control analysis using an admission-stable outcome is optional only if a valid outcome is identified before modeling.

Supportive evidence is preferential loss of process-aware discrimination/calibration/net benefit on held-out hospitals, attenuation after process ablation or target recalibration, and consistency across target sites. This supports workflow dependence as a transportability explanation, not a causal mechanism or a universal deployment rule.

Adverse evidence is no preferential loss, or loss fully explained by case mix/prevalence, or no benefit from process ablation/recalibration. It would reject or bound the proposed explanation while not proving transportability of every future model. Inconclusive evidence is inadequate events/sites, irreconcilable laboratory identity or timestamp coverage, wide site-level intervals, or strongly heterogeneous effects without a prespecified interpretation. Computationally checkable outputs are counts, feature-window integrity, split membership, predictions, metrics, calibration, decision curves, ablations, recalibration, standardization, and uncertainty. Clinical adjudication is required for any non-mortality deterioration label, clinically chosen alert threshold, or claim that changing testing practice improves care. This retrospective design cannot identify a biological mechanism, causal treatment effect, or deployment safety.

## Alternatives, compute, and deferred branches

The primary logistic baseline is CPU-feasible and transparent. The temporal/process-aware learned model is the scientifically substantive alternative because it preserves irregular timing and process information; its CPU hierarchical fallback is predeclared. The solver planning envelope is 16 CPUs, 262144 MiB, up to 8 GPUs, and 28800 seconds. These are planning estimates, not measured full-study runtimes; discovery did not solve the study. A one-GPU fit may be requested only if the readiness audit shows it is needed, using the cached GPU image and `cuda:0) inside the allocation. A bounded raw coverage scan was attempted but cancelled at 600 seconds with no output; therefore event/site counts remain readiness requirements, not observed findings.

The natural-history demonstration motivated the temporal alternative but its UKB/Danish modalities are unavailable. The Bayesian demonstration's genetic inputs are unavailable, so no reproduction is claimed. The cancer demonstration's main article and complete STAR Methods remain unavailable; its accessible supplement supports only general ablation/external-validation principles. Among seeds, eICU-02 is the true parent. eICU-03 hypotension is a plausible physiology alternative but does not isolate observation-process transportability; eICU-08 reduced testing overlaps but is confounded by discharge, interface interruption, and end-of-life limitation. Vasopressor strategy, hyperoxia, ventilatory burden, nighttime discharge, transfer risk, hypoglycemia, and recovery-speed seeds require timing/adjudication dependencies not verified here and are deferred.
