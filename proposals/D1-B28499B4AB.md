# Capacity-constrained mortality-risk review: does late lactate improve who is prioritized at 24 hours?

## Question and substantive advance

Among adults who remain hospitalized 24 hours after an early recorded norepinephrine/Levophed entry and have a valid baseline and repeat lactate, does adding the repeat lactate to the frozen laboratory-plus-physiology model improve the ranking of people for a fixed-capacity mortality-risk review queue?

The leading parent established a carefully calibrated modeled net-benefit question, but its fixed probability thresholds require a clinically elicited false-positive/true-positive exchange rate that EICU does not contain. This successor makes the decision layer testable without inventing that exchange rate: a review queue has a prespecified capacity, so the primary estimand is the additional fraction of eventual hospital deaths captured among the same number of reviewed people. Capacity is still hypothetical; EICU does not record a mortality-review action, its burden, or its benefit. Therefore this is a computable ranking-and-capacity result, not clinical utility.

### Primary falsifiable hypothesis

Let q0 be the nested hospital-held-out calibrated risk from the frozen comparator R0 and q1 the corresponding risk after adding late lactate (R1). Within each held-out hospital h, flag the top ceil(0.10 n_h) people by qj, breaking equal-risk ties by ascending patientunitstayid. The primary hypothesis, Hqueue, is:

Capture10(q1) - Capture10(q0) >= 0.03

where Capture10 is the number of D=1 people flagged divided by the total number of D=1 people in the frozen cohort. The locked materiality margin is 3 absolute percentage points (about 15 of the 507 deaths), and the primary interval is a two-sided 95% copied-hospital bootstrap interval for the paired difference. The queue has the same number of slots under R0 and R1 in every hospital; this is a re-ranking comparison, not a comparison of different review burdens.

The primary supportive label requires the lower bound of that interval to be at least +3 percentage points, both q0 and q1 to pass every prespecified pooled and hospital-level calibration guardrail below, at least 80% of fixed-prediction hospital deletions to retain a positive Capture10 difference, and no failed computation gate. Report, but do not use to rescue the primary label, Capture20 at a 20% within-hospital quota, precision among flagged people, deaths not flagged, site-level differences, and the parent’s calibrated net-benefit estimand.

This hypothesis is clinically consequential only in the conditional sense: if a hospital truly has a defined 10%-capacity mortality-risk review queue, the experiment tests whether late lactate changes which people enter that queue. It does not test whether clinicians would choose that capacity, whether a review changes treatment or goals of care, or whether patients benefit.

## Evidence and unresolved claim

The strongest available evidence is the executed prior EICU analysis in 1,084 APACHE-IVa-complete people from 31 hospitals: adding observed late lactate to entry lactate and retrospective APACHE-IVa predicted mortality improved discrimination and loss and produced a positive uncalibrated modeled decision-value estimate (reported difference in net benefit +1.982 per 100, bootstrap interval +0.483 to +3.351). That supports serial prognostic information against a sparse and partly untimestamped comparator. The exact parent audit independently established feasibility of the stricter cohort: 1,229 people, 507 deaths, 722 survivors, 33 hospitals, and adequate physiology coverage. It did not establish Hqueue or the present calibrated queue margin.

The parent also records the relevant inspected literature evidence: Baysan et al. (2022, DOI 10.1097/CCE.0000000000000750, PMCID PMC9444407) reported a modest C-statistic increase after adding 24-hour lactate to recalibrated APACHE IV in a different 4,440-person sepsis analysis. Vickers et al. (PMCID PMC6261531) explains that probability-threshold net benefit depends on a clinically chosen action and harm/benefit trade-off. These support the motivation for serial prognostic testing and for not treating a mathematical threshold as utility; they do not support this cohort’s queue effect, a treatment benefit, or a review threshold. No supplementary material is claimed to have been inspected. The research-ambition README and availability restrictions were inspected; its demonstrations are not evidence for this hypothesis.

The unresolved claim is narrower and falsifiable: after a richer comparator, honest hospital-held-out prediction, and nested recalibration, late lactate changes the top 10% within-hospital mortality-risk ranking enough to capture at least three additional deaths per 100 deaths at the same review capacity. A positive result supports only this modeled ranking property in this selected EICU network. It does not establish that lactate measures occult hypoperfusion, that it should be ordered, that a review is appropriate, or that review or any treatment changes survival.

## Hospital-held-out prediction and calibration

Use the unchanged 33-fold leave-one-hospital-out analysis. In each outer fold, fit R0 and R1 on the other hospitals as unweighted L2 logistic regression with intercept, C=1, lbfgs, max_iter=10,000, tol=1e-8, no class weighting, seed 20260961. Winsorize continuous predictors at training 1st/99th percentiles, standardize with training mean and population SD, mean-impute missing continuous predictors after scaling to zero, and do not scale fixed missing indicators. A feature with no finite training values or zero post-winsorization SD makes the fold noncomputable; no feature may be dropped.

Construct inner hospital out-of-fold predictions for all outer-training people using the same full feature definitions, preprocessing and model settings. Fit separate two-parameter Platt recalibrators for R0 and R1 on those inner predictions by unweighted L2 logistic regression of D on (1, logit(praw)), with the same solver, C, tolerance, and seed. Apply only to the outer-hospital raw predictions:
qj = logistic(aj + bj logit(pj)).
Before every logit, clip a finite probability to [1e-15,1-1e-15]; nonfinite values are failures, not clipping opportunities. A failed inner fit is noncomputable and cannot fall back to raw predictions. Pool one finite q0 and q1 per person. Outer outcomes never influence training, calibration, thresholds, or feature choices.

This is transportability within the EICU hospital network, not external validation. It cannot test a new health system, changed assay, workflow drift, or actual availability at the bedside.

Calibration guardrails are unchanged: for each qj, pooled calibration-in-the-large absolute risk difference <=0.05; pooled calibration slope from D ~ 1 + logit(qj) in [0.80,1.20]; and at least 80% of hospitals with finite n, mean prediction, and observed death rate within 0.10 risk-scale units of one another. Computability also requires the exact cohort counts, at least 1,000 people/30 hospitals/200 deaths/200 survivors, both outcome values at each hospital, maximum hospital share <=15%, all physiology coverage gates, finite positive training variation for every feature in every fold, one finite q0/q1 per person, and matching catalog/source hashes and headers.

## Uncertainty, analyses, and baselines

The primary interval is a 2,000-replicate copied-hospital bootstrap with seed 20260961. Each replicate repeats outer fitting, inner hospital cross-fitting, recalibration, within-hospital queue formation, and Capture10/Capture20 evaluation. The original hospital and all copies are kept as one held-out group and excluded from training when appropriate. At least 95% of replicates must be finite and converged. Report the bootstrap percentile interval and the paired difference for every hospital. Fixed-prediction leave-one-hospital-out deletions report Capture10 difference, Capture20 difference, pooled calibration intercept/slope, and site pass rates; at least 80% must retain the full-cohort Capture10 direction for the primary supportive label. Deletions are diagnostics, not a rescue.

The mandatory decision baseline is calibrated R0 at the same within-hospital quota. Treat-all and treat-none are not queue baselines because they violate a fixed review capacity, but retain the parent’s calibrated decision-curve guardrail as a secondary diagnostic: at p=0.30,...,0.50 calculate NB=TP/N-FP/N*p/(1-p) for calibrated R0, calibrated R1, treat-all, and treat-none, and calculate the exact parent threshold-averaged ΔINBcal by the trapezoid rule divided by the 0.20 threshold width. Report raw and calibrated models descriptively; raw results cannot relabel Hqueue.

Queue computations must be outcome-blind: the within-hospital quota uses only qj, n_h, and patientunitstayid tie-breaking. Report flagged count, death capture, sensitivity, precision, false-positive count, deaths not flagged, and the paired membership changes from R0 to R1. Do not choose the quota, margin, or favorable subgroup after seeing results.

## Interpretation and falsification

Apply computation gates before scientific labels.

- Not computable: any source/header/hash, cohort, timing, feature, fold, calibration, prediction, queue, bootstrap, or deletion gate fails. Do not substitute pooled random splits, raw probabilities, changed windows, altered quotas, or a different endpoint.
- Supportive Hqueue: all gates and calibration guardrails pass; the copied-hospital 95% interval lower bound for Capture10(q1)-Capture10(q0) is >=+3 percentage points; at least 80% of finite hospital deletions are positive; and Capture20 is not directionally opposite at the full-cohort point estimate. This supports a calibrated modeled re-ranking advantage for a fixed-capacity queue in the selected EICU network.
- Calibration-failed / operationally uninterpretable: the Capture10 lower bound meets +3 but either q model fails a calibration guardrail. The ranking result may be an association, but the stated probabilities and quota interpretation are not transportably calibrated.
- Opposite: the Capture10 interval upper bound is <0. This opposes incremental late-lactate re-ranking for the fixed comparator and quota in this setting.
- Adverse to materiality: the interval upper bound is <+3 but >=0. This refutes the locked three-point materiality claim, not every smaller gain.
- Inconclusive: the interval spans +3 without meeting another branch, deletion direction is unstable, or the 20% sensitivity is materially discordant without a clean opposite result.

A supportive result must be reported as “calibration-supported modeled queue re-ranking; clinical utility and impact unvalidated.” It cannot be described as clinically useful, as a reason to order repeat lactate, as proof of occult hypoperfusion, or as improved survival. An adverse or opposite result does not prove lactate has no physiologic or prognostic information; it rejects the fixed incremental ranking claim. An inconclusive result motivates a larger external cohort with predeclared capacity and assay/workflow definitions.

## What EICU cannot establish and the next study

EICU can establish only the association/prediction of hospital discharge death after L24 and the behavior of an honest, recalibrated risk ranking under a stated mathematical review quota. It cannot establish actual norepinephrine delivery or dose, ordering indication, specimen identity, assay/device validity, clinician view time, whether q was available before a decision, review workload, cost, patient or surrogate preferences, accepted false-positive burden, death time/cause, treatment benefit, safety, or benefit/harm from acting. It also cannot establish external health-system transportability.

Clinical informatics adjudication is needed for lactate specimen/assay semantics, pressor semantics, and display/order timestamps. Clinician and stakeholder elicitation is needed to decide whether a 10% or 20% queue is feasible, what review action is intended, and what burden is acceptable. A prospective external validation should predefine the queue capacity, capture outcome, assay/workflow, and order/display/review timestamps. Only a prospective impact study, ideally randomized for a policy change, can test whether acting on the queue changes outcomes, workload, safety, or cost.
