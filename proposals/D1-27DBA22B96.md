# Documented 7-day sustained liberation after post-shock IV loop initiation

## Unresolved question, evidence boundary, and clinical advance

Positive fluid balance after resuscitation is associated with poor outcomes, but association does not show that removing fluid improves outcomes. The indexed abstract of the 2017 systematic review by Silversides et al. (DOI `10.1007/s00134-016-4573-3`; frozen Europe PMC search `[source checksum]`; full paper not inspected) reports 11 heterogeneous randomized conservative/deresuscitative trials (2,051 patients), with more ventilator-free days but no precise mortality benefit. RADAR-2 was a 180-patient randomized feasibility trial of a bundled conservative-fluid/active-removal strategy, not isolated loop initiation; its indexed report (DOI `10.1007/s00134-021-06596-8`; frozen source `[source checksum]`; full text not inspected) reports lower day-3 balance but underpowered clinical outcomes. A 2026 full-text RADAR-2 secondary analysis (McMullan et al., DOI `10.1097/CCE.0000000000001404`; frozen Europe PMC XML source `[source checksum]`, source [source checksum]) found no statistically detectable biomarker kidney/perfusion differences but states that its size and imprecision cannot exclude clinically important effects. The 2026 Orieux et al. review (DOI `10.1016/j.aicoj.2026.100083`; full Europe PMC XML inspected from frozen source `[source checksum]`, source [source checksum]) calls ICU renal congestion hypothesis-generating, without validated diagnostic criteria, therapeutic targets, or outcome-driven interventional evidence.

The strongest evidence-supported claim is therefore that heterogeneous conservative/deresuscitative bundles can reduce recorded balance and may improve ventilator-free days; available evidence does not establish the benefit or renal safety of initiating actual IV furosemide/bumetanide at this stable landmark. The unresolved claim is a treatment effect for a narrower bedside decision: initiate or re-initiate an ICU-recorded IV loop within 24 hours versus withhold all loops for 24 hours after post-shock stabilization. The advance is a reproducible target-trial emulation with an exact decision time, actual administration exposure, treatment-confounder feedback adjustment, patient-centered benefit, separately bounded mortality and RRT signals, and explicit observation limits. It is not a claim that an EHR phenotype proves renal congestion or that a null observed RRT contrast proves renal safety.

## Hypothesis and estimand

Among invasively ventilated adults at ICU hour 48 after vasopressor-treated shock who are stable off vasopressors for six hours, have at least 50 mL/kg documented positive ICU balance, adequate recent urine output, potassium at least 3.0 mmol/L, and no loop administration in the preceding 12 hours, initiating or re-initiating ICU-recorded IV furosemide or bumetanide within 24 hours increases the probability of **documented successful liberation from invasive ventilation by day 7** versus withholding every loop diuretic for 24 hours.

Successful liberation is deliberately narrower than true extubation or VFD28: an accepted structured extubation event must be followed by 48 hours alive without recorded re-intubation or invasive ventilation, all within the day-7 horizon. The primary estimand is the per-protocol risk difference A minus B, partially identified when follow-up leaves MIMIC observation. The clinical-relevance gate is a lower-bound risk difference greater than +5 percentage points with its 95% bootstrap lower limit above zero. The safety gates remain an upper 95% bound below +3 percentage points for excess recorded 28-day mortality and below +5 points for excess observed new RRT by day 7. These are decision thresholds, not equivalence margins or proof of safety.

The 28-day VFD identification interval remains a secondary outcome for comparability with prior trials. A secondary exploratory modifier tests a larger liberation contrast when a pre-t0 result-available chest report explicitly describes pulmonary vascular/interstitial edema; without clinical validation this is only a text interaction, not a renal-congestion phenotype.

## Primary respiratory outcome: bounded documented sustained liberation

MIMIC does not continuously observe ventilation after ICU transfer or hospital discharge. The parent therefore made VFD28 observation-aware, but its conservative effect bounds can be structurally wide. A corrected-partition broad audit performed for this episode (not the final clinical cohort and not treatment-effect evidence) found 7,735 first hour-48 ventilated landmarks: 4,575 (59.1%) were discharged alive before day 28 with median 390.9 unobserved hours, versus 1,061 (13.7%) before day 7 with median 46.9 hours. A separate structured audit found 4,263 explicit extubation events by day 7 and 3,952 followed by no recorded invasive re-ventilation for 48 hours or through the horizon. These counts establish source feasibility only; they omit the shock, balance, laboratory, washout, ECMO and treatment filters and cannot be used as final arm counts.

Across every linked ICU stay and readmission for the subject, reconstruct accepted procedure events from `icu/procedureevents`. A candidate liberation time is the earliest delivered event after t0 with itemid 227194 (Extubation), 225468 (Unplanned Extubation, patient-initiated), or 225477 (Unplanned Extubation, non-patient-initiated). Retain `subject_id,hadm_id,stay_id,starttime,endtime,storetime,itemid,statusdescription,continueinnextdept`; reject Rewritten, Canceled, Not Started, missing times, and events recorded only after the day-7 horizon. Planned and unplanned events are pooled primarily and reported separately.

A **definite successful liberation by day 7** requires a candidate event at or before t0+5 days followed for 48 hours by: no accepted intubation event (itemid 224385), no new or overlapping invasive-ventilation interval (itemid 225792), and no death. Follow-up must be continuous for the entire 48-hour confirmation period within the union of linked `icustays.intime,outtime` intervals. Same-hospital ward time is unobserved for this primary endpoint because MIMIC has no ward ventilation stream; any ICU-interval gap, ward transfer, hospital discharge, or external transfer before confirmation is unknown, not success. A **definite failure** is death by day 7 before confirmed liberation, or accepted invasive ventilation continuously documented through the day-7 boundary. All others whose MIMIC observation ends or has a gap before classification are **unknown**, not failures or successes.

For person i define `LIB_L=1` only for definite success and 0 otherwise; define `LIB_U=0` only for definite failure and 1 otherwise. The treatment-effect identification interval is `RD_L = mean_w(A,LIB_L)-mean_w(B,LIB_U)` through `RD_U = mean_w(A,LIB_U)-mean_w(B,LIB_L)`. Compute both with clone-censor weights and paired subject-level bootstrap intervals. Emit each person's event evidence, confirmation interval, re-intubation/re-ventilation, death, observation gaps and L/U class. This interval reflects missing outcome observation and is distinct from sampling uncertainty.

Required endpoint sensitivities are: planned extubation 227194 only; any end of a delivered 225792 segment corroborated within six hours by explicit extubation; 24- and 72-hour sustainment; counting unplanned extubation as failure; and a note-assisted adjudication sensitivity. For the latter, `note/discharge` is joined by `subject_id,hadm_id` and may tighten unknown status only after a frozen section/negation/time-aware classifier is reviewed on at least 200 stratified summaries by two blinded clinicians with third adjudication and PPV at least 0.90 for both liberation and discharge-on-ventilation classes. A broad audit found one discharge summary for 6,369/7,735 landmarks (82.3%), 4,738 with an extubation term and 178 crude discharge/transfer-on-ventilator patterns; these are keyword counts, not labels. Missing summaries stay unknown. The primary result never depends on note inference, discharge destination, absence of a procedure row, or presumed home ventilation status.

Report the cross-arm identification interval and alive-discharge carry-forward only as a sensitivity. Also report time to documented liberation, alive ICU-free days, index-hospital length of stay, 24/48-hour balance and urine output. None substitutes for the primary liberation or safety gates.

## Result logic

Supportive requires all of: the lower 95% bootstrap limit for `RD_L` is above zero and the point estimate of `RD_L` exceeds +5 percentage points, so benefit survives adverse completion of every unknown outcome; upper 95% bound for excess recorded 28-day death <+3 points; upper 95% bound for excess observed day-7 RRT <+5 points under both ascertainment brackets; expected balance/urine process direction; sufficient arm size/ESS; acceptable exposure and missingness; and passed falsification. This supports external validation and a pragmatic randomized trial, not efficacy as fact, renal safety, standard care or practice change.

Adverse means the upper 95% bootstrap limit for `RD_U` is <=0, so benefit is absent even under the most favorable completion, or a mortality-harm lower bound is >=+3 points, or an observed RRT-harm lower bound is >=+5 points under the prespecified bracket. Favorable fluid balance cannot rescue respiratory or safety harm. An RRT gate changing across date brackets is inconclusive, not favorable or harmful.

Inconclusive/mixed includes `RD_L` failing support while `RD_U` remains compatible with benefit; wide sampling or identification intervals; benefit without both safety gates; disagreement across extubation-duration or exposure sensitivities; nonpositivity; residual pre-t0 imbalance; material missingness; incomplete 48-hour confirmation; unavailable/unvalidated note adjudication; or an unvalidated imaging interaction. Nonsignificance is neither equivalence nor safety. A null or opposite imaging interaction falsifies only the modifier. The secondary VFD28 result cannot override the primary branch.
