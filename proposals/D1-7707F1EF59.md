# Episode 68 repaired exact-reconciled successor: does late lactate retain material decision information after laboratory and cardiorespiratory context?

## Unresolved question, clinical importance, and advance

At a fixed 24-hour reassessment after an early recorded norepinephrine entry, a late lactate may alter perceived hospital-mortality risk. Episode 59 asks whether that value adds material information beyond pressor-entry lactate and the non-lactate laboratory record released by the landmark. That comparator still omits continuously recorded bedside physiology. The unresolved question is whether late lactate carries a distinct signal after oxygen saturation, respiratory rate, and heart rate over the preceding six hours are represented, or mainly summarizes instability already captured by those streams.

**Primary falsifiable hypothesis:** in the exact inherited age-inclusive EICU 24-hour risk set, adding the released 12-to-24-hour lactate to a fixed model containing pressor-entry lactate, the released leave-lactate-out laboratory snapshot, and prespecified six-hour heart-rate, respiratory-rate, and SpO2 summaries increases uniformly threshold-averaged decision-curve net benefit over hospital-mortality thresholds 0.30-0.50 by at least **1 net true-positive-equivalent review per 100 people**.

This is incremental modeled prognostic information conditional on survival to the landmark and repeat lactate measurement. It is not an effect of ordering lactate, monitoring, resuscitation, or acting on either model. The substantive advance over Episode 59 is a clinically closer comparator; the advance over Episode 61 is computational: the approximate 1,255-person physiology audit is replaced by an independent exact reconstruction of the authoritative 1,229-person population using the parent's asymmetric laboratory revision clocks.

## Supported evidence versus the claim under test

The strongest existing result is the executed Episode 58 analysis. Among 1,084 APACHE-IVa-complete people in 31 hospitals, adding observed late lactate to entry lactate and retrospective APACHE-IVa predicted mortality produced ΔINB +1.982 per 100 (2,000-refit 95% interval +0.483 to +3.351), improved AUROC, Brier score, and log loss, and had positive fixed-prediction deletion estimates in all 31 hospitals. This supports a positive modeled increment against that sparse, partly untimestamped comparator. It does not support the locked at-least-+1 materiality claim because +1 lies inside the interval, and it establishes no ordering or treatment benefit.

Episode 59 is a valid but unexecuted stricter design, not evidence that late lactate remains useful after contemporaneous laboratories. The present complete-source audit establishes feasibility only: it exactly recovers the Episode 59 cohort and shows that all three physiology streams clear their prespecified coverage gates. It does not test the primary ΔINB hypothesis.

Relevant passages of Baysan et al. 2022 (DOI 10.1097/CCE.0000000000000750; PMCID PMC9444407) were inspected in frozen full-text XML, source [source checksum], [source checksum]. In 4,440 critically ill adults with sepsis, adding 24-hour lactate to recalibrated APACHE IV increased the C-statistic from 0.62 to 0.64; admission plus 24-hour lactate had the best reported fit. This supports modest serial prognostic information, not incremental decision value beyond a released laboratory-plus-physiology record. Supplementary material was not inspected.

A current eICU report by Ye et al. 2026 (DOI 10.1186/s40842-026-00323-2; PMCID PMC13435416) was acquired as full-text XML, source [source checksum], [source checksum]. Its abstract and relevant methods/results passages report a random 7:3 internal validation in 1,394 patients coded with sepsis and heart failure; lactate, respiratory rate, and oxygen saturation were among eight selected predictors. That supports their joint prognostic relevance in a different selected population, not this fixed-landmark incremental question, hospital-held-out transportability, or a +1-per-100 margin. Supplementary material was not inspected.

Relevant passages of the Vickers et al. decision-curve guide (PMCID PMC6261531), frozen HTML source [source checksum], [source checksum], were inspected. They support comparison against treat-all and treat-none across clinically reasonable thresholds and emphasize that thresholds encode benefit-harm preferences. They do not validate 0.30-0.50, uniform weighting, or +1 per 100 for this review decision.

The research-ambition README and its availability restrictions were inspected. None of the three demonstrations is used as evidence for this hypothesis, and no unavailable paper text is claimed to have been read.

## Models, baselines, estimand, and uncertainty

Use hospital-level leave-one-hospital-out prediction. In each training fold, winsorize observed continuous predictors at training 1st/99th percentiles, standardize with training mean and population SD, and mean-impute missing continuous values after scaling to zero. Fixed missing indicators remain unscaled. Apply training transformations unchanged to the held-out hospital. A continuous feature with no observed training values or a nonfinite/zero post-winsorization training SD makes that fold noncomputable; no feature may be silently dropped.

Fit R0 and R1 as unweighted L2 logistic regressions with intercept, C=1, lbfgs, max_iter=10000, tol=1e-8, no class weighting, and seed 20260961. Each person must receive exactly one finite held-out prediction from each model. Held-out outcomes inform no preprocessing, fitting, calibration, threshold, or feature decision.

At thresholds p={0.30,0.31,...,0.50}, compute NB=TP/N-FP/N*p/(1-p). Treat-none is zero; treat-all uses pooled prevalence. The primary comparator is physiology-augmented R0. The primary estimand remains:

ΔINB = 100/(0.50-0.30) × integral from 0.30 to 0.50 of [NB_R1(p)-NB_R0(p)] dp,

using the trapezoid rule over all 21 points. The labs-only Episode 59 R0-versus-R1 comparison is descriptive only. Treat-all and treat-none are separate default reference strategies: the supportive guardrail requires R1 to beat the better of these defaults at every threshold, while neither default replaces the primary physiology-augmented R0 comparator.

For uncertainty, sample the 33 hospitals with replacement 2,000 times using seed 20260961. Copies of an original hospital remain one held-out group; all copies are excluded from that fold's training set. Multiplicity weights preprocessing, fitting, and evaluation exactly as materialized row copies would. Refit both models in every replicate and use finite converged replicates for percentile 95% intervals.

Report ΔAUROC, Brier improvement, log-loss improvement, mean absolute probability change, 21 threshold-specific ΔNB values, R1 calibration slope and calibration-in-the-large, and all 33 fixed-prediction hospital deletions as non-rescuing diagnostics.

Computability requires matching hashes and headers; the exact audited flow and 1,229/507/722/33 cohort; at least 1,000 people, 30 hospitals, 200 deaths and 200 survivors; both outcomes at every hospital; maximum site share <=15%; at least 80% coverage separately for every physiology stream; every continuous feature observed with finite positive training SD in every fold; exactly one finite prediction per person/model; all thresholds; at least 95% finite converged bootstrap refits; and at least 80% finite deletions.

## Frozen falsification and interpretation

Apply the inherited ordered decision rules after computability gates:

- **Supportive:** ΔINB 95% lower bound >=+1; R1 calibration slope 0.80-1.20; absolute calibration-in-the-large <=0.05; R1 beats the better default at all 21 thresholds; no threshold-specific ΔNB is below -0.5 per 100; at least 80% of site deletions are positive; and both Brier and log-loss point estimates improve.
- **Mixed:** the lower bound clears +1 but any supportive guardrail fails.
- **Opposite:** the 95% upper bound is <0.
- **Adverse to materiality:** the upper bound is <+1 but >=0, without an earlier branch. This refutes the locked +1 increment for this fixed model and setting, not every smaller increment.
- **Inconclusive:** none of the preceding result branches applies, including an interval that still spans +1.
- **Not computable:** any required source, cohort, coverage, feature, fit, prediction, bootstrap, threshold, or deletion gate fails.

Support would establish only a material out-of-hospital modeled increment among selected repeatedly measured EICU survivors under the assumed review thresholds. Adverse results would weaken the fixed +1 materiality claim after laboratory and physiology context. Opposite results would mean the fixed extended model worsened modeled decisions, not that repeat testing caused harm. Mixed or inconclusive results require independent data, not threshold narrowing, stream removal, clearance, or a favorable secondary metric.
