# Provisional HCC trajectory repair draft

## Proposed relationship
Among adults admitted with a documented HCC diagnosis, a prespecified 7-day trajectory of liver, coagulation, renal, and tumor-marker laboratory measurements may improve held-out prediction of a subsequent inpatient escalation-of-care proxy over a static admission-baseline model. The direction and magnitude are untested.

## Concrete evidence currently available
The parent candidate reports direct source scans of HCC encounter, diagnosis, laboratory, procedure, medication, and clinical-document tables, including exact Chinese headers and many-to-one joins on 患者主索引 + 就诊号. It reports that exact 诊断名称=肝细胞癌 occurs in 336,570 diagnosis rows and 104,554 patient/admission keys, and that relevant laboratory names include 白蛋白, 总胆红素, 国际标准化比值, 甲胎蛋白, 肌酐, 丙氨酸氨基转移酶, 血小板计数, 凝血酶原时间, and 尿素. The parent also reports procedure labels such as central-venous catheterization, TACE/arterial embolization, and liver resection, but does not yet establish a validated escalation dictionary or event counts. These are inherited observations, not new results from this branch.

## Uncertainties and missing evidence
The index cohort, assay domains, units, and escalation proxy still require an exact audit in this branch. In particular: (1) whether the four liver-tumor department restriction is defensible and how it changes the cohort; (2) whether procedure labels can be mapped to a clinically interpretable escalation category without conflating planned treatment with deterioration; (3) whether lab values have usable units/specimen consistency; and (4) whether the proxy is sufficiently supported by event counts and timing. No model has been fitted and no clinical-harm conclusion is claimed. Death, ICU admission, and validated clinical adjudication are not available from the reported source fields.

## Falsifiable test
Fit a patient-level split comparison between a penalized logistic snapshot model and a time-aware sequence/state-space model using only measurements from admission through day 7, with the first qualifying post-window procedure before discharge as the primary proxy. Support requires held-out improvement with uncertainty excluding a prespecified trivial margin and consistency under department, missingness, assay, and timing sensitivity analyses. Adverse results include no improvement, degraded performance, or negative-control failure. Sparse events, unstable units, or an unadjudicable proxy make the result inconclusive rather than supportive or refuting.
