## 1. The claim, the evidence, and why the fork matters clinically

### 1.1 Strongest claim the existing evidence already supports (not retested here)

Deeper **achieved** sedation early in mechanical ventilation is *associated with* longer time to
extubation, more delirium and higher mortality, in a graded dose-dependent fashion. The sharpest
version I inspected is the SPICE harmonised prospective cohort [K1]: RASS assessed every 4 h, a
"Sedation Index" built from the sum of negative RASS divided by the number of assessments, and an
adjusted hazard ratio for *early extubation* of 0.80 (95% CI 0.73–0.87) per unit of escalating sedation
intensity, with parallel effects on 180-day death and delirium, in 703 patients in 42 ICUs. Guidelines
accordingly recommend light sedation.

Equally supported, and usually under-weighted: **the randomized record does not reproduce the benefit of
strategies aimed at lighter sedation.** In NONSEDA [K2], 710 ventilated patients randomized to a plan of
no sedation versus light sedation with daily interruption had 90-day mortality 42.4% vs 37.0%
(difference 5.4 percentage points, 95% CI −2.2 to 12.2) and *no* difference in ventilator-free or
ICU-free days, despite achieving a real separation in delivered depth (mean RASS day 1 −1.3 vs −2.3).
A meta-analysis separating designs reaches the same place: lighter sedation shortens ventilation in
cohort studies (mean difference −1.52 d) but not in RCTs (−1.44 d, 95% CI −3.79 to 0.91)
(Aitken et al., Thorax 2021, doi:10.1136/thoraxjnl-2020-216098; abstract inspected, supplementary
citation, not one of K1–K3).

The standard reconciliation of that discrepancy is **confounding by indication**: sicker patients are
sedated more deeply and also take longer to liberate. That is precisely the blocking objection recorded
against the parent seed, and it is the reason this proposal does not re-estimate "deep sedation →
delayed liberation".

### 1.2 The unresolved claim this proposal tests

Observed sedation depth is the *sum* of two quantities that existing studies do not separate:

- **the depth the clinician intended** — in MIMIC-IV, charted as `icu/chartevents` itemid 228299
  "Goal Richmond-RAS Scale" — which is an *indication*: it encodes the clinician's reading of
  intracranial pressure, ARDS with proning, shock, agitation risk, ventilator dyssynchrony; and
- **the depth actually delivered minus that intended depth** — achieved RASS (itemid 228096) minus the
  clinician's own earlier documented goal — which is a *titration* quantity.

**Unresolved claim (the fork).**
- **E1 (indication / severity).** The association's carrier is the goal component. Being below target is
  a severity marker: organ failure slows sedative clearance and increases pharmacodynamic sensitivity
  (Peeters et al., Clin Pharmacol Ther 2008, doi:10.1038/sj.clpt.6100309, abstract inspected,
  supplementary citation: in 26 critically ill patients SOFA score influenced propofol
  *pharmacodynamics* more than pharmacokinetics — deeper sedation at the same exposure with rising
  SOFA), and encephalopathy/brain injury produce low RASS with no drug at all. Under E1, the
  below-target component should carry no independent delay once the goal and severity are in the model,
  and should behave identically in patients receiving no sedative infusion.
- **E2 (titration overshoot).** The below-target component carries independent delay *at the same
  documented goal*, concentrates in intervals with a sedative infusion running, and shows dose-response.

That sub-optimal sedation relative to a target is common, and skewed toward over-sedation, is already
established descriptively [K3] — a systematic review of ICU studies reporting the incidence of
over- and under-sedation, which found a substantial incidence with a greater tendency toward
over-sedation, and which concluded that the *definition and measurement* of optimal sedation are
inconsistent. [K3] establishes the *prevalence* of the exposure. It does not connect it to a
liberation outcome, and it does not separate it from the target itself. That is the gap.

### 1.3 Why the fork changes a clinical decision

| | If **E1** is right | If **E2** is right |
|---|---|---|
| What a bedside clinician does | Nothing about titration changes; the lever is the *decision* to set a deep target, and the reason to revisit it is the diagnosis (ARDS, ICP, dyssynchrony), not the number | Re-checks delivered depth against the *order already written* — the plan is not in dispute, its execution is |
| What a QI program measures | Proportion of ventilated patients with a deep *order* (goal ≤ −3) and whether each has a documented indication; audits ordering, not nursing | Hours with achieved RASS below the stay's own documented goal while an infusion runs — a numerator and denominator that already exist in the chart, with no new instrument and no new consensus on "what light is" |
| What trial to run next | Another target-setting / protocol trial — the class that has repeatedly failed [K2] | A titration-execution trial: alarm/closed-loop titration against the *already-written* order; the DESIST cluster-randomized trial (Walsh et al., Lancet Respir Med 2016, doi:10.1016/s2213-2600(16)30178-3, abstract inspected, supplementary citation) found monitoring technology increased care periods free from excessive sedation (OR 1.59, 1.09–2.31) while generic quality feedback did not, so the execution lever is at least movable |
| Interpretation of the negative RCTs | Consistent: the trials changed the target, which was never the carrier | Explains them: trials randomized *targets*, and if overshoot is the carrier, target assignment is a diluted intervention |

This is a question about **which of two measurable things to act on**, and the two actions have
different owners, different costs and different evidence bases. That is the clinical consequence.

### 1.4 Association, prediction, mechanism — kept separate

This study can establish, at best, a **conditional association with a time-varying exposure under a
stated adjustment set and an explicit causal diagram**, plus a standardized (g-formula) summary of that
model. It is not a mechanism. Three specific reasons, stated up front rather than in a limitations
paragraph:

1. **An association surviving adjustment is not a mechanism.** Below-target depth is measured, not
   assigned. Any unmeasured driver of both deep delivered sedation and slow liberation (undetected
   sepsis, aspiration, a subclinical stroke) reproduces an E2-looking coefficient.
2. **Treatment–confounder feedback.** The sedative infusion rate is simultaneously a confounder of the
   next interval's overshoot and a *consequence* of the previous interval's overshoot. Conditioning on
   it in an ordinary pooled logistic blocks part of the causal path and can induce collider bias; this
   is why the design carries a marginal-structural/g-formula version (§5.4) and why the conditional
   coefficient is labelled an adjusted association.
3. **Prediction is not the deliverable.** A model that predicts extubation well is not evidence for
   either E1 or E2. The deliverables are the *decomposition* coefficients and their behaviour under the
   negative control (F3), the artifact control (F4) and the dose-response (F6).

The mechanistic alternative in §5.5 is called "mechanistic" because it writes down a
pharmacodynamic generative model, not because it licenses a causal claim; it still rests on the same
no-unmeasured-confounding assumption for the outcome stage.

### 3.2 The stabilization landmark (time zero) — restating the implemented criteria

Criteria are those implemented in
`recovered-work/work/a52179c66018cafad/s05_cohort_landmark_outcomes.py` (script read in full this
episode). They are evaluated on an **hourly grid from vent start + 4 h to vent start + 120 h**; each
criterion uses the most recent value **at or before** the candidate hour within its stated look-back, so
no future information enters. The landmark is the **first** hour at which all criteria hold
simultaneously. In plain clinical language:

- **S1 — oxygenation support is modest.** Inspired O2 fraction (223835) ≤ 50%, charted within 8 h.
- **S2 — PEEP is modest.** PEEP set (220339) ≤ 8 cmH2O, charted within 8 h.
- **S3 — oxygenation is adequate.** SpO2 (220277) ≥ 90%, within 4 h.
- **S4 — perfusion pressure is adequate.** Mean arterial pressure ≥ 65 mmHg, arterial line (220052)
  preferred, non-invasive (220181) as fallback, within 4 h.
- **S5 — no significant fever or hypothermia.** Temperature (223761) between 96.0 and 101.3 °F, within 8 h.
- **S6 — no neuromuscular blockade.** No cisatracurium/vecuronium/rocuronium infusion running and no NMB
  administration ending in the prior 6 h (inputevents 221555, 222062, 229233).
- **S7 — minimal vasopressor burden.** Norepinephrine-equivalent rate ≤ 0.10 mcg/kg/min, summing
  norepinephrine (221906, weight 1.0), epinephrine (221289/229617, 1.0), vasopressin (222315, 2.5),
  phenylephrine (221749/229630/229631/229632, 0.1) and dopamine (221662, 0.01), with units normalised to
  mcg/kg/min using `rateuom` and `patientweight` (default weight 80 kg when missing).
- **S8 — still invasively ventilated, airway intact.** The candidate hour is before the end of the 225792
  episode and no extubation (227194/225468/225477) or tracheostomy (225448/226237) is recorded at or
  before it.
- **120 h cap.** A stay that never satisfies all criteria within 120 h of vent start is **not** in the
  cohort. This deliberately removes patients who never become "weanable" in the first five days; the
  question is about patients who *have* reached a conventional readiness state.

(The Lead's §3 refers to "S1..S7"; the implemented script has eight criteria, S8 being the
airway-intact requirement. I state all eight as implemented and have changed none of them.)

[OBSERVED s05.log]: **18,047** of 25,445 stays reach the landmark within 120 h. Hours from vent start to
landmark: median 5.0, p25 4.0, p75 12.0, p90 41.0. Among stays never reaching it, the most frequent
unmet criteria at their last evaluated hour were S5 temperature (4,688), S4 MAP (2,648), S2 PEEP
(2,201) and S7 vasopressors (2,060).

### 3.3 Time zero, intervals, follow-up

- **Time zero T0 = the landmark hour.** Nothing before T0 enters the outcome model except as a baseline
  covariate (including pre-landmark cumulative sedative dose and pre-landmark mean achieved RASS).
- **Discrete time in 4 h intervals** k = 1, 2, … from T0. Four hours matches the charting cadence of
  RASS in this snapshot and the assessment cadence used in [K1].
- **Analysis horizon.** Follow-up runs to the first of: liberation, death, tracheostomy, ICU
  discharge/transfer (`icu/icustays.outtime`), or an administrative horizon. The Lead's design does not
  fix the horizon; I propose **14 days (84 intervals) post-landmark, with a 7-day sensitivity analysis**,
  and flag this as an implementation parameter for Lead confirmation rather than a change of estimand.
  Justification: liberation is median 24.4 h from the landmark [OBSERVED s05.log], and tracheostomy
  median 217.3 h, so a 14-day horizon retains essentially all liberation events and most tracheostomies.

### 3.4 Competing outcomes (definitions exactly as implemented in `s05`)

- **Liberation (successful extubation).** The first airway-removal event at or after T0 — procedureevents
  227194 Extubation, 225468 or 225477 unplanned extubation, or the recorded `endtime` of the first 225792
  episode, whichever comes first — that is **not** followed within **48 h** by (a) re-intubation
  (procedureevents 224385), (b) a new 225792 invasive-ventilation episode, (c) non-invasive ventilation
  (225794), or (d) death (`hosp/admissions.deathtime`). The index time is the airway-removal time, not
  the end of the 48 h window; the 48 h look-forward is a *definition* of success, and the discrete-time
  model treats liberation as occurring in the interval containing the removal event (see §7 on the
  immortal-time consequence of this choice).
- **Tracheostomy** (competing): first 225448 or 226237 at or after T0.
- **Death** (competing): `hosp/admissions.deathtime` for the stay's `hadm_id`.
- **Administrative censoring**: ICU discharge/transfer (`icu/icustays.outtime`) while still ventilated,
  or the analysis horizon.

[OBSERVED s05.log, analytic cohort n = 14,309]: liberated 12,016 (84.0%; hours from landmark median 24.4,
p25 9.3, p75 68.0); death 1,549 (10.8%; median 107.1 h); tracheostomy 261 (1.8%; median 217.3 h);
censored 483 (3.4%).

### 3.5 Objection to the Lead's design (recorded, not acted on)

I implement the design as frozen. Three objections the Lead should see here, and a fourth — the
**floor-censoring of deviation at deep goals**, which makes goal and measurable overshoot mechanically
non-orthogonal — in §8.4, where the diagnostic that revealed it is reported:

1. **The landmark is permissive, and the framing oversells it.** Median time from vent start to landmark
   is 5.0 h with p25 = 4.0 h, i.e. the earliest evaluable hour [OBSERVED s05.log]. For at least a
   quarter of the cohort, "after basic stabilization criteria have been met" is operationally "4 h after
   intubation" — many of these are routine post-operative patients, not patients lingering past
   readiness. This does not invalidate the decomposition (the exposure contrast is within the
   post-landmark period either way), but it weakens the seed's rhetoric about "continued" sedation and it
   makes the cohort heterogeneous. **Recommended, not substituted:** a prespecified secondary cohort
   requiring the landmark to be sustained for 4 consecutive hours *and* the stay to still be ventilated
   12 h after T0, reported alongside the primary. I did not substitute it.
2. **RASS is ordinal; deviation = achieved − goal treats it as interval-scaled.** The gap between −4 and
   −5 is not the gap between 0 and −1. The design's primary exposure is the arithmetic difference. I
   implement it and add a prespecified ordinal-robustness check (§5.3) rather than silently changing the
   exposure.
3. **Even a strictly-lagged goal may not be a deliberate plan.** 94.87% of matched goal/achieved pairs
   share an exact `charttime`, 99.9% the same `caregiver_id`, and 95.7% an identical `storetime`
   [OBSERVED s03_step0.log]. Lagging removes *simultaneity* by construction; it cannot establish that the
   goal reflects a decision rather than an order-set default copied forward. That claim requires chart or
   note adjudication (§6, §8) and no computation in this proposal settles it.

## 4. Estimand and the baseline analysis

### 4.1 Exposure construction

For interval k (start time t_k):

- **Lagged goal** `G_{k}` = the value of the most recent 228299 goal RASS charted at a `charttime`
  **strictly before t_k**, with staleness ≤ 8 h (otherwise the interval contributes a missing-goal
  indicator and is handled by the missingness model, not dropped silently). Where `storetime` is
  available and informative, the goal must also have `storetime` < t_k (strictly-lagged-goal
  specification; feasibility = diagnostic D1, §8).
- **Lagged deviation** `D_{k}` = mean over achieved 228096 RASS values charted in the **previous**
  interval [t_{k−1}, t_k) of (A − G_at_that_time), where G_at_that_time is the goal LOCF (≤8 h) in
  force strictly before that achieved observation. Deviation is negative when the patient is deeper than
  the clinician's own target.
- **Prespecified secondary transformation (not a replacement):** below-target depth
  `B_k = max(0, −D_k)` and the indicator `B_k ≥ 2`. The clinical quantity is overshoot, which is
  one-sided; agitation above target is a different phenomenon and is carried as a separate term
  `max(0, D_k)`.

[OBSERVED s03_step0.log, on-vent rows]: 397,261 of 458,263 achieved-RASS rows have a goal within 8 h
(86.7% matched); deviation is exactly 0 in 43.0%, ≤ −1 in 44.9% and ≤ −2 in 28.6%. Goal rows on-vent:
0 (30.9%), −1 (43.7%), −2 (7.5%), −3 (4.2%), −4 (5.0%), −5 (8.8%); deep by documented intent (goal ≤ −3)
= 18.0% of goal rows and 44.4% of stays ever; 69.5% of stays have ≥2 distinct on-vent goal values. So
the target is an informative, moving quantity, not a constant default.

### 4.2 Baseline model (primary)

A **discrete-time competing-risks pooled logistic** (multinomial logit with four states in each interval:
remain ventilated / liberated / died / tracheostomy), fitted over stay-interval rows:

```
logit P(event_j in interval k | still at risk) =
      s_j(k)                       # restricted cubic spline in interval index, per cause
    + a_j * G_k                    # lagged documented goal (the indication component)
    + b_j * D_k                    # lagged deviation (the titration component)  <- F2
    + c_j' X_k                     # time-varying confounders, all lagged to <= t_k
    + d_j' Z                       # baseline covariates
```

- **X_k (time-varying, strictly lagged):** sedative infusion rate per class (propofol mg/kg/h,
  midazolam, dexmedetomidine, lorazepam, ketamine), opioid rate (fentanyl-equivalents), bolus counts,
  norepinephrine-equivalent vasopressor rate, inotrope indicator, PaO2/FiO2 (labevents 50821 with
  chartevents 223835) or SpO2/FiO2 (220277/223835) when no blood gas, PEEP (220339), ventilator mode
  class (223849), respiratory rate (224690), tidal volume (224685), creatinine (50912), total bilirubin
  (50885), platelets (51265), lactate (50813), temperature (223761), GCS-proxy motor component (223901),
  and an indicator for *any* sedative infusion running (which defines the F3 strata).
- **Z (baseline, at T0):** age, sex, first_careunit, admission_type, race, hours from vent start to
  landmark, pre-landmark mean achieved RASS, pre-landmark cumulative sedative dose, an admission-type
  surgical/medical flag, and a neurological-diagnosis flag.
- **IPW for censoring:** stabilized inverse-probability-of-censoring weights from a pooled logistic for
  ICU discharge/transfer while still ventilated, using the same X_k and Z; weights truncated at the 1st
  and 99th percentiles, with the weight distribution reported. Censoring is rare (3.4% [OBSERVED]), so
  this is a robustness device rather than a load-bearing correction.
- **Uncertainty:** cluster-robust standard errors by `stay_id`, plus a stay-level bootstrap (1,000
  replicates) for the standardized summaries; a nurse-level (`caregiver_id`) random intercept or
  two-way clustering as a sensitivity analysis (§7).

### 4.3 Estimand and what conditioning does and does not buy

The **primary estimand** is the cause-specific discrete-time hazard ratio for liberation per 1 RASS point
of *lagged deviation below the clinician's own documented goal*, holding the lagged goal and the
time-varying confounder history fixed: `exp(b_liberation)`. Its interpretation is **"among patients whose
clinician documented the same target at the same point after stabilization, with the same measured
severity trajectory, does being one point deeper than that target predict a lower instantaneous chance
of successful extubation in the next 4 h?"**

What this conditioning **does**: it removes the part of the depth–outcome association that runs through
the *choice* of a deep target — the exact confounding-by-indication objection that blocked the parent
seed — because the goal is in the model as its own term.

What it **does not** do, stated plainly:

- It does not make `b` a mechanism. Deviation is not randomized; unmeasured acute illness remains.
- Conditioning on the concurrent sedative rate (a confounder of this interval's deviation *and* a
  descendant of the previous interval's deviation) means `b` is **not** the total effect of overshoot;
  it is closer to a direct effect given current dosing, with possible collider bias. Reported as such.
- The goal coefficient `a` is *not* "the causal effect of setting a deep target" either — it is an
  indication marker standing in for the reasons the clinician set it.

### 4.4 Standardized secondary summary and a marginal-structural sensitivity analysis

From the same fitted model, a g-formula/Monte-Carlo standardization produces the **cumulative incidence
of liberation by 24/48/72 h and 7 d** under (i) the natural course and (ii) a hypothetical intervention
setting `D_k = 0` in every interval while leaving the goal at its natural value, with confounders
simulated forward from their own fitted models. This yields a clinically legible number (percentage-point
difference in liberation by 72 h) instead of a hazard ratio. Because of treatment–confounder feedback
(§4.3) the primary causal version of this contrast is a **marginal structural model with stabilized
IPTW** for the deviation history, reported as a prespecified sensitivity analysis with its weight
diagnostics; positivity is doubtful at extreme overshoot and will be examined before interpretation.
This is a summary layer on the Lead's model, not a different estimand; the Lead may delete it without
affecting F1–F6.

### 4.5 The strictly-lagged-goal specification

The default LOCF goal can, in principle, be a value charted *at the same instant* as the achieved RASS it
is compared with — 94.87% of matched pairs share a `charttime` [OBSERVED]. Under the strictly-lagged
specification the goal must come from an **earlier charting event** (strict `charttime` inequality) and,
where `storetime` ordering is informative, an earlier `storetime`. This makes back-charting of the target
to match the achieved value impossible *by construction* for the exposure used in the model. It costs
sample: the price is diagnostic D1 (§8). F4 is the decision rule on it.

**Updated by diagnostic D1 (§8.4):** the strict rule retains 75.19% of achieved observations (vs 86.69%
for simultaneity-allowing LOCF) in 20,798 stays, with a nearly identical deviation distribution
[OBSERVED, `work/branchB-diag/logs/d1.log`]. The strictly-lagged goal therefore becomes the **primary**
exposure definition and the simultaneity-allowing version the sensitivity analysis — the reverse of the
ordering above, which is retained to show what changed and why.

## 5. Method comparison: regression baseline vs mechanistic alternative

Both target the **same** question (does the delay ride on the goal or on the overshoot?) and the same
cohort, landmark and outcomes. They differ in what "deviation" means.

### 5.1 Why a comparison is scientifically necessary here, not decorative

The baseline's `D_k` is a single number that conflates two physically distinct situations that map
**one-to-one onto E2 and E1**:

- the clinician ran more drug than this patient needed to reach the stated target → **dose excess**
  (E2, a practice quantity, actionable by titration); and
- the clinician ran an ordinary dose and this particular patient went deeper → **sensitivity**
  (E1, a physiology quantity: organ failure, encephalopathy, age — exactly the SOFA-driven
  pharmacodynamic effect reported by Peeters et al. 2008).

The baseline cannot separate them, because it never models dose → depth. If the baseline finds
`b_liberation < 1`, E1 and E2 both survive. **That is the information the baseline loses**, and it is the
whole fork. A second consequence: the baseline treats `D_k` as a contemporaneous state, whereas
sedative effect accumulates (context-sensitive decrement time); the carryover of dosing from six hours
ago is precisely the mechanism by which overshoot would delay liberation now, and a lagged scalar cannot
represent it.

### 5.2 Baseline — inputs, fitting, evaluation, budget

- **Inputs:** the stay-interval panel of §4 (≈14,309 stays × a median of ~6 intervals to liberation, plus
  longer tails; order 2–4 × 10^5 stay-interval rows). All from the tables in §2.
- **Fitting target:** the multinomial pooled-logistic likelihood with IPC weights.
- **Split:** no train/test split for the primary inferential fit (this is estimation, not prediction);
  a 70/30 stay-level split is used only for the IPC-weight and g-formula nuisance models to check
  over-fitting, and for the ordinal-robustness check.
- **Uncertainty:** cluster-robust SEs + stay bootstrap; weight-truncation sensitivity; E-value for the
  primary coefficient (how strong would unmeasured confounding have to be).
- **Comparison target:** the mechanistic model's two-component decomposition must reproduce the
  baseline's single-coefficient result when the components are summed; if it does not, the state-space
  model is mis-specified and that is reported.
- **Budget:** CPU only; 16 CPUs; **[UNVERIFIED]** estimate ≈1–2 h wall clock for the full panel build
  from the zip (the single filtered chartevents pass alone scanned 59.5 M rows in the recovered attempt)
  plus minutes for the fits and ~30 min for 1,000 bootstrap replicates in parallel. No GPU.

### 5.3 Baseline robustness checks (prespecified)

Ordinal-robustness (objection 2 of §3.5): refit with deviation entered as (i) a set of indicators for
D ∈ {0, −1, −2, −3, ≤−4}, and (ii) the one-sided `B_k` of §4.1. Conclusions must be stable across the
three parameterizations or the instability is the result.

### 5.4 What is actually new that must be fitted

**Deliverable of the baseline arm:** estimates `a_j`, `b_j` for all three causes, their joint CI, the
F3 stratum-specific `b`, the F4 strictly-lagged `b`, the F6 dose-response, and the standardized
liberation CIF contrast. Nothing in this list exists in the recovered work; only the gate counts do.

### 5.5 Substantive alternative — hierarchical pharmacodynamic state-space model

**Structure.** For stay i and continuous time t (hourly grid):

1. **Exposure.** For each drug class c ∈ {propofol, midazolam/lorazepam, dexmedetomidine, ketamine,
   opioid}, a dose rate r_{i,c}(t) reconstructed from `icu/inputevents` (`rate`, `rateuom`, `amount`,
   `starttime`, `endtime`, `patientweight`), normalised per kg; boluses as impulses.
2. **Effect site.** A one-compartment effect-site state
   dCe_{i,c}/dt = ke0_c (r_{i,c}(t)/CL_c − Ce_{i,c}), with class-level ke0_c and CL_c given weakly
   informative priors centred on published adult ICU values and allowed a per-stay random effect for
   clearance (organ failure).
3. **Latent sedation state.** S_i(t) = b_i(t) + Σ_c θ_{i,c} · g(Ce_{i,c}(t)), where g is a saturating
   (Emax) link, θ_{i,c} is the **per-stay latent sensitivity** (hierarchical, log-normal, with covariates
   age, SOFA components, renal/hepatic function), and b_i(t) is a slowly varying **drug-free brain
   state** (Gaussian random walk) absorbing encephalopathy, sedative-free coma and brain injury.
4. **Measurement.** Observed achieved RASS A_i(t) at its actual `charttime` via an **ordered probit**
   over the 10 observed categories [OBSERVED s02.log: exactly 10 distinct values for 228096, 0 parse
   failures], with observation-time modelling (RASS is charted more often when staff are awake — §7).
5. **Decomposition.** Posterior quantities per interval:
   - **dose-excess** `E_i(k) = Ŝ(dose_i(k); θ̄, b̄) − G_i(k)` — how far below the documented target the
     patient's *actual dosing* would drive a population-typical patient; and
   - **sensitivity** `V_i(k) = Ŝ(dose_i(k); θ_i, b_i) − Ŝ(dose_i(k); θ̄, b̄)` — the extra depth
     attributable to this patient's own sensitivity and drug-free brain state.
   By construction E + V ≈ the baseline's deviation, up to measurement error.
6. **Outcome stage.** Re-fit the §4.2 competing-risks pooled logistic with `E_i(k)` and `V_i(k)` entered
   in place of `D_k` (goal still entered separately), propagating posterior uncertainty by refitting over
   posterior draws (multiple-imputation-style Rubin combination).

**Prespecified mapping to the fork:** E2 predicts the liberation delay loads on **E** (dose excess);
E1 predicts it loads on **V** (sensitivity / drug-free brain state). A result where both load equally,
or where V dominates and E is null, is a *clean, publishable refutation of the overshoot story* and a
direct quantification of the Peeters-type severity-pharmacodynamics explanation in a modern ICU cohort.

- **Inputs:** exactly the tables of §2; nothing additional.
- **Fitting target:** joint posterior over {ke0_c, CL_c, Emax parameters, θ_i, b_i(·), probit cutpoints}
  maximizing the ordered-probit likelihood of all achieved RASS observations; NumPyro/JAX, SVI for
  initialization then NUTS on a stratified subsample, or full SVI with a structured guide if NUTS does
  not mix.
- **Split:** stay-level 70/15/15 train/validation/test, **plus** a unit-held-out split on
  `icu/icustays.first_careunit` (fit excluding one ICU, evaluate on it) to check that the sensitivity
  parameter is not absorbing unit-level charting culture.
- **Outcome/uncertainty evaluation:** held-out ordinal log-score and ranked probability score for the
  *next* achieved RASS category, against three baselines — (i) last-RASS-carried-forward,
  (ii) multinomial logistic on current dose rates and covariates, (iii) the same with a 6-h dose
  history; posterior predictive checks on observed RASS trajectories; calibration plots of predicted vs
  observed category frequencies; posterior intervals on θ_i shrinkage. A model that does not beat
  (iii) on held-out ordinal log-score is declared uninformative and the decomposition is not reported.
- **Comparison to the baseline:** (a) the E+V sum must reproduce the baseline coefficient (§5.2);
  (b) the decomposition answers the fork, which the baseline cannot; (c) agreement/disagreement of the
  F3 negative control with the V-component estimate is a cross-check — if V carries the effect, the
  sedative-free stratum should show the same association.
- **Resource/time budget:** ~14,309 stays × up to 14 days hourly ≈ 1–3 × 10^6 latent time steps,
  ~10^5–10^6 RASS observations. Plan: 1 × A100-80GB via `job_submit(resources={"cpus":8,
  "memory_mb":65536,"gpus":1})`, batched over stays. **[UNVERIFIED]** estimate: 2–6 h for SVI and
  6–12 h for a short NUTS run on a 2,000-stay stratified subsample; both inside
  `solver_planning_limits` (16 CPUs, 8 GPUs, 28,800 s) with checkpointing every 30 min. **No timing
  probe was run this episode**, so these numbers carry no measurement. Hardware facts read from
  `skills/phai-hardware/SKILL.md` (8 × A100-SXM4-80GB; ordinary shells get `CUDA_VISIBLE_DEVICES=""`,
  so device selection must happen inside an allocated job on `cuda:0`).
- **Selection / deferral:** the **baseline is primary** because F2–F6 are decidable from it alone and it
  is auditable by a clinician; the **state-space model is the designated second deliverable, not an
  optional extra**, because without it a supportive F2 leaves E1 and E2 both standing. It is **deferred**
  only if (a) diagnostic D3 shows too few informative intervals, (b) `rateuom` normalization fails QC for
  the major sedatives (a unit-conversion error would be indistinguishable from dose excess), or (c) the
  held-out ordinal log-score does not beat baseline (iii). Each deferral condition is checkable before
  committing GPU time.

### 5.6 Alternatives considered and not chosen (kept on the record)

- **A tokenized longitudinal-trajectory transformer** (the Delphi-style class from
  `references/research-ambition/`): rejected for *this* question — it would predict liberation well and
  say nothing about whether depth below target is dose or sensitivity. Revisit if the question changes
  from mechanistic to prognostic.
- **A stay-mean landmark 2×2 contrast** (goal-deep × deviation-deep): rejected by the Lead's §4.2 and
  the data support it — the deep-goal/deep-deviation cell has only 119 stays (0.8%) [OBSERVED s05.log],
  and requiring a 24 h exposure window conditions on being slow (median time to liberation is 24.4 h).
  Retained only as a descriptive table.
- **Propensity-score matching on "ever deeply sedated"**: rejected because the exposure is time-varying
  and the matching variable would be post-landmark.

## 6. Falsification criteria F1–F6

Each lists the computed quantity and the decision rule. "Materiality" is fixed in advance:
a cause-specific liberation hazard ratio of **0.90 per 1 RASS point below target** corresponds, at the
observed event rate, to roughly a **6-hour** shift in median time to liberation for a 2-point overshoot
sustained over the first day — a quantity an ICU would act on. Thresholds are prespecified here and must
not be moved after seeing results.

- **F1 — binding gate. PASSED [OBSERVED].** Both components measurable at usable scale: 23,709 stays
  (74.2%) with both goal and achieved RASS on-vent; goal takes 6 distinct values; 44.9% of achieved
  observations are ≥1 point below the clinician's own target; analytic cohort after the landmark 14,309
  with 12,016 liberations (s02/s03_step0/s05 logs). No further computation needed.
- **F2 — primary.** *Quantity:* `exp(b_liberation)` per 1 RASS point of lagged deviation below target,
  with the lagged goal in the model, IPC-weighted, cluster-robust 95% CI; plus the standardized 72-h
  liberation CIF difference under `D ≡ 0`.
  *Supportive (E2):* point estimate ≤ 0.90 **and** CI upper bound < 0.95.
  *Adverse (E1):* the deviation CI contains 1.00 and excludes 0.90, **while** the goal coefficient
  `exp(a_liberation)` has point ≤ 0.90 with CI excluding 1.00 — i.e. the delay rides on the indication.
  *Inconclusive:* the deviation CI spans both 0.90 and 1.00.
- **F3 — sedative-free negative control.** *Quantity:* `b_liberation` estimated separately in intervals
  with **no** sedative or opioid infusion running versus intervals with one running, and the formal
  interaction term. [OBSERVED s05.log: 2,332 stays (16.3%) had zero sedative-infusion hours in the first
  24 h after the landmark, so the stratum exists at stay level; interval-level support is diagnostic D3.
  **Updated by D2 (§8.4):** 122,556 of 458,352 on-vent achieved-RASS observations (26.74%) in 8,663
  stays are sedative-free; the two strata have very different goal mixes, so F3 must be computed as a
  **goal-standardized or goal-stratified** contrast (or a deviation × infusion interaction with
  goal × infusion terms), not as two unadjusted stratum fits.]
  *E2 reading:* the sedative-free coefficient is attenuated toward 1 by ≥50% of the log-HR and the
  interaction CI excludes 0.
  *E1 reading (overrides a supportive F2):* the two coefficients are statistically indistinguishable and
  the sedative-free point estimate is at least as extreme — below-target RASS predicts delay even when no
  drug is running, so it is a brain-state/severity marker.
- **F4 — artifact control (strictly lagged goal).** *Quantity:* `b_liberation` re-estimated with the
  strictly-lagged-goal exposure of §4.5, and the ratio of log-HRs to the primary.
  *Rule:* the effect must retain ≥50% of the primary log-HR with a CI still excluding 1.00. If it
  vanishes, the finding is reported as a **charting artifact** (the apparent overshoot effect depended on
  goals charted simultaneously with the achieved value) — a negative result worth publishing, since the
  same artifact would afflict any EHR-based "time in target range" quality metric.
- **F5 — competing risks.** *Quantity:* all three cause-specific coefficients and the standardized
  cumulative incidence functions for liberation, death and tracheostomy.
  *Rule:* a supportive F2 is downgraded if the same deviation increase raises the death hazard by an
  equal or larger relative amount (that pattern indicates severity, not titration), or if the liberation
  CIF difference is not mirrored by a corresponding change in the overall ventilation-free time.
- **F6 — dose-response within the infusion stratum.** *Quantity:* `b_liberation` across quartiles of
  cumulative below-target depth-hours (Σ_k 4·B_k) within sedative-infusion intervals, plus a monotone
  trend test; and the association with sedative dose intensity at fixed goal.
  *Rule:* supportive if monotone with trend CI excluding 0; a flat or non-monotone pattern with a
  significant F2 suggests a threshold/indicator phenomenon (e.g. deep sedation documented at the moment a
  procedure is performed) and is reported as such.

### 6.1 What the three result classes would mean

- **Supportive (F2 supportive, F3 shows attenuation without drug, F4 survives, F6 monotone).** The
  sedation-depth association has a component that is *not* indication and that is already measurable in
  any EHR with target and achieved RASS. It justifies (i) a QI metric defined on each unit's own charted
  orders, and (ii) a trial of titration execution rather than another target trial. It would **not**
  show that reducing overshoot improves liberation — that needs the interventional study.
- **Adverse (F2 adverse: the goal carries it, deviation null).** The seed's premise is wrong in a useful
  direction: deep sedation's association with delayed liberation is indication, and the negative
  randomized record [K2] stops being a paradox. This is publishable and would argue against spending QI
  effort on time-in-target-range metrics. Strength depends on precision: with 12,016 liberations the
  design should detect HR 0.90 comfortably, but that is an assertion, not a computed power calculation
  [UNRESOLVED — a simulation-based power calculation on the actual panel is part of the solver work].
- **Inconclusive (wide CI, or F2 supportive but F4 kills it, or F3 shows equality).** Report as such and
  name the next step: F4 failure → a documentation-practice study (chart review of how goals are
  entered); F3 equality → a brain-state study (the mechanistic model's V-component, EEG, or delirium
  assessment data this snapshot lacks).

### 6.2 Which claims are computationally checkable, and which are not

| Claim | Status |
|---|---|
| Both exposure components exist and vary at scale | **Computationally checkable — already done** [OBSERVED, F1] |
| Deviation predicts liberation independently of the documented goal and measured severity | **Computationally checkable** (F2) |
| The association differs between drug-exposed and drug-free intervals | **Computationally checkable** (F3) |

## 7. Alternative explanations and threats

1. **Severity and slowed clearance (the core rival, E1).** Addressed by: the goal term itself; the
   time-varying severity covariates (lactate, bilirubin, creatinine, platelets, vasopressor dose,
   P/F ratio); F3; and, substantively, the V-component of §5.5. **Accepted limitation:** no SOFA-style
   neurological or coagulation score is computed from a validated pipeline here, and residual severity
   confounding cannot be excluded — hence the E-value.
2. **Encephalopathy, sedative-free coma, brain injury.** A patient with a hypoxic-ischaemic brain injury
   is at RASS −5 against a goal of 0 with no drug running: this is the single most important alternative
   generator of "overshoot". Addressed by F3 (such intervals are drug-free), by the GCS-proxy covariate,
   by a baseline neurological-diagnosis flag, and by b_i(t) in §5.5. **Accepted limitation:** MIMIC-IV
   has no routine EEG or sedation-interruption flag in this snapshot; CAM-ICU-related item 228302 has
   only 1,116 rows [OBSERVED s02.log] and cannot serve.
3. **Documentation artifacts: back-charting and copy-forward.** 94.87% of matched pairs share a
   `charttime`, 99.9% a `caregiver_id`, 95.7% an identical `storetime` [OBSERVED s03_step0.log]. The
   strictly-lagged specification (§4.5) and F4 address *simultaneity*. Copy-forward of an order-set
   default is **not** addressed by any computation here; it is the adjudication item in §6.2. A partial
   computational probe: the distribution of goal-change events per stay (69.5% of stays have ≥2 distinct
   on-vent goal values [OBSERVED]) argues against pure copy-forward for most stays but not for all.
4. **Immortal time.** Two sources. (a) *Landmark design*: time zero is the landmark, so no exposure is
   measured before a patient has survived to it — correct by construction, and the reason the recovered
   24 h fixed exposure window was rejected (requiring 24 h on-vent exposure conditions on being slow;
   median time to liberation is 24.4 h [OBSERVED]). (b) *The 48 h success definition*: an extubation is
   only "successful" if the patient avoids reintubation/NIV/death for 48 h, which uses future
   information. The model therefore treats the event as occurring at the removal time but a patient who
   dies 20 h after extubation is classified as a death, not a liberation. Sensitivity analyses: a 24 h
   and a 72 h success window, and a version where the first removal event is the outcome regardless of
   what follows (pure "extubation") reported alongside.
5. **Informative censoring.** ICU discharge while still ventilated (3.4% [OBSERVED]) is plausibly
   related to prognosis (transfer to a long-term ventilator unit). Addressed by stabilized IPC weights
   (§4.2) and by a worst-case bound in which all censored stays are treated as tracheostomy.
6. **Nurse and unit clustering, staffing.** RASS and its goal are nurse-charted; a nurse who titrates
   deeply may also chart differently and work in a unit that extubates slowly. Addressed by: a
   `caregiver_id` random intercept (crossed with stay) as a sensitivity analysis, `first_careunit` fixed
   effects, and the unit-held-out split for §5.5. **Accepted limitation:** `icu/caregiver` carries only
   `caregiver_id` — no role, no shift, no staffing ratio — so staffing intensity cannot be adjusted for;
   it is named as unmeasured.
7. **Time-of-day charting patterns.** RASS is charted on nursing rounds; night-time values cluster deep
   and night-time extubations are rare for organizational reasons, which can manufacture an
   overshoot-delays-liberation association. Addressed by: hour-of-day and day/night terms in both the
   outcome and censoring models, interval-index splines, and a sensitivity analysis restricted to
   daytime intervals (07:00–19:00 by `charttime`). **Note:** MIMIC-IV date-shifting preserves time of day
   within a patient, so this is legitimate; it does not permit calendar-season analysis.
8. **Reverse causation within an interval.** A patient about to be extubated is lightened first, so a
   contemporaneous achieved RASS predicts extubation trivially. Addressed by lagging *both* exposure
   components by a full interval (§4.1) — this is why the design is lagged, not contemporaneous.
9. **Competing-risk substitution.** Overshoot could appear to delay liberation merely by moving patients
   into tracheostomy or death. F5 is the check.
10. **Unit conversion errors in `rateuom`.** A misconverted dexmedetomidine rate would masquerade as
    dose excess in §5.5. Pre-committed QC: per-itemid `rateuom` frequency table and plausibility ranges
    reported before fitting; failure is a deferral condition (§5.5).

### 8.1 D1 — strictly-lagged-goal feasibility

*Question:* after requiring the goal to come from a strictly earlier `charttime` (and, where ordered, an

*Prior stay-level evidence:* 2,332 of 14,309 stays (16.3%) had zero sedative-infusion hours in the 24 h

### 8.3 D3 — interval event support

*Question:* with 4 h intervals from the landmark, how many stay-intervals exist, how many carry a lagged
goal and a lagged deviation, and how are the 12,016 liberation / 1,549 death / 261 tracheostomy events

*Decision:* if events in the first 6 intervals dominate to the point that later splines are unstable, the

1. **Below-target depth is, descriptively, *more* severe where no drug is running.** That is exactly

**D3 exploratory cross-tab, and a measurement boundary the Lead's design does not anticipate.**
The diagnostics worker also printed a crude, unadjusted liberation-per-person-interval cross-tab,
explicitly labelled `[EXPLORATORY] ... NOT an effect estimate`. I reproduce it only for the structural
fact it exposes, not as evidence: under the strictly-lagged rule, crude liberation per person-interval
was 6.69% (goal 0/−1/−2, deviation 0 or positive), 4.39% (same goal, deviation −1/−2), 1.38%
(same goal, deviation ≤ −3), 3.66% (goal ≤ −3, deviation 0 or positive), 1.40% (goal ≤ −3,
deviation −1/−2) and **0 person-intervals** in the cell (goal ≤ −3, deviation ≤ −3). These crude numbers
are confounded by everything F2 adjusts for — severity, time since landmark, drug — and must not be read
as an effect. **The structural fact is the empty cell:** RASS has a floor of −5, so a patient whose
documented goal is already −3 cannot be observed more than 2 points below it, and a patient with goal
−5 cannot be observed below target at all. Goal and measurable deviation are therefore
**mechanically non-orthogonal**: the overshoot exposure is floor-censored precisely where the
indication is deepest. Objection 4 to the Lead's design (added here on the strength of D3): the primary
contrast should be reported **within the goal stratum where overshoot is measurable with full range
(goal ≥ −2, which is 82.0% of goal observations [OBSERVED s03_step0.log: 0/−1/−2 = 30.89 + 43.66 + 7.48
= 82.03%])**, with the deep-goal stratum reported separately and acknowledged as range-restricted.
I have not substituted this restriction into the primary analysis; it is proposed to the Lead, and
unlike the F3 correction above it would change the primary estimand's population.

## 9. Summary of what changed relative to the parent seed, and what remains uncertain

**Changed (the repair).** The parent seed's exposure was "continued deep sedation" (achieved depth, or
sedative dose as a proxy). Its blocking objection was confounding by indication, and its own caution was
that dose cannot substitute for measured depth. This proposal replaces the single exposure with a
**two-component decomposition** — the clinician's own charted target (the indication, itemid 228299) and
the achieved-minus-target deviation (the titration quantity) — so that the confounding-by-indication
objection becomes an estimable term in the model rather than an unmeasured threat, and so that no dose
proxy is needed for the primary exposure (dose enters only as a confounder in the baseline and as the
*driver* in the mechanistic model, where it is explicitly not a substitute for measured depth). The
landmark, competing-risks structure and the 4 h discrete-time analysis come from the Lead's frozen
design; the negative control (F3), the artifact control (F4) and the mechanistic decomposition (§5.5)
are the three devices that make the fork decidable.

**Remains uncertain.** (i) Whether a charted goal is a plan or a default — adjudication, not
computation. (ii) Whether overshoot is modifiable and whether modifying it helps — interventional.
(iii) Whether the PD state-space model is identified well enough to separate dose excess from
sensitivity with 4-hourly ordinal observations — the held-out score and the unit-held-out split are the
honest tests, and failure is a reportable outcome. (iv) Lab and ventilator-mechanics coverage in this
cohort is unmeasured (§2.3). (v) The permissiveness of the landmark (§3.5, objection 1).
(vi) **Open items returned to the Lead** (§8.4): whether the tracheostomy cause (88 covered events under
the now-primary strictly-lagged exposure) is retained or collapsed with death, and whether the primary
contrast is restricted to the goal ≥ −2 stratum where overshoot is not floor-censored. Both change an
outcome or population definition and are explicitly not decided here.
(vii) D2 already shows that below-target depth is *descriptively more severe* where no sedative is
running (mean deviation −1.28 vs −0.56), which is the E1 pattern. The honest prior going into F2/F3 is
therefore **not** that E2 will win; a substantial part of the overshoot signal in this dataset is
plausibly brain state, and the proposal is built so that this outcome is reportable rather than
embarrassing.

## Key references

Three inspected works, each abstract-only (Europe PMC `resultType=core` records; the attached files are
the bibliographic + abstract text actually read). Receipts, hashes and locators are in
`work/branchB/key-references.json`; the attached bytes are in `work/branchB/refs/`.

- **[K1]** Shehabi Y, Bellomo R, Kadiman S, et al. *Sedation Intensity in the First 48 Hours of
  Mechanical Ventilation and 180-Day Mortality: A Multinational Prospective Longitudinal Cohort Study.*
  Crit Care Med 2018. doi:10.1097/ccm.0000000000003071. The strongest measurement-based statement of the
  achieved-depth association, with 4-hourly RASS, a sedation-intensity index, and time to extubation
  HR 0.80 (0.73–0.87). It measures achieved depth only; the clinician's target is not an exposure.
- **[K2]** Olsen HT, Nedergaard HK, Strøm T, et al. *Nonsedation or Light Sedation in Critically Ill,
  Mechanically Ventilated Patients.* N Engl J Med 2020. doi:10.1056/nejmoa1906759. The strongest
  randomized counter-evidence: a real separation in delivered depth with no mortality, ventilator-free
  or ICU-free day benefit — the empirical reason to doubt that the *target* is the operative lever.
- **[K3]** Jackson DL, Proudfoot CW, Cann KF, Walsh TS. *The incidence of sub-optimal sedation in the
  ICU: a systematic review.* Crit Care 2009. doi:10.1186/cc8212. The closest inspected work on
  target-versus-achieved discordance: substantial sub-optimal sedation with a tendency to over-sedation,
  and explicit heterogeneity in how "optimal" is defined. It establishes prevalence; it does not link
  discordance to liberation, and it does not separate discordance from the target.

Supplementary citations discussed above and inspected as abstracts, deliberately **not** occupying a K
slot: Aitken et al. Thorax 2021 (doi:10.1136/thoraxjnl-2020-216098) on the RCT/cohort inconsistency;
Walsh et al. Lancet Respir Med 2016 (doi:10.1016/s2213-2600(16)30178-3, DESIST) on whether titration
quality is movable; Peeters et al. Clin Pharmacol Ther 2008 (doi:10.1038/sj.clpt.6100309) on
severity-driven propofol pharmacodynamics, which is the physiological content of the E1 arm and of the
sensitivity component in §5.5.
