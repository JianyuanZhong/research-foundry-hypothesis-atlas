# Episode 50: The 24-hour lactate trajectory has a small, imprecise modeled review increment

## Result, tested hypothesis, and strongest supported claim

This result-bearing child executes the frozen Episode 49 design without changing its 1,229-person/33-hospital population, norepinephrine-entry clock, 24-hour landmark, M0/M1 models, uniformly integrated 30%-50% threshold range, +1-per-100 materiality margin, copied-hospital refit bootstrap, classification hierarchy, outcomes, source bindings, or noncausal limits.

The unresolved hypothesis was:

> Among the same adults alive and still hospitalized at the 24-hour norepinephrine-entry landmark, adding continuous lactate trajectory to a model already containing the late lactate increases uniformly threshold-averaged net benefit for a modeled high-mortality-risk review decision by at least 1 net true-positive-equivalent review per 100 people across predicted mortality thresholds 0.30-0.50.

The experiment is **inconclusive**. The point estimate was +0.4482 net true-positive-equivalent reviews per 100, and the locked 2,000-replicate copied-hospital refit-bootstrap 95% percentile interval was **-0.4631 to +1.5798 per 100**. The lower bound is below +1, so material gain is not supported. The upper bound exceeds +1, so material gain is not ruled out. The interval includes zero, so the direction is also unresolved under refit site-clustered uncertainty. All computability gates and supportive guardrails passed; none rescues an interval that spans both zero and the materiality margin.

The strongest claim now supported is narrower: in this selected EICU cohort and under the locked models, retaining the earlier lactate produced a small positive point increment in threshold-averaged modeled net benefit, positive fixed-prediction deletion estimates for every hospital, and acceptable pooled calibration. The evidence does **not** support clinical utility, changed decisions, causal benefit, or the claim that one net review-equivalent per 100 is achieved.

## Clinical importance and advance over prior knowledge

The 24-hour clinician already has the late lactate. The clinically useful question is therefore incremental: does retaining its earlier value change high-risk prioritization enough to justify added information and workflow burden? The inspected full-text Baysan et al. article (2022, DOI 10.1097/CCE.0000000000000750; frozen XML source `[source checksum]`, file [source checksum]) found that adding 24-hour lactate to recalibrated APACHE IV increased the C-statistic from 0.62 to 0.64 and that admission plus 24-hour lactate had the best fit. That supports modest serial prognostic information, not trajectory's decision value beyond an already available late value in this phenotype.

The inspected Vickers et al. decision-curve reporting guide (PMCID PMC6261531; frozen HTML source `[source checksum]`, file [source checksum]) states that thresholds encode clinical harm-benefit tradeoffs, must be clinically reasonable, and require comparison with treat-all and treat-none. This run computes that potential decision value but cannot adjudicate whether 30%-50% reflects real review preferences or whether review benefits patients.

The substantive advance over Episode 49 is empirical resolution of its frozen experiment. It shows that the favorable +0.448 point estimate is not sufficiently precise to establish or exclude the +1 material target after complete model/preprocessing refits within hospital resamples. The refit interval is materially wider than Episode 49's explicitly nondefinitive fixed-prediction feasibility interval (-0.205,+1.057), confirming why already-fitted predictions could not substitute for the planned uncertainty analysis.

## Frozen population and temporal boundaries

The complete EICU snapshot was reconstructed without source sampling.

1. Map literal `> 89` and numeric ages above 89 to 90; retain age at least 18.
2. Define `t0` as the earliest `infusionDrug.infusionoffset` in [0,1440] whose case-folded `drugname` contains literal `norepinephrine` or `levophed`. This is a recorded infusion entry, not verified administration or dose.
3. Per `uniquepid`, retain the stay with lexicographically smallest `(t0,patientunitstayid)`.
4. Require `hospitaldischargestatus` in {Alive,Expired} and `hospitaldischargeoffset >= L24`, where `L24=t0+1440`.
5. Baseline `B`: case-insensitive lactate, strict case-insensitive system unit mmol/L, finite result [0.2,30], collection in [t0-360,t0], and revision by t0+60. Keep the greatest eligible revision at a collection; conflicting values at that revision invalidate it; identical ties use smallest `labid`. Select the latest valid collection and require B>=2.
6. Late `F`: the same analyte/unit/value/revision/conflict rules, collection in [t0+720,t0+1440], revision by L24, nearest t0+1080, then earlier collection, then smallest `labid`.
7. Preserve the outcome-blind legacy hospital gate of at least five `X=0` and five `X=1`, where `X=1[(B-F)/B<0.20]`. The implementation freezes the hospital list before materializing binary outcome `D`; X is not a model feature, exposure, estimand, subgroup, or rescue analysis.
8. After hospital selection, define D=1 for Expired and 0 for Alive.

The exact flow reproduced: 200,859 patient rows; 200,234 adult rows, including 7,081 mapped older-adult rows; 16,299 early-pressor stays; 14,786 selected people; 14,672 known outcomes; 13,023 hospitalized through L24; 5,077 valid baselines; 3,117 with B>=2; 1,506 measured people in 96 hospitals; and 1,229 retained people in 33 hospitals. The retained cohort had 507 deaths, 722 survivors, 31 mapped older adults, both outcomes in every hospital, and maximum site share 9.36%.

## Models, estimand, baselines, and uncertainty

M0 contains age and age squared; Female and Male indicators with Other/Unknown omitted; t0/1440 and its square; baseline collection lag `(t0-B_offset)/360`; late-measurement age `(L24-F_offset)/720`; and `ln(F)` and its square. M1 adds only `T=ln(F/B)` and `T²`.

Within each LOHO training fold, every continuous design column was winsorized at training 1st/99th percentiles and standardized using its training mean and population SD. Constants were applied unchanged to the held-out hospital. Both models used unweighted L2 logistic regression with intercept, C=1.0, lbfgs, max_iter=10000, tol=1e-8, no class weights, and model seed 20260924. No held-out outcome informed fitting, preprocessing, recalibration, or thresholds.

At each p in {0.30,0.31,...,0.50},

`NB_m(p)=TP_m(p)/N - FP_m(p)/N * p/(1-p)`.

The primary estimand is

`ΔINB = 100/(0.50-0.30) * ∫[0.30,0.50] (NB_M1(p)-NB_M0(p)) dp`,

using the trapezoid rule over all 21 locked points. Treat-none is zero; treat-all uses pooled prevalence; M0 is the primary comparator; the training-site prevalence predictor is a sanity baseline. Uniform threshold weighting remains an unvalidated working preference distribution.

Bootstrap seed 20260949 sampled 33 hospitals with replacement 2,000 times. Copies of an original hospital stayed together as one held-out group; all copies were excluded when that hospital was held out, training hospitals entered with sampled multiplicity, fold preprocessing and both models were refit, and held-out rows received the sampled hospital multiplicity. Weighted threshold TP/FP/NB, proper scores, discrimination, and calibration were retained. All 2,000 replicates converged and were finite.

## Frozen classification and interpretation

Every source, flow, sample-size, event, site-composition, fit, bootstrap, deletion, and 21-threshold computability gate passed. Every supportive guardrail passed: point M1 calibration was within bounds; M1 exceeded defaults at every threshold; no threshold had ΔNB below -0.5 per 100; and 100% of finite site deletions were positive.

The hierarchy therefore reaches its final branch: **inconclusive**. It is not supportive because the lower ΔINB bound is below +1. It is not adverse to materiality because the upper bound is above +1. It is not opposite because the upper bound is above zero. The empirical fraction of bootstrap draws above +1 or below zero is not interpreted as a posterior probability.

A supportive result would have justified external/prospective validation of a trajectory-assisted review tool, not treatment. An adverse result would have ruled out the +1 modeled increment for this locked setting while leaving smaller gains possible. An opposite result would have indicated worse threshold-averaged modeled decisions. This inconclusive result means additional independent data are required to resolve materiality; selecting favorable thresholds, using the old AUROC point estimate, or binarizing clearance cannot change the label.

An automatic verifier can check exact hashes/headers, joins, clocks, revision rules, one-person selection, outcome-blind hospital gating, exact flow, feature matrices, fold-only transformations, estimator settings, prediction uniqueness, every DCA cell, integration, copied-hospital multiplicities/refits, proper scores, calibration, intervals, deletions, gates, and label. No automatic computation can establish that the thresholds reflect clinician/patient preferences; that a true-positive-equivalent review is worth its burden; that an alert changes review, treatment, or outcomes; or that mortality is preventable. EICU cannot establish verified pressor administration/dose, arterial versus venous lactate, indication, confounding treatments, tissue-perfusion mechanism, limitations of treatment, exact death time/cause, what clinicians knew, or actions taken. Those require clinical, patient, operational, and ethics adjudication. External temporal/geographic validation is needed for transport, and a prospective impact study—ideally randomized if a trajectory-triggered action is proposed—is needed for changed-decision or outcome claims.
