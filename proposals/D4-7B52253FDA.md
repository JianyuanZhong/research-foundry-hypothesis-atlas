# Within-person metabolic deterioration and prospective cardiorenal multimorbidity

Parent: [prior hypothesis] (valid expert seed, [starting question])

## Unresolved clinical question

Do within-person changes in repeated metabolic and renal blood biomarkers add prospective information about the development of cardiorenal multimorbidity beyond the participant's current biomarker levels?

This matters because a patient with acceptable current values may still have a worsening trajectory, while a stable abnormal value may carry a different prognosis. If change adds robust information, repeat testing and trajectory-aware prevention could be justified for selected people. If it does not, current levels may be sufficient and repeated testing may add cost, noise and false alarms.

The expert seed states the question but does not establish field meanings, timing, disease definitions or feasibility. This candidate makes the estimand explicit and binds it to the local snapshot.

## Evidence-supported claims versus hypothesis

The local export contains paired instances of eight blood-biochemistry fields: cholesterol 30690, creatinine 30700, cystatin C 30720, glucose 30740, glycated haemoglobin 30750, HDL cholesterol 30760, LDL direct 30780 and triglycerides 30870. Public UK Biobank Showcase evidence retrieved on 2026-09-14 describes these as recruitment and repeat-assessment blood assays, primarily 2006-2010 and 2012-2013. The local catalog explicitly says field-instance arrays are not elapsed-time fields, so exact participant-level collection dates are unavailable here.

A recent open-access prospective UKB study (Xue et al., 2026, PMCID PMC13491610; locally acquired as source [source checksum]) reported that baseline atherogenic indices were associated with incident cardiometabolic multimorbidity in 346,868 participants and improved discrimination/reclassification over conventional factors. The strongest relevant supported claim is therefore that current cardiometabolic levels can stratify future multimorbidity. It does not establish that within-person change in this eight-marker panel adds information beyond the second/current measurement, nor that change is causal or clinically useful.

The falsifiable hypothesis is:

> Among UKB participants with both biomarker instances and no recorded pre-index endpoint domain, adding standardized within-person change from instance 0 to instance 1 to current instance-1 levels will improve prospective prediction of a first post-index two-domain cardiorenal/metabolic event, with direction and incremental effect reproducible in held-out data.

This is not a causal hypothesis and does not require every marker to change in the same direction.

## Experiment and model comparison

Use exactly the same cohort, index and outcomes for all alternatives.

M0, simple current-level baseline:

- Standardize instance-1 values using training-fold parameters.
- Fit age, sex and all eight current levels in a cause-specific Cox model for time to the primary endpoint, with death as censoring; also fit 5-/10-year logistic models for directly comparable held-out risk metrics.
- Report coefficients, hazard ratios and uncertainty.

M1, simple longitudinal baseline:

- Add eight changes, instance 1 minus instance 0, standardized within training folds.
- Pre-specify a joint Wald/likelihood-ratio test of the change block, not eight unconstrained discovery claims.
- Compare M1 with M0 using held-out time-dependent AUC, average precision, Brier score, calibration slope/intercept and decision-curve net benefit, with bootstrap confidence intervals.

M2, substantive learned alternative:

- Use the same current levels, changes, age and sex, plus pre-specified pairwise current/change interactions and missingness indicators if the cohort is expanded beyond complete cases.
- Fit one gradient-boosted risk model for each domain and horizon (5 and 10 years) inside patient-level 5-fold cross-validation; tune only within training folds.
- Report grouped out-of-fold importance for current levels versus change features and partial-dependence or accumulated-local-effect plots for the largest pre-specified trajectory patterns.
- M2 can reveal threshold effects and discordance, such as renal-marker deterioration with stable HbA1c, that M1's additive linear change coefficient cannot. It is scientifically useful only if patterns replicate across folds and domains, not merely if AUC is marginally larger.

The actual deliverable is the fitted M0/M1/M2 models, fold assignments, out-of-fold predictions, endpoint/population flow counts, uncertainty intervals, calibration and falsification outputs, and a machine-readable comparison table. Readiness is not completion and no conclusion may be reported without linked computed outputs.

## Falsification and interpretation

Supportive result: M1's change block has a reproducible positive incremental association and improves held-out calibration/discrimination for the primary endpoint, with similar direction across domain-specific outcomes; M2 identifies stable, clinically interpretable nonlinear or discordant patterns. This supports added prognostic information, not causation or a recommendation to change care.

Adverse result: the change block has a near-null or unstable held-out effect, fails calibration/decision-curve improvement, or only predicts pre-index disease in the negative temporal control. That falsifies the added-prognostic-information claim for this operational phenotype; current levels may be adequate for this use.

Inconclusive result: endpoint counts after complete-repeat and no-pre-index filters are too small, intervals are wide, performance varies across folds, or results change materially with missingness and endpoint definitions. This indicates the snapshot cannot resolve the question, not that deterioration is absent.

Hard falsification checks are: age/sex-stratified permutation of change vectors must remove any claimed increment; a pre-index diagnosis control must not be predicted by future change after current levels; all outcome dates must be strictly after the fixed index for incident analyses; and no outcome fields may enter exposure fitting.

Clinical adjudication and stronger claims require unavailable evidence: exact individual blood-draw dates, primary-care and self-report disease history, medication and treatment changes, serial clinical creatinine/urine-output data for AKI, assay quality metadata, adjudicated CKD/heart-failure status and external validation. This study cannot establish causality, treatment benefit, screening thresholds or transportability.
