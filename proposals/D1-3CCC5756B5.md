# Time-weighted hypotension burden versus MAP for early AKI in eICU: measurement-density and time-aware validation

## Scientific question

Among adult ICU patients with adequate systolic and diastolic blood-pressure measurements in the first 24 hours of ICU care, does a prespecified time-weighted hypotension burden measure improve prediction of acute kidney injury (AKI) within 48 hours beyond the 24-hour mean arterial pressure (MAP), after controlling for baseline illness severity, vasopressor exposure, baseline kidney function, hospital, and measurement density?

The unresolved claim is that duration and time-weighting of hypotension add clinically meaningful prognostic information beyond MAP. The strongest rival is that apparent burden is driven by recording density or measurement timing rather than hemodynamic injury. A null result would weaken the claim that a more complex burden measure is useful; a positive result would support further validation and prospective study.

## Why it matters

MAP is the common bedside summary and a natural baseline for hemodynamic risk. Minimum systolic blood pressure alone can overstate a single low reading, while MAP may understate sustained hypoperfusion. If time-weighted burden improves early AKI risk stratification, it may inform monitoring and resuscitation decisions. If it does not, clinicians and researchers should not add a more complex measure without evidence.

## Population and temporal design

Use eICU 2.0 adult ICU admissions with at least one systolic and one diastolic blood-pressure measurement in the first 24 hours of ICU care. Define ICU admission time from the patient-level admission timestamp. Exclude admissions with fewer than 10 blood-pressure measurements in the first 24 hours, because the burden estimand is not identifiable with sparse data. Exclude patients with missing baseline creatinine or with creatinine measured after the 24-hour exposure window.

The exposure window is the first 24 hours after ICU admission. The outcome window is the next 24 hours, so AKI is assessed from baseline creatinine through 48 hours after admission. This temporal separation prevents outcome leakage into the exposure.

## Variables and data bindings

Use the eICU-CRD 2.0 source files and tables as documented in the dataset README and data dictionary. The required bindings are:

- `patient.csv`: patient-level demographics, ICU admission timestamp, hospital identifier, and baseline comorbidities.
- `vital.csv`: systolic and diastolic blood-pressure measurements with timestamps, joined to patient by `patient_id`.
- `lab.csv`: creatinine measurements with timestamps, joined to patient by `patient_id`.
- `infusion.csv`: vasopressor infusion records with timestamps and rates, joined to patient by `patient_id`.
- `hospital.csv`: hospital-level identifiers and characteristics, joined to patient by `hospital_id`.

The exact column names and archive members must be verified against the eICU-CRD 2.0 data dictionary before execution. The join keys are `patient_id` for patient-level tables and `hospital_id` for hospital-level tables. The time fields are the ICU admission timestamp for the exposure window and the measurement timestamps for vitals, labs, and infusions.

## Estimands and methods

The primary estimand is the 24-hour time-weighted hypotension burden, defined as the fraction of the first 24 hours during which the MAP is below 65 mmHg. The MAP is calculated from systolic and diastolic blood-pressure measurements as (systolic + 2*diastolic)/3. The burden is calculated by linear interpolation between measurements, with a prespecified maximum interpolation gap of 4 hours.

The baseline is the 24-hour mean arterial pressure. The time-aware alternative is a model that uses the full sequence of MAP measurements in the first 24 hours, with time since admission as a feature, rather than a single summary measure. The time-aware model is a gradient-boosted tree with prespecified hyperparameters, trained on the same population and outcome as the simple burden model.

The primary comparison is the burden measure versus the MAP baseline, adjusted for baseline illness severity, vasopressor exposure, baseline kidney function, and hospital. The secondary comparison is the burden measure versus the time-aware model, to test whether the simple burden measure adds information beyond a model that uses the full sequence of measurements.

## Outcome and analysis

The outcome is AKI within 48 hours, defined as a creatinine increase of at least 0.3 mg/dL from baseline or a creatinine increase of at least 2 times baseline. The baseline creatinine is the first creatinine measurement in the first 24 hours. The outcome creatinine is the first creatinine measurement in the next 24 hours.

The primary analysis is a logistic regression model with the burden measure, MAP, and covariates. The burden measure is modeled as a continuous variable, with a prespecified nonlinear term to capture potential threshold effects. The covariates are baseline illness severity, vasopressor exposure, baseline kidney function, and hospital. The model is evaluated using the area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC) and the Brier score, with 95% confidence intervals calculated using bootstrap resampling of patients within hospitals.

The secondary analysis is a comparison of the burden measure versus the time-aware model, using the same evaluation metrics. The time-aware model is evaluated using the same bootstrap resampling, with the same covariates and outcome.

## Hospital transportability

The primary analysis is stratified by hospital, with hospital as a fixed effect. The secondary analysis is a hospital-level meta-analysis, with the burden measure effect estimated separately in each hospital and pooled using a random-effects model. The hospital-level estimates are reported with 95% confidence intervals, and the heterogeneity is quantified using the I-squared statistic.

## Falsification criteria

The burden explanation is falsified if the burden measure does not improve prediction of AKI beyond the MAP baseline, after adjusting for measurement density and hospital. Specifically, the burden measure is falsified if the 95% confidence interval for the AUC improvement includes zero, or if the burden measure effect is not consistent across hospitals.

The burden explanation is also falsified if the burden measure is not stable under a sensitivity analysis that excludes patients with fewer than 20 blood-pressure measurements in the first 24 hours. This sensitivity analysis tests whether the burden measure is driven by patients with sparse data.

## Supportive, adverse, and inconclusive results

A supportive result is one in which the burden measure improves prediction of AKI beyond the MAP baseline, with a 95% confidence interval that excludes zero, and the improvement is consistent across hospitals. A supportive result would support further validation and prospective study.

An adverse result is one in which the burden measure does not improve prediction of AKI beyond the MAP baseline, or the improvement is not consistent across hospitals. An adverse result would weaken the claim that a more complex burden measure is useful.

An inconclusive result is one in which the 95% confidence interval for the AUC improvement includes zero, or the burden measure effect is not consistent across hospitals. An inconclusive result would require further validation and prospective study.
