# Episode 90 successor: does intercept recalibration transfer across repeat-lactate practice?

## Unresolved question and clinical importance

The latest valid parent asks whether an intercept-only recalibration, learned with historical labels from other hospitals in the target practice, can repair a transported 0.10 seven-day mortality threshold while preserving the fixed-capacity queue ranking. That is a useful remedy, but it leaves a deployment-critical question unanswered: does the remedy require outcome labels from the *same repeat-lactate practice*, or can calibration learned in one practice environment be used in the other?

The strongest currently supported claim is narrower. In the audited EICU snapshot, a repeat lactate is available in some hospitals and not others, and a source-trained model can be evaluated with hospital-held-out queue and threshold metrics. The parent provides a computable local-label recalibration procedure, but no result has established that either unmodified or recalibrated probabilities transfer across practice strata. The inspected literature supports the importance of longitudinal observation-process representation and external validation, but does not establish this EICU threshold or this recalibration remedy. I inspected the full Baysan and Sisk source records used in the lineage; I do not claim to have read the unavailable cancer article or its full STAR Methods, for which only the supplement and metadata are available.

The clinical decision is whether a receiving ICU needs local outcome labels before using a transported probability threshold, or whether a recalibration learned in a different testing-practice environment is adequate for an interim screen. A positive result would justify only a stronger validation plan for probability display; it would not establish that review occurs, that lactate results are visible, that deaths are preventable, or that care improves.

## Primary falsifiable hypothesis

Let low and high practice strata be the frozen, outcome-blind hospital strata defined by the parent from repeat-lactate availability. For each directed transfer d in {low→high, high→low}, fit R_full using source-stratum hospitals only. On the opposite target stratum, compare:

- p_source: unmodified source-trained probability;
- p_local: leave-one-target-hospital-out intercept recalibration, using D7 labels from other target hospitals;
- p_cross: an intercept recalibration learned from all source-stratum labels and applied to the target stratum.

The primary hypothesis is that cross-practice recalibration is transportable: in both directions, p_cross reduces the worst absolute observed-minus-predicted D7 error across the prespecified repeat-availability × 0.10-threshold-side cells by at least 0.02 versus p_source and reaches q<=0.05, without changing R_full queue membership. The primary estimand is the worst-direction paired improvement

I_cross,* = min_d [q_source(d)-q_cross(d)]

and q_cross,* = max_d q_cross(d), where q is the maximum absolute cell error. Support requires the joint two-sided 95% lower bound of I_cross,* >=0.02 and upper bound of q_cross,* <=0.05, every original target cell has at least 30 people, and at least 1,900 of 2,000 joint copied-hospital replicates are finite.

The local-label procedure is a prespecified positive control for what can be achieved when receiving-practice historical labels are available. Cross-practice recalibration must be compared with it, not allowed to replace it. A secondary remedy-gap estimand is q_cross,*−q_local,*; a positive gap means cross-practice calibration is inferior even if it improves over the unmodified score. Exact intercept shifts preserve the within-hospital R_full ordering, so the queue-invariance hypothesis is that all seven equal-capacity queue memberships and D7/Dinf counts are identical between p_source, p_cross and p_local, up to deterministic numerical ties.

## Falsification and interpretation

Not-computable overrides all labels for source hash/header/schema mismatch, cohort/outcome mismatch, changed clocks/features/strata/capacities, target-label leakage into p_cross, invalid calibration roots, nonfinite fits, cell n<30, fewer than 1,900 finite joint replicates, wrong queue cardinality or missing artifacts.

Supportive cross-practice calibration requires both directions, both primary gates, cell/deletion checks, and exact queue invariance. It supports only that a source-stratum-label intercept shift produced the specified historical EICU threshold-calibration pattern. It does not validate the clinical meaning of a probability, the repeat-lactate process, deployment without local labels, or patient benefit.

Adverse evidence is valid computation with lower95(I_cross,*)<0 and upper95(q_cross,*)>0.05, or a remedy gap whose lower95 bound is >0.02, showing that cross-practice calibration is materially worse than local calibration. If I_cross improves but q_cross remains >0.05, classify as inconclusive for a usable threshold and report improvement separately. If q_source is already <=0.05, no calibration remedy is needed; this does not count as support for cross-practice transport. A rank-supportive/threshold-adverse result permits only a rank-only research screen; threshold support with queue failure cannot justify a review queue. Adverse or inconclusive results do not refute lactate prognosis.

Automatic verification can check source fidelity, cohort flow, leakage, calibration arithmetic, uncertainty, cell gates, queue invariance and conclusion-to-output linkage. It cannot adjudicate whether norepinephrine was administered, whether lactate was ordered or visible, whether review was feasible, whether deaths were preventable, or whether recalibration is fair, safe, causal or beneficial. Those require adjudicated medication/specimen/result-visibility/workflow data, stakeholder-defined utility and capacity, independent temporal/external validation, subgroup review and a prospective impact study or randomized evaluation. HCC, MIMIC and UKB remain read-only and are not pooled as external validation because no validated harmonization exists for this revision-aware EICU estimand.
