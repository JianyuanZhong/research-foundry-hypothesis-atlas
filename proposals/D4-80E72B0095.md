# Does cystatin C identify a creatinine-masked AKI-surveillance group?

## Scientific question and unresolved claim

The strongest directly relevant evidence I inspected is the locally available critical-illness study at `references/expert-seeds/papers/kidney-function/article.readable.txt`. In 38 mechanically ventilated adults it compared creatinine, cystatin C, iohexol clearance and rectus-femoris ultrasound, and reported that creatinine-based eGFR could overestimate dysfunction as muscle was lost. This establishes biological plausibility in a selected ICU sample; it does not establish prognostic value in community adults, the meaning of low grip in UK Biobank, or a surveillance benefit.

The falsifiable unresolved claim is: among UK Biobank adults without a recorded renal event at biomarker assessment, a positive creatinine-eGFR minus cystatin-C-eGFR discordance (D), particularly with lower grip strength, predicts a clinically material excess of a first subsequent hospital-coded N17 event beyond routine creatinine-based information. The decision relevance is whether this pattern is sufficient to justify repeat kidney testing or closer surveillance. The experiment estimates prognostic association and incremental information only. It does not test cystatin-C ordering, treatment changes, causal mediation, or patient benefit.

## Exposure and outcome construction

Use the 2021 CKD-EPI equations without race. The unit gate is explicit: the exported field definitions must confirm creatinine field 30700 is in micromoles/L and cystatin C field 30720 is in mg/L before fitting. Convert creatinine to mg/dL as `Scr = 30700/88.4`; use `Scys = 30720` mg/L. If the field units cannot be verified from the frozen local metadata/source definition, stop at a labeled inconclusive unit audit rather than infer units from distributions.

For sex female, set k=0.7 and a=-0.241; for male, k=0.9 and a=-0.302:
`eGFRcr = 142 * min(Scr/k,1)^a * max(Scr/k,1)^(-1.200) * 0.9938^Age * (1.012 if female else 1)`.
`eGFRcys = 133 * min(Scys/0.8,1)^(-0.499) * max(Scys/0.8,1)^(-1.328) * 0.996^Age * (0.932 if female else 1)`.
Define `D=eGFRcr-eGFRcys`; positive D is the hypothesized creatinine-reassuring direction. Standardize D using training-participant mean and SD only. Define primary grip as the maximum of valid left/right values, retaining weaker-side and sex-standardized grip as prespecified sensitivities. BMI and weight are adjustment/sensitivity variables, not proxies for measured muscle mass.

The primary endpoint is the first post-index hospital record beginning N17, using `41270` and its same-suffix `41280` date and requiring date strictly after index. Use the first post-index N18 code as a secondary coded CKD outcome. Use primary inpatient `41271`/`41281` as an outcome ascertainment sensitivity. Treat the earliest post-index `40000` death date as a competing event and separately estimate mortality. Do not call N17 severe AKI, persistent AKI, or adjudicated CKD: the local data lack KDIGO stage, urine output, inpatient laboratory trajectories and adjudication.

## Estimands and analysis

The primary estimand is the five-year cause-specific AKI hazard and competing-risk cumulative-incidence contrast between prespecified low, median and high grip profiles per training-set SD increase in D, including the D-by-grip interaction. Report absolute five-year risk differences with bootstrap 95% intervals. Define a clinically material contrast before fitting as at least 0.5 percentage points absolute five-year cumulative-incidence difference between the low- and median-grip profiles at the 90th versus 10th training-set percentile of D; this threshold is a decision-oriented information threshold, not a claim that surveillance benefit has been established.

Fit nested transparent cause-specific Cox models, with death censored for the cause-specific hazard and retained as a competing event in Aalen--Johansen cumulative incidence:

- M0: age, sex, BMI, weight, primary grip, and creatinine-derived eGFRcr;
- M1: M0 plus cystatin-C-derived eGFRcys (equivalently report D, but do not include algebraically redundant eGFRcr, eGFRcys and D together);
- M2: M1 plus D-by-grip interaction and prespecified restricted cubic splines for age and eGFRcr.

Use participant-level SHA-256(`eid`) buckets 0--79 for a deterministic 60/20/20 train/tuning/test split; reserve 80--99 as an audit-only bucket and do not call it external validation. Estimate standardization, spline knots, missingness rules, thresholds and any tuning only in training data. Use participant bootstrap within split for uncertainty. Report held-out five-year Brier score, calibration intercept and slope, risk-classification/threshold contrasts, hazard ratios and cumulative incidence; use Fine--Gray only as a sensitivity analysis.

The substantive learned alternative is a CPU gradient-boosted discrete-time survival model using exactly the M0--M2 inputs, endpoint, split and five-year target. Use shallow trees and early stopping selected on the tuning set. It tests whether nonlinear thresholds or D-by-grip heterogeneity missed by the transparent model change calibrated risk stratification; a small C-index gain alone is not evidence of clinical value. Report held-out Brier/calibration, the same threshold contrasts, and accumulated-local-effect curves with participant-bootstrap intervals. Expected post-extraction budget is <=4 GB RAM and <=20 CPU minutes; GPU is unnecessary. The baseline comparison is therefore scientific—incremental cystatin-C information and heterogeneity—not a contest for model complexity.

The previously deferred joint latent measurement-error/competing-risk repeat-landmark model could distinguish persistent discordance from visit noise and regression to the mean, but instance numbers are not elapsed time, exact repeat dates are not locally available, and the prior audit exceeded the job RSS limit. Revisit only if the primary experiment demonstrates adequate repeat-field support, event support and convergence with a chunked implementation or newly verified dates. A Delphi-like disease-sequence model is deferred because the question is biomarker increment rather than disease-order generation. A Bayesian measured-GFR model is deferred because no measured-GFR anchor exists.

## Falsification and interpretation

Supportive evidence requires all of the following: the prespecified positive D-by-low-grip contrast reaches the 0.5 percentage-point decision threshold with uncertainty excluding a clinically negligible or reversed effect; the direction persists under primary-inpatient and broad renal-history sensitivities; and M1/M2 improve held-out calibration/Brier and threshold risk contrasts over M0, with concordant learned-model curves. This supports incremental prognostic association in this selected UKB cohort only.

Adverse evidence is a null or reversed interaction, intervals compatible with effects below the decision threshold, loss of direction under outcome or renal-history sensitivity, no held-out calibration improvement, or worsened calibration after cystatin C. That argues against using this pattern to prioritize surveillance in this setting; it does not show that cystatin C lacks biological value.

Inconclusive evidence includes inability to verify units, sparse N17 events, severe exposure missingness, invalid or unpaired dates, unstable split/bootstrap estimates, failed model convergence, or intervals spanning both the negligible and material thresholds. Do not replace an inconclusive N17 analysis with an easier outcome.

Computation can verify source hashes, one-to-one joins, raw-value audits, unit gates, CKD-EPI calculations, paired code/date ordering, risk sets, split integrity, model fitting, calibration, Brier scores, bootstrap intervals and whether the interpretation label follows the prespecified gates. It cannot establish true AKI severity, measured GFR, causality, treatment benefit, transportability, or that surveillance improves outcomes. Those claims require adjudicated AKI and laboratory trajectories, body-composition/measured-GFR anchors, treatment data, expert review and independent or prospective validation.
