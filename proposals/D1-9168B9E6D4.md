# Opportunity-separated detected AKI after fixed first-day monitored invasive-MAP burden

## Status, fixed question, and actual deliverable

This substantive child of `[prior hypothesis]` preserves the audited one-person index, invasive-MAP exposure in `[0,1440)`, hour-24 landmark, duration-normalized monitored MAP<65 burden, nadir comparator, hospital split, and strictly post-hour-24 pair-timed creatinine question. It resolves the leading remaining vulnerability by changing the primary analysis set from “people later tested” to the entire fixed 10,998-person opportunity cohort and by making testing, live ICU discharge, ICU death, and detected AKI separate observable processes.

No post-hour-24 creatinine result was inspected, no AKI label was constructed, and no MAP–AKI association was opened during this evolution. The future solver must newly reproduce the inherited exposure audit, construct exact post-landmark test/result/exit histories, fit the transparent marked-process baseline and ordered alternative, evaluate held hospitals and uncertainty, and produce a claim ledger. Completion is correct estimation and interpretation under the frozen gates; confirmation is not required.

The primary estimand is explicitly **detected**, not latent, AKI: the day-7 cumulative incidence of a pair-timed creatinine-defined AKI first recorded after hour 24 and before index-ICU exit, with live ICU discharge and ICU death as competing events. An untested person has “no detected AKI before exit/day 7,” not “no AKI.”

## Scientific opening, supported claim, and unresolved hypothesis

The strongest literature-supported claim remains limited. ICU hypotension definitions and analyses are highly heterogeneous; most studies report hypotension–AKI associations, but pooled AKI evidence was not confirmatory [K1]. FINNAKI associated lower first-day time-adjusted MAP with five-day AKI progression in a severe-sepsis cohort with prospectively validated dense hemodynamics and creatinine plus urine-output ascertainment [K2]. KDIGO supplies the timed creatinine and urine-output criteria, while warning that creatinine-only, incompletely observed implementations can miss or misclassify AKI and that monitoring frequency follows clinical risk and judgment [K3].

The executed evidence now additionally supports only an observation claim. In the fixed 10,998-person cohort, recorded testing and live discharge vary strongly with MAP observation geometry; adding monitored burden to a landmark model did not materially improve held-hospital opportunity prediction. This does not make testing ignorable and says nothing about post-landmark creatinine outcomes.

The unresolved hypothesis is:

> Among the fixed one-person eICU opportunity cohort alive and in the index ICU at hour 24, greater 24-hour-equivalent monitored invasive-MAP<65 burden is associated with greater seven-day cumulative incidence of pair-timed **detected** creatinine AKI beyond a flexible MAP-nadir model, and a positive contrast remains in the positive-creatinine-mark component when measured testing intensity, live discharge, and death opportunity are held to a common modeled distribution.

The leading explanation is that sustained low pressure carries subsequent renal-risk information lost by a single nadir. The strongest rival is selective ascertainment: severity, baseline renal trajectory, arterial-monitoring geometry, treatment intensity, and hospital practice keep some patients observable and trigger more creatinine draws, while early live discharge suppresses detection and death truncates it. The rival predicts that burden may improve test/retention hazards or a later-tested complete-case model but not the probability that an observed test is a positive pair-timed AKI mark. The leading explanation predicts a positive mark-component contrast after modeled opportunity is equalized. Both can coexist; neither adjusted persistence nor a mark association identifies renal hypoperfusion.

The advance over the closest inspected work is not another hypotension association. It is a falsifiable separation of an observable detected-AKI incidence from the processes that create detection opportunity, using an all-cohort competing-event target rather than silently coding untested people as non-events or conditioning the primary analysis on future testing. A positive result would justify carrying cumulative monitored hypotension, not only nadir, into prospective risk studies. A precise adverse or opportunity-only result would argue against that promotion. No result selects a MAP treatment target.

## Executed outcome-blind opportunity diagnostic

The protocol in `pre-outcome-opportunity-protocol.md` was frozen before execution. Job `[research job]` used 12 CPUs, 64 GiB, no GPU, and 51.46 seconds; output SHA-256 is `[source checksum]`. It selected only post-landmark creatinine row identity, units, and timestamps—never `labresult` or `labresulttext`.

The diagnostic reproduced 10,998 people across 165 hospitals and 5,428/3,057/2,513 train/validation/test people. By 48 hours after the landmark, the test partition had 2,024 first recorded tests, 456 live ICU discharges before a test, 21 ICU deaths before a test, and 12 with none of those events; 80.6% had a recorded test by day 7.

Raw 48-hour testing rose 7.44 points and live discharge before testing fell 8.18 points from the lowest to highest burden quartile. More importantly, testing fell 11.22 points and live discharge before testing rose 10.84 points across logical-width quartiles, reproducing the inherited geometry signal. In a multinomial held-hospital check, adding burden and frozen burden×nadir/burden×width terms changed log loss from 0.479299 to 0.479188; gain was 0.000111 (95% hospital-bootstrap interval −0.001891 to 0.002323). Standardized p75-minus-p25 burden changes were +1.55 points for testing, −1.62 points for live discharge, +0.16 point for death, and −0.10 point for no event. Thus the adjusted 5-point material gate was not met, but the raw >10-point geometry gate was.

Interpretation: burden itself did not materially improve this coarse opportunity model, but future-test conditioning still selects on a strongly structured severity/geometry/retention process. The diagnostic is feasibility and observation evidence only.

## Pre-landmark renal gate and exact detected-AKI event

Use rows with normalized `labname='creatinine'`, system/interface units compatible with mg/dL, and numeric value 0.1–20 mg/dL; median-collapse values at the same stay/offset and report unit conflicts.

Admission creatinine is the earliest valid value in `[-360,360]`. Require a valid value in `(-360,1440]`. Exclude progression through and including minute 1,440: any value ≥admission+0.3 mg/dL or ≥1.5×admission. Minute 1,440 is pre-landmark.

Follow every otherwise eligible person from minute 1,440 until the earliest of detected AKI, live index-ICU discharge, index-ICU death, or minute 10,080. Only values in `(1440,min(10080,unitdischargeoffset)]` can be marks. Let `t_last` and `c_last` be the last valid pre-landmark test/time and `t_adm`, `c_adm` the admission pair. A test is a positive AKI mark if:

- `c(t)-c_last >=0.3 mg/dL` and `0<t-t_last<=2880`; or
- `c(t)>=1.5*c_adm` and `0<t-t_adm<=10080`.

The earliest positive mark is detected AKI. At a tie with unit exit, process labs first because the window includes `unitdischargeoffset`. Normalize `unitdischargestatus='expired'` as ICU death; every other nonmissing status is live discharge, with missing/unknown status reported and analyzed both ways. A general all-post-landmark-pairs 48-hour criterion is sensitivity-only.

This is not complete KDIGO AKI: urine-output AKI, timed RRT, pre-illness baseline, events after unit discharge, and etiology are unavailable. Unit death is a competing event for detection, not independent censoring.

## Primary estimand and smallest rival-separating comparison

The population estimand is the standardized day-7 cumulative-incidence risk difference and risk ratio for detected AKI under M set to training p75 versus p25 within 5-mmHg nadir strata and empirical common support, averaging other measured covariates over the opportunity cohort. This is a model-standardized descriptive contrast, not an intervention effect.

The smallest comparison that separates the leading rival factorizes the observed process into:

1. recurrent analyzable-creatinine test intensity while alive and in the index ICU;
2. the probability that a test carries a positive pair-timed AKI mark;
3. live-discharge hazard; and
4. ICU-death hazard.

From the fitted joint process report:

- **full contrast**: p75 versus p25 M in all four components;
- **mark-only diagnostic contrast**: p75 versus p25 M in the positive-mark component while test/live-discharge/death components use M=p50;
- **opportunity-only diagnostic contrast**: p75 versus p25 M in test/live-discharge/death components while the positive-mark component uses M=p50.

These component interventions are model diagnostics and need not add algebraically. They are not causal mediation effects. A full positive contrast with no positive mark-only contrast, alongside material test/retention dependence, supports the ascertainment rival. A positive mark-only contrast shows incremental information among observed test occasions after measured opportunity standardization, but still cannot establish latent AKI in untested time.

## Transparent baseline and ordered/selection-aware alternative

All models use the identical one-person cohort, clocks, endpoint construction, hospital split, static covariates, exact event history, train-only preprocessing, and hospital-cluster bootstrap.

**B0 transparent marked-process baseline.** Use prespecified six-hour piecewise baseline hazards. Fit a penalized recurrent-test intensity model, a penalized logistic positive-mark model at each analyzable test, and cause-specific live-discharge and ICU-death models. Split counting-process intervals at exact test, exit, and six-hour boundaries. Dynamic observation covariates are prior test count and time since last analyzable test; they are updated only from history available at each time. Static inputs are a restricted cubic spline of nadir; age, sex, ethnicity, source, ICU type, weight; admission/last pre-landmark creatinine, change, slope, count and timing; MAP coverage, boundary/internal gaps, logical width, gap-after-low and artifact features; day-1 pressor entry summaries; and non-BP/non-creatinine APACHE physiology. No future test count, discharge, death, or post-landmark result enters a landmark predictor.

**B1 transparent burden model.** Add a restricted cubic spline of M and frozen M×nadir, M×logical-width, and M×gap-after-low interactions to every process component. No feature search is permitted. Numerically integrate the fitted process to obtain individual detected-AKI/test/live-discharge/death cumulative incidences. Compare B1 with B0 on held hospitals using day-7 detected-AKI log score and Brier score, calibration-in-the-large/slope, component likelihoods, the full/mark/opportunity contrasts, and 1,000 full-pipeline hospital bootstraps.

**T1 ordered selection-aware alternative.** Use the same process likelihood, population, targets, static inputs, and raw day-1 channels as B1, but retain the 24 ordered hourly low fractions, hourly nadirs, observation masks/time-since-observation, gap starts/re-entry, and pressor-entry masks through six cubic functional basis coefficients per channel with a second-difference penalty selected only on validation hospitals. Shared low-rank coefficients may borrow information across test, mark, discharge, and death components; transition-specific heads remain separately reported. T1 reveals whether late persistence, fragmentation, recovery, or care-response timing predicts a positive mark differently from testing or retention—information B1's scalar M loses.

T1 must be compared with B1 by paired held-hospital detected-AKI log score/Brier score, mark log loss, calibration, and frozen ablations: permute hour order while preserving M/nadir; remove MAP observation-mask channels; and remove pressor channels. T1 is supported only if it improves held-test detected-AKI score with a 95% hospital-bootstrap interval excluding zero, retains calibration, and nonconstant ordered information survives ablation. A temporal signal confined to test/exit heads supports the rival rather than renal-risk information.

The inherited later-tested B0/B1 logistic model is retained as a **diagnostic comparator**, never the primary estimand. A complete-case-only positive result that disappears in the all-cohort marked process is adverse evidence.

## IPCW, multistate, and pattern-mixture estimand audit

The multistate/marked-process analysis is primary because it targets fully observable detected events and treats exits as competing events. It does not identify biological AKI under standardized testing.

Stabilized inverse probability of any-test weighting and inverse-intensity-of-visit weighting are sensitivities only. Their estimand is an assumption-dependent pseudo-population of observation while alive/in ICU. They require correct models, positivity, and conditional/sequential missing-at-random assumptions that eICU cannot verify. Report 1st/99th-percentile truncation, weight distribution, effective sample size, and balance. Never censor death as though future creatinine remained observable, and never claim weighting recovers AKI after discharge.

Pattern-mixture sensitivity is also secondary. For untested or incompletely tested intervals, vary latent-AKI odds relative to modeled observed-mark odds over `delta={0.5,0.75,1,1.5,2,4}`, separately for live-discharge and death patterns, and report the first delta that reverses the sign. Delta is not estimated from eICU. These curves bound dependence on explicit MNAR assumptions; they do not repair the detected-AKI estimand or establish latent incidence.

A hypothetical “AKI by day 7 if everyone survived, stayed in ICU, and was tested on schedule” is not identified here. It requires scheduled surveillance or linked post-discharge labs, reliable death timing under an appropriate estimand, and expert causal review.

## Frozen readiness and falsification rules

Before opening held-test outcomes, reproduce the index/exposure audit, the 10,998-person split, all clock boundaries, and recorded-to-analyzable test flow. Require at least 100 detected-AKI events in each partition, at least 20 held-test hospitals with events, and empirical p25/p75 common support within the frozen nadir strata. Otherwise the target is inconclusive and the branch stops without a held-test claim.

Support for incremental **detected-AKI prognostic information beyond measured opportunity** requires all of:

1. B1 full day-7 p75-versus-p25 detected-AKI risk difference >0 with a 95% hospital-bootstrap interval excluding zero.
2. B1 improves held-test detected-AKI log score over B0 with a paired 95% interval excluding zero and no material calibration deterioration.
3. The B1 mark-only diagnostic contrast is >0 with its 95% interval excluding zero, and T1 agrees in direction; improvement confined to test/live-discharge/death components does not qualify.
4. Direction persists under D1 exposure, ≥90%-coverage/boundary-supported restriction, fixed 48-hour test window, all-pairs outcome sensitivity, within-hospital/equal-hospital weighting, triplet-artifact exclusion, high-leverage-hospital removal, and valid inverse-intensity/IPCW checks.
5. No reversal occurs across logical-width, sampling-density, gap-after-low, or pressor-adjacency strata.

Adverse evidence is any of:

- the upper 95% bound for the B1 full risk difference is ≤+1 absolute percentage point together with no held-test detected-event score gain;
- a stable reverse full and mark-only association;
- a positive later-tested or full contrast but a mark-only upper 95% bound ≤0 while opportunity-only dependence is positive/material; or
- a positive signal that is confined to T1 test/exit heads and is removed by observation-mask or hospital controls.

These findings argue against incremental monitored-burden information under this design; they do not show hypotension is harmless.

Inconclusive evidence includes intervals spanning both zero and +1 point; insufficient common support/events; failed calibration; extreme weights or effective sample size <50% of the eligible cohort; B1/T1 sign disagreement; instability to D1, logical width, fixed window, or hospitals; a mark-only interval crossing zero despite a positive full contrast; or sign reversal within `delta=0.75–1.5` pattern-mixture scenarios. An imprecise null does not refute the hypothesis.

Even fully supportive results establish only transportable conditional prediction of observed positive creatinine marks under the recorded testing/exit process. They do not establish causal hypoperfusion, treatment benefit, optimal MAP, true line presence, complete KDIGO AKI, or AKI etiology.
