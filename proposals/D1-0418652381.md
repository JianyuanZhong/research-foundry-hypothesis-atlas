# Does residual instability modify the observed association between nighttime ICU discharge and early ICU return?

## Scientific deliverable and clinical advance

The future solver must construct an auditable eICU index-discharge ledger and estimate whether residual physiologic instability modifies the observed association between nighttime ICU discharge and a documented early return to ICU-level care. Required outputs are: frozen cohort flow and exact source audit; hospital-held-out interaction estimate with clustered uncertainty; separate cumulative incidence of ICU return, recorded hospital death and alive hospital discharge; matched transparent and time-ordered state/trajectory fits on identical inputs; calibration/transport; and prespecified falsification analyses. Discovery fitted no clinical model and asserts no result.

The advance is effect modification, not a generic prediction score: it asks whether any nighttime signal is concentrated among patients leaving with unstable or non-recovering physiology. A positive interaction would identify a subgroup for prospective discharge-readiness or enhanced-handoff research; a null interaction would argue against treating nighttime as a simple instability marker. Neither result establishes a discharge effect.

## Evidence and unresolved claim

The local snapshot contains 200,859 ICU-unit rows, ICU-relative discharge offsets and clock fields, Alive/Expired/blank unit-discharge status, hospital and health-system identifiers, repeated unit rows, and timestamped physiology. A bounded read-only probe found 189,918 Alive exits, 10,907 Expired exits, 34 blank statuses, 132,454 discharges under the exact 19:00–06:59 rule, and 34,504 multi-unit health-system sequences. These are audit counts, not eligible-cohort or outcome estimates.

The expert seed is explicitly untested. Public searching found prior literature on after-hours discharge, ICU readmission and residual organ dysfunction, including an eICU-based 48-hour readmission/death prediction study, but those results are contextual only. An attempted acquisition of that paper returned HTTP 404; no unavailable full text is claimed as inspected.

The strongest supported claim is only that this snapshot can support an observational same-health-system ICU-return analysis with pre-discharge physiology. It cannot support a general post-hospital ED or acute-care-return claim.

Primary falsifiable hypothesis:

Among adults discharged alive from an ICU to an observed in-hospital acute-care destination, the observed night-versus-day difference in documented ICU return within 48 hours is larger when residual physiologic instability is present at discharge than when it is absent.

Define the interaction:
Delta = [CIF(return | night, high) - CIF(return | day, high)] -
        [CIF(return | night, low) - CIF(return | day, low)].
Standardize to the development-hospital eligible-discharge distribution and adjust using the matched baseline. A clinically material result is |Delta| >= 3 percentage points, a two-sided 95% hospital-cluster interval excluding zero, the same direction in both methods, and no held-out transport failure. This is a decision-scale criterion, not a treatment-benefit claim.

## Population and time

Unit of analysis is patientunitstayid. Do not concatenate ICU stays. Choose the first eligible alive ICU-to-acute-care discharge per patienthealthsystemstayid. Cluster inference by uniquepid; keep connected hospital components together in a split sensitivity.

Exclude death, Other ICU, ICU, Other ICU (CABG), home, external, skilled nursing, rehabilitation, operating room, blank and unresolved Other destinations.

Night is exactly 19:00:00 through 06:59:59 from unitdischargetime24; day is 07:00:00 through 18:59:59. Sensitivities use 20:00–06:59 and 18:00–07:59. No staffing, local-time/DST, weekend, holiday or bed-capacity field is available.

Let U = unitdischargeoffset. Features use [U-360,U), raw event offsets, 30-minute bins anchored at U, no interpolation in primary analysis, and a one-hour gap-bridging sensitivity. Values at or after U are excluded.

## Outcome and competing exits

There is no explicit ED-return, ward-readmission, claims or post-hospital encounter table. The primary endpoint is therefore a documented ICU-unit return in the same health-system stay, not broad acute-care return.

For each patient row, reconstruct hospital-relative time using hospitaladmitoffset:
unit discharge absolute time = -hospitaladmitoffset + unitdischargeoffset;
hospital discharge absolute time = -hospitaladmitoffset + hospitaldischargeoffset.
Order later rows with the same patienthealthsystemstayid by unitvisitnumber and reconstructed admission time. Return is the first subsequent ICU-unit admission strictly after index discharge and no later than index discharge + 2,880 minutes. Record same-time/near-zero transitions separately; primary analysis requires a positive gap, with a >360-minute sensitivity. This endpoint does not establish unplannedness or deterioration.

Competing events are hospital death before return and alive hospital discharge before return. Hospital discharge before 48 hours without return is not a negative return. Blank or inconsistent hospital-discharge status is unknown-status censoring, with a location-based sensitivity; never recode it as alive. Malformed timelines are excluded and counted. Secondary horizons are 1,440 and 7,200 minutes where observable.

## Residual instability

Primary sources are vitalPeriodic fields systemicmean, heartrate, respiration, sao2 and temperature, and the separate vitalAperiodic field noninvasivemean. Per 30-minute bin use medians of valid values and preserve counts, coverage and missingness. Predeclare plausible ranges: MAP 20–200, heart rate 20–250, respiration 3–80, SaO2 50–100 and temperature 25–45.

A domain is abnormal in a bin if MAP <65, heart rate <50 or >=110, respiration <8 or >=24, SaO2 <92, or temperature <36 or >=38. High instability means at least two abnormal domains in at least two bins, or one abnormal domain in at least four bins, subject to the coverage gate. Sensitivities use continuous abnormal-domain fraction, last-bin-only state, MAP <60/<70 and SaO2 <90/<94. These are recorded-physiology proxies, not adjudicated organ dysfunction.

From infusionDrug, report named norepinephrine, epinephrine, vasopressin, phenylephrine or dopamine rows in the final six hours as a process sensitivity. The table lacks validated administration/start, indication, intent, dose equivalence and complete capture; do not call it active vasopressor support.

## Matched baseline

In development hospitals, match night to day exact on instability stratum, destination and unit type; coarsen age, admission source and ICU duration; nearest-match on pre-discharge domain summaries, slopes, coverage and missingness within a frozen caliper. Report overlap and unmatched observations.

Fit a regularized additive cause-specific pooled-logistic model in 30-minute intervals with night, instability, their interaction and prespecified covariates, with separate hazards for ICU return, hospital death and alive hospital discharge. Estimate standardized CIFs and Delta with 500 hospital-cluster bootstrap replicates and a uniquepid-clustered sensitivity. This transparent baseline answers the primary interaction but compresses the last six hours into summaries.

## Substantive mechanistic alternative

Fit, in development hospitals only, a three-state hidden semi-Markov physiology model on the identical twelve-bin sequence: stable, transient/recovering instability and persistent instability. Emissions are the same five domains plus missingness. Fit without exposure, outcomes, hospital identity or post-discharge rows. Carry posterior final-state probabilities, transitions, time in unstable states and coverage into the same competing-risk model with night-by-state interactions.

This alternative tests whether modification is tied to persistence or failed recovery rather than an abnormal summary, information the baseline loses. Agreement supports a robust recorded-process interaction; a larger state-only interaction indicates trajectory sensitivity and needs validation; divergence means no clinically actionable conclusion. A neural sequence model is deferred unless both methods leave reproducible held-out trajectory miscalibration and the state model demonstrates non-Markovian structure; this is a scientific, not hardware, deferral.

## Split, uncertainty and interpretation

Hash hospitalid deterministically into 80% development and 20% held-out hospitals before summaries, matching, fitting, imputation or threshold selection. Keep uniquepid-connected hospital components together in a sensitivity. Apply frozen parameters to held-out hospitals without refitting or local recalibration. Require at least 20 development hospitals with >=100 eligible discharges and >=10 returns each, and 10 held-out hospitals with outcome support for a transport claim; otherwise transport is inconclusive. Do not rank hospitals.

Support requires Delta >=3 percentage points, interval excluding zero, same direction in both models, preservation under alternate night rule, >360-minute return gap, separate MAP-channel analysis and acceptable held-out transport. Adverse evidence is null/reversal, interval compatible with zero, disappearance after matching/coverage adjustment, dependence on one channel, or disappearance after near-zero transfers are removed. Inconclusive evidence is insufficient returns, poor overlap, missingness, unsupported hospitals, unstable state fitting, disagreement or failed transport.

The study cannot establish that nighttime discharge causes return, instability causes return, discharge should be delayed, or staffing/bed pressure explains the pattern. Clinical adjudication, ward monitoring and handoff data, discharge intent, readmission reason, prospective review and external validation are required.

## Alternatives and compute

A generic boosted tree/transformer is deferred because score gain would not identify persistence, recovery or missingness as the modifier. A causal discharge, staffing or bed-capacity model is unavailable. A broad post-hospital acute-care-return analysis is unavailable because no encounter table exists.

Measured discovery work is catalog/schema/header inspection, the read-only probe and its counts. The probe used 4 CPU, 16 GiB and 104 seconds while reading all available aperiodic rows and a bounded periodic sample; it fitted no model. Future solver planning is 8–16 CPU, 64–256 GiB and 2–8 hours, unverified; CPU is appropriate for this low-dimensional tabular/state analysis and GPU is optional only after profiling. The solver must report the ledger, fitted interaction contrasts, event counts, uncertainty, calibration, transport and falsifications; none are known yet.
