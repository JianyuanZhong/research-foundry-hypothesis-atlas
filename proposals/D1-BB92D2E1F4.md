# Provisional hypothesis: discordant AKI recovery after an ICU landmark

Status: provisional draft for registration; no computed results are claimed.

## Proposed relationship

Among adult MIMIC-IV ICU patients with an acute kidney injury episode and a creatinine measurement at or near a prespecified 48-hour landmark, patients whose creatinine has improved but whose urine output remains low, or whose cumulative fluid balance remains positive, will have higher subsequent in-hospital death or persistent/worsening kidney dysfunction than patients with concordant creatinine and urine-output recovery. The comparison will adjust for pre-landmark illness severity and treatment.

The clinically useful claim is narrower than “creatinine is wrong.” Discordance may represent delayed clearance, ongoing tubular injury, fluid overload, or differences in measurement and monitoring. The experiment tests whether the discordant phenotype adds prognostic information beyond a creatinine-only recovery phenotype.

## Population and temporal design

Use MIMIC-IV only. Candidate population: adults with an ICU stay, an AKI episode defined from pre-landmark creatinine and urine-output measurements, and a creatinine measurement at or near 48 hours after the prespecified landmark. Exclude stays without adequate pre-landmark measurements or with outcomes determined before the landmark. The exact AKI rule, landmark definition, and inclusion/exclusion flow must be frozen before analysis.

Primary follow-up begins at the 48-hour landmark and ends at in-hospital death, discharge, or a prespecified kidney-dysfunction endpoint. No post-landmark measurements may enter baseline covariates or phenotype assignment.

## Variables and outcomes

Candidate predictors:
- Creatinine trajectory and absolute value before and at the landmark, with units and item ID verified from MIMIC-IV lab metadata.
- Urine-output trajectory and cumulative fluid balance before and at the landmark, using the available ICU output and input event tables and their documented item/units.
- Pre-landmark illness severity, organ support, vasopressors, renal replacement therapy, and relevant comorbidity/treatment variables.

Primary outcomes:
- In-hospital death after the landmark.
- Persistent or worsening kidney dysfunction after the landmark, defined prospectively from creatinine and/or urine output using a prespecified rule.

Secondary outcomes may include ICU-free days, escalation of renal support, and length of stay, but they are not substitutes for the primary estimand.

## Baseline and substantive alternative

Simple baseline: a prespecified creatinine-only recovery phenotype (for example, improvement versus non-improvement) with a Cox model or logistic regression for the primary outcome, adjusted for baseline severity and treatment.

Substantive alternative: a time-aware model using the pre-landmark sequence of creatinine, urine output, fluid balance, and organ-support measurements, with a leakage-safe split at the landmark and uncertainty estimated by bootstrap or repeated resampling. The alternative can reveal whether discordance is informative beyond the current creatinine level and whether the ordering of recovery matters; the baseline loses trajectory, fluid-state, and measurement-timing information.

The alternative is not justified by predictive gain alone. It is scientifically useful if it changes the interpretation of discordance versus a single creatinine recovery marker. If the sequence model cannot be fitted within the solver envelope, the baseline remains the minimum computable test and the alternative is deferred, not silently replaced.

## Analysis and falsification

Compare the two phenotype groups with prespecified adjusted risk differences/hazard ratios and 95% confidence intervals. Report calibration and discrimination only as secondary descriptive checks. Use a held-out or temporally separated validation split if feasible; otherwise use internal resampling and label it exploratory.

Supportive result: the discordant phenotype has a reproducible, clinically meaningful excess risk beyond the creatinine-only baseline, with uncertainty excluding a negligible effect and robustness to monitoring-intensity adjustment.

Adverse result: no excess risk, or the association disappears after adjustment for severity, fluid balance, measurement frequency, or renal support. This would weaken the proposed recovery interpretation and favor a severity/monitoring explanation.

Inconclusive result: sparse events, unstable urine-output coverage, wide intervals, or non-identifiable item units. This would motivate a narrower measurement-validation study rather than a clinical conclusion.

Falsification checks: repeat the analysis using a negative-control outcome/time window, permute phenotype labels within matched severity strata, and compare results when urine-output ascertainment is restricted to similarly monitored patients. These checks do not prove causality.
