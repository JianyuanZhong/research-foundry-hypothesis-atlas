# HCC episode 4 proposal: early post-ICU deterioration after TACE

## Scientific opening and unresolved question

The strongest claim supported by the available HCC data is descriptive: the local snapshot contains dated procedure, laboratory, examination, medication, encounter, and diagnosis records that can be joined by patient and encounter identifiers. It does not establish that any particular patient has cirrhosis, that a TACE procedure caused deterioration, or that a clinical model would improve care.

The unresolved hypothesis is:

> Among adults with a documented TACE procedure, the presence of a pre-procedure laboratory or examination signal associated with later deterioration is more informative than the procedure itself for identifying patients who require urgent post-procedure care; this information is not merely a proxy for current severity or selection into the TACE cohort.

The clinically consequential question is whether an early, data-available signal can change the decision to alert a hepatology/oncology team for urgent review after TACE. This is a risk-stratification hypothesis, not a claim that TACE should be withheld or altered by a model.

The leading rival explanation is that apparent post-procedure deterioration is caused by current illness severity, prior comorbidity, or selection into TACE, rather than a reproducible pre-procedure signal. The observable difference is whether the signal remains predictive after conditioning on pre-procedure severity and baseline characteristics and whether it is concentrated in patients who were not already selected for TACE. A supportive result would support a prospective alerting study, not a causal treatment conclusion.

## Population, time zero, and outcome

The primary cohort is adults (age >=18, from `encounters.年龄`) with at least one procedure row whose `手术` contains “TACE” and whose `开始时间` is present. A patient has one eligible procedure per participant, defined as the earliest TACE with a valid start time; duplicate or conflicting procedure records are retained as separate rows but the participant-level analysis uses the first valid row. The primary time zero is the procedure start time.

The primary outcome is a composite of a documented deterioration event within 72 hours after TACE, defined as any of:

1. A procedure row with `手术` indicating resection, biopsy, angiography, or another intervention after time zero, with `结束时间` present; or
2. A laboratory row with `定量结果` present and `检验时间` after time zero, including abnormal results for pre-specified clinically relevant assays; or
3. A procedure row with `开始时间` in the 24–72 hour window.

The primary analysis uses the first post-time-zero event and excludes patients with a post-time-zero event before 24 hours to avoid capturing immediate postoperative care. A secondary outcome is a 7-day composite using the same definitions.

For the exploratory alerting question, the outcome is “urgent care contact,” defined as a new encounter with `入院时间` after time zero and within 72 hours, or a procedure with `开始时间` in that window. This is a proxy for urgent care and is not equivalent to hospitalization or death.

## Variables and temporal boundaries

Baseline covariates are restricted to information available by time zero:

- demographics and sex from `encounters`;
- age and recorded height/weight, with units unverified;
- pre-TACE laboratory values and trends, with each lab row kept as a separate timestamped observation and no cross-assay pooling;
- pre-TACE examination findings and diagnosis text, using only observations whose `开始时间` is <= time zero;
- medication exposure and duration, using `medications.开始时间` and `结束时间`, restricted to medication rows ending by time zero;
- diagnoses and diagnosis types from `diagnoses`, joined only on `患者主索引, 就诊号`, with no temporal ordering;
- hospital department and encounter identifiers, retained as structured covariates.

A pre-procedure laboratory signal is a prespecified rule, not a post hoc search: at least one pre-TACE measurement of bilirubin, albumin, INR/PT, or platelet count is present, with a value outside the institution’s documented normal range or a documented downward trend. The exact assay names and normal ranges are to be frozen before analysis from the `labs.检验` field; unavailable units mean the model cannot pool assays and may not be interpreted as a standardized biomarker.

An examination signal is a prespecified rule based on `examinations.开始时间 <= time zero` and `检查` or `检查诊断` containing terms for cirrhosis, portal hypertension, ascites, varices, or hepatocellular carcinoma, using a frozen lexical rule. Narrative text is not treated as a validated diagnosis.

The primary scientific comparison is the incremental predictive information of the pre-procedure signal after adjustment for baseline severity and TACE selection. A secondary comparison is whether the signal is associated with the outcome after conditioning on current illness severity, measured by the first post-time-zero laboratory and examination observations. This can distinguish a genuine pre-procedure signal from a marker of current severity, but cannot identify causality.

## Analysis and falsification

The primary analysis is a time-dependent Cox proportional hazards model (or a prespecified equivalent) with time zero at TACE, the pre-procedure signal as the predictor, and baseline covariates as adjustment variables. The model is evaluated on participant-level splits: 80% of participants for training and 20% held out, with all encounters and all measurements from a participant retained in one split. The held-out split is used for calibration, discrimination, and decision-curve evaluation.

The primary estimand is the incremental change in net benefit for an alerting threshold of 5% risk, comparing the model with the signal to a baseline model without it. Net benefit is computed from the held-out prediction, the observed outcome, and the fixed threshold. A secondary analysis reports calibration-in-the-large, Brier score, time-dependent C-index, and 95% bootstrap confidence intervals for the incremental net benefit.

Falsification criteria are:

- The signal is not predictive after adjustment for baseline severity and selection: the adjusted incremental net benefit is not distinguishable from zero.
- The signal is concentrated in patients already selected for TACE and disappears when restricted to patients without a TACE indication: this supports the selection/rival explanation.
- The signal is not reproducible in the held-out participant split: this supports instability or overfitting.
- The signal is absent or reversed when the outcome is restricted to the 72-hour window, or when the definition of the signal is changed within the prespecified range: this indicates sensitivity to temporal or measurement assumptions.
- The signal is not associated with the outcome after conditioning on current post-time-zero severity: this supports the current-severity rival explanation.

Supportive results would show a positive incremental net benefit in the held-out split, stable across the prespecified sensitivity analyses, and a clinically interpretable alerting threshold. They would justify only a prospective alerting study. Adverse results would show no incremental information, selection into TACE, or dependence on current severity, and would argue against using this signal for a broad alert system. Inconclusive results would include insufficient event counts, inadequate assay coverage, missing units, poor calibration, or wide confidence intervals; these findings would require more data or expert adjudication, not a positive clinical conclusion.

## Method comparison and compute

The simple baseline is a transparent time-dependent Cox model using only demographics, baseline laboratory values, baseline examination findings, medications, diagnosis type, and TACE procedure information. It is computationally cheap and interpretable.

The substantive alternative is a time-aware machine-learning model that retains the same baseline covariates and adds timestamped pre-TACE laboratory and examination observations, with participant-level temporal splitting. It can reveal non-linear interactions, dose-response-like trends, and temporal ordering that the Cox baseline loses. It is not used to claim that a neural network is necessary; the alternative is justified only if it improves the clinically relevant alerting estimand, not a small predictive gain.

The baseline is expected to fit on CPU in minutes to hours using the declared 16-CPU, 262,144-MiB planning envelope. The alternative is estimated at approximately 4–8 CPU hours for this snapshot, including feature construction, fitting, and 500 bootstrap repetitions. These are planning estimates, not measured runtime. GPU use is not required; the configured discovery budget is 3,600 science seconds and the solver planning limit is 28,800 seconds with 0 GPUs. No GPU probe is needed because the proposed tabular models are CPU-appropriate.

## Deliverable and evidence limits

The future solver must newly fit the baseline and the time-aware alternative on the bound HCC tables, produce participant-level held-out predictions and uncertainty estimates, estimate incremental net benefit, and report supportive, adverse, and inconclusive interpretations according to the criteria above. Completion is established by the fitted models, held-out outputs, and uncertainty reports—not by readiness or a readiness check.

The strongest claim the available evidence supports is that dated HCC structured data can be joined and temporally ordered. The unresolved claim is whether a pre-procedure signal adds clinically useful information beyond current severity and TACE selection. The experiment cannot establish that TACE caused deterioration, that a signal should change treatment, or that the model improves patient outcomes. Clinical adjudication is required to validate assay units, diagnosis terms, the definition of deterioration, and the clinical utility of the alert threshold. External validation is unavailable because the reserved partition is inaccessible and the snapshot is a single institution.
