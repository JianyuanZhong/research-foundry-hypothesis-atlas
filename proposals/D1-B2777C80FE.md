## Unresolved question and clinical importance

In adults who remain recorded as invasively ventilated at ICU hour 48, had recent but no longer active vasopressor support, have substantial recorded positive fluid balance, and satisfy the frozen perfusion, urine, potassium, renal-replacement, ECMO, and loop-washout gates, is a furosemide/bumetanide input record first initiated in the next 12 hours (A12) associated with a higher probability of being observed in a MIMIC non-ICU location at day 7, with definite death counted as failure, without exceeding the prespecified renal, RRT, death, terminal-disposition, or unfavorable-disposition harm margins?

This matters because active fluid removal may help de-escalate intensive care, while excessive removal or treatment of an unrecognized unstable patient could be harmful. A day-7 endpoint that treats HOME, rehabilitation, skilled nursing, hospice, LTAC, and acute-hospital transfer as one favorable state cannot establish that clinical ordering. The available data can observe some in-system topology, but not functional recovery, appropriateness of destination, post-discharge survival, or ICU care outside MIMIC.

The strongest available evidence supports only that the frozen MIMIC-IV 3.1 snapshot contains the required admission, transfer, ICU-stay, medication-record, and death-date fields; the inherited treatment-blind audit found substantial heterogeneous discharge composition and identifiable open ICU/non-ICU locations; and the inherited source audit supports interpreting a Paused input row as recorded delivery on its half-open `[starttime,endtime)` interval. Those observations do not establish medication receipt, benefit, safety, or causality. The research-ambition README was inspected: the three demonstrations are references rather than a topic prescription; the cancer main article and STAR Methods remain unavailable, and no unavailable paper text is used here.

The falsifiable hypothesis is:

> In the repaired retrospective population, the assignment-compatible A12-minus-B12 lower partial-identification contrast for strict D7-RINO is greater than +0.05 (one-sided 95% subject-bootstrap lower confidence limit), while all frozen harm families, temporal/measurement gates, exposure-semantics checks, endpoint-component checks, and topology sensitivities pass.

A supportive result would justify external replication or prospective randomization of a narrowly defined de-resuscitation strategy. It would not justify treatment. The substantive advance is that the primary outcome is now an observed topology state, not a destination utility: only an actually observed non-ICU location at D7 can be primary success; definite death is primary failure; leaving the observable MIMIC system is not silently rewarded.
