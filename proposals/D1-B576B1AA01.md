# Does interhospital-transfer provenance remain a seven-day receiving-hospital mortality marker after hour-24 trajectory, observation, and disposition are represented?

## Status, scientific opening, and frozen hypothesis

This is a substantive endpoint child of `[prior hypothesis]`. It preserves the adult first-stay hour-24 landmark cohort, concordant interhospital-transfer/direct definitions, same-receiving-hospital overlap restriction, two-percentage-point clinical margin, and noncausal residual-risk-marker interpretation. It replaces first incident organ support or 72-hour death because the strict support endpoint failed its event/validity gate: only 75 transferred composite events were found, with 0 strict transferred IMV and 4 strict transferred RRT events.

Kudlow et al. found no established adjusted 30-day mortality excess in a pathway-specific severe-AKI transfer cohort and retained residual-confounding limits [K1]. Rush et al. found similar hospital mortality but longer hospital stay in matched transferred ventilated-sepsis patients, making disposition opportunity a consequential rival rather than a nuisance [K2]. Patel et al. found that transfer-day organ dysfunction, high arrival oxygen requirement, and pre-transfer arrest predicted early mortality in transferred patients, showing why receiving-state adjustment matters but not whether transfer provenance adds information [K3].

The strongest evidence-supported claim is therefore limited: transfer cohorts differ in selection, arrival severity, observation, and disposition; published mortality findings are context-specific; and the eICU transfer cohort has measured same-hospital overlap. Neither these works nor the bounded probe establishes an eICU transfer–mortality association.

The unresolved hypothesis is:

**Among adults on their first health-system ICU stay, alive and still in the indexed receiving ICU at hour 24, and inside frozen same-hospital common support, concordant interhospital-transfer provenance is associated with at least a 2-percentage-point higher standardized cumulative incidence of in-hospital death during the next seven days than concordant direct admission, after the same measured arrival/hour-0–24 trajectory is represented, with live hospital discharge and live interhospital discharge treated as competing events.**

The primary estimand is the overlap-restricted transferred-population standardized risk-marker contrast at minute 11,520: the mean predicted death cumulative incidence with the provenance indicator encoded transferred minus the mean with only that indicator encoded direct, holding measured receiving-ICU history and hospital fixed. This conditional standardization is descriptive model reliance, not a transfer intervention or causal effect.

Clinical importance is receiving-ICU risk calibration. Robust residual information would justify retaining provenance in early prognostic assessment and prioritizing collection of referral/transport variables. Precise attenuation below two points would argue against an extra provenance penalty after measured trajectory. Either result can change a consequential triage-modeling decision; neither determines whether transfer was appropriate or harmful.

## Outcome choice and bounded event-support result

The primary hard event is `patient.hospitaldischargestatus='Expired'` with `hospitaldischargeoffset` in `(1440,11520]`, seven days after the hour-24 landmark. It avoids reconstructed incident-support labels and retains deaths after indexed-ICU exit. Live receiving-hospital discharge by the same horizon competes and is split into `hospitaldischargelocation='Other Hospital'` versus all other live destinations. A separate intermediate transition records alive indexed-ICU exit using `unitdischargeoffset/status/location`; it is not treated as survival because ward or later-ICU death remains observable in the repeated hospital fields.

A completed bounded diagnostic inspected only cohort, terminal-status, clock, and event-support fields—no adjusted transfer association or trajectory-outcome relation. In the frozen 19-hospital population before final overlap:

- 1,606 transferred and 14,937 direct stays;
- 135 transferred deaths across 17 hospitals and 961 direct deaths by day 7;
- 755 transferred and 8,755 direct live hospital discharges by day 7;
- 44 transferred and 241 direct live discharges to another hospital by day 7;
- 44/135 transferred deaths and 301/961 direct deaths occurred after alive indexed-ICU exit;
- five transferred and 87 direct terminal events by day 7 had missing hospital status; all hospital discharge offsets were present, and every recorded expired status had location `Death`.

Thus death clears a frozen >=120 transferred-event and >=15-hospital gate and is materially harder than indexed-ICU death. Missing terminal type is an explicit competing state, not silently called alive. The diagnostic establishes endpoint support and internal clock consistency only.

## Competing-event and observation rival

Death is observed only within the receiving hospitalization. Live discharge, especially transfer to another hospital, removes later mortality observation. The multi-state analysis starts in indexed ICU and represents:

1. indexed ICU alive;
2. alive after indexed-ICU exit but still in the receiving hospitalization;
3. live receiving-hospital discharge to another hospital;
4. other live receiving-hospital discharge;
5. receiving-hospital death;
6. terminal discharge with unknown status.

Transitions use unit and hospital discharge offsets; terminal events take precedence on equal offsets. An alive unit exit is intermediate, so the 44 transferred post-ICU deaths remain deaths. Report Aalen–Johansen cumulative incidence for every terminal state and death decomposed before/after alive ICU exit. Never treat live transfer-out as ordinary censoring in the primary analysis.

The leading explanation is residual illness/referral information absent from the receiving record. The strongest rivals are:

- **measured-severity/order rival:** apparent provenance risk attenuates when asynchronous hour-0–24 recovery/worsening is represented;
- **surveillance rival:** transferred patients have different chart density, entry delay, and source mix, causing a model to use observation behavior as severity;
- **disposition rival:** direct and transferred patients leave the ICU/hospital at different rates, changing time under receiving-hospital observation;
- **selection rival:** healthy-enough-to-transfer and referral/bed-selection processes remain unavailable and can generate either direction.

Differing prediction: a true residual marker should remain >=2 points under both transparent and admissible ordered models and under opportunity/disposition checks. A surveillance artifact should shrink after mask-process calibration/downsampling; a disposition artifact should change materially when transfer-out, other live discharge, and post-ICU death are separated. Selection cannot be resolved with eICU; robust association still does not identify a mechanism.

The smallest decision-changing check is the frozen endpoint/overlap gate followed by matched B0/M1 fitting. Continue if event support and overlap pass. Revise if death is supported but unknown/transfer-out bounds dominate. Abandon the eICU residual-marker claim if both valid models precisely exclude two points. Do not reopen sparse support labels because mortality is unfavorable or null.

## Same-hospital support and empirical anchor

Cross-fit transfer propensity using hospital, demographics, diagnosis, unit type, admission timing/weight, treatment limits, APACHE day-one physiology/support/comorbidities, the common six-bin summaries, and observation counts. Within each hospital retain transferred rows inside the direct 1st–99th propensity range and frozen robust calipers on age, diagnosis family, landmark ventilation, APACHE physiology, and family-level observation counts. Use capped ATT weights for a descriptive Aalen–Johansen anchor.

Required gates: >=15 hospitals; >=1,200 transferred; >=120 transferred deaths from >=15 hospitals; >=80% transferred retained; direct weighted ESS >=50% transferred N; every prespecified weighted absolute SMD <0.10; no hospital supplies >25% of transferred information. The prior outcome-blind diagnostic found 89.0% retained, direct ESS 6,597, and maximum SMD 0.055; the solver must repeat this with final trajectory variables.

The anchor is an overlap-weighted, within-hospital death cumulative-incidence difference with competing live destinations. It is not exchangeability evidence and does not replace multivariable models.

## Matched transparent and ordered alternatives

B0 and M1 use identical stays, predictor families, six bins, masks, elapsed times, event/entry-delay summaries, labels, folds, overlap set, terminal states, and uncertainty resamples. They differ in representation, not data access.

### B0: transparent multi-state baseline

Fit elastic-net multinomial pooled-logistic transition models in 12-hour post-landmark intervals. From the indexed-ICU-alive state predict death, alive indexed-ICU exit, live hospital discharge destination, unknown terminal status, or remaining; after alive ICU exit predict death, live discharge destination, unknown status, or remaining. Convert hazards to seven-day Aalen–Johansen cumulative incidence.

For every raw family compress the identical six bins to first-six-hour and 24-hour worst/last, OLS slope, observed-bin count, unique observed hours, longest gap, source count, and entry-delay median/p95. Include hospital fixed effects, provenance, and only prespecified provenance interactions with broad admission category and landmark ventilation. Fit paired models with and without provenance. Report coefficients, domain ablations, held-out multinomial log loss, death Brier, calibration intercept/slope, cumulative incidence, paired marker contrast, and decision curves over 5–20% death risk.

B0 shows which physiology or process domain attenuates provenance. It loses asynchronous ordering and can make identical extrema/slopes from clinically different deterioration and recovery paths.

### M1: ordered observation-aware alternative

Fit a compact hierarchical GRU-D/state-space model with 16–32 latent dimensions over the identical six four-hour bins. Inputs are values, masks, time since last, source count, and event-to-entry delay. Auxiliary heads predict the next-bin values and masks; a multi-state transition head predicts exactly B0's terminal/intermediate states over the same 12-hour intervals. Hospital effects are regularized. Provenance enters the final transition heads only, never the latent trajectory update. Fit three seeds.

M1 can reveal whether ordered recovery/worsening and informative chart timing explain the marker—information B0 summaries discard. It cannot reconstruct sending-hospital physiology or prove a transfer mechanism.

M1 is admissible only if held-out death Brier is not worse than B0 by >0.005, calibration-slope absolute error is not worse by >0.10, overall and exposure-specific next-mask calibration error is <=0.05, all three seed contrasts vary <1 point, and source-removal/downsampling results are stable. B0 governs if M1 fails; model shopping is prohibited.

### Observation-opportunity falsification

Using training folds only, estimate each family's probability of observation in a four-hour bin from prior-bin measured history, hospital, and static severity—never future values or outcomes. Report calibration by provenance and documentation quartile. Repeat both models after bidirectional within-hospital downsampling to the shared family/bin observation distribution and after excluding the sparsest documentation quartile. Post-landmark observation is never adjusted as a baseline confounder.

Observation falsification passes only if mask calibration error <=0.05 overall and by provenance, held-out missing-window and entry-delay distributions differ by <=0.10 standardized units after standardization, and each model's marker contrast moves <1 point and does not cross both 0 and 2.

## Fixed gates, uncertainty, and falsification

Use 2,000 nested resamples of hospitals and people, retaining complete folds, for 95% intervals; report leave-one-hospital-out results and between-hospital heterogeneity. The actual scientific deliverable is newly fitted B0/M1 transition models, held-out calibration/utility, standardized death contrasts, all competing-state cumulative incidences, opportunity-standardized contrasts, and a gate-governed interpretation.

Endpoint validity additionally requires <=2% unknown terminal status by day 7 in each group, absolute group difference <=1 point, and a worst-case analysis assigning unknown terminal states to death versus live discharge that shifts the marker contrast <1 point and does not cross both 0 and 2. For live interhospital discharge, report bounds treating downstream seven-day death as 0 versus 1; because post-transfer death is unavailable, crossing both boundaries makes the result inconclusive, not negative.

**Supportive:** every event, overlap, calibration, observation, site, and disposition gate passes; B0 and admissible M1 each have a marker RD >=2 points with 95% lower bound >0; their point estimates differ <1 point; the empirical anchor is directionally concordant; and provenance improves held-out integrated death Brier by >=0.001 or decision net benefit in the 5–20% range without calibration harm. This supports only a receiving-ICU residual risk marker at the prespecified clinical margin.

**Adverse:** all gates pass and both models' 95% upper bounds are below 2 points. If M1 attenuates B0 by >=50% and has upper bound <2 while remaining admissible, ordered measured trajectory/observation is favored over provenance for the fixed clinical margin. This does not establish equivalence, transfer benefit, or absence of causal harm.

**Inconclusive:** either interval includes 2; B0 and admissible M1 disagree; unknown/transfer-out bounds cross both 0 and 2; observation standardization changes an RD >=1 point or across both boundaries; any overlap/event/calibration/site gate fails; or leave-one-hospital-out direction reverses in >20% of supported hospitals. An imprecise null does not refute the marker.

Mandatory sensitivities: 72-hour death; 14-day death (support only, not alternate primary); death before/after alive ICU exit; live transfer-out separate versus all live discharge; unknown-status bounds; exclude pre-landmark DNR/no-escalation; exclude deaths within six hours after landmark; landmarks 18 and 30 hours; source-family removal; event versus entry/revision availability; duplicate collapse; cross-hospital repeat-person exclusion; leave-one-hospital-out. No favorable sensitivity replaces the primary.

## Verification, clinical limits, resources, and deferred alternatives

Automatically checkable: exact cohort/joins/clocks, terminal-state precedence, event counts, folds, no leakage, overlap/balance, B0/M1 fitting, calibration, mask prediction, cumulative incidence, standardized contrasts, resamples, sensitivities, and whether the submitted conclusion follows the frozen gates. The verifier must accept well-supported adverse or inconclusive results and reject a positive causal interpretation.

Clinical/external adjudication remains essential for transfer appropriateness, referral eligibility, treatment intent, and whether an apparent residual marker reflects unavailable sending-hospital/transport illness. eICU lacks sending physiology, transfer indication/request/acceptance, transport events, bed constraints, and outcomes after live transfer to another hospital. Causal effects, preventability, downstream post-transfer mortality, and implementation policy require linked data, expert review, and another study.

The endpoint probe took 4.9 seconds on 4 CPUs/16 GiB. Estimated future extraction/B0 workload is 8–16 CPUs, 64–96 GiB, 1–3 hours; 2,000 nested resamples require ~16 CPUs for 2–4 hours. M1 is estimated at one allocated A100-80GB plus 8 CPUs/64 GiB for 1–3 hours per outer fold; these future estimates are unverified. GPU use is selected for repeated sequence fits, not as scientific merit.

Alternatives not selected: 72-hour death has only 50 transferred events; support/death composite failed label support; ICU death discards 44 transferred post-ICU deaths; death-or-transfer-out conflates adverse outcome with disposition; hospital-free days mix mortality and discharge preference; causal emulation lacks eligibility, time zero, and confounders; a large transformer adds capacity before ordered information is shown useful. Revisit support only after clinical label adjudication, and causal questions only with linked sending/transport data.
