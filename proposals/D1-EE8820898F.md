# Scientific Proposal: Hyperoxia Exposure Duration Predicts ICU Outcomes Beyond Traditional Risk Scores

## Hypothesis

**Primary Hypothesis**: After adjusting for traditional ICU risk scores (APACHE II, SOFA), the duration of hyperoxia exposure (PaO₂ > 80 mmHg or SpO₂ > 92% for ≥8 hours) in the first 72 hours post-admission is an independent predictor of mortality and prolonged ventilation (>48 hours).

**Secondary Hypotheses**:
- H2: Patients in the highest quartile of hyperoxia duration have worse outcomes than the lowest quartile, even after controlling for baseline severity.
- H3: The association between hyperoxia duration and outcomes is independent of baseline organ dysfunction and admission severity.

## Unresolved Question

Current critical care practice uses standardized risk stratification tools (APACHE II, SOFA, NEWS) to predict ICU outcomes based on admission vitals, comorbidities, and organ dysfunction severity at admission. However, these scores do not capture **post-admission exposure to hyperoxia**, which may have independent prognostic value and potentially confound recovery trajectories.

Specifically, this study addresses whether:
1. Prolonged hyperoxia exposure after admission is an independent predictor of mortality beyond traditional risk scores
2. Hyperoxia duration provides incremental predictive value for identifying high-risk patients
3. Hyperoxia exposure duration could inform real-time oxygenation target optimization

## Clinical Importance

This question has direct clinical importance because:

1. **Current practice limitation**: Standard risk scores (APACHE II, SOFA) do not incorporate post-admission oxygenation patterns, potentially misclassifying patients who develop prolonged hyperoxia exposure despite normal admission scores

2. **Oxygen toxicity concerns**: Prolonged hyperoxia is associated with oxidative stress, ventilator-induced lung injury (VILI), and potential organ dysfunction. The prognostic implications of hyperoxia duration are not well-quantified in clinical practice

3. **Ventilation strategy optimization**: Identifying patients at high risk due to hyperoxia exposure could inform more conservative oxygenation targets and lung-protective ventilation strategies

4. **Quality metrics**: Hyperoxia duration may serve as an important metric for evaluating ICU team performance in oxygenation management

5. **Risk re-stratification**: Patients with normal admission scores but prolonged hyperoxia exposure may be at higher risk than scores suggest

## Substantive Advance Over Existing Knowledge

This proposal advances beyond existing work by:

1. **Temporal dynamics**: Moving from admission snapshots to post-admission exposure patterns (first 72 hours)

2. **Quantitative exposure**: Using duration as a continuous predictor rather than binary exposure (hyperoxia vs normoxia)

3. **Independence testing**: Demonstrating whether hyperoxia adds value beyond traditional scores (APACHE II, SOFA)

4. **Clinical translation**: Providing actionable metrics for oxygenation management and risk re-stratification

5. **Mechanistic insight**: Suggesting pathways (oxidative stress, VILI) through which hyperoxia affects outcomes

## Data Bindings

**Dataset**: EICU-CRD v2.0

**Required Tables** (verified in EICU dataset catalog):
- `vitalAperiodic` - PaO₂ measurements with timestamps (paO2, timestamp, patientunitstayid)
- `vitalPeriodic` - SpO₂ measurements with timestamps (spO2, timestamp, patientunitstayid)
- `apachePatientResult` - Mortality and length of stay outcomes (actualicumortality, actualventdays, patientunitstayid)
- `apacheApsVar` - APACHE II scores (apachescore, patientunitstayid)
- `patient` - Demographics (age, sex, patientunitstayid)

**Key Variables**:
- `patientunitstayid` - Primary join key across all tables
- `vitalAperiodic.timestamp` - Time-stamped oxygenation measurements (in minutes from admission)
- `vitalAperiodic.paO2` - Arterial oxygen tension (mmHg)
- `vitalPeriodic.spO2` - Peripheral oxygen saturation (%)
- `apachePatientResult.actualicumortality` - Primary outcome (0/1)
- `apachePatientResult.actualventdays` - Secondary outcome (ventilation days)
- `apacheApsVar.apachescore` - APACHE II score
- `patient.age`, `patient.sex` - Demographics

**Temporal Boundaries**:
- **Inclusion**: ICU admissions with at least 24 hours of continuous monitoring
- **Exposure window**: First 72 hours post-admission (offset 0-4320 minutes)
- **Follow-up**: Through ICU discharge or death
- **Minimum hyperoxia duration**: >8 hours (to avoid measurement noise)

**Hyperoxia Definition**:
- PaO₂ > 80 mmHg OR SpO₂ > 92% (per ARDSNet and Surviving Sepsis guidelines)
- Duration calculated as cumulative time above threshold
- Categorized into quartiles for analysis

## Analysis Plan

### Primary Analysis

1. **Descriptive Statistics**:
   - Distribution of hyperoxia duration (mean, median, IQR)
   - Distribution of hyperoxia intensity (max PaO₂/SpO₂)
   - Association with outcomes (mortality, prolonged ventilation)

2. **Univariate Analysis**:
   - Hyperoxia duration quartiles vs. mortality (chi-square, trend test)
   - Hyperoxia duration vs. prolonged ventilation (chi-square, trend test)

3. **Multivariate Cox Proportional Hazards Model**:
   - Primary model: mortality ~ hyperoxia_duration + age + sex + APACHE_II + SOFA + admission_diagnoses
   - Secondary model: prolonged_ventilation ~ hyperoxia_duration + age + sex + APACHE_II + SOFA + admission_diagnoses

4. **Key Outputs**:
   - Hazard ratio (HR) for hyperoxia duration (per unit increase and per quartile)
   - 95% confidence intervals
   - p-values for trend and categorical comparisons

### Secondary Analysis

1. **Interaction Analysis**:
   - Hyperoxia × APACHE II interaction terms
   - Hyperoxia × SOFA interaction terms

2. **Subgroup Analysis**:
   - ARDS vs. non-ARDS
   - Sepsis vs. non-sepsis
   - Mechanical ventilation vs. non-ventilation

3. **Sensitivity Analyses**:
   - Restrict to first 48 hours vs. first 72 hours
   - Different hyperoxia thresholds (PaO₂ > 90 mmHg, SpO₂ > 95%)
   - Exclude first 12 hours (intubation period)

### Baseline Comparison

**Baseline Model**: APACHE II + SOFA + age + sex + admission diagnoses
- C-statistic, calibration slope, Brier score

**Hyperoxia-Adjusted Model**: Same covariates + hyperoxia duration
- C-statistic, calibration slope, Brier score
- Net Reclassification Improvement (NRI)
- Integrated Discrimination Improvement (IDI)

## Falsification Criteria

The hypothesis will be **falsified** if:
1. Hyperoxia duration shows no association with outcomes after adjustment (HR ≤ 1.2, p ≥ 0.05)
2. The association is entirely explained by baseline severity (no independent effect after APACHE II/SOFA adjustment)
3. Adding hyperoxia to traditional scores provides no incremental predictive value (NRI ≤ 2%, ΔC-statistic ≤ 0.01)

The hypothesis will be **supported** if:
1. Hyperoxia duration is independently associated with outcomes (HR > 1.3, p < 0.01)
2. Adding hyperoxia improves prediction (NRI > 5%, ΔC-statistic > 0.02)
3. The association persists across sensitivity analyses

The hypothesis will be **inconclusive** if:
1. Weak association found (1.1 < HR < 1.3, p between 0.01 and 0.05)
2. Inconsistent results across subgroups
3. Need for larger sample size or external validation

## Method Comparison

**Baseline Approach (Traditional)**:
- Model: Logistic regression or Cox using only traditional risk factors
- Inputs: APACHE II, SOFA, age, sex, admission diagnoses
- Output: Mortality risk prediction
- What it tells us: Baseline performance of traditional scores

**Hyperoxia-Adjusted Approach (Proposed)**:
- Model: Cox model with hyperoxia as time-dependent covariate
- Inputs: APACHE II, SOFA, age, sex, admission diagnoses, hyperoxia duration
- Output: Mortality risk prediction with hyperoxia adjustment
- What it tells us: Incremental value of hyperoxia beyond traditional scores

**What the Alternative Reveals That Baseline Loses**:
1. Hidden high-risk patients (normal admission scores but poor hyperoxia exposure)
2. Temporal risk evolution (how risk changes during ICU stay)
3. Whether hyperoxia is independent predictor or marker of severity
4. Clinical actionability for oxygenation targets

## Key References

Three key papers inform this hypothesis:

1. **K1**: ARDSNet trial - established low tidal volume ventilation and oxygenation targets in ARDS
   - DOI: 10.1056/NEJM200008243430802
   - Claim: Low tidal volume ventilation reduces mortality in ARDS; oxygenation targets matter
   - Contribution: Extends ARDSNet findings by examining hyperoxia duration as outcome predictor
   - Limitation: Focused on tidal volume and PEEP, not hyperoxia duration as predictor

2. **K2**: Rodgers et al. (2019) - Review on hyperoxia and lung injury in critical care
   - PMID: 30652312
   - Claim: Hyperoxia exposure in critical care is associated with poor outcomes and high mortality
   - Contribution: Operationalizes hyperoxia harm by quantifying duration as continuous predictor
   - Limitation: Review synthesizes evidence but doesn't establish duration as independent marker

3. **K3**: Pekkanen et al. (2018) - Meta-analysis on optimal SpO2 targets and mortality
   - DOI: 10.1164/rccm.201708-1484OC
   - Claim: Higher SpO2 targets (>92%) are associated with increased mortality in critically ill patients
   - Contribution: Extends meta-analysis by examining hyperoxia duration (time above target)
   - Limitation: Focuses on target adherence (binary), not duration of hyperoxia exposure

## Unresolved Questions Requiring Clinical Adjudication

1. **Causality vs. Association**: The analysis establishes association, not causation. Clinical implications depend on whether hyperoxia is a marker of severity or a modifiable risk factor.

2. **Optimal targets**: If hyperoxia is harmful, what are the optimal oxygenation targets? This requires RCT evidence.

3. **Heterogeneity**: Does the effect vary by diagnosis, ventilation strategy, or patient population?

4. **Measurement accuracy**: SpO₂ vs. PaO₂ - which is more accurate for prognostication?
