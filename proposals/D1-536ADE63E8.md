# Provisional hypothesis (checkpoint draft)

## Proposed relationship
Among adults with a documented acute myocardial infarction, greater early post-discharge functional recovery will be associated with lower subsequent cardiovascular readmission risk, beyond baseline illness severity and early clinical instability. The unresolved question is whether functional recovery is a clinically actionable recovery trajectory rather than merely a marker of who was less severely ill or more intensively observed.

## Why it matters
If supported, the finding could help distinguish patients who may benefit from structured post-discharge recovery support from those whose apparent recovery is fragile or measurement-driven. It would inform follow-up intensity without implying that a score alone causes improved outcomes.

## Evidence status and exact sources
This is a provisional opening, not a completed literature or dataset review. No claim is made that the relationship has already been demonstrated. The proposed observation is grounded in the clinical plausibility that recovery after myocardial injury and subsequent readmission are linked, but the exact evidence and closest prior work still require inspection. The data binding below is likewise provisional until the catalog and source files are checked.

## Provisional data design
- Population: adults with a myocardial infarction diagnosis and a defined discharge date, followed for 30 days after discharge.
- Time zero: hospital discharge.
- Exposure: change in a functional-status measure from pre-discharge to early follow-up, where such a measure exists; otherwise this branch is infeasible and must be revised.
- Outcome: cardiovascular-related readmission or emergency department visit within 30 days, with a prespecified secondary outcome of any readmission.
- Baseline adjustment: age, sex, comorbidity burden, acute severity, discharge disposition, and follow-up observation intensity where available.
- Baseline analysis: a prespecified logistic or survival model with functional recovery, severity, and observation variables.
- Alternative analysis: a time-to-event or trajectory model that estimates whether the shape of recovery (not only its endpoint) predicts outcomes, with the same population and split.
- Falsification: no association after controlling for pre-discharge functional status and observation intensity, or a materially different association in a negative-control outcome/time window, would weaken the interpretation.
- Supportive result: a reproducible association with clinically meaningful effect size and uncertainty, consistent across prespecified sensitivity analyses.
- Adverse result: no association, reversal, or strong dependence on observation intensity.
- Inconclusive result: sparse functional measurements, wide uncertainty, or inability to define the outcome/time window.
