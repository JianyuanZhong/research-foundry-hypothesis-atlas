# Peri-second-TACE systemic initiation as a recorded combination strategy: a landmark comparison with TACE-only management

## Unresolved clinical question

After a patient has undergone a second transarterial chemoembolization (TACE), does recording a systemic HCC-directed medication in the immediate post-procedure period identify a different subsequent hepatic-reserve trajectory than continuing with TACE-only management? The clinically meaningful decision is whether to add systemic treatment to the second-TACE episode, not whether a later drug order represents a response-informed switch.

The parent proposal treated medication dates up to 90 days after TACE2 as a later treatment decision. The complete audit shows that 569/619 systemic records (92.0%) begin within 14 days of TACE2, with median day 2 (IQR 1–3), whereas the first third-TACE occurs median day 51 (IQR 39–65). This makes a day-14 mutually exclusive landmark exposure more honest: “TACE2 plus a recorded systemic initiation during days 1–14” versus “TACE2-only recorded pathway during days 1–14.” It does not establish treatment intent, administration, adherence, or a causal benefit.

The strongest supported claim is feasibility of a recorded-pathway association. It is not evidence that combination treatment preserves liver function, improves survival, treats TACE refractoriness, or is preferable for a patient.

## Hypothesis and estimand

**Hypothesis.** Among adults with HCC and two qualifying TACE calendar-day episodes 14–180 days apart, who have no matched systemic-treatment record through TACE2 and are observed in the institution through the day-14 landmark, peri-TACE2 systemic initiation is associated with a different (prespecified direction: less decline in albumin without greater bilirubin increase) hepatic-reserve trajectory than TACE-only recorded management over fixed post-TACE2 windows.

The primary estimand is an observational, landmark-conditional difference in mean within-assay change from a pre-TACE2 baseline to the closest value in TACE2 days 30–90, separately for exact standard-assay `Albumin` and `Total Bilirubin`, comparing the two recorded pathways. Use TACE2 as the common clinical time zero; exposure is ascertained only in days 1–14; no outcome measurement before day 30 enters the primary endpoint. A secondary window is days 60–120. This is a treatment-pathway association among patients meeting the day-14 landmark, not a per-protocol or causal effect.

“Combination strategy” means TACE2 plus systemic treatment, not a requirement that two systemic drugs be co-administered. Drug class and TKI/ICI composition are descriptive only; do not claim a specific regimen effect.

## Outcomes and timing

The primary outcome is change from the closest valid baseline value in the 30 days strictly before TACE2 to the closest valid value in TACE2 days 30–90. Use exact assay names only: `albumin` and `total bilirubin`. Report albumin and bilirubin separately; a joint two-component profile is descriptive and does not create an unvalidated ALBI endpoint. The source has `quantitative result`, `qualitative result`, `specimen type`, and `test time` but no unit column. Therefore restrict comparisons to the same exact assay name and do not assert unit equivalence or clinical thresholds.

The secondary laboratory window is TACE2 days 60–120. Do not use a day-90 landmark, which would give the two groups unequal time since treatment; do not use post-exposure labs as confounders. Exploratory recorded diagnosis signals from `[internal dataset path]` inherited through encounters may include ascites/ascites, encephalopathy, variceal bleeding, hepatic insufficiency, portal-vein tumor thrombus, and prespecified metastasis terms. They have no native timestamp and are observation-sensitive; report only as coding proxies, not decompensation, progression, toxicity, or survival endpoints.

## Analysis that avoids immortal-time bias

This is a fixed-grace-period landmark design, not a comparison of people guaranteed to survive to a later drug date. Everyone enters at TACE2. Exposure is defined prospectively from records in days 1–14. Patients without a qualifying systemic record at day 14 are eligible for TACE-only only if they also lack a generic systemic-treatment procedure label; the analytic follow-up for both arms starts at the day-14 landmark, and primary laboratory outcomes are measured only after day 30. Patients with no institutional observation through day 14 are excluded before arm comparison and counted as a landmark-observation failure. This estimand is explicitly conditional on day-14 institutional observability and cannot answer an intention-to-treat question from TACE2 for patients lost immediately after the procedure.

Use the following pre-index covariates only: age, sex, TACE1–TACE2 interval, TACE2 calendar year/month, department, prior encounter/procedure counts in 90 and 180 days, prior resection/ablation/transplant labels, diagnosis proxies for cirrhosis/portal hypertension/ascites/encephalopathy/variceal bleeding/portal-vein invasion/metastasis, and closest pre-TACE2 exact-assay albumin, bilirubin, INR, platelets, AFP, creatinine, and sodium when present. Imaging reports from `[internal dataset path]` (catalog table `examinations`: `examination`, `examination findings`, `examination diagnosis`, `start time`, `examination number`) can be used as retrieval evidence only. Any post-TACE2/pre-day-14 imaging response variable requires locked clinician abstraction; automated lexical flags must not be represented as response adjudication.

Estimate an unadjusted descriptive contrast and an adjusted contrast using a prespecified propensity model with flexible year/month terms and overlap weights, followed by weighted outcome regression (or weighted doubly robust linear regression) for each laboratory change. Report standardized differences, propensity distributions, arm-specific effective sample sizes, and 95% confidence intervals. Do not use clone-censor-weight here unless a separate time-varying treatment question is introduced; the fixed 14-day grace-period landmark is simpler and more robust for this source. A clone-censor-weight sensitivity is unnecessary methodological ornament and could obscure the recorded-pathway estimand.

Use inverse-probability-of-observation weights or an outcome-observation model based only on pre-index data as a sensitivity, because lab follow-up is selective. Require complete-case and observation-weighted results to be shown together. A later encounter is not proof of survival, and absence of a lab is not proof of clinical stability.

## Falsification, stop rules, and interpretation

Pre-specify a negative-control outcome consisting of the analogous pre-TACE2 laboratory change in two earlier windows. A material arm contrast in this pre-exposure outcome indicates channeling, temporal leakage, or inadequate adjustment; a null negative control does not prove exchangeability. Report a timing placebo check using a pseudo-index before TACE2 only if it can be constructed without reusing future information.

Stop or downgrade to feasibility-only if any of the following occurs: strict versus broad TACE ontology reverses the direction materially; either arm has effective sample size below 100; propensity overlap is absent in any retained year and cannot be repaired by pooling adjacent years prospectively; fewer than 100 systemic-arm paired outcomes remain in the primary 2018–2024 restriction; observation weights are unstable; complete-case and observation-weighted directions disagree; or clinician-adjudicated response information is required for a claim but unavailable. The expected 2020–2024 sensitivity has only 94 systemic paired outcomes and therefore cannot replace the 2018–2024 primary if the 100-outcome rule is retained.

**Supportive result:** under the strict ontology, common era, adequate overlap, and observation-weighted analysis, albumin declines less (or improves) in the TACE-plus-recorded-systemic arm and bilirubin does not increase more, with compatible direction in the 2020–2024 sensitivity and any adjudicated imaging subset. This supports only a recorded-pathway association.

**Adverse result:** albumin declines more, bilirubin increases more, or coding proxies for hepatic decompensation are more frequent in the systemic arm. This argues against assuming a liver-preserving association but cannot show systemic treatment caused harm because treatment selection is strongly channelled by unmeasured disease severity and response.

**Inconclusive result:** estimates are imprecise, change direction with ontology/era/observation handling, fail overlap, or the paired-outcome and response-adjudication gates are not met. Report negative feasibility rather than choosing the most favorable specification.

This source cannot establish administration, adherence, HCC indication for every order, treatment intent, radiographic response or progression, TACE refractoriness, Child-Pugh/performance status, toxicity, mortality, outside treatment, transplant, or generalizability. Those claims require medication administration data, clinician review of imaging and intent, validated laboratory units and clinical thresholds, vital-status/out-of-system linkage, or a prospective/multi-institutional study.

## Substantive change from the parent

This child abandons the unsupported “response-informed switch” interpretation and makes the observed phenomenon—near-concurrent systemic initiation around TACE2—the exposure of interest. It replaces a 90-day future-event comparison with a common TACE2 time zero, a mutually exclusive 1–14-day grace-period exposure, a day-14 landmark, and fixed TACE2-anchored laboratory windows. It tightens the TACE ontology, explicitly handles placebo and bevacizumab ambiguity, adds a strict TACE and era-overlap gate, reports actual arm-specific paired-lab counts and later crossover, and rejects clone-censor weighting unless the scientific question changes. Residual uncertainty remains substantial because the systemic arm is selected, most records occur too soon for response adjustment, outcome observation is selective, and treatment administration, intent, imaging response, mortality, toxicity, and outside care are unavailable.
