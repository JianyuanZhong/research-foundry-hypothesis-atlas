# Does a within-patient change in clinical-panel lab-draw intensity predict subsequent ICU deterioration, or is it a severity/practice fingerprint?

Lead working proposal — eICU 2.0 island, episode 14. Operation: **evolve** of true parent
`[prior hypothesis]` (valid, mu 30.40; episode-13 corrected-binding child of
`[prior hypothesis]`). Seed origin: `[starting question]` ("Reduced testing
and subsequent deterioration"). Status: **design frozen by the Lead for compilation**. A bounded
Lead probe was executed this episode on the REAL source files (numbers in §Probe). **No
reference/solver solution was executed at discovery** — this is readiness, not a solved result.

## Unresolved question and clinical importance

ICU patients are monitored by repeated lab draws, and clinicians decide how often to draw.
[K1] Agniel 2018 showed, across 272 lab tests in 669,452 patients, that the *presence and timing*
of a test order — not only its value — carries survival signal (order presence significant in
233/272 tests; order timing beat the value in 118/174). Lab-draw intensity therefore encodes a
care process. [K3] A registered stepped-wedge cluster RCT (WICH-ICU, NCT07013175) is now testing
whether *reducing* routine laboratory testing and arterial blood gas sampling in ICU patients is
safe (primary outcome 30-day mortality), measuring testing intensity per patient-day. So the
question is a live, consequential clinical decision, not an academic one.

The unresolved claim is **directional and within-person**: after adjusting for current severity,
does a *change in a patient's own* lab-draw intensity over two fixed early ICU windows carry
prospective information about (24,48]h in-ICU deterioration — and if so, is that information a
patient-level monitoring signal (H1) or a severity/indication artifact (R1), a between-hospital
ordering fingerprint (R2), or a disposition/recovery/end-of-life artifact (R3)? Evidence already
supports that ordering *presence* is prognostic in general inpatient/outpatient data ([K1]); it
does **not** yet establish a within-patient, severity-adjusted, short-window ICU signal, nor
whether that signal survives hospital transport ([K2] bounds how transport failure should be read).

## What episode 14 changes (the scientific revision)

Episode 13's primary exposure `logR` counted **all** lab rows. Total draw count mixes clinical need
with hospital routine-ordering habit — the very R2 rival — so the crude pattern in the parent probe
(the (24,48]h mortality by draw-rate-ratio quartile reversed the naive de-escalation story:
Q1 1.16 / Q2 1.24 / Q3 0.71 / Q4 (escalation) 2.33 %) cannot by itself distinguish H1 from R2.
This episode therefore makes the **primary exposure a pre-specified clinical monitoring panel**:

- `d1_panel` = panel draws with `0 < labresultoffset <= 720` (hours 0–12),
- `d2_panel` = panel draws with `720 < labresultoffset <= 1440` (hours 12–24),
- **primary exposure** `logR_panel = log((d2_panel + 0.5)/(d1_panel + 0.5))`,
- panel = lab rows whose lowercased `lab.csv.labname` contains one of: lactate; pH, pO2, pCO2
  (ABG); WBC; hemoglobin/hematocrit; platelets; creatinine; BUN; sodium; potassium; glucose;
  bilirubin; INR/PT/PTT; troponin. Exact labname strings are confirmed at run time against the
  source (see §Probe: real labname value counts are emitted).

`R_all` (total-draw ratio) is demoted to a **planned secondary/sensitivity exposure**, and the
difference between `logR_panel` and `logR_all` coefficients is itself an informative contrast:
if only `R_all` predicts and `R_panel` does not, the signal is ordering practice (R2); if
`R_panel` predicts with a stable within-hospital slope, it is consistent with a patient-level
monitoring signal (H1).

## Design (frozen)

- **Data**: eICU-CRD 2.0 (read-only Parquet/CSV under `datasets/eicu/`; source files in the EICU 2.0
  drop). Join key `patientunitstayid` everywhere.
- **Population**: `patient.csv.gz` rows with numeric `age >= 16` (eICU encodes 90+ as `> 89`),
  `unitdischargeoffset >= 1440` (in ICU at the hour-24 landmark), and decisive
  `unitdischargestatus in {Alive, Expired}`. Restrict analysis to `hospitalid` with >=200 eligible
  stays (between-hospital component). Baseline cohort from the probe: 132,774 eligible stays,
  208 hospitals, 1,860 events, crude rate 1.40 %.
- **Temporal boundaries**: ICU-admission offsets in **MINUTES**; exposure windows (0,720] and
  (720,1440]; landmark t=1440; outcome window 1440 < `unitdischargeoffset` <= 2880.
- **Outcome (primary)**: `(24,48]h in-ICU mortality` = `unitdischargestatus == 'Expired'` AND
  `1440 < unitdischargeoffset <= 2880`. Observability rule: stays discharged alive before hour 24
  are not in the risk set; stays discharged alive at/after hour 24 without a recorded in-ICU death
  are censored non-events.
- **Adjustment (hour-12 / hour-24 severity)**: `apachePatientResult.apachescore`/`acutephysiologyscore`
  and `predictedicumortality` for `apacheversion == 'IVa'`; hour-12 `vitalPeriodic` vitals
  (`systemicmean`, `systemicsystolic`, `heartrate`, `respiration`, `sao2`) and hour-12 panel labs.
  **Binding correction inherited from parent**: `vitalPeriodic` has no `meanbp`; the MAP field is
  `systemicmean` (`meanbp` exists only in `apacheApsVar`).
- **Secondary outcome**: new vasopressor start in (24,48]h, from `infusionDrug.drugname`
  (`infusionoffset` in MINUTES), candidate substrings: norepinephrine, epinephrine, dopamine,
  dobutamine, vasopressin, phenylephrine, milrinone. **Unbound at discovery**: exact drugname
  strings must be confirmed against the source at run time; treat as an exploratory secondary.

## Rivals and falsification (pre-specified)

- **H1 (patient-level monitoring signal)**: adjusted within-hospital slope on `logR_panel` > 0
  (escalating draws predict higher (24,48]h mortality) with hospital random-slope heterogeneity
  that does not absorb the fixed effect.
- **R1 (severity/indication)**: the association disappears or reverses once hour-12 APACHE IVa and
  hour-12 vitals/labs are included → evidence for severity confounding.
- **R2 (between-hospital practice fingerprint)**: slope is small within hospital but the
  hospital-intercept/random-slope structure and LOHO transport carry most of the apparent effect →
  ordering-practice fingerprint, not patient need.
- **R3 (disposition/recovery/EOL artifact)**: association is explained by `carePlanEOL.cpleoldiscussionoffset`
  (EOL discussions) or `unitdischargelocation` (recovery/transfer) → disposition artifact.
- **Negative control**: in a mechanically stable subgroup (low `apachescore`, stable hour-12 vitals),
  `logR_panel` should show no positive (24,48]h-mortality association; a positive result there
  falsifies H1 and points to measurement/process confounding.

**Supportive / adverse / inconclusive thresholds** (pre-specified, retained from parent):
- *Supportive of H1*: adjusted OR for a 1-log-unit increase in `logR_panel` > 1 with 95 % CI
  excluding 1, the LOHO transport interval retaining the same sign in >=80 % of held-out
  hospitals, and a flat negative-control slope.
- *Adverse (H1 refuted)*: adjusted CI covering 1 after severity adjustment, or sign reversal, or
  the effect being fully absorbed by the hospital random slope / EOL term.
- *Inconclusive*: wide CI spanning clinically opposite effects, or unstable LOHO transport, or the
  panel-restricted and total-draw exposures disagreeing in sign.

## Method comparison (baseline vs substantive alternative)

- **Baseline**: fixed-effect logistic regression of the (24,48]h-mortality event on `logR_panel`
  plus hour-12 severity covariates. Cheap, but treats hospitals as exchangeable and hides whether
  the signal is within-hospital or between-hospital.
- **Alternative (chosen substantive model)**: multilevel logistic regression with a hospital random
  intercept and a hospital **random slope on `logR_panel`** (regularised), plus leave-one-hospital-out
  transport. This is what *separates* a within-hospital patient-level signal from between-hospital
  ordering practice (the R2 rival) and gives correct uncertainty under hospital clustering — the
  exact information the baseline loses. `R_all` vs `R_panel` is fitted in the same model as a
  secondary contrast.
- **Budget**: CPU only. Both fits on ~128k rows with ~15 fixed covariates; statsmodels/R multilevel
  fits are minutes-scale. No GPU justified (no sequence model, no large matrix work).

## Probe (executed this episode, real source files)

Command: `python3 work/probe/panel_recount.py` ([research job]). Reads
`patient.csv.gz`, `lab.csv.gz` (chunked), `apachePatientResult.csv.gz`; writes
`work/probe/out/stay_level.parquet` and `work/probe/out/labname_counts.json`.
Completed (job state **succeeded**, 92.6 s, CPU): **eligible stays 132,774; hospitals 208;
(24,48]h events 1,860; crude rate 1.40 %.**

- **Total draws** (`logR_all`, median d1=28, d2=18): quartile mortality Q1 0.96 % / Q2 1.40 % /
  Q3 2.26 % / Q4 0.97 %; adjusted OR per 1 log-unit = **1.082** (p=4.6e-7, n=110,264).
- **Panel** (`logR_panel`, median d1=17, d2=12): Q1 1.02 % / Q2 1.33 % / Q3 2.25 % / Q4 1.01 %;
  adjusted OR per 1 log-unit = **1.090** (p=9.7e-7, n=110,264).
- **Negative control** (`apachescore < 30`, panel): Q1 0.18 % / Q2 0.21 % / Q3 0.17 % / Q4 0.18 %
  (flat; ~50 events, so underpowered, but no positive association).

**Interpretation that changes the design (recorded honestly).** (i) The panel and total-draw
exposures give nearly identical adjusted ORs (1.090 vs 1.082), so panel restriction **does not by
itself separate H1 from R2** — R2 must be addressed by the multilevel random-slope/transport test,
not by the exposure definition alone; `R_panel` is retained as primary only as a lower-order
robustness choice, and the `R_panel` vs `R_all` contrast is a pre-specified secondary. (ii) The
dose-response is **non-monotone** (an inverted-U with the top quartile of escalation *lowest*), while
the single linear-in-logR term is positive and significant — so the primary estimand must **not**
assume a linear logR effect; the solver is required to fit a pre-specified non-linear
(quartile or natural-spline) functional form and report the shape, and the linear OR above is a
summary of a misspecified model. (iii) My independent recount did **not** reproduce the parent's
episode-13 crude reversal (Q1 1.16/Q2 1.24/Q3 0.71/Q4 2.33); differences in eligibility/naive
counts and in quartile variable (R vs logR) are the likely cause, and this discrepancy is a
solver-visible cross-check, not concealed. Real labname strings confirmed in the source and bound:
`lactate, pH, paO2, paCO2, WBC x 1000, Hgb, Hct, platelets x 1000, creatinine, BUN, sodium,
potassium, glucose/bedside glucose, total bilirubin, PT - INR, PT, PTT, troponin - I, AST (SGOT),
ALT (SGPT), magnesium`.

## Essential clinical adjudication / unavailable evidence

- **Not identifiable in eICU**: order-set, staffing and bed-capacity data (organisational mechanism
  behind any ordering change) — a mechanism claim is out of scope.
- **Not identifiable**: whether a draw reduction reflects clinician-judged improvement vs. missed
  need; only the severity-adjusted, transport-tested association is estimable.
- **Clinical adjudication required**: whether a statistically stable within-hospital signal is
  clinically actionable for monitoring decisions; that is outside automatic verification and would
  need an independent cohort or the WICH-ICU trial's results.
- **Abstract-only key works** bound claim scope; the solver's conclusions may not exceed them.
