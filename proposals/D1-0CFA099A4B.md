# When apparent AKI recovery is discordant: a MIMIC-IV landmark study

## Unresolved question and clinical value

Serum creatinine may fall during critical illness because filtration improves, but also because creatinine generation falls or fluid accumulation dilutes concentration. Urine output offers a partially independent, clinically used signal, but is affected by diuretics, fluid administration, catheter practice, and incomplete recording. The unresolved, clinically consequential question is whether an apparent creatinine decline after an ICU AKI episode, when urine output remains low, identifies patients with higher subsequent risk than patients with concordant improvement, after accounting for pre-landmark severity, fluid balance, treatment, and measurement intensity. If the signal is robust, clinicians should be cautious about labeling biochemical decline as recovery and a prospective validation study with direct renal-function measurement would be warranted. If not, this supports avoiding an unvalidated discordance label.

This is a prognostic/descriptive hypothesis, not a causal claim that oliguria or fluids cause injury or that a treatment should change. Strongest rival: apparent discordance reflects severity, fluid-related creatinine dilution, diuretic exposure, selective laboratory testing, urine-output charting gaps, and survival/ICU-stay selection—not distinct renal recovery. The primary comparison tests whether the discordant pattern carries a reproducible conditional prognostic signal beyond measured severity and fluid balance. Attenuation after these adjustments favors the rival; residual association cannot prove the leading physiological explanation.

## Hypothesis, claims, and evidence boundary

**Hypothesis to test:** Among adults with an incident ICU AKI episode who remain alive and in the ICU 48 hours after first meeting the prespecified creatinine-based AKI criterion, patients with a falling serum creatinine but persistent oliguria in the preceding 24 hours have a higher risk of death or new renal replacement therapy (RRT) over the following 7 days than patients with both creatinine decline and non-oliguric output, after adjustment for baseline severity, fluid balance, and care intensity.

**Already supported:** KDIGO uses serum creatinine and urine-output criteria to identify AKI; critical illness can make creatinine-based estimates overstate renal function as muscle mass declines [K1]. Fluid accumulation in critically ill AKI cohorts has been associated with mortality and delayed/non-recovery, but its causal direction is unresolved [K2]. Diagnostic-accuracy evidence in AKI has substantial heterogeneity and reporting limitations [K3]. These works motivate caution; none establishes this proposed MIMIC association.

**Unresolved claim:** a fixed-time post-onset mismatch between improving creatinine and persistently low urine output is associated with a consequential future outcome, beyond measured acuity, fluid balance, treatment, and observation frequency. Supportive results would establish only an adjusted prognostic association in this retrospective cohort. They would not establish true GFR recovery, intrinsic renal injury, a causal effect of urine output/fluid treatment, or clinical utility.

The catalog verifies archive member names and schemas; item-dictionary diagnostic confirmed serum creatinine item 50912 and ICU chart creatinine 220615. Output-event item IDs and charted-weight mappings must be verified against dictionary labels and units before the solver commits cohort counts; do not assume all output is urine.
- Cohort: age ≥18 using `hosp/patients` (`anchor_age`, `anchor_year`, `gender`) joined on `subject_id`; first ICU stay per hospital admission (`icu/icustays` and `hosp/admissions`) to limit recurrent-event dependence. Exclude patients already receiving RRT at index or missing sufficient pre/post landmark measurements. Define incident creatinine AKI onset only within ICU: first time creatinine meets KDIGO-compatible rise (≥0.3 mg/dL within 48 h or ≥1.5× lowest preceding value in prior 7 d), using only values available by then. This is creatinine-defined AKI, not complete KDIGO staging because urine output may be incompletely observed.
- Time zero is the 48-hour landmark after AKI onset. Include those alive, still in ICU, and not already on RRT then; use index-window measurements only from onset through landmark. Follow from landmark to day 7, hospital discharge, or death, whichever occurs first. Report landmark exclusions and compare characteristics; this survival/continued-ICU condition limits generalizability and can induce selection.
- Primary exposure: creatinine decline from the maximum serum value between onset and landmark, combined with persistent oliguria during the final 24 hours before landmark. Define persistent oliguria as urine volume <0.5 mL/kg/h on at least 6 hours, with valid charted weight and sufficiently complete hourly intervals. This operational threshold is prespecified as a conventional clinical threshold, not validated recovery criterion. Compare discordant decline+oliguria with concordant decline+non-oliguria. Also retain four cells (creatinine declined/not declined × oliguria/no oliguria) and continuous creatinine change and urine rate; prespecified sensitivity analyses vary the decline threshold and require alternative coverage completeness.
- Fluid balance: sum compatible `icu/inputevents.amount` and eligible `icu/outputevents.value` volumes through index, with matching units and item definitions, as a confounder/alternative explanation proxy—not a complete measure of total body fluid. Report missingness and coverage. Medication records needed to identify diuretics may be drawn from `hosp/emar` or `hosp/prescriptions`; exact administration coverage must be evaluated before adjustment. Adjust for age, admission type, pre-index creatinine, creatinine peak, urine measurement density, fluid balance, diuretic exposure where ascertainable, and pre-landmark severity proxies (vasopressors/ventilation and available vitals/labs), without using post-index information.
- Primary outcome: all-cause death or new RRT within 7 days from landmark, derived from `hosp/admissions.deathtime/hospital_expire_flag` and procedures, with explicit RRT coding/procedure validation. Secondary outcomes: 7-day death alone and hospital death. RRT and death are competing and clinically distinct events; report cumulative incidences and component risks rather than implying the composite is a single mechanism. RRT is clinician-selected and procedure coding can miss cases; claims using this endpoint need clinician review.

## Estimand, analysis, and alternative methods

Primary estimand is the adjusted 7-day risk difference (and risk ratio) in the landmark population between discordant and concordant creatinine-decline groups, standardized over measured pre-landmark covariates. This is associational, not a treatment effect. Use patient-level bootstrap confidence intervals, accounting for within-subject episode selection; additionally report unadjusted risks and adjusted regression with prespecified covariates, nonlinearity checks and calibration. Handle death/RRT and discharge carefully, and report competing-risk estimates alongside the composite. Missing measurements are not imputed as normal; primary complete-coverage rules are paired with missingness/measurement-frequency sensitivity analyses. No statistical significance threshold alone establishes or rejects the hypothesis.

**Simple baseline:** four-cell exposure and a parsimonious adjusted logistic/competing-risk model for 7-day outcomes, plus stratified absolute risks and bootstrap intervals. It directly estimates whether the hypothesized discordant category differs from concordant recovery and is easy to audit.

**Substantive learned/mechanistic alternative:** fit a regularized joint longitudinal trajectory model using repeated creatinine, hourly urine-output summaries, fluid-balance trajectory, and observation-process indicators from onset through the 48-hour landmark. Fit target: latent patient trajectory/state and 7-day outcome probability, with joint random effects or a low-dimensional state-transition model; contrast states dominated by improving creatinine versus jointly improving creatinine/output, then estimate standardized risks and uncertainty. Split by `subject_id` into development and held-out test sets (e.g. 80/20, deterministic seed); tune only in development using grouped cross-validation. Compare on identical patients against baseline by held-out log loss, calibration slope/intercept, Brier score, and prespecified risk contrasts with bootstrap uncertainty. Its scientific purpose is not a small AUC gain: determine whether trajectory ordering/missingness distinguishes a sustained discordant pattern from sparse measurement, transient fluctuation, or fluid-related dilution, and whether category-based estimates conceal graded trajectories. If event support is inadequate, do not fit the latent alternative; report it deferred, not replaced by a smaller claim. This is a CPU-sized tabular longitudinal model; plan ≤8 CPU cores, ≤32 GiB RAM, under 2 hours for preprocessing and repeated fits after parquet/SQL staging. These are planning estimates, not measured benchmarks; no GPU is required absent evidence that model fitting dominates the workload. An exploratory fluid-balance-corrected creatinine is a sensitivity analysis only because body-water assumptions and recorded fluid balance are incomplete.

## Falsification and result interpretation

- Supportive: discordant group has higher 7-day absolute risk with uncertainty excluding a clinically negligible difference under the prespecified main analysis; direction persists in reasonable threshold/coverage and severity/fluid-adjustment analyses and is not explained by observation frequency. This justifies independent validation and a prospective study with measured cystatin C/iohexol/GFR and expert-adjudicated RRT, not a treatment recommendation.
- Adverse: precise null or lower risk, with adequate event support and valid urine coverage, undermines the proposed prognostic signal in this selected MIMIC population; it does not prove no discordance matters elsewhere. Marked attenuation after severity, fluid-balance, diuretic or measurement-frequency adjustment supports the strongest rival explanation that mismatch is largely severity/dilution/ascertainment, not kidney-specific recovery.
- Inconclusive: too few eligible patients/outcomes, broad confidence intervals, unstable direction across output item mappings/coverage rules, substantial missingness, or unreliable RRT ascertainment. Do not treat imprecision as refutation or impute a solved conclusion.
- Computationally checkable: population assembly, timeline precedence, unit conversion, exposure/outcome construction, leakage-free patient split, model fitting, uncertainty and metric calculations, sensitivity output, and whether textual claims match computed estimates. Clinical adjudication required: output-event eligibility/urine completeness, RRT indicators/procedure mapping, clinically negligible risk-difference margin, meaningful confounding control, and whether a residual association represents kidney physiology. Cystatin C, iohexol/measured GFR, muscle mass, validated fluid status, medication administration, and prospective external confirmation are unavailable or incomplete and needed for stronger mechanism/clinical-utility claims.

## Method and topic disposition

Before method selection, the baseline and joint trajectory alternative are specified on the same cohort, temporal window and outcome. The alternative is retained only if repeat measurements and output coverage support stable state estimation; otherwise defer it, because the simple contrast already answers the primary prognostic question. Delphi (learned dated sequences) and ALADYNOULLI (latent longitudinal trajectories) were considered from the demonstrations: their methods evidence is available in `references/research-ambition/methods-and-compute.md`, but they are not automatically suitable models; this question centers on two short ICU renal measurements and does not need a transformer or GP latent disease sequence. Oncoformer combines longitudinal EHR with chest X-rays; MIMIC-CXR image files are not in the configured source, and its main article/full STAR Methods remain unavailable, so neither reproduction nor image-based adaptation is justified here. The three examples are research context, not a required method list.

Other MIMIC seed dispositions: asynchronous multi-organ recovery would broaden the question beyond its kidney-specific measurement uncertainty; diuretic timing and transfusion require causal treatment emulation with substantial time-varying confounding; persistent lactate asks a separate phenotype question; acute-vs-chronic hyperglycemia depends on selective HbA1c; deep sedation asks for depth measurement beyond drug dose; antimicrobial de-escalation depends on result-availability time and source control; cardiorenal decongestion overlaps but does not improve this AKI-focused question; pre-ICU-discharge instability is a separate monitoring question. All imported seeds were assessed repairable because each requires substantive binding/design; none is evidence of a tested claim.

## Three key references

[K1] Haines RW, Fowler AJ, Liang K, Pearse RM, Larsson AO, Puthucheary Z, Prowle JR. “Comparison of Cystatin C and Creatinine in the Assessment of Measured Kidney Function during Critical Illness.” CJASN. 2023;18:997–1005. DOI: 10.2215/CJN.0000000000000203. Abstract-only inspection: 38 mechanically ventilated patients; in a subgroup with iohexol clearance, creatinine-based eGFR overestimated measured GFR and muscle loss correlated with creatinine/cystatin divergence. Bounds the creatinine interpretation; does not study the MIMIC discordant-output phenotype or validate an output threshold.

[K2] Bouchard J, Soroko SB, Chertow GM, et al. “Fluid accumulation, survival and recovery of kidney function in critically ill patients with acute kidney injury.” Kidney International. 2009;76:422–427. DOI: 10.1038/ki.2009.159. Abstract-only inspection: prospective multicenter observational cohort of 618; fluid overload associated with mortality and less recovery around creatinine peak, while authors explicitly state causality unresolved. Supports fluid accumulation as a serious rival/adjustment, not proof of dilution or a treatment effect.

[K3] Yu H, Li Y, Mo GP, et al. “STARDaki: a consensus-based STARD extension for standardized reporting of diagnostic accuracy in acute kidney injury.” Intensive Care Medicine. 2026;52:1662–1678. DOI: 10.1007/s00134-026-08549-5. Abstract-only inspection: review/panel found heterogeneous and incompletely reported AKI biomarker accuracy studies and proposed patient-selection/reference-standard reporting standards. Bounds diagnostic claims; this proposed outcome association is not a diagnostic accuracy validation study.
