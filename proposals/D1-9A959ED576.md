# Provisional HCC endpoint repair

## Hypothesis
Among adults admitted with exact diagnosis 肝细胞癌, an ordered 7-day laboratory trajectory will predict a prespecified non-routine post-day-7, pre-discharge procedure better than an admission snapshot after adjustment for routine HCC treatment and documentation intensity. The endpoint is a procedure proxy, not death, ICU admission, or clinical harm.

## Supporting evidence and uncertainty
The parent candidate reports a bounded audit of 104,554 exact HCC patient/admission keys, 321,207 timed HCC procedure rows, 15,774 untimed HCC procedure rows, and 20,052 HCC procedure rows after day 7 and before discharge (6,779 keys). These are support counts, not outcome validity. The procedure source has 338,040 rows and 7,781 distinct labels, including frequent TACE/arterial embolization/arterial chemoembolization and hepatic resection/ablation/transplant variants. The remaining uncertainty is whether the non-routine dictionary and event support can be frozen reproducibly from observed labels before fitting.

## Falsifiable test
Fit a snapshot baseline and an ordered temporal alternative on the same HCC cohort, with patient-level splits, held-out uncertainty, and timing-shifted/order-permutation negative controls. Support requires a nontrivial trajectory advantage on the frozen non-routine endpoint, stability across department/documentation/missingness/assay sensitivities, and no positive timing-shifted control. Adverse evidence is no improvement, process-only improvement, or positive negative control. Sparse events, missing timing, unstable dictionary, or inability to adjudicate clinical meaning are inconclusive.

## Missing evidence and limits
