# UKB-03 repaired proposal: MRI thigh fat infiltration and same-visit functional limitation

Status: delivery-ready hypothesis and Harbor experiment specification; not fitted. This is a repaired child of expert seed [prior hypothesis]. It does not inherit the prior UKB-36 outcome question or use any non-Assessment_Center outcome linkage.

## 1. Scientific opening and actual deliverable

The clinically unresolved boundary is whether MRI-derived thigh muscle fat infiltration measures lower-limb muscle quality that is functionally informative beyond muscle quantity and hand-grip strength, or whether it is simply another correlate of age, body size, low grip and existing disability. This matters because a clinician deciding whether a patient with apparently preserved muscle mass needs additional functional assessment needs evidence that composition adds information rather than another measurement of the same reserve construct.

The strongest inspected evidence supports three bounded claims. A UK Biobank study reports that higher MRI-measured thigh muscle fat infiltration (TMFI) is associated with mortality and system-specific disease, but its abstract does not establish functional validity beyond quantity or grip [K1]. A review of imaging studies links myosteatosis with gait, balance, strength and physical function, while emphasizing depot definitions, cross-sectional confounding and the need for standardized imaging [K2]. The EWGSOP2 consensus treats strength as central, muscle quantity/quality as confirmatory, and poor physical performance as severe disease, motivating joint measurement but not proving incremental value for this specific MRI field [K3].

The unresolved claim is therefore:

> At the same Assessment_Center visit, among UKB participants with complete instance-2 MRI thigh measurements, posterior-thigh muscle fat infiltration has a positive, clinically material adjusted association with slow usual walking pace after muscle quantity, grip strength, age, BMI and body-fat percentage are accounted for; a concordant but secondary association is expected for any fall in the prior year.

This is a cross-sectional, descriptive/prognostic information claim about current or recalled functional limitation. It is not a causal effect, a diagnosis of sarcopenia, a claim about future falls, a treatment rule, or a mechanism.

Actual scientific deliverable: newly fit B0/B1/B2 interpretable models and B3 learned models on the locked participant split; output the cohort-flow and complete-case/missingness table, the MFI incremental risk contrast, calibration and held-out uncertainty for walking pace and falls, the instance-3 unpooled sensitivity, and the frozen falsification report. Completion requires output-linked estimates and uncertainty; no fitted result is asserted in this proposal.

## 2. Why this advances inspected work

[K1] establishes broad health relevance of TMFI in UK Biobank but leaves functional incremental validity open. [K2] motivates composition as potentially informative for function, yet notes that myosteatosis is heterogeneous and often confounded by age, disease, disuse and body size. [K3] gives a clinical framework that combines strength, quantity/quality and performance, but its consensus recommendation is not an empirical test of whether the additional MRI measure changes functional classification.

This experiment adds a deliberately narrow, falsifiable boundary test: identical-visit posterior-thigh MFI, bilateral thigh fat-free volume and bilateral grip are entered in nested models for two clinically interpretable outcomes. The key contrast is not a small predictive score improvement; it is the adjusted absolute risk difference for slow pace when MFI is set to the training-derived 75th versus 25th percentile, with quantity and grip retained. A null result would be useful: it would argue that, for this population and measurement, quantity and grip capture most of the available same-visit functional information. A positive result would justify external validation and prospective functional testing, not immediate clinical use.

Leading explanation: MFI reflects lower-limb muscle quality and retains a residual relationship with functional limitation after quantity and grip adjustment. Strong rivals are: (1) quantity/body-size confounding, so MFI attenuates after fat-free volume, BMI and body-fat adjustment; (2) common age, disease, inactivity, pain or neurologic limitation, so the association is non-specific or disappears in age/body-composition sensitivity analyses; (3) measurement/selection, because MRI attendees with complete fields differ from the broader cohort; and (4) hand-grip mismeasurement, because upper-limb pain or neuropathy makes grip an imperfect comparator. Same-visit data cannot separate MFI causing disability from disability/disuse causing MFI, and no bound field provides pain, physical activity, neurologic examination or raw-image QC.

## 5. Estimand and analysis

For the primary slow-pace outcome Y, let C be age, BMI, body-fat percentage and visit calendar year, Q be thigh_quantity, G be grip_max, and M be MFI_mean. The primary standardized information contrast is:

Delta_MFI = E[ P(Y=1 | M=Q75, Q, G, C) - P(Y=1 | M=Q25, Q, G, C) ],

where Q25 and Q75 are calculated from the training split only and the expectation averages over the locked eligible population's observed Q, G and C. This is a model-standardized association contrast, not an intervention effect. Report Delta_MFI in percentage points with a 95% participant-bootstrap interval and the corresponding B1-to-B2 contrast. The secondary estimand repeats this for any fall. Report one-SD MFI contrasts as well because quartile contrasts are scale-dependent.

Models are:

- B0: age, BMI, body-fat percentage and calendar year, with prespecified restricted cubic splines for age and BMI.
- B1 baseline: B0 plus thigh_quantity and grip_max. This is the quantity/grip comparator.
- B2 primary incremental model: B1 plus linear MFI_mean.
- B3 learned alternative: a shallow gradient-boosted binary model using the identical participants, split and information set, represented by the raw bilateral MFI, four quantity, two grip, age, BMI, body fat and calendar-year fields plus deterministic summaries. A fixed small grid is selected on validation log loss/Brier score only. It can reveal stable MFI thresholds, side asymmetry or interactions with quantity/grip that B2 erases; effect plots are descriptive and not mechanisms.

All models use a deterministic eid-hash 70/15/15 participant split into train/validation/locked test, with event/date-stratum balance only after participant assignment. No participant may contribute to more than one split. Scaling, spline fitting, quartiles, imputation, tuning and thresholds use training data only. The locked test is read once. Fit and report both outcomes under the frozen specification; do not select the primary endpoint after viewing results.

Report event counts and prevalence, complete-case flow, B1/B2 coefficients and standardized contrasts, B3 held-out calibration intercept/slope, Brier score and AUROC, and paired participant-bootstrap intervals (at least 500, preferably 1,000). A 0.01 Brier change is secondary and is not clinical evidence without a stable MFI contrast. Repeat the frozen analysis at instance 3 without pooling or retuning; because it may reuse participants and has only 808 fully observed rows, it cannot serve as independent validation.

## 6. Falsification and interpretation rules

Run once under the frozen specification and retain adverse results.

1. Quantity/grip attenuation: if MFI loses direction or material size after Q and G are added, the claim that MFI adds information is not supported.
2. Body-composition sensitivity: compare B2 with and without body-fat percentage and, where overlap permits, visceral fat volume. Large attenuation identifies body-composition confounding or overadjustment as unresolved; it does not prove a mechanism.
3. Age and calendar sensitivity: compare flexible age with age bands and visit-year adjustment. A signal confined to one age band or date period is a boundary/selection finding, not a general functional marker.
4. Measurement structure: compare bilateral mean, each side, side difference, complete-four and minimum-three rules. A signal driven by one side or one missingness rule is adverse to a stable MFI interpretation.
5. Comparator limitation: repeat with grip_max, both grips, and grip residualized on age/body size. A large change shows that grip is an imperfect or collinear comparator; it does not prove lower-limb biology.
6. Outcome coding: repeat binary versus ordinal walking pace and falls coding, retain -3/-7 exclusion counts, and report whether the same direction appears for the two distinct outcomes. A pace-only result is not evidence for falls.
7. Selection: compare complete MRI attendees with all dated instance-2 rows and apply the observation-weight sensitivity. Failure of weight overlap or material instability makes the result inconclusive for the source population.
8. Leakage/permutation: shuffle MFI within age/BMI strata and permute outcomes once with fixed seeds. Any retained locked-test signal invalidates the corresponding computational result.
9. Later visit: repeat at instance 3 as a sensitivity. Concordance is supportive of transport across visit instances but is not independent replication; discordance is a reason to investigate selection and measurement timing.

Supportive result: in the locked instance-2 analysis, Delta_MFI for slow pace is positive with a 95% interval excluding zero and the prespecified point estimate reaches at least 5 percentage points (a planning threshold for clinical materiality, not a validated MCID); the direction is not removed by quantity/grip/body-composition/missingness sensitivities; no permutation signal appears; and the secondary falls result is directionally compatible or explicitly reported as null. B3 may reveal stable nonlinear structure, but predictive improvement alone cannot turn an adverse B2 result into support.

Adverse result: Delta_MFI is zero/reversed, below the planning materiality threshold, is explained by quantity/grip or missingness, is confined to a coding/side/date subgroup, or the same signal appears under permutation. This supports the rival that MRI MFI is redundant, confounded or selected, and the incremental-functional-information claim should be abandoned or narrowed.

Inconclusive result: wide intervals, too few falls, failure of source/date or overlap checks, unstable coding, non-overlapping observation weights, or inability to verify MFI measurement validity. An imprecise null is not a refutation. Continuing would require expert review of MRI segmentation/QC and functional coding, validated clinical assessments, or an external cohort. Clinical adjudication is required for disability severity, physical examination, pain/neurologic causes, raw-image measurement validity and generalization. The computation can check source binding, same-instance joins, cohort flow, model outputs, uncertainty and whether conclusions match those outputs; it cannot establish causality, mechanism, future falls risk or treatment benefit.

## 7. Alternatives, compute and limits

The interpretable nested models are retained because they directly answer the incremental question and expose attenuation after quantity and grip. The learned alternative is retained because threshold and component interactions are scientifically substantive possibilities: a linear mean MFI term can lose a high-risk tail or side-specific pattern. Both use the same source fields, participant split, targets and uncertainty evaluation. A transformer/sequence model is deferred because this is a one-visit question and no repeated dated trajectory is needed. A mechanistic model is deferred because raw images, histology, physical activity, pain, neurologic findings and treatment are unavailable. Revisit either only after those dependencies are admitted and the question changes.

The measured audit was CPU-only and completed in seconds for selected columns. Full model fitting and bootstrap are unmeasured; plan 4-8 CPUs, 16-32 GiB and under two hours, within the future solver planning envelope of 16 CPUs, 262,144 MiB and 28,800 seconds. No GPU is required or scientifically justified for these tabular models.
