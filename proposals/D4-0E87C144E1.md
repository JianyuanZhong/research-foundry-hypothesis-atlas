# UKB two-panel metabolic change and first transition to a second cardiometabolic domain

## Child repair and scientific deliverable

This child preserves the leading parents' verified two-panel UK Biobank binding and repairs a real estimand mismatch. The primary comparison is now restricted to participants who have exactly one coded cardiometabolic domain at the risk origin. Both the current-level Cox baseline and the transition-specific alternative use that identical population, landmark, endpoint, competing-death rule, preprocessing and participant split. A separate zero-domain 0→1 analysis is secondary and is never pooled into the primary comparison.

The solver must newly produce: a read-only source/header and overlap audit; a frozen ICD codebook and position-matched first-event-per-domain construction; participant flow through the one-domain landmark and washout; panel-change and missingness/selection summaries; fitted M0/M1 coefficients with uncertainty; locked five-year predictions, calibration, Brier and decision-curve outputs; route-specific cumulative-incidence results; and washout, gap, selection-weighting, competing-death, negative-control and within-state permutation falsifications. Completion is those computed outputs with uncertainty, not readiness or a successful fit.

## Question, evidence boundary and falsifiable hypothesis

The strongest claim supported by current evidence is that baseline metabolic measures are prognostically associated with later coded diabetes, cardiovascular or kidney disease, and that a subset has two dated laboratory panels. The available evidence does not establish that observed within-person change adds prognostic information after the latest level, or that it identifies illness deepening from one coded domain to a second.

Primary hypothesis:

> In UK Biobank participants aged 40–69 with exactly one of diabetes, cardiovascular disease or chronic kidney disease at a dated repeat-panel landmark, annualized worsening in HbA1c, triglycerides and/or HDL provides incremental prognostic information for the first transition to any second domain beyond the latest levels, existing-domain identity and prespecified covariates. The incremental association may differ by the existing domain and by the newly occurring domain, and should not be reproduced for acute appendicitis.

This is a prognostic association/prediction estimand, not a causal effect of changing a biomarker, evidence that monitoring improves outcomes, or a claim of chart-adjudicated disease. A secondary, separately labelled analysis compares 0→1 onset; it is not part of the primary hypothesis or its model-comparison denominator.

## Outcome phenotype and first-event construction

Before any locked outcome is inspected, freeze a codebook that normalizes case and whitespace, preserves decimal detail for audit, and matches by explicit ICD prefix:

- diabetes: ICD-10 prefixes E10, E11, E12, E13, E14; ICD-9 prefix 250;
- cardiovascular disease: ICD-10 prefixes I20–I25, I50, I60–I69; ICD-9 prefixes 410–414 (ischaemic heart disease), 428 (heart failure), and 430–438 (cerebrovascular disease);
- chronic kidney disease: ICD-10 prefixes N18 and N19; ICD-9 prefix 585;
- negative control: acute appendicitis, ICD-10 prefix K35 and ICD-9 prefix 540.

The solver must record the exact normalized-code rule, counts by code family, and any code that is not assigned. It must iterate all suffixes in both complete arrays, pair diagnosis and date at the identical suffix, require a valid parsed date, and retain code-only/date-only mismatches in the audit without allowing either to create an event. A domain's first event is the minimum valid paired date over all codes assigned to that domain, not the first array position. This is a first coded occurrence, not a confirmed clinical onset; no narrative extraction or unverified phenotype algorithm is substituted.

For each participant, define domain state at a time t from these first dates. Baseline history uses only events dated on or before t. If two domains first occur on the same date, both are recorded and the participant moves directly to the two-domain state; for the primary one-domain risk set this is a washout exclusion if it occurs by risk origin. If a domain event and death share a date during follow-up, death is the absorbing event in the primary competing-risk convention; a tie-rule sensitivity may report the reverse convention.

## Population, timing and censoring

Eligibility is age 40–69 inclusive at recruitment, valid sex, finite field-valid HbA1c/TG/HDL at both main instances 0 and 1, valid assessment dates d0 and d1 with d1>d0 and a primary gap of 365–3,650 days, and no contradiction in nonmissing assessment dates 53-0.0 through 53-3.0. Main 53-0/1 dates are an integrity cross-check; disagreement after date normalization is a data-quality failure, not a silent choice.

The biological change is computed only from the two observed panels:
`g_j=(x_{j,1}-x_{j,0})/((d1-d0)/365.25)`; HDL is sign-reversed so worsening is positive. Training-partition means and SDs standardize changes. No instance label is treated as elapsed time, and no unavailable instances 2/3 are imputed or synthesized.

The index is d1. The primary risk origin is d1+365 days. First construct domain history through d1, then exclude anyone with two or more domains on or before d1, anyone who dies on or before d1, and anyone with a second-domain event or death in (d1, risk origin]. The primary population is therefore exactly one domain at risk origin, with the identity retained. This explicitly prevents a washout event from being silently discarded while the person remains in a one-domain risk set. Follow-up begins strictly after risk origin and ends at the first second-domain event, death, administrative end, or five years after risk origin. The administrative end is the maximum valid trusted outcome date, reported with the number having a full five-year opportunity. Sensitivities use 0, 2 and 5 years of washout and 182–5,475-day panel gaps.

Death is an absorbing competing event. Cause-specific models censor at death for the domain hazard and report competing-risk cumulative incidence; the multistate model includes death as an explicit absorbing state. Full follow-up from d1 is secondary and cannot replace the delayed-entry primary analysis.

Use deterministic participant hash buckets 0–59 for fitting, 60–69 for all model/threshold decisions, 70–79 for one locked test evaluation, and 80–99 reserved and never read for fitting or threshold selection. Split by eid, never by rows, dates or events; record the namespace and hash construction without publishing eids.

## Missingness and selection estimand

The primary estimand is conditional on complete two-panel core-marker availability and the stated dated landmark: the change coefficients and five-year prediction performance among participants who actually have two usable panels. It is not silently generalized to all UKB participants.

Among participants with a valid panel-0 core panel, define repeat availability S=1 only when panel-1 HbA1c, TG, HDL and d1 pass the same field/date rules. Fit an availability model using panel-0 values, age, sex, BMI, smoking and BP only. Report S by age, sex and panel-0 risk strata, missingness patterns, positivity and weight diagnostics. If positivity is adequate, repeat the primary fit with stabilized inverse-probability-of-S weights truncated at the 1st/99th percentiles; label this as a transported/weighted association estimand, not a causal correction. It cannot recover unmeasured repeat-testing indication, treatment, adherence or socioeconomic selection. No imputation of core markers or outcome dates is permitted. Secondary covariate missingness uses field-specific validity rules and indicators only when prespecified; negative numeric values are never globally recoded.

## Matched baseline and substantive alternative

M0 is a current-level cause-specific Cox baseline for the same one-domain-at-origin participants and same first transition to any second domain, with death competing. Inputs are latest main HbA1c `30750-1.0`, TG `30870-1.0`, HDL `30760-1.0`; existing-domain identity; population age/sex; latest assessment BMI, BP, smoking and center dated no later than d1; panel gap; and prespecified missingness indicators. Fit training-frozen splines, robust participant-level uncertainty and no outcome-driven selection. Creatinine `30700-1.0` and cystatin-C `30720-1.0` are a secondary extension, not primary inputs.

M1 is a transition-specific multistate alternative fitted on exactly the same primary population and split. Its states are one-domain identity (diabetes-first, CVD-first or CKD-first), each of the possible second-domain states, and death. It estimates separate 1→2 route hazards (existing-domain/new-domain) and death hazards, using all M0 inputs plus the three dated changes; changes may interact with existing-domain identity only by a prespecified training rule. Primary M1 predictions are aggregated to five-year risk of any second domain and compared with M0 on the same participants. Thus M1 adds scientifically meaningful information about which illness-deepening route the change signal follows; it does not answer a different 0→1 question. A separate zero-domain 0→1 analysis may be reported with its own risk set and no claim that it validates the primary comparison.

Evaluate coefficients, joint change null, route-specific hazards, cumulative incidence, five-year Brier, calibration slope/intercept, integrated calibration error, discrimination, RMST and paired participant-bootstrap intervals. Preserve uncertainty from the locked test bootstrap and record seeds and indices.

## Thresholds and clinical interpretation

The 5%, 10% and 20% predicted five-year risks are fixed operating-point sensitivities, inherited as a useful grid from the parent design; none is asserted to be a validated clinical cutoff. At each point report threshold crossings, alert volume, sensitivity, specificity, PPV, number needed to review, calibration and decision-curve net benefit using the corresponding harm/benefit odds. No threshold is selected from the test set.

A positive net-benefit result at a grid point is computationally checkable but is not by itself clinical utility. A clinically meaningful threshold requires an expert-specified review action, acceptable alert burden and harm/benefit tradeoff, none of which is available in these files. Therefore the solver must state whether results are supportive, adverse or inconclusive for the prognostic question, and separately mark threshold transport/policy value as requiring clinical expert review and ideally an implementation study. Do not convert an arbitrary 0.01 Brier difference or a 10% operating point into a recommendation.

## Falsification and interpretation

Supportive evidence requires a prespecified worsening-direction joint association after latest levels and gap, route-specific estimates with uncertainty compatible with the proposed transition heterogeneity, locked-test improvement in calibration/Brier or decision-curve net benefit without subgroup calibration harm, persistence under washout/gap/missingness/selection-weighting and competing-death sensitivities, a null or materially weaker appendicitis signal, and no comparable within-state/sex/gap-bin permutation signal.

Adverse evidence is a null or reverse-direction joint change association, loss after current levels and gap, a comparable appendicitis signal, a permutation signal, or a locked performance/calibration deterioration. This falsifies the incremental prognostic claim in the selected population; it does not prove that biomarker change is harmless or that no causal mechanism exists.

Inconclusive evidence includes insufficient complete panels or 1→2 events, code/date mismatch, inadequate repeat-availability positivity, wide intervals spanning clinically relevant benefit and no benefit, or severe subgroup calibration failure. A threshold result without a clinically specified action is inconclusive for policy value even if its computation is valid.

Automatically checkable claims are source paths/hashes, columns, joins, field rules, code/date pairing, first-event dates, cohort counts, timing exclusions, split integrity, model fits, uncertainty, predictions, calibration, Brier, decision curves, permutations and sensitivity outputs. Clinical adjudication, exact assay dictionaries, treatment changes, repeat-assessment indication, external validation, threshold transport and intervention benefit are unavailable and require expert review or another study.
