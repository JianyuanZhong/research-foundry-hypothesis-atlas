# Scientific Proposal: Asynchronous Organ Recovery Predicts Post-Stabilization Deterioration

## Unresolved Question

Current critical care practice relies heavily on single-point SOFA (Sequential Organ Failure Assessment) scores to assess recovery after circulatory stabilization. However, organ systems may recover at different rates and through different mechanisms, creating a critical blind spot: patients whose kidney or respiratory function remains impaired after hemodynamic stability may be at elevated risk of subsequent deterioration, yet this discordant recovery pattern is not captured by standard monitoring.

## Clinical Importance

This question has direct clinical importance because:

1. **Current practice limitation**: Single SOFA assessments at admission or discharge do not capture the dynamic, asynchronous nature of organ recovery
2. **Patient safety**: Patients with discordant organ recovery may receive inappropriate de-escalation of care or be at risk of delayed deterioration
3. **Resource allocation**: Identifying high-risk patients with discordant recovery could inform extended monitoring and escalation strategies
4. **Mechanistic insight**: Understanding which organ systems drive subsequent deterioration could reveal pathophysiological mechanisms of critical illness progression

## Substantive Advance Over Existing Knowledge

Existing literature focuses on:
- Single time-point SOFA scores as prognostic markers
- Aggregate organ failure scores without temporal dynamics
- Cross-sectional comparisons of organ function

This proposal advances by:
1. **Temporal granularity**: Using repeated measurements to define recovery trajectories rather than single assessments
2. **Discordant recovery phenotype**: Identifying patients where one organ system recovers while another remains impaired after initial stabilization
3. **Mechanistic specificity**: Testing whether specific patterns of discordant recovery (e.g., kidney recovery without respiratory improvement) predict different outcomes than aggregate failure
4. **Clinical actionability**: Providing a framework for monitoring that captures recovery heterogeneity

## Hypothesis

**Primary hypothesis**: After circulatory stabilization in patients with sepsis or shock, those exhibiting discordant organ recovery (kidney or respiratory function remaining impaired while other systems recover) have significantly higher risks of subsequent deterioration (requiring re-intubation, renal replacement therapy, or death) compared to patients with synchronous recovery or no organ impairment.

**Secondary hypothesis**: The magnitude and timing of discordant recovery (how long after stabilization organ impairment persists) predict the timing and severity of subsequent deterioration.

## Population

- **Source**: MIMIC-IV database
- **Inclusion**: ICU patients with documented infection or shock (ICD-10 codes for sepsis, septic shock, or hypotension requiring vasopressors)
- **Exclusion**: Patients who die within 24 hours of admission, patients with missing organ support data
- **Study period**: All available MIMIC-IV admissions (2016-2021)
- **Sample**: Approximately 50,000-100,000 ICU patients meeting criteria

## Baseline Comparison

**Baseline model**: Traditional single-point SOFA assessment at admission or discharge
- Uses one SOFA score to classify patients as "stable" vs "unstable"
- Does not capture recovery dynamics

**Proposed model**: Discordant recovery phenotype
- Defines recovery trajectories across multiple organ systems
- Identifies patients with discordant recovery patterns
- Provides temporal risk stratification

## Analysis Plan

1. **Population selection**: Identify patients with documented sepsis/shock requiring ICU admission using ICD-10 codes from hosp/d_icd_diagnoses
2. **Circulatory stabilization definition**: Define as time from admission to first stable SOFA score or first vasopressor-free period
3. **Recovery assessment**: Calculate time to recovery for each organ system:
   - Kidney: Time to creatinine <1.5 mg/dL or urine output >0.5 mL/kg/hr for 6 hours
   - Respiratory: Time to ventilator weaning or FiO2 <35% for 12 hours
   - Cardiovascular: Time to vasopressor discontinuation
4. **Discordant recovery identification**: Patients where ≥2 organ systems show delayed recovery after stabilization
5. **Outcome analysis**: Cox proportional hazards models comparing discordant vs non-discordant recovery groups
6. **Temporal analysis**: Kaplan-Meier curves for time to deterioration by recovery pattern

## Falsification Criteria

The hypothesis would be **falsified** if:
1. Discordant recovery patterns show no significant association with subsequent deterioration (p > 0.05 after adjustment)
2. Single-point SOFA scores perform equally well or better than discordant recovery phenotypes for predicting deterioration (net reclassification improvement <2%)
3. Discordant recovery patterns are equally common across all patient subgroups (suggesting no selective risk)

The hypothesis would be **supported** if:
1. Discordant recovery patterns significantly increase risk of subsequent deterioration (adjusted HR >1.5, p < 0.001)
2. Discordant recovery patterns provide incremental predictive value beyond single-point SOFA scores (net reclassification improvement >5%)
3. Specific discordant patterns (e.g., kidney recovery without respiratory improvement) show distinct risk profiles

The hypothesis would be **inconclusive** if:
1. Discordant recovery patterns show weak associations (HR 1.1-1.3) that may be clinically meaningful but not statistically significant
2. Results are confounded by hospital-level factors or data collection practices
3. Discordant recovery patterns are rare (<5% of population) limiting statistical power

## Method Comparison

### Baseline: Single-Point SOFA Assessment
- **Approach**: Use admission or discharge SOFA score as proxy for recovery status
- **Limitations**: Static assessment, ignores recovery dynamics, may misclassify patients with discordant recovery
- **What it reveals**: Overall organ failure burden at a single time point

### Proposed: Discordant Recovery Phenotype
- **Approach**: Define temporal recovery trajectories for multiple organ systems; identify discordant patterns
- **Advantages**: Captures heterogeneity in recovery, identifies high-risk subgroups, provides actionable monitoring framework
- **What it reveals**: Which organ systems are lagging in recovery, when deterioration risk increases

### Scientific Value
The proposed approach provides mechanistic insight into recovery heterogeneity that the baseline cannot capture. While both approaches predict overall outcomes, only the discordant recovery phenotype can inform specific monitoring and escalation strategies for patients with particular recovery patterns.
