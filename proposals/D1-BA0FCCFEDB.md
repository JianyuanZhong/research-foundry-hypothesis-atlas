# Support-dependent normalization and early failure after ICU discharge

## Scientific deliverable

The future solver must newly construct a leakage-safe adult first-ICU-discharge cohort; derive one-hour physiologic, observation-mask, infusion-interval and respiratory-support-transition features; fit an auditable competing-risk baseline and a constrained latent recovery-state alternative; and report locked held-out estimates. Completion requires: an exclusion/cohort table; feature and support-evidence coverage tables; prespecified support-context counts; held-out predictions for first ICU return and first death; Aalen-Johansen cumulative-incidence, adjusted association, calibration and uncertainty outputs; latent emission/transition diagnostics; falsification ablations; and a conclusion-to-output map. Readiness checks and the discovery probe are feasibility evidence only, not clinical findings.

## Unresolved question and falsifiable hypothesis

The prior valid design showed why residual respiratory and hemodynamic burden at ICU outtime could be clinically important, but it left an interpretation problem: when final MAP and respiratory burden look normalized, is that stable recovery or normalization maintained by support that is still active or was withdrawn only recently? This matters for the decision to transfer a patient to a less monitored setting and for targeting early ward surveillance.

Primary hypothesis: among adults at a valid first ICU-discharge landmark with adequate and comparable final-six-hour physiologic coverage and low final MAP/respiratory burden, patients with evidence of active vasoactive or ventilatory support, or support evidence in the preceding six hours that ends before outtime, have a different (prespecified directional expectation: higher) 48-hour cumulative incidence of first ICU return than patients with the same low burden and no recorded support evidence in the final 12 hours. The direction for first death is not assumed; death is a competing event and is estimated separately. A secondary hypothesis is that ordered support withdrawal and physiologic persistence improve calibrated competing-event estimation over a snapshot phenotype.

The strongest claim supported before fitting is data feasibility, not an association: the catalog and source archive contain dated ICU stays, admissions/death times, charted MAP/respiratory measurements, infusion intervals and procedure records. The parent’s inherited feasibility scan reported 70,138 eligible first-hospital-ICU landmarks, 3,872 48-hour ICU returns, 856 exact-timestamp deaths and 4,630 composite events; these counts are not evidence for this child’s support-context association. Support prevalence, valid interval coverage, outcome counts after the child’s exclusions, positivity, model convergence and effect estimates must be recomputed. The corrected episode probe is an additional declared feasibility artifact if it completes.

## Population, index and temporal boundaries

Use MIMIC-IV adults with hosp/patients.anchor_age >=18, retaining the dataset’s >89 top-code limitation. Within each subject_id and hadm_id, sort icu/icustays by intime and retain the first valid ICU stay. Require non-null intime and outtime, outtime > intime, a linked hosp/admissions row, and admissions.dischtime > outtime so the patient is discharged from ICU before the hospitalization ends. Exclude impossible joins and times, and retain an auditable count for every exclusion. Split by subject_id so no patient contributes to more than one split.

Index time is icu/icustays.outtime. All predictors must have event/chart time strictly before outtime. Physiologic records use icu/chartevents.charttime in [outtime-24 hours, outtime), binned into one-hour intervals; storetime is not used to move observations across the index. The primary support context uses [outtime-12 hours,outtime-6 hours) as the preceding window and [outtime-6 hours,outtime) as the final window. An interval is active in a bin only if its starttime is before the bin end and its endtime is after the bin start. Null or non-increasing endpoints are invalid for interval exposure and are counted, not silently made active.

Follow each index to min(outtime+48 hours, admissions.dischtime). Define first ICU return as a later icu/icustays.intime for the same subject_id and hadm_id, strictly after outtime and within the horizon. Define first death as admissions.deathtime after outtime and within the horizon; if return and death are tied, classify death first. Otherwise censor at the administrative boundary. Primary estimands are 48-hour cumulative incidence of first ICU return and first death as competing events. Cause-specific hazard ratios and the parent composite are secondary. Call the endpoint ICU return, not unplanned readmission: intent is unavailable, and hospital_expire_flag is not an event time.

## Exposure construction and estimand

Resample selected chart streams to one-hour medians plus observed/not-observed masks. Define low physiologic burden in a window as less than one third of observed MAP bins below 65 mmHg and less than one third of observed respiratory-burden bins (FiO2 >=0.40 or O2 flow >=4 L/min); retain arterial and non-invasive MAP and FiO2/O2 flow as separate sensitivity channels. Require at least two observed bins in each six-hour half-window for the primary contrast. Mark inadequate coverage as unavailable and report it.

Construct support evidence in three non-equivalent layers: (1) active vasoactive infusion from valid inputevents interval overlap; (2) respiratory procedure transition evidence from valid intubation/extubation/NIV intervals, never interpreted as continuous support without overlap; and (3) charted respiratory support evidence from FiO2/O2-flow/PEEP values. The primary context has four prespecified categories among adequate-coverage, low-burden patients: stable off-support (no valid vasoactive overlap and no respiratory support/transition evidence in the final 12 hours); active/recently withdrawn support (valid support evidence in the final six hours or in the preceding window that ends before outtime); residual physiology/support (final burden at least one third in either domain, analyzed secondarily with support layers retained); and mixed/unavailable. Because absence of chart evidence is not proof that support was absent, the primary contrast is a prognostic support-documentation context, not an assigned treatment.

The primary estimand is the adjusted difference in 48-hour competing cumulative incidence of first ICU return between active/recently withdrawn and stable-off-support contexts, within the low-burden, adequate-coverage population. Estimate first-death incidence in the same strata without assuming the return direction. Use the four-category analysis, support-layer sensitivity, and seven-day ICU return as secondary estimands.

## Baseline and substantive alternative

The simple baseline is two cause-specific Cox models for first ICU return and first death using support context, parent respiratory/hemodynamic burden, early-to-late burden change, final values, observation masks, age, gender, ICU LOS, first/last careunit, admission_type and a small prespecified admission covariate set. A nested snapshot model removes early-window and support-transition features. Estimate Aalen-Johansen cumulative incidence, absolute risk differences, cause-specific associations, calibration intercept/slope, IPCW Brier scores and subject-level bootstrap confidence intervals.

The substantive alternative is a constrained four-state hourly latent recovery model fitted only on training data with observed MAP/respiratory streams, support layers and masks as emissions. States are interpreted operationally as stable-off-support, normalized-on-support/recent-withdrawal, residual respiratory, and residual hemodynamic/dual burden. Use sparse transitions and state-specific discrete hazards for eight post-index six-hour bins; fit by EM or a documented Bayesian equivalent and propagate posterior state probabilities. Evaluate locked held-out competing-event risks using the same patient-level stratified train/validation/test splits and three split seeds. Report state emission/transition stability, calibration, IPCW cause-specific and integrated Brier scores, separation and bootstrap uncertainty. Thresholds and state definitions are fixed or learned on training data only.

The latent alternative can reveal persistence, support withdrawal followed by destabilization, cross-domain order and uncertainty in state membership that the fixed-feature Cox model loses. It is chosen because this is a scientific question about recovery-state interpretation, not a small predictive leaderboard gain. A masked GRU/temporal convolution was considered because the demonstrations show value in dated order and masks; it is deferred because the parent already tested a masked sequence comparison and this child’s new uncertainty is the validity and persistence of support contexts. Revisit it if the latent model fails convergence or state compression demonstrably loses clinically relevant order.

## Falsification and interpretation

Pre-specified falsification includes: permuting hourly physiology/support order within stay; mask-only and mask-removed models; removing support channels; physiology-only and support-only models; replacing interval overlap with storetime/last-value coding; arterial versus non-invasive MAP and FiO2 versus O2-flow sensitivity; threshold/window sensitivity; careunit/admission-type strata; and a pre-index negative-control horizon in which the recorded transition cannot cause a post-index event. A contrast that vanishes after support-channel removal, mask removal, or correction from storetime to interval time suggests documentation, care-context or timestamp bias rather than recovery-state evidence. Repeat analyses with procedure rows excluded from continuous-support definitions.

Supportive results require adequate coverage, non-positivity checks passed, a reproducible clinically meaningful active/recent-versus-off-support separation for ICU return, uncertainty excluding a prespecified null or clinical margin, and latent state/transition signals that survive order, mask and support-layer falsification while improving held-out competing-event calibration. This would support a prognostic interpretation and justify clinician-adjudicated prospective validation or a future causal study; it would not show that continuing support or delaying discharge improves outcomes.

Adverse results include null or reversed contrasts, rare or unstable strata, no calibration/state improvement, convergence failure, or disappearance after timestamp, support-layer, mask or care-context controls. These falsify the operationalized support-dependent-normalization hypothesis or show it is not reliable enough for discharge-monitoring decisions. Inconclusive results include inadequate coverage, non-positivity, unreliable interval endpoints, rare events, wide intervals crossing the clinical margin, or unstable competing-risk fits; report those as uncertainty and do not select a rule.

MIMIC cannot adjudicate bedside work of breathing, actual ventilator settings between chart events, clinician intent, planned versus unplanned ICU return, discharge rationale, staffing, treatment limitations/goals of care, or whether delaying discharge/continuing support changes outcome. Notes are not a validated substitute. Expert review, support/discharge-readiness adjudication, external validation in another hospital (eICU is available but not an independent partition here), and a prospective or causal study are required for clinical adoption.
