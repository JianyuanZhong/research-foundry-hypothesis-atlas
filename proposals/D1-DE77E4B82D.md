# Is the LATE component of the MAP<65 burden an independent time-to-correction signal, or a proxy for a concurrently deteriorating patient?

Lead working proposal — eICU 2.0 island, **episode 38**. Operation: **evolve** of the valid,
delivery-complete board leader `[prior hypothesis]` (mu 32.42,
rubric `life-science-stage-aware-v1`). Status: **design owned and frozen by the Lead for
compilation**; discovery executed only read-only literature receipts and (this episode)

## 1. Unresolved question and why it matters
ICU teams titrate fluids/vasopressors to a MAP target. The parent
(`[prior hypothesis]`) decomposed the (0,24]h MAP<65 burden into
EARLY (0,12]h and LATE (12,24]h components and asks whether LATE burden adds follow-up
KDIGO-AKI risk conditional on total burden and depth — separating **H-dose** (cumulative
ischemic dose; no timing effect) from **H-persistence** (unresolved hypoperfusion /
time-to-correction). The closest inspected work [K1] reports only total MAP<65 burden and
depth (time-weighted area) and does **not** decompose timing, so it cannot separate these.

**What the parent leaves untested (this child's advance):** the LATE window (12,24]h is
temporally *adjacent to the outcome window* (>24h). A positive LATE coefficient is
therefore equally compatible with a third explanation — **H-trajectory**: patients whose
global physiology is already deteriorating (rising lactate, rising creatinine, escalating
vasopressor requirement in (0,24]h) accumulate MAP<65 time *and* meet the AKI outcome for
reasons not attributable to the *timing* of their hypotension. Static admission severity
(`apachescore`, `predictedicumortality`) does not capture this **time-varying** confounding.
This is decision-relevant: if H-persistence survives concurrent-trajectory adjustment,
time-to-MAP-correction is a plausible monitoring target; if the LATE signal is fully
absorbed by H-trajectory, timing adds no information beyond "this patient is getting
sicker", and a MAP-timing monitoring target would be misguided (cf. the live
permissive-hypotension design question [K2]). [K3] demonstrates that perfusion-adequacy
framings routinely co-move with metabolic trajectory (lactate), which is exactly the
coupling this child tests rather than assumes.

## 3. Baseline vs substantive alternative (same question)
- **Baseline (= parent's alternative, H-dose vs H-persistence only):** hospital-FE logistic
  with depth + total burden + EARLY + LATE (+ depth interaction). The LATE coefficient is the
  H-persistence readout.
- **Substantive alternative (this child, H-trajectory):** the *same* model **plus** the
  pre-specified concurrent-trajectory covariates. The scientific question is whether the LATE
  coefficient **attenuates toward the null** once concurrent deterioration is accounted for.
- **What the alternative reveals that the baseline loses:** whether the LATE timing contrast is
  an independent exposure-timing quantity or a proxy for global physiologic deterioration —
  i.e. whether "late hypotension" carries information beyond "the patient is decompensating".
- **Estimand / analysis:** change in the LATE log-odds coefficient and its 95% CI between the
  two nested models (attenuation delta, with a likelihood-ratio test on the added trajectory
  block); reported with a depth-stratified LATE OR and a leave-one-hospital-out (LOHO) transport
  check inherited from the parent.
- **Budget (estimate, unverified):** CPU only; tabular fits over ~1e5 stays; well inside the
  solver envelope (<=16 CPU / 64 GB; <<8h). No GPU required for the primary; not excluded.

## 4. Falsification criteria
- **Supportive (H-persistence survives):** LATE coefficient > 0 with 95% CI excluding 0 AND
  attenuation delta < the pre-registered threshold (|ΔlogOR| < 0.5 × baseline LATE logOR, i.e.
  <50% attenuation) after adding trajectory covariates.
- **Adverse (H-trajectory explains it):** LATE coefficient 95% CI includes 0, or attenuation
  delta >= 50% of baseline LATE logOR (trajectory block absorbs the timing signal).
- **Inconclusive:** CI crossing 0 with a wide interval / small LATE-stratum effective sample;
  reported as underpowered rather than as evidence for either mechanism.
- **Separability from rivals:** parent's R-DETECT (creatinine-draw intensity), R-SEV (static
  severity), R-PRACTICE (hospital FE) and treatment-intensity arm are retained; a placebo
  timing split is retained. A null LATE after trajectory adjustment is a *useful negative*
  (timing target not supported), not a failed study.
- **Not established by this design:** causality; a MAP-target policy claim; external validity
  beyond eICU 2.0.

## 5. Strongest claim the evidence supports now vs what the experiment tests
- **Supported now (by [K1] and the parent):** cumulative MAP<65 burden and depth associate with
  downstream organ injury in ICU/perioperative cohorts (association, not mechanism).
- **Untested claim this experiment addresses:** the *timing* (LATE) component is an independent
  time-to-correction signal rather than a proxy for concurrent physiologic deterioration.

## 6. Honest limits / what needs other evidence
- Readiness only: the estimator, LHO, trajectory covariate construction and placebo split are
  NOT executed here. No scientific result is claimed.
- eICU-internal LOHO transport only; MAP is a 5-min median; invasive vs cuff MAP unrecorded;
  `labname` not harmonised; NEE-equivalent conversion approximate.
- Trajectory covariates (delta-lactate, delta-creatinine, vasopressor trend, fluid-balance rate)
  are *proxies* for time-varying severity; residual time-varying confounding by unmeasured
  factors (e.g., source control of sepsis) is possible and would require clinical adjudication or
  an external cohort to address.
