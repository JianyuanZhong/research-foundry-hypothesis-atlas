# Episode 23 child — respiratory records versus latent physiology and treatment timing

Status: planned substantive child of [prior hypothesis]. This is a design and source-validation proposal. It reports no cohort count, fitted coefficient, prediction, metric, confidence interval, or scientific result.

## Clinical opening and falsifiable claim

The parent asks whether an outcome-blind respiratory recording block adds a held-out association with a later strict recorded respiratoryCare interval-start after generic nursing/vital/care-plan process, hospital regime, state, and continuation selection are controlled, and whether the same block fails to predict a strict non-respiratory nursing endpoint. The strongest remaining clinical rival is that the respiratory block is a proxy for unmeasured respiratory physiology or a bedside treatment decision whose timing is only imperfectly represented in eICU.

The strongest evidence available supports only bounded claims. Pollard et al. [K1] describe eICU as a multi-center archive containing structured vital, treatment and care documentation while bounding the completeness of archived bedside monitoring. Rajkomar et al. [K2] support temporally structured EHR representations and held-out-center evaluation, not a physiologic or treatment mechanism. Che et al. [K3] show that observation masks and missingness can carry predictive information, not that missingness or a respiratory row is a biological state. None validates a respiratory row as physiologic onset, a treatment row as administration, or an offset as bedside event time.

The unresolved claim is:

In the parent’s exact adult first-ICU/S6 population, does the respiratory-process increment for the strict recorded respiratoryCare start remain after a prespecified cross-stream decomposition of recorded vital changes and lead-lag timing, or is it concentrated in a vital-change to respiratory-record pattern compatible with latent respiratory severity or in treatment-offset/entry-timing artifacts?

The physiology-rival prediction is that recorded respiratory rows follow a pre-existing concordant change in recorded sao2, respiration or noninvasivemean, and that adding the vital trajectory plus cross-stream concordance attenuates the parent’s respiratory increment. The recorded-process/timing prediction is that respiratory rows or treatment labels precede or coincide with subsequent recorded vital changes, the respiratory increment remains after inherited vital state, and treatment/entry audits expose ties, backfill, or post-change concentration. Either pattern remains compatible with unmeasured confounding; neither establishes physiology, treatment delivery, indication, mechanism, deterioration, or causality.

This changes the next clinical decision. A stable residual signal with a reproducible lead-lag pattern merits expert linkage to orders, device logs, administration records and bedside time. A vital-change-preceded signal redirects the next study toward physiologic adjudication. A timing-dominated signal argues for provenance calibration. This is a noncausal recorded-association experiment, not a treatment rule.

## Cross-stream lead-lag and physiologic-concordance decomposition

Construct all streams in 30-minute bins b=0,...,11, retaining row IDs, raw offsets, duplicate flags, occupancy, counts, last offset and missingness. Define V as the inherited six-channel vital state. For respiratory concordance specifically use the three recorded channels sao2, respiration and noninvasivemean; do not call them a respiratory gold standard. Define R as respiratoryCare interval/status rows and respiratoryCharting clinical-offset rows, including entry-offset lag, backfill, duplicate, exact-tie and malformed flags. Define T as treatment rows with a frozen, training-fold respiratory/support and non-respiratory string vocabulary, counts, occupancy, first/last treatmentoffset, duplicates and string/offset audit. activeupondischarge remains a recorded field, never administration. G is the inherited generic process block from nurseCharting, vital observation counts, carePlanEOL and hospital regime, plus parent state and selection variables.

For every bin with two adjacent finite vital medians, define a recorded directional-change indicator using predeclared signs: lower sao2, higher respiration, or lower noninvasivemean. Magnitude thresholds are frozen inside each training fold from the fitting-fold distribution of absolute 30-minute changes; require two of three channels for V_resp_change and retain per-channel and missingness indicators. A sensitivity uses continuous standardized changes without thresholds. This is a measurement construct, not a diagnosis.

For each pair, create only outcome-blind features with both offsets within [0,360]:
1. V→R: V_resp_change in b and first R row in b+1 or b+2, with lag, source and treatment co-occurrence.
2. R→V: R in b and V_resp_change in b+1 or b+2.
3. same-bin: R and V_resp_change in the same bin, with exact offset tie and nearest-offset distance where finite.
4. T→V, V→T and treatment/R ties using treatmentoffset; label these offset-order associations only because treatment has no entry timestamp.
5. R and T recency, total/occupied bins, early [0,180] and late [300,360] counts, late-minus-early rate, source completeness and entry-lag summaries.

Pairs crossing 360 are censored, not wrapped. R rows without valid offsets remain in a quality audit and cannot supply lead-lag order. No R row is called a treatment and no V_resp_change is called physiologic deterioration.

Nested transparent models are M0: parent baseline plus V, G, hospital H/D and exact selection audit; M1: M0 plus raw R/T; M2: M1 plus V→R, R→V, same-bin and treatment timing; M3: M2 plus continuous/thresholded concordance interaction with missingness. The primary contrasts are held-out M2-M1 and attenuation of the parent respiratory increment after M2/M3, repeated identically for N_start_doc. The V→R versus R→V comparison is descriptive, not mediation or causality.

## Falsification and interpretation

Freeze source/header/schema/row-ID hashes, cohort and endpoint manifests, vocabulary, folds, V thresholds, lead-lag definitions, weights, null seeds and output names before fitting.

1. Vital-state attenuation: compare M1 with M2/M3. V→R dominance plus attenuation supports a latent-physiology-compatible explanation, not proof of physiology.
2. Direction and timing: compare V→R, R→V and same-bin terms. Concentration after the endpoint, exact ties, or entry offsets later than clinical offsets is adverse to pre-landmark interpretation. Treatment associations at ties or only after V changes are adverse to bedside-timing claims.
3. Entry/backfill: report respiratoryCharting clinical-to-entry lag, positive/negative lag, duplicates, same-offset and missing-offset rows separately. Treatment has no entry timestamp, so no treatment association can be called delivery or order timing.
4. Generic endpoint swap: repeat M0–M3 for N_start_doc. Similar increments support workflow; respiratory selectivity is required for a source-specific recorded interpretation.
5. Source-swap null: within hospital × start-state × generic-density strata, permute whole R/T trajectories while retaining V, generic schedule, total density and endpoint risk set. Persistence indicates density leakage; disappearance is required for source-specific evidence.
6. Lead-lag null: cyclically shift the 12-bin R/T sequence by a frozen nonzero number of bins. Similar results indicate density or timing artifact.
7. Vital-sequence null: permute V trajectories within hospital × start-state × V-density strata while preserving masks and marginal distributions. Persistence of cross-stream terms suggests source-density artifact.
8. Boundary and selection: repeat with [0,330]+gap and unweighted/W6/W6^G/W6^{G+R}; failed positivity, low ESS, one-hospital dominance, unstable vocabulary or fewer than five hospitals makes the affected estimand inconclusive.
9. Measurement sensitivity: compare continuous and thresholded concordance without choosing after outcomes.

Support for the physiology rival requires a predeclared V→R pattern, attenuation after V terms, held-out replication and valid positivity/ESS. Support for a respiratory-specific recorded process requires a stable pre-landmark residual R increment, reproducible R→V or same-bin structure, respiratory selectivity over N_start_doc, loss under source/lead-lag permutations and no post-start/backfill concentration. Neither supports treatment delivery, physiologic onset or causality.

Adverse results include similar respiratory and nursing increments, persistence under permutations, disappearance after generic/V adjustment, post-landmark/entry-lag concentration, treatment-only dependence, W6 attenuation or non-transport. These favor workflow, selection, backfill or measurement and do not refute true physiology. Inconclusive results include sparse source×hospital×bin cells, unresolved interval semantics, unstable V thresholds, low ESS/positivity, broad hospital-cluster intervals, missing respiratory offsets or treatment’s absent entry timestamp. An imprecise null is not refutation.

## Deliverable and evidence limits

The new deliverable is a frozen outcome-blind held-out comparison that estimates V→R/R→V/same-bin and treatment-offset decomposition and its attenuation of the parent respiratory contrast. Completion requires source/header/row-ID audit; adult/all-eligible/S6 flow; strict endpoint and negative-control manifests; 12-bin V/R/T/G table; threshold and continuous concordance manifests; W6 audit; held-out M0–M3 predictions for both endpoints; uncertainty; falsification tables; and a claim-to-output report.

Elastic-net is primary because grouped source and lead-lag contrasts are inspectable. The temporal convolution is retained because it can expose short-lived cross-stream order and nonlinear synchrony. A raw transformer is deferred because it would obscure the source/timing decomposition and treatment lacks an entry timestamp. Causal treatment-effect, ventilator-state and mechanistic physiology models are deferred because treatment order/administration/dose/delivery/indication, device state, FiO2, raw waveforms and bedside event time are unavailable. Revisit only after expert adjudication.
