# Is nighttime ICU discharge risk concentrated among patients still physiologically unstable?

## Scientific deliverable and substantive advance

The future solver must newly construct a leakage-safe eICU discharge-landmark cohort and estimate one primary clinical-risk quantity:

> the difference between nighttime and daytime 48-hour cumulative incidence of recorded hospital expiration among patients discharged alive from the ICU while persistently physiologically unstable, contrasted with the same nighttime/daytime difference among patients whose measured physiology had recovered before discharge.

The key effect-modification estimand is the interaction contrast
[
Delta_{mathrm{int}} =
[CIF_{48}^{night}-CIF_{48}^{day}]_{persistent}
-
[CIF_{48}^{night}-CIF_{48}^{day}]_{recovered}.
]
The clinically meaningful interpretation margin is 2 percentage points, fixed before fitting. The analysis is observational and database-bounded: it tests whether the recorded nighttime association is concentrated in a measurable pre-discharge state, not whether nighttime discharge causes death.

Completion requires:

1. a source/header/schema/value audit and reproducible cohort-flow table;
2. one index discharge per hospital admission, exact exposure and outcome times, and a 30-minute physiologic ledger for the final six ICU hours;
3. fitted transparent competing-risk and mechanistic state-transition models on identical stays, inputs, splits and outcome definitions;
4. standardized 48-hour cause-specific and cumulative-incidence estimates with hospital-clustered uncertainty and held-out-hospital transport;
5. prespecified falsification/sensitivity results; and
6. a conclusion labelled supportive, adverse or inconclusive and linked to computed outputs.

No model has been fitted in discovery and no clinical result is asserted here.

## What is known and what remains unresolved

The strongest claim supported before fitting is only that the frozen eICU snapshot has ICU-relative unit discharge offsets and time-of-day fields, hospital disposition offsets/statuses, hospital identifiers/context, and repeated physiologic measurements that can be joined by the documented keys. A bounded literature check found prior studies associating nighttime ICU discharge with hospital mortality but also inconsistent findings; one read article discussed potential premature discharge/flow mechanisms and the absence of occupancy data. This establishes motivation and a known association question, not the present interaction hypothesis.

The unresolved claim is whether any nighttime excess is concentrated among patients with persistent, unresolved physiologic abnormalities immediately before discharge, rather than being a broad time-of-day association or a product of different case mix. A positive interaction would identify a measurable subgroup for future prospective discharge-safety review. A null interaction would argue against treating clock time as a proxy for residual instability, while still not proving nighttime discharge is safe.

This is substantively distinct from the mature hypotension/creatinine branch. The primary phenotype excludes MAP and creatinine and instead uses oxygenation, heart rate, respiratory rate and temperature. The outcome is a fixed post-ICU hospital-expiration risk after alive discharge, not an observed creatinine process during ICU observation. MAP and renal rows are not predictors, endpoints or adjustment variables in the primary analysis.

## Population, clock and competing risks

Use eICU 2.0 snapshot `[source checksum]`. All offsets are minutes from ICU admission; calendar dates and time zones are not available.

Start with `patient` rows satisfying:

- adult age >=18; parse `age="> 89"` as 90 and retain an age-censored flag;
- nonmissing, nonnegative numeric `unitdischargeoffset` and parseable `unitdischargetime24`;
- `unitdischargestatus="Alive"`;
- unit discharge offset D >=360, so a full six-hour pre-discharge window exists;
- one index row per `patienthealthsystemstayid`: select the earliest row in that hospital-system stay meeting these criteria, breaking ties by `unitdischargeoffset` then `patientunitstayid`; retain all eligible rows in a flow audit and report later ICU stays separately.

For the primary outcome, require a nonmissing numeric `hospitaldischargeoffset` H, `hospitaldischargestatus` in {Alive, Expired}, and H>D. This excludes repeated-ICU rows whose hospital disposition precedes the selected unit discharge; it is a deliberate observability rule, not an imputation. The source audit found such negative intervals in raw repeated rows.

Exposure is defined only from the recorded ICU discharge clock, never from hospital discharge clock:

- night: `unitdischargetime24` hour 19:00 through 07:59;
- day: hour 08:00 through 18:59.

Treat the recorded time as the site-local hour; no timezone or daylight-saving correction is possible. Sensitivities use the balanced 19:00–06:59 versus 07:00–18:59 split and the restricted 00:00–04:59 night window. Do not use hospital discharge time to define exposure.

Time zero is D. The outcome window is strictly post-discharge `0 < H-D <= 2880` minutes (48 hours). The primary event is hospital expiration (`hospitaldischargestatus="Expired"`) in that window. An alive hospital discharge in the same window is a competing event. If H-D >2880, the stay is event-free at 48 hours. Unknown status, missing H, H<=D, or H-D=0 are excluded from the primary endpoint and reported by exposure/state; a boundary sensitivity includes H-D=0 as an immediate event only if its semantics are defensible. Do not use post-discharge labs, vitals, notes, or ICU observations to define outcomes or censoring. This prevents post-discharge ascertainment from substituting for a clinical endpoint. Hospital expiration is a documented disposition, not adjudicated death timing or cause.

## Residual-instability phenotype

Construct a 30-minute ledger for `[D-360,D)`, using only rows with observation time strictly less than D. For each bin, deduplicate exact source IDs; if multiple observations remain in a bin, use the median per variable. Do not carry values across bins or interpolate in the primary analysis. Retain physiologic values only in plausible ranges: SpO2 50–100%, heart rate 30–220/min, respiration 4–80/min, temperature 30–43 C. Report all exclusions and coverage.

A domain is abnormal in an observed bin when its bin median is:

- oxygenation: `sao2 < 92`;
- heart rate: `heartrate < 50` or `heartrate > 120`;
- respiration: `respiration < 8` or `respiration > 30`;
- temperature: `temperature < 35` or `temperature >=38.5`.

A bin is observed if at least two domains are available. It is Stable if observed and no available domain is abnormal, Unstable if observed and at least one available domain is abnormal, and Unknown otherwise. These cutoffs are transparent operational definitions, not validated discharge criteria.

For the primary state analysis require the final two 30-minute bins before D to be observed and require at least six observed bins in all 12 bins. Define:

- Persistent: both final bins are Unstable and at least three of the 12 bins are Unstable;
- Recovered: both final bins are Stable and at most one of the 12 bins is Unstable;
- Intermediate: all other eligible sequences.

The primary hypothesis concerns the interaction of night with Persistent versus Recovered; Intermediate is retained for descriptive and secondary three-level contrasts. A continuous burden measure (fraction of observed bins Unstable) and a recency measure (number of Unstable bins among the final four) are secondary, not replacements for the prespecified states. The final two-bin rule makes the exposure state available before D and prevents immortal-time bias from using future observations.

## Transparent baseline and mechanistic alternative

Use identical index rows, physiology, outcomes, splits and uncertainty procedures for both methods.

**Transparent competing-risk baseline (B0/B1).** Build 30-minute post-D intervals up to 2,880 minutes, ending at the earliest recorded hospital expiration, alive hospital discharge, or 2,880-minute boundary. Fit cause-specific pooled-logistic hazards for hospital expiration and alive hospital discharge with shared predictors: night, Persistent/Intermediate/Recovered state, age, sex, ethnicity, ICU unit type/stay type, admission source, log ICU duration D, hospital context, vital coverage, and calendar-year indicators if available from `hospitaldischargeyear`. Use night-by-state interaction in B0; B1 adds burden and recency as prespecified nonlinear-free linear terms. Do not add discharge location, hospital outcome fields, or post-D measurements as predictors. Estimate standardized 48-hour CIFs by g-computation over the development covariate distribution and evaluate the held-out hospital distribution using the training model/intercept rule. Use regularization fixed by grouped development-hospital inner validation; no test-hospital fitting.

**Mechanistic state-transition alternative (M1).** On the same 12 pre-D bins, fit a piecewise-exponential/Markov state model with observed states Stable, Unstable and Unknown, allowing Stable↔Unstable transitions and absorbing discharge-at-D. The post-D model has competing absorbing states Hospital Expired and Hospital Alive Discharge. Its transition hazards use the same baseline covariates and night indicator, plus the final state, number of Unstable-to-Stable recoveries, final four-bin state sequence, and persistent/recovered trajectory. Unknown is retained as an observation state, not recoded as stable. Standardize the same 48-hour CIFs and compute the same interaction contrast. The model’s scientific contribution is decomposition of identical abnormal-bin burden into persistence, recovery and recency; it is not a search for a marginal discrimination gain. If the state model is nonidentified or unstable under hospital holdout, report it as inconclusive and retain the transparent result.

Use hospital-held-out splits formed before value-map freezing, imputation, scaling or model fitting. Assign all stays from a hospital to one fold; within each fold keep all rows sharing `uniquepid` together. Prefer one index per `patienthealthsystemstayid` as above, then cluster uncertainty by hospital with a `uniquepid` bootstrap sensitivity. Report the number of development/held-out hospitals and patient-system overlap (which must be zero).

Primary outputs are standardized 48-hour CIFs, `Delta_int`, the two stratum-specific night/day contrasts, 95% confidence intervals, calibration intercept/slope and Brier score for each event, and held-out-hospital results. AUROC/AUPRC are secondary diagnostics only. The primary conclusion cannot be based on a small predictive improvement.

## Falsification, interpretation and unavailable evidence

Falsification checks are prespecified:

- Repeat with the balanced 19:00–06:59 definition and restricted 00:00–04:59 night definition.
- Require no interpolation (primary), then a clearly labelled <=30-minute interpolation sensitivity.
- Replace `vitalPeriodic` with independently available `vitalAperiodic.noninvasivemean` only as a secondary measurement sensitivity; this does not enter the primary phenotype and does not reintroduce MAP/creatinine.
- Repeat after restricting to hospitals with at least 20 Persistent and 20 Recovered index discharges and at least two outcome events in each state/time cell.
- Add APACHE sensitivity covariates only with their timing caveat.
- Audit whether the interaction is driven by unitdischargelocation categories (floor/step-down/telemetry versus other); location is a post-discharge destination and is diagnostic, not an adjustment variable.
- Negative-control timing: shift the six-hour physiologic window 360 minutes earlier, `[D-720,D-360)`, where coverage permits. A strong identical interaction only in the discharge-adjacent window is expected; a similar effect in the remote window weakens a discharge-state interpretation.
- Endpoint specificity: repeat with alive hospital discharge as the event and hospital expiration as competing event; a mechanically identical effect for both endpoints suggests disposition/clock selection rather than patient harm.

Supportive evidence requires a positive `Delta_int` of at least 2 percentage points with a 95% interval excluding 0, the Persistent night/day contrast positive, directionally similar estimates in held-out hospitals, acceptable calibration, and survival of the balanced-clock, no-interpolation, endpoint-specificity and destination diagnostics. Agreement between B0 and M1 strengthens the claim that persistence/recovery—not only an additive clock effect—is informative.

Adverse evidence is an interaction estimate <=0 with its uncertainty excluding a positive 2-point concentration, or a positive estimate that disappears/reverses with the balanced clock, remote-window negative control, endpoint-specificity check, or hospital holdout. This rejects the proposed concentration interpretation; it does not show nighttime discharge is safe, that residual instability is causal, or that staffing is irrelevant.

Inconclusive evidence includes too few eligible Persistent/Recovered stays or hospitals, missing/ambiguous clock or disposition values, sparse physiologic coverage, unknown-state dominance, nonconvergence, intervals spanning the 2-point margin, or materially different held-out versus development estimates. A main nighttime association without a stable interaction is inconclusive for the seed hypothesis, not supportive.

Computationally checkable claims include source paths/schema hashes, headers, row counts, value maps, joins, state construction, no future rows in the feature window, one-index-per-hospitalization logic, positive H-D rule, competing-risk event ledger, split integrity, fitted parameters, CIFs, uncertainty, calibration, transport and sensitivity outputs. Clinical adjudication or additional data are required for claims about true physiologic instability, ICU discharge appropriateness, ward monitoring, preventable harm, causal nighttime effects, staffing, occupancy, bed pressure, discharge intent, cause of death, post-ICU symptoms, and treatment recommendations. The configured eICU release has no images, raw waveforms, reliable narrative clinical context, current occupancy, staffing rosters, discharge orders, or ward-level follow-up vitals; `vitalPeriodic` is a five-minute summary stream.

## Alternatives not chosen and revisit evidence

- A neural temporal model (GRU/transformer) on the same 12-bin physiologic sequences, with target hospital expiration/alive discharge and the same hospital-held-out split, could reveal nonlinear sequence combinations. It is deferred because its extra flexibility would not identify staffing, discharge intent or true ward instability, and the mechanistic state model directly tests the clinically relevant persistence/recovery distinction. Revisit only if the mechanistic model has adequate state/event support but fails a prespecified residual sequence-shape adequacy check; compare standardized CIFs and calibration, never only AUC.
- A causal marginal structural model of “night versus day discharge” is not selected: the exposure is a discharge-time decision with unmeasured occupancy, staffing, readiness and urgency, so eICU cannot identify a credible intervention effect. It should be revisited only with discharge-order timestamps, time-varying bed pressure/staffing and richer post-ICU follow-up.
- The mature hypotension/creatinine analysis is not reused as a parent scientific question. MAP/creatinine may be added only as a clearly labelled comparator in a future external adjudication study; the primary deliverable here excludes them.
