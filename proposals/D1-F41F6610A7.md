# Creatinine Rise During Decongestion in Acute Heart Failure: A Falsifiable MIMIC-IV Experiment

## 1. Hypothesis

### Supported claim (established by prior literature, not re-tested here)
- Cardiorenal syndrome type 1 (CRS-1), defined as AKI caused by acute decompensated heart failure, is an independent predictor of worse outcomes in ADHF. [K1] Savic et al. (2026) reviewed the epidemiology, pathophysiology, and prognostic impact of CRS-1 in ADHFpEF, confirming that CRS-1 is associated with increased mortality and rehospitalization.
- Diuretic resistance occurs in 20–30% of ADHF patients and is an independent predictor of worsening clinical outcomes, immediate post-treatment death, and re-admission. [K2] Soerarso et al. (2026) identified DM, IV loop diuretic history, oral loop dose >80 mg/day, LVEF ≤49%, BUN ≥21 mg/dL, and serum chloride <98 mmol/L as independent predictors of diuretic resistance in 535 ADHF patients.
- Acute heart failure is associated with high mortality and hospital readmission rates, with current treatment centered on decongestive drugs. [K3] Arrigo et al. (2020) provided a comprehensive review of AHF pathophysiology and management, noting the unmet need for individualization of in-hospital management.

### Unresolved claim (this experiment tests)
**In adult ICU patients with acute heart failure (AHF) who receive diuretic therapy, a rise in serum creatinine during the decongestion window (hours 0–48 from ICU admission) is associated with higher in-hospital mortality when congestion persists (UO not improving) compared to when congestion improves (UO improving), after adjustment for baseline severity.**

This is a 2×2 design:
- **Creatinine axis:** creatinine rise (ΔCr ≥ 0.3 mg/dL from baseline to 48h) vs no rise
- **Congestion axis:** congestion improving (UO slope positive in hours 0–48) vs congestion persistent (UO slope flat or negative)

The hypothesis predicts that the creatinine rise × persistent congestion group has the highest mortality, and that this interaction is not explained by baseline severity alone.

### Strongest rival explanation
The creatinine rise may reflect baseline severity (sicker patients have both higher creatinine and worse outcomes) rather than a decongestion-specific effect. This is addressed by:
1. Requiring a baseline creatinine measurement (excluding patients without one)
2. Adjusting for baseline creatinine, age, sex, and ICU LOS
3. Including an interaction term (creatinine rise × congestion status) in the model
4. Comparing the interaction effect to a null model with only main effects

## 2. Study Population

### Inclusion criteria
- Adult (age ≥ 18) ICU stay in MIMIC-IV 3.1
- ICD-10 AHF diagnosis: I50.2x, I50.3x, I50.4x, I50.8x, I50.9x (stored without dots: I502, I503, I504, I508, I509)
- At least one diuretic administration (furosemide itemid 221794 or 228340, or bumetanide itemid 229639) in the first 48h of ICU stay
- At least one serum creatinine measurement in the first 48h (itemid 50912, 51081, 51977, or 52546)
- At least one urine output measurement in the first 48h (itemid 226559, 226560, 226561, 226567, or 226627)

### Exclusion criteria
- ICU stay < 24h (insufficient observation window)
- Creatinine measurement only after hour 24 (no valid baseline)
- UO measurement only after hour 24 (no valid baseline)

### Expected sample size (from feasibility probe)
- AHF admissions: 42,891
- AHF ICU stays: 14,097
- AHF stays with diuretic: 5,916 (42.0%)
- AHF stays with UO: 13,116 (93.0%)
- AHF hadms with creatinine: 40,630 (94.7%)
- **Expected analyzable cohort: ~3,000–5,000 ICU stays** (intersection of diuretic + creatinine + UO + ≥24h stay)

### Phenotype (2×2)
| | Congestion improving (UO slope > 0) | Congestion persistent (UO slope ≤ 0) |
|---|---|---|
| **Cr rise (ΔCr ≥ 0.3)** | Group A: Cr↑, congestion↓ | Group B: Cr↑, congestion persistent |
| **No Cr rise (ΔCr < 0.3)** | Group C: Cr stable, congestion↓ | Group D: Cr stable, congestion persistent |

**Primary hypothesis:** Group B (Cr↑, congestion persistent) has the highest in-hospital mortality, and the Cr rise × congestion persistent interaction is significant after adjustment.

## 4. Analysis Plan

### Primary analysis
1. **Descriptive:** 2×2 table of phenotype groups with in-hospital mortality rates
2. **Unadjusted:** Chi-square test for mortality differences across 4 groups
3. **Adjusted logistic regression:**
   - Model 1 (baseline): in-hospital death ~ age + sex + baseline Cr + baseline MAP + baseline HR + ICU LOS
   - Model 2 (extended): Model 1 + ΔCr + UO slope + diuretic dose + ΔCr × UO slope interaction
   - Compare Model 1 vs Model 2: likelihood ratio test, AUC improvement
4. **Interaction test:** Coefficient and 95% CI for ΔCr × UO slope interaction term

### Secondary analyses
- Sensitivity analysis with ΔCr threshold at 0.5 mg/dL
- Subgroup analysis by baseline Cr (normal vs elevated)
- Time-to-event analysis (Cox) for in-hospital death, accounting for ICU LOS

### Baseline vs extended model comparison
- **Baseline model:** logistic regression with age, sex, baseline Cr, baseline MAP, baseline HR, ICU LOS
- **Extended model:** baseline + ΔCr, UO slope, diuretic dose, interaction term
- **Evaluation:** AUC, calibration (Hosmer-Lemeshow), likelihood ratio test
- **Alternative:** XGBoost with the same features, 5-fold cross-validation, to check for non-linear effects

### Falsification criteria
- **Supportive:** Group B mortality significantly higher than Groups A, C, D (p < 0.05); interaction term significant (p < 0.05); extended model AUC > baseline model AUC by ≥ 0.01
- **Adverse:** No significant difference in mortality across the 4 groups; interaction term not significant; extended model AUC not improved
- **Inconclusive:** Small sample size (< 500 in any group); wide CIs crossing null; missing data > 30% in key variables

## 7. Method Comparison: Baseline vs Extended

### Baseline (simple) model
- Logistic regression with 6 covariates: age, sex, baseline Cr, baseline MAP, baseline HR, ICU LOS
- **What it captures:** Overall severity at admission
- **What it loses:** The dynamic information about how creatinine and UO change during the first 48h

### Extended (learned) model
- Logistic regression with baseline covariates + ΔCr, UO slope, diuretic dose, ΔCr × UO slope interaction
- **What it adds:** The 2×2 phenotype information; tests whether the interaction is significant
- **Alternative:** XGBoost with the same features, 5-fold CV, to check for non-linear effects and feature importance

### Why both are needed
- The baseline model establishes the severity-adjusted mortality rate
- The extended model tests whether the creatinine-congestion interaction adds prognostic information
- If the extended model does not improve AUC or the interaction is not significant, the hypothesis is not supported
- XGBoost is a sensitivity analysis, not the primary test

## 8. Limitations

1. **ICD code specificity:** I50.2x/3x/4x/8x/9x captures acute-on-chronic and acute HF, but not all AHF presentations (e.g., de novo HF coded as I50.9x is included, but some AHF may be coded under other ICD codes)
2. **Diuretic coverage:** Only 42% of AHF ICU stays have diuretic inputevents; many patients may receive diuretics via prescriptions (hosp/prescriptions) rather than ICU inputevents
3. **UO measurement:** UO is selectively measured; patients with indwelling catheters are more likely to have UO recorded
4. **No measured GFR:** Creatinine is an imperfect marker of kidney function, especially in critical illness with muscle loss
5. **No external validation:** Single-center (MIMIC-IV) data; findings may not generalize
6. **Confounding by indication:** Sicker patients may receive more diuretics and have higher creatinine; addressed by adjustment but not fully eliminated
7. **BNP coverage:** Only 24.5% of AHF admissions have BNP/NT-proBNP; cannot be used as a primary variable
