# Does an early source-concordant renal process improve transportable prediction of 48-hour ICU-unit death?

## Scientific deliverable and substantive advance

The future solver must reconstruct the assigned parent’s exact eICU cohort and source dictionaries, then newly estimate whether an early, source-concordant post-creatinine-rise renal-support/confirmation process adds transportable prognostic information for recorded ICU-unit death within 48 hours of the first qualifying creatinine rise.

The primary clinical decision estimand is a six-hour post-rise update. Among parent-eligible stays still observed in the ICU unit six hours after the rise, compare an adjustment-only model with a matched process-augmented model for the cumulative incidence of recorded unit death by r+2880 minutes. The adjustment-only model contains the parent’s MAP history, severity, demographics/admission descriptors, creatinine/testing opportunity, and leakage-controlled respiratory documentation process. The augmented model adds only renal process information observed from r through r+360: repeat-test opportunity, strict creatinine confirmation, treatment-source support, intake/output-source support, and two-source corroboration/order. The six-hour update is a risk-review prediction estimand, not an intervention effect.

Completion requires newly fitted or estimated:

- the frozen parent cohort flow, six-hour update risk set, and raw 30-minute event ledger;
- the exact strict renal dictionaries and source-specific audit counts;
- recorded unit-death, alive-unit-exit and unknown-status event definitions through r+2880;
- the nested adjustment-only, transparent renal-process, and ordered multi-state predictions;
- development and hospital-held-out 48-hour death risks, calibration, Brier/log scores, integrated Brier score, discrimination, and prespecified risk-threshold decision curves;
- the incremental prognostic contrast from adding renal process information, its hospital-cluster uncertainty, and its held-out transport performance;
- process-order contrasts showing whether the information is carried by testing opportunity, confirmation, one-source capture, two-source corroboration, or competing exits;
- falsification and timing/permutation outputs; and
- a supportive, adverse or inconclusive conclusion linked to saved computed outputs.

The parent’s descriptive documentation heterogeneity remains an ascertainment audit, not the new clinical endpoint. The advance is to test whether the early process is informative about an observable near-term clinical outcome—recorded death during the same ICU-unit episode—and whether that information survives MAP, severity, testing opportunity, respiratory-documentation adjustment, source decomposition, and hospital-held-out transport. A positive result would justify chart/order validation of a candidate risk-review signal; it would not justify dialysis, fluid, pressor, or ventilator treatment changes.

## Evidence-supported claim, unresolved claim and importance

The inspected eICU snapshot and catalog support only feasibility: structured ICU-relative laboratory, MAP, treatment, intake/output, respiratory, patient, severity, hospital and unit-disposition records exist and can be joined on the stated keys. The exact source rows and field headers are available read-only. The available evidence does not establish that a treatment row is an order or administration, that an intake/output label is dialysis delivered, that a creatinine rise is biological KDIGO AKI, or that absence of a record means absence of care.

The strongest pre-fit claim is therefore:

> This snapshot can define a recorded creatinine rise, subsequent recorded testing/confirmation, strict renal-support records from two source families, respiratory documentation, and recorded ICU-unit death or alive exit on ICU-relative time; it cannot yet establish their clinical meaning, completeness, causality or transportability.

The unresolved claim is:

> After adjustment for the parent’s early MAP and severity, creatinine/testing opportunity, and respiratory-documentation process, does adding the early source-decomposed renal-support/confirmation process improve prediction and standardized risk estimation for recorded ICU-unit death by 48 hours after the first qualifying recorded creatinine rise in hospitals not used for fitting?

The primary hypothesis is frozen before inspecting target-cohort outcomes. Let M0 be the adjustment-only six-hour update model and M1 the matched transparent model adding the strict renal process. Let M2 be the ordered multi-state alternative. Define ΔIBS_heldout = IBS(M0) - IBS(M1) for 48-hour recorded unit death, evaluated in the fixed held-out hospitals with hospital-cluster bootstrap uncertainty. Supportive evidence requires a positive held-out improvement of at least 0.005 absolute integrated Brier score with a two-sided 95% hospital-cluster interval excluding zero, no material held-out calibration deterioration, and concordant direction in M2. This is a predeclared prognostic margin, not a treatment-benefit threshold.

The process-specific claim additionally requires that:

- the renal increment remains after the MAP/severity/testing/respiratory block;
- the source-specific treatment and intake/output components point in the same qualitative direction, with their standardized risk contrasts within 5 percentage points when both source gates are met;
- two-source corroboration or confirmation/order contributes in M2 beyond a generic count of documentation rows;
- the result is present in at least 10 development hospitals and is evaluable in at least 10 held-out hospitals with at least 25 recorded unit deaths and 25 strict-union renal-process events; and
- M1 and M2 preserve direction and calibration in held-out hospitals and the second fixed split.

These gates make a small in-sample score change insufficient. If event support or overlap fails, the study is inconclusive rather than positive.

This matters because a creatinine-triggered ICU review system could be overconfident if it mistakes testing intensity or broad documentation for a renal-specific sign of imminent deterioration. Conversely, a source-concordant process that improves risk estimation in hospitals not used for fitting could identify a reproducible, observable workflow signal for earlier expert review. The result would still be prognostic association with recorded unit death, not evidence that renal support causes or prevents death and not a hospital-quality ranking.

## Matched transparent baseline and ordered alternative

The transparent baseline is a regularized cause-specific pooled-logistic model on identical 30-minute intervals and competing outcomes. Its nested fits are:

- A0: parent MAP, severity, demographics/admission descriptors, time and unit-observation variables;
- A1: A0 plus pre-rise creatinine/testing opportunity and source-availability fields;
- A2: A1 plus the leakage-controlled pre-rise respiratory block and post-rise respiratory sentinel available by the prediction time;
- M0: A2, the primary adjustment-only model;
- M1: M0 plus the early confirmation indicator, repeat opportunity, treatment-only support, intake/output-only support, strict union/corroboration and source/order indicators.

Use prespecified elapsed-time terms, common clinical slopes, transition-specific regularization, and no hospital identity in the primary transport model. A secondary hospital-random-intercept fit may quantify residual site variation, but held-out random effects are set to the development mean and never recalibrated. Derive death CIFs and all scores from the same fitted predictions. M1 makes additive source and confirmation contributions auditable but loses exact waiting-time/state order and can treat distinct process histories as equivalent.

M2 is a piecewise-exponential ordered multi-state model on exactly the same risk set, raw rows, grid, adjustment vector, respiratory variables, process dictionary, split, standardization distribution and bootstrap procedure. Its states include: post-rise/no follow-up; follow-up opportunity without confirmation; confirmed rise without support; treatment-only support; intake/output-only support; two-source corroboration; respiratory mark as nonabsorbing; confirmation-before-support; support-before-confirmation; same-minute tie; recorded death; alive exit; and unknown-status censoring. Transitions are allowed only in observed temporal order, and death/alive/unknown exits compete from every non-exit state. The respiratory mark does not absorb the stay.

M2 can reveal whether any M1 increment is carried by the timing/order of confirmation and support, source corroboration, or exit censoring—information the additive baseline loses. M1 versus M0 answers the primary incremental-information question; M2 versus M1 tests whether process order adds scientifically meaningful information beyond additive source indicators. Concordant direction and held-out calibration strengthen the inference. Disagreement is model uncertainty and cannot be resolved by selecting the better-looking result.

## Falsification and interpretation

Supportive evidence requires the frozen primary gate: positive held-out ΔIBS >=0.005 with a two-sided hospital-cluster interval excluding zero; no material calibration deterioration; at least 10 development and 10 held-out hospitals with the stated event/source support; concordant M1 and M2 direction; source-specific agreement; and stability under the second split, revised-lab clock, status, MAP and strict-source sensitivities.

Adverse evidence includes no held-out improvement, an interval compatible with zero, worse calibration, an improvement that vanishes after testing or respiratory adjustment, one-source dominance, lack of source corroboration, failure to transport, or an apparent gain only for broad fluid-removal labels. A large gain from confirmation opportunity without a gain from confirmed value/support is adverse to a renal-specific process interpretation but may support a laboratory-surveillance interpretation. None of these outcomes proves absence of biological AKI or treatment need.

The process claim is falsified or materially weakened if:

- shuffling renal process timestamps within hospital, outcome-risk and source strata while preserving counts produces a similar ΔIBS;
- assigning process records to another stay within hospital/stratum produces a similar gain;
- post-rise process information dated after a prediction time changes that prediction;
- the gain persists when all process labels are replaced by exposure-matched placebo timestamps, indicating generic time/documentation leakage;
- exact-ID deduplication, revised-lab timing, or unit-discharge boundary changes reverse the primary result; or
- the renal gain is unchanged when the independent respiratory rows are permuted in a way that should expose broad documentation dependence, or is explained entirely by the respiratory sentinel.

Additional sensitivities are 3/12-hour confirmation gaps, 30/60/120-minute corroboration windows, raw versus bridged MAP, periodic versus non-invasive MAP, strict versus broad fluid-removal definitions, permissive versus conservative discharge ties, NULL-status handling, and outcome/process permutation. A placebo process test must preserve the same availability window and hospital/event strata.

Inconclusive means sparse source or death events, fewer than the transport gates, extreme weights or inadequate overlap, unstable bootstrap, failed convergence, strong dependence on unknown-status handling, semantic dictionary instability, model-family disagreement, or held-out calibration/process failure. Inconclusive is not a positive or negative clinical finding.

Computationally checkable claims include source and schema hashes, live headers, exact joins, archive-member status, unit filtering, deduplication, landmark/rise/exit boundaries, strict dictionaries, split integrity, process timestamps, model fits, predicted CIFs, scoring, uncertainty, calibration, transport and permutation reproducibility. Every reported conclusion must cite a saved output and interval.

Clinical adjudication or another study is required to determine whether a treatment or intake/output row represents an order, administration, initiation, dose, indication, intent, chronic dialysis, fluid removal or documentation artifact; whether creatinine rise is biological AKI; whether respiratory documentation represents ventilation or severity; whether missingness means no care; and whether a risk-review signal changes outcomes. The configured data lack validated treatment administration/intent, complete orders, uniform urine collection intervals and denominators, reliable narrative review, post-discharge outcomes, patient preferences, intervention utilities and causal treatment assignment. Expert chart/order adjudication, an independent cohort and prospective impact evaluation are required before clinical policy.

## Method-selection record

The transparent M1 model is the primary baseline because its contributions from testing, confirmation, treatment source, intake/output source, source corroboration and respiratory documentation can be audited and scored directly. The ordered M2 model is retained as the scientifically substantive alternative because the unresolved uncertainty is whether timing and state order carry prognostic information that the additive baseline loses. Both methods operate on identical rows, fields, risk set, 30-minute grid, split, standardization distribution, outcomes, and 500-replicate clustered uncertainty.

No neural or GPU method is excluded categorically. A learned longitudinal sequence model is deferred because the present scientific uncertainty is source semantics and process order, not a generic predictive score, and event support/held-out miscalibration are unmeasured. Revisit it only if both M1 and M2 show reproducible, shape-specific held-out miscalibration or fail to represent process order, source events are adequate, and a model can preserve source decomposition, the six-hour information boundary, calibration, hospital-held-out transport and clustered uncertainty. A generic boosted tree is likewise deferred because a score gain without source/order interpretation would not answer the question. A urine/KDIGO branch is not feasible as a primary claim because \`intakeOutput\` lacks validated collection duration and a uniform time-varying denominator. Causal renal-support or pressor analysis is deferred because the records lack validated administration, dose, indication, intent and complete exposure.

Measured discovery facts are catalog/schema/header inspection and inherited parent feasibility audits; no target-cohort model, score, bootstrap, transport estimate or clinical result has been fitted. The future solver planning envelope is 16 CPU, 262,144 MiB memory, up to eight NVIDIA A100-SXM4 80-GB GPUs and 28,800 seconds. CPU-first is appropriate for the low-dimensional pooled-logistic and piecewise-exponential fits; GPU is optional only if profiling finds a real bottleneck. Discovery budget and future solver budget are separate. The actual scientific deliverable is the saved cohort/ledger, fitted M0/M1/M2 predictions, incremental held-out scores and uncertainty, standardized death-risk contrasts, calibration/transport, and falsification outputs.
