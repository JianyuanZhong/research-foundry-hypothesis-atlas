# Early post-TACE AFP/hepatic-reserve trajectories and 30-day return hospitalization in HCC

## Clinical question and hypothesis

Among patients with hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) undergoing a first observed TACE-type procedure, does the trajectory of AFP and hepatic-reserve laboratory measurements from the pre-procedure period through index discharge predict a subsequent inpatient encounter within 30 days after discharge, beyond a pre-TACE clinical baseline?

The prespecified hypothesis is that a post-procedure pattern of rising AFP, rising bilirubin or coagulation time, and falling albumin or platelets will be associated with higher risk of a subsequent inpatient encounter. The scientific test is incremental prognostic value: does the dynamic model improve held-out discrimination, calibration, and clinically relevant risk stratification over the static pre-TACE model? This is not a causal claim about TACE, a claim of tumor response, or a recommendation to admit or retreat.

This materially repairs the parent branch. Repeat liver-directed treatment remains a secondary management outcome, but the primary outcome is a subsequent inpatient-care event that is less directly selected by a clinician’s decision to perform another liver-directed procedure.

## What is established and what remains unresolved

Established from the frozen local snapshot:

- The HCC source contains 105,044 encounters for 43,815 patients from 2010-07-27 through 2026-01-01.
- The procedure source contains explicit and combined TACE-type labels, including 8,609 exact `经导管肝动脉造影化疗栓塞术（TACE）` rows, 27,411 `经导管肝动脉栓塞术` rows, and 26,385 `动脉化疗栓塞` rows. These are availability facts, not a validated treatment registry.
- The lab source has native measurement times, a numeric-result field, and distinct assay labels. A bounded scan found 190,022 rows labelled exactly `甲胎蛋白` and 82,592 labelled `甲胎蛋白异质体`; the two will not be pooled. Hepatic laboratory labels, including albumin, total bilirubin, prothrombin/coagulation measures, platelets, creatinine and sodium, are present.
- Encounters have admission and discharge timestamps, so a subsequent inpatient encounter can be operationalized without using repeat-treatment choice as the primary endpoint.

The unresolved claim is that early AFP/reserve dynamics add reproducible, calibrated prognostic information for later inpatient burden. The source facts do not establish this claim. Under a provisional first-observed TACE/HCC definition, a bounded diagnostic produced 17,373 eligible patients and 808 patients with a later >=24-hour inpatient encounter within 30 days after index discharge (4.65%). Those counts are feasibility diagnostics; the solver must freeze the exact label dictionary, cohort flow, join checks, and final counts before fitting.

## Population, landmark, and temporal boundaries

Use only discovery participants in catalog hash buckets 0-79. Buckets 80-99 are inaccessible and are not an external validation set. All rows remain read-only.

1. Index is the earliest observed qualifying TACE-type procedure per patient. Use procedure start time `手术.开始时间`, restricted to 2011-01-01 or later. Qualifying labels are frozen before outcome inspection and must include the exact observed TACE family: `经导管肝动脉栓塞术`, `动脉化疗栓塞`, `经导管肝动脉造影化疗栓塞术（TACE）`, and clearly equivalent combined strings containing these TACE components. Angiography-only rows are excluded. The row must have a valid procedure start and a matching encounter.

2. Require HCC at or before the index: a diagnosis `诊断名称` containing `肝细胞癌` or `肝癌`, attached to its encounter’s `就诊时间`. `diagnoses` has no native date, so the encounter time is the only available temporal anchor. Exclude participants with any earlier qualifying TACE in observed history. This is first observed, not first-ever.

3. Require a matching index encounter with valid `入院时间` and `出院时间`, and require discharge at or after the procedure start. The prediction landmark is the index discharge time. Do not use length of stay as a primary predictor; it is reserved for a sensitivity analysis because it may simply proxy clinical severity.

4. To ensure a complete primary outcome window within the snapshot, require index discharge no later than 2025-12-01 23:59:59, using the observed snapshot end 2026-01-01. Report all excluded late discharges and incomplete follow-up rather than silently treating them as non-events.

5. The primary outcome is the first subsequent inpatient encounter for the same patient with a different encounter number, `入院时间 > index 出院时间`, admission within 30 days after index discharge, nonmissing `出院时间`, and length of stay `出院时间 - 入院时间 >= 24 hours`. The primary analysis is a binary 30-day event. A time-to-event analysis from discharge to admission is secondary. This outcome must be called a “subsequent inpatient encounter,” not “unplanned readmission,” because the source has no validated planned/unplanned field.

6. Secondary outcomes are: any later encounter (including shorter encounters) within 30 days; the number of subsequent inpatient encounters; a subsequent encounter whose first 24 hours has no qualifying liver-directed procedure; repeat TACE separately; and the parent endpoint of any repeat liver-directed procedure during days 31-180. None may be called recurrence, progression, treatment response, liver failure, or survival.

## Estimands and model comparison

The primary estimand is the difference in out-of-sample 30-day event prediction between:

### Simple baseline

Fit a training-only standardized, regularized logistic regression for the primary binary outcome. Covariates are age, sex, index department, calendar era, qualifying procedure subtype, HCC/liver-disease/portal-hypertension history before index, prior liver-directed procedure count, prior inpatient-encounter count in the preceding 180 days, nearest pre-index AFP and reserve values, days since each measurement, and pre-index measurement density/missingness.

This baseline deliberately answers what can be predicted before the treatment episode’s post-procedure trajectory is observed. It must not contain post-index labs, discharge duration, later diagnoses, later procedures, or future encounters.

### Selected substantive alternative: irregular latent hepatic-reserve trajectory model

Fit an irregular longitudinal model in which each patient has a latent reserve state evolving from day -90 through index discharge. AFP is modeled as a separate tumor-marker trajectory; albumin, bilirubin, coagulation and platelets are separate observed channels with assay-specific noise. The latent state is linked to the probability of a subsequent inpatient encounter through a logistic outcome model at discharge. Random intercepts and slopes, elapsed-time features, observation/missingness indicators, and shrinkage are required. This is a prognostic state model, not a mechanistic proof of liver physiology and not a causal model.

The model’s fitting target is the observed dated laboratory values plus the 30-day binary subsequent-inpatient-encounter outcome. Its scientific value over the baseline is the ability to distinguish a current value from a worsening or recovering trajectory while accounting for irregular sampling and patient-level uncertainty. Report the latent-state contrast and uncertainty, not only a black-box score.

A small one-layer GRU or temporal-attention model is a recorded, deferred alternative: it would consume the same dated assay tokens, value, assay identity, time gap, missingness, diagnosis/procedure tokens, and pre-index history, with the same binary target and patient split. It would be revisited only if the state model is nonconvergent or if held-out residuals show nonlinear timing interactions that the state model cannot represent. A tiny AUC gain alone is not a reason to select it. A full multimodal transformer is deferred because the HCC source contains no image files and the cancer demonstration’s main article/complete STAR Methods were unavailable; no reproduction claim is made.

## Split, analysis, and uncertainty

Use a participant-level deterministic split within discovery buckets: hash buckets 0-59 for fitting, 60-69 for hyperparameter/regularization selection, and 70-79 as the locked internal test set. No patient or encounter may cross partitions. Use a secondary chronological stress test, for example testing the latest available calendar era after training on earlier eras, only if event support permits. It is not external validation.

Before fitting, save the exact cohort dictionary, label dictionaries, exclusion counts, join multiplicity checks, and the number of analyzable patients/events. Report the number with each assay in the baseline and dynamic windows, median measurement count, missingness, assay-label mix, and follow-up completeness.

Primary held-out outputs:

- Brier score and calibration intercept/slope for the baseline and trajectory model.
- AUROC and PR-AUC with 95% patient-level bootstrap intervals.
- Calibration plots and observed-versus-predicted risk by prespecified risk bins.
- Difference in Brier score and PR-AUC from the same locked patients, with paired bootstrap intervals.
- Decision-curve net benefit over a broad, explicitly exploratory range of thresholds appropriate to an approximately 5% event rate; no threshold becomes a clinical policy claim.
- Association estimates for prespecified worsening AFP/reserve directions, expressed per training-standardized unit with 95% intervals.
- Subgroups by calendar era, index department, measurement density, and availability of each assay.

Use bootstrap resampling at the patient level, repeated fitting only within training partitions when needed. Hyperparameters are selected only on the fitting/selection partitions. If the dynamic model uses imputation, fit the imputation and scaling procedures on training data only and compare with a complete-case or observed-measurement sensitivity analysis.

## Falsification and failure criteria

The hypothesis is falsified or materially weakened if the trajectory model has a null or adverse held-out association, no meaningful improvement over the static baseline, poor calibration, or instability after accounting for baseline burden and measurement intensity. “Meaningful” must be prespecified before test unblinding as a clinically relevant absolute Brier improvement or calibration/risk-stratification improvement, not a tiny favorable AUC difference.

Run these leakage and robustness checks:

- Permute within-patient laboratory dates while preserving values and assay labels. Trajectory-specific improvement should attenuate; persistence suggests that the model is using static severity, coding artifacts, or leakage.
- Repeat after excluding measurements whose `就诊时间` or `检验时间` is after index discharge, and audit any encounter with overlapping admission/discharge intervals.
- Repeat without measurement-density and missingness variables, and with them, to quantify surveillance-intensity dependence.
- Repeat with index length of stay excluded and included. If the effect appears only after adding length of stay, it is a discharge-severity association rather than evidence that AFP/reserve kinetics add information.
- Repeat using exact AFP only versus a prespecified sensitivity dictionary that includes AFP-related labels; do not pool them in the primary analysis.
- Examine calendar-era and department heterogeneity. A signal confined to one coding era or department is not a general HCC trajectory finding.
- Use the parent repeat-intervention outcome only as a secondary comparison; concordance does not validate either outcome as tumor progression.

If fewer than 100 primary events remain after all landmark, assay, and follow-up requirements, or if assay labels cannot be reliably frozen because numeric units/semantics are ambiguous, declare the result inconclusive and park the branch rather than shrinking the question until it is easy.

## Interpretation

Supportive results require: a prespecified directionally coherent AFP/reserve association; improved held-out calibration and at least one clinically meaningful risk metric over the static baseline; stable results across measurement-density and calendar-era checks; and no evidence of date leakage. This would support a local, evidence-bounded prognostic signal for subsequent inpatient-care burden and justify prospective validation of an early post-TACE follow-up risk tool.

Adverse results include a null/opposite trajectory association, no incremental held-out value, poor calibration, or loss of the signal after accounting for measurement intensity. That would argue against using early AFP/reserve kinetics alone to prioritize post-discharge surveillance and would support parking the original repeat-treatment branch as a clinically entangled endpoint.

Inconclusive results include too few events, nonconvergence, assay mismatch, wide intervals spanning meaningful benefit and harm, incomplete follow-up, or coding/measurement domination. These results identify the need for better data rather than supporting or refuting the biology.

Even supportive results cannot establish radiographic response, recurrence, progression, hepatic failure, mortality, transplant eligibility, or a benefit from changing follow-up or treatment. Those conclusions require expert clinical/radiology adjudication, validated staging and treatment-intent data, laboratory units/reference ranges, death and transplant linkage, complete outside-hospital capture, and prospective or external validation. A causal care recommendation requires another design with treatment strategy, indication, confounding control, and outcome adjudication.
