# Child proposal: residual physiologic instability at ICU discharge and near-term post-ICU failure

Parent: `[prior hypothesis]` (repairable; expert seed `[starting question]`)

## 1. Clinical question and falsifiable hypothesis

Among adults discharged alive from an ICU during a MIMIC-IV hospital admission, does residual instability recorded during the final 24 hours before ICU `outtime` identify patients at increased risk of a first ICU readmission or death before readmission within the next 7 days?

The primary hypothesis is prognostic, not causal:

> After adjustment for prespecified admission and ICU-utilization variables, greater repeatedly observed physiologic instability and greater charted oxygen-support intensity in the final 24 hours are associated with higher cause-specific risk of (a) ICU readmission and (b) death before ICU readmission, with clinically useful calibration in a temporally held-out period.

The question is deliberately narrower than “patients should not be discharged.” MIMIC can test whether the recorded phenotype predicts outcomes; it cannot establish that delaying discharge, changing oxygen therapy, or increasing monitoring would improve outcomes.

The strongest claim supported before this study is only that MIMIC-IV contains linked hospital admissions, ICU stays, timestamped ICU measurements, transfers, and in-hospital outcomes. The parent’s clinical claim—that high oxygen need or fluctuating vital signs predicts failure after ICU discharge—remains untested. This child makes the exposure, index time, competing events, leakage exclusions, and falsification criteria explicit. It does not claim that an oxygen item proves a delivery device was actually in use or that a charted code status is an adjudicated treatment limitation.

Clinical importance: a reproducible signal at the ICU-to-ward transition could support targeted post-ICU surveillance or a prospective discharge-readiness study. A null result would be equally useful: it would argue against treating these isolated charted measures as a reliable discharge-risk marker and would redirect work toward unmeasured functional, cognitive, or ward-level factors.

## 2. Population, index, temporal boundaries, and estimands

Use the MIMIC-IV 3.1 snapshot documented in `datasets/mimic/README.md` and `datasets/mimic/metadata.json`. Include all available anchor-year groups (2008–2010, 2011–2013, 2014–2016, 2017–2019, 2020–2022), and report counts by group.

Cohort construction:

1. Join `icu/icustays` to `hosp/admissions` on `subject_id, hadm_id`.
2. An eligible ICU discharge has non-null `intime` and `outtime`, `outtime < dischtime`, and no `deathtime <= outtime`. The admission’s `deathtime` is used only to establish that the patient was alive at the index discharge; it is not a predictor.
3. Use the first eligible ICU discharge per `subject_id, hadm_id` as the primary index to avoid correlated multiple discharge episodes and to ensure that a later ICU stay is an outcome rather than another index. A prespecified secondary analysis includes every eligible ICU discharge with standard errors clustered by `hadm_id`.
4. The prediction origin is the ICU `outtime`. The exposure window is `[outtime - 24 hours, outtime)`; `charttime`, never `storetime`, determines whether a measurement was available. Values at exactly `outtime` and all post-`outtime` rows are excluded.
5. Primary follow-up ends at the earliest of 7 days after `outtime`, hospital `dischtime`, or the first event. The secondary full-hospitalization analysis follows to `dischtime`.

A post-index ICU readmission is the first later `icu/icustays.intime > index outtime` for the same `subject_id, hadm_id`, before the follow-up end. No arbitrary minimum gap is inserted; the exact gap is reported, and a sensitivity analysis requires at least 2 hours to test whether administrative near-contiguous stays drive results. Death is a competing event when `hosp/admissions.deathtime > outtime` and occurs before a readmission and before the follow-up end. Hospital discharge without either event is censoring. The primary competing-risk estimands are 7-day cumulative incidence and cause-specific hazard for readmission and for death-before-readmission. A composite “readmission or death” is secondary. Death after a readmission is retained in a separate secondary all-cause in-hospital death outcome rather than being misclassified as the competing event.

Do not use hospital `discharge_location`, `hospital_expire_flag`, `deathtime` as covariates, or any data recorded after `outtime`; these are outcome/future information.

## 3. Exact exposure definitions

All selected rows come from `icu/chartevents`, joined to `icu/d_items` by `itemid`. Keep `valuenum` for numeric items and retain `value`, `valueuom`, `warning`, `charttime`, and `storetime` for audit. Do not silently substitute similarly named items.

### Oxygen-support signals

Use these exact item IDs:

- `223834`, “O2 Flow,” unit L/min;
- `227287`, “O2 Flow (additional cannula),” unit L/min;
- `227582`, “BiPap O2 Flow,” unit L/min;
- `223835`, “Inspired O2 Fraction,” unit recorded as None in `d_items`;
- `226732`, “O2 Delivery Device(s),” text.

For flow items, retain nonnegative numeric values in L/min. Define `flow_max` as the maximum of the three flow streams in the window and also report each stream separately; do not add primary and “additional cannula” values because their semantics may overlap. For `223835`, accept numeric values in [0.21, 1.0] as fractions and values in (1, 100] after division by 100; values outside the resulting [0.21, 1.0] range are invalid and reported, not winsorized into validity. Define `fio2_max` as the maximum valid fraction. Normalize `226732` text only for descriptive device-frequency tables; no device category is a primary exposure unless a clinician reviews the mapping.

The primary oxygen covariates are continuous `flow_max`, `fio2_max`, and indicators for whether each was observed. A prespecified descriptive high-support indicator is `flow_max >= 4 L/min OR fio2_max >= 0.40`; it is not interpreted as a treatment threshold and is not used to choose a cut point after looking at outcomes.

### Vital-instability signals

Use:

- HR `220045`, bpm;
- respiratory rate `220210`, insp/min;
- SpO2 `220277`, %;
- non-invasive MAP `220181`, mmHg;
- arterial MAP `220052`, mmHg;
- temperature Fahrenheit `223761`, °F, and Celsius `223762`, °C.

For each 4-hour block in the final 24 hours (six non-overlapping blocks), calculate the minimum/maximum/median where valid and a binary abnormality flag. Prespecified validity and abnormality rules are:

- HR: valid 20–250; abnormal if <50 or >100 bpm.
- RR: valid 4–80; abnormal if <8 or >24 insp/min.
- SpO2: valid 50–100%; abnormal if <92%.
- MAP: valid 20–200; abnormal if <65 mmHg. Combine the two MAP items by taking the minimum valid MAP within a block, while retaining a source indicator and separate-source sensitivity analysis.
- Temperature: convert Fahrenheit to Celsius as ((F-32) / 1.8); valid 30–43 °C; abnormal if <36 or >=38 °C.

These are operational screening bounds, not diagnoses and not treatment targets. Do not include alarm items, APACHE-derived items, arterial oxygen pressure, arterial oxygen saturation, or ventilator-set respiratory rate in the primary vital composite.

The primary repeated-instability exposure is the proportion of the six blocks with at least one abnormal vital signal, provided that at least one valid vital measurement is present in the block. Also report the number of valid vital blocks and the fraction of observed vital measurements that are abnormal. Require at least four of six blocks with any valid vital data for the primary complete-observation analysis; the all-eligible analysis includes the validity counts and missingness indicators rather than treating unobserved as stable. The oxygen signals are not forced into this composite because oxygen measurement is selectively charted and a high `FiO2) does not have the same meaning as hypotension or tachypnea.

A secondary, prespecified joint phenotype is “repeated residual instability”: vital-instability proportion >=0.50 and either valid `flow_max >=4) or valid `fio2_max >=0.40. The continuous analyses remain primary to avoid making the entire conclusion depend on this descriptive cut point.

## 4. Required covariates and treatment-limitation audit

The minimum baseline model uses only information known by `outtime`:  `hosp/patients.anchor_age`, `gender`, `anchor_year_group`, `hosp/admissions.admission_type`, `admission_location`, `insurance`, index `icu/icustays.first_careunit`, `last_careunit`, ICU `los`, hospital time from `admittime` to index `outtime`, and count of earlier ICU stays in the same hospitalization. Add missingness indicators rather than complete-case deletion. Report age >89 as the MIMIC value 91, without treating it as exact age.

Do not use diagnoses, procedures, discharge destination, discharge notes, or all-hospital mortality indicators as ordinary adjustment variables: their timing and relation to the index are not sufficiently safe for a primary discharge-time model. A secondary clinical-risk model may add diagnosis/procedure features only if the compiler can prove they were available by `outtime`; otherwise they remain descriptive.

Treatment limitation is a major confounder and is not adequately observed. The item dictionary contains code-status item `223758`, “Code Status,” as text in `icu/chartevents`; audit its final-24-hour observation rate and raw values. Do not call lexical values “DNR,” “DNI,” or “comfort measures” without an explicit clinical mapping and review. If a clinician-reviewed mapping is supplied, add the latest pre-index category plus a missingness indicator in a sensitivity model. If it is not supplied, report code-status availability and stratify descriptively where possible, but state that residual confounding by treatment limitation remains. Do not use discharge-note text (`note/discharge`) to infer goals of care because it is post-index, unstructured, and potentially leakage. The available notes and radiology reports cannot substitute for adjudication.

## 5. Analysis plan and baselines

First report cohort flow, missingness, number of measurements per item, six-block coverage, and event counts. Use no post hoc item selection or outcome-driven threshold search.

Inferential analysis:

- Estimate cause-specific Cox models for readmission and death-before-readmission, with time zero at `outtime`,  censoring at hospital discharge/7 days, and robust standard errors by `subject_id`.
- Model `flow_max`, `fio2_max`, vital-instability proportion, observation counts, and prespecified baseline covariates. Standardize continuous exposures using training-cohort units; report hazard ratios per SD and the prespecified joint phenotype.
- Estimate Aalen–Johansen cumulative incidence at 24 hours, 72 hours, and 7 days. Report subdistribution hazard only as a descriptive competing-risk summary; it is not a causal effect.
- Report calibration (Brier score, calibration slope/intercept and observed versus predicted risk by decile) and discrimination (AUROC and AUPRC with bootstrap confidence intervals) for prediction models. Do not call a small AUC gain clinically useful without calibration and decision-curve or threshold-impact context.

Simple baseline (B0): cause-specific Cox and 7-day logistic/multinomial models containing only the minimum baseline covariates above, with one-hot categorical variables and prespecified linear continuous terms.

Bounded residual model (B1): B0 plus no more than 16 prespecified features: the four continuous residual summaries (`flow_max`, `fio2_max`, vital-instability proportion, abnormal-observed fraction), six validity/measurement-count features, the joint-phenotype indicator, and the remaining pre-index oxygen/vital source indicators. Use ridge-penalized logistic regression for the 7-day outcome and two one-vs-rest cause models; choose the penalty only inside the training data. For time-to-event inference, use the prespecified Cox model, not automated feature selection. No neural network, transformer, hyperparameter sweep, or GPU is needed to answer this question.

Use patient-level splits: train on anchor-year groups 2008–2016, validation on 2017–2019, and locked test on 2020–2022, with all admissions from a subject in one partition. Because dates are independently shifted by subject, do not use deidentified calendar dates to claim hospital-wide temporal trends. The full cohort estimates are primary for association; the locked-period analysis tests transportability within the available source periods. If any split has too few events, report it as infeasible and preserve the full-cohort estimate rather than silently changing the split.

## 6. Leakage, missingness, competing risks, and sensitivity checks

The executable pipeline must assert:

- no `charttime >= outtime`;
- no rows from a later ICU stay in exposure construction;
- no `storetime) used as measurement time;
- no `dischtime`, `deathtime`, `hospital_expire_flag`, `discharge_location`, future ICU rows, discharge-note text, or radiology text in B0/B1;
- every exposure row joins to exactly one `stay_id), and every index has one hospital admission;
- the first-index rule is applied before outcome computation.

Missingness is clinically informative. Report it, include measurement-density features in B1, and repeat complete-observation and all-eligible analyses. A large effect confined to patients with dense charting is evidence of ascertainment/selection sensitivity, not a robust physiologic claim.

Prespecified sensitivity analyses are: 12-hour and 48-hour exposure windows; 2-hour minimum readmission gap; all eligible ICU discharges clustered by hospitalization; separate NBP versus arterial MAP; excluding text/device items; excluding patients with any valid final-window code-status limitation after clinical mapping; and full-hospitalization follow-up. These are robustness checks, not opportunities to select the most favorable result.

## 7. Falsification and interpretation

Falsification criteria are fixed before outcome inspection:

- The primary hypothesis is adverse if adjusted associations are consistently below 1 or if cumulative incidence is lower in the high-instability group.
- It is unsupported/inconclusive if confidence intervals are wide, event counts are insufficient, oxygen item coverage is too sparse, or estimates reverse materially across measurement-coverage definitions.
- It is supportive only if the direction is prespecified and consistent for both the continuous burden and the joint phenotype, the 95% interval excludes no association for at least one primary competing event, the Aalen–Johansen curves agree, calibration improves over B0 in the locked period, and the result is not confined to one oxygen item, one MAP source, or dense-charting patients. A statistically detectable but very small change without calibration or plausible absolute-risk separation is prognostic evidence, not a discharge-policy justification.
- A higher death-before-readmission signal with no readmission signal means the phenotype may identify treatment limitation or non-readmission death, not ward readmission risk.
- A readmission signal that vanishes when death is treated as competing means a composite or naive logistic analysis was misleading.
- A signal that appears only in discharge-note text or after `outtime` is leakage and invalid.
- A permutation of final-window exposure vectors within the training partition is a computational negative control; it should remove predictive performance. Failure indicates pipeline leakage or split contamination, not biology.

What the study can establish: reproducible associations, event timing, measurement coverage, competing-event-specific risk, and bounded out-of-period predictive performance within MIMIC-IV.

What it cannot establish: that instability caused readmission/death; that ICU discharge caused harm; that changing oxygen, delaying transfer, or adding monitoring improves outcomes; the true treatment-limitation state; functional status, cognition, ward staffing, post-discharge oxygen availability, or unrecorded physiology; or external clinical utility. Those claims require clinician adjudication, richer prospective data, intervention studies, or external validation.
