# Robustness of time-weighted hypotension burden for early AKI: severity conditioning and explicit solver deliverable

## Scientific question

Among adult ICU patients with adequate systolic and diastolic blood-pressure measurements in the first 24 hours of ICU care, does a prespecified time-weighted hypotension burden measure improve prediction of acute kidney injury (AKI) within 48 hours beyond the 24-hour mean arterial pressure (MAP), after controlling for baseline illness severity, measurement density, and after showing that the signal is robust to the choice of blood-pressure summary (MAP, systolic, diastolic, combined)?

The unresolved claim is that duration and time-weighting of hypotension add clinically meaningful prognostic information beyond MAP, after accounting for baseline illness severity, vasopressor exposure, baseline kidney function, hospital, and measurement density. The strongest rival is that the apparent burden signal is an artifact of recording density, measurement timing, or baseline illness severity: sicker patients both have more hypotensive time and higher AKI risk, so an unadjusted or inadequately adjusted burden signal may reflect severity rather than an independent hemodynamic mechanism. A null result after severity conditioning would weaken the claim that a more complex burden measure is useful; a positive result would support further validation and prospective study.

## Why it matters

MAP is the common bedside summary and a natural baseline for hemodynamic risk. Minimum systolic blood pressure alone can overstate a single low reading, while MAP may understate sustained hypoperfusion. If time-weighted burden improves early AKI risk stratification beyond MAP after conditioning on baseline severity, it may inform monitoring and resuscitation decisions. If it does not, clinicians and researchers should not add a more complex measure without evidence.

## Prior evidence

- [K1] The INSPIRE database study by Liu et al. (2026) demonstrates that sustained intraoperative MAP <65 mmHg, particularly for >=30 min, is associated with higher odds of postoperative ICU admission after adjustment for measured clinical and surgical factors. Time-weighted area below MAP 65 mmHg and duration within the strict operation window are associated with ICU admission. This supports the concept that time-weighted hypotension burden is a clinically relevant measure, but the study is limited to gastrointestinal cancer surgery patients and does not address AKI prediction.
- [K2] The Cleveland Clinic study by Tarbaghia et al. (2026) demonstrates that early hemodynamic response to dobutamine in septic shock with myocardial dysfunction is associated with more ICU-free days and lower adjusted 28-day ICU mortality. Baseline echocardiographic parameters did not identify responders. This supports the clinical importance of hemodynamic measures in ICU patients, but the study is limited to septic shock patients and does not address hypotension burden or AKI prediction.
- [K3] The StatPearls review by Goyal et al. (2026) provides a summary of KDIGO AKI criteria, including an increase in serum creatinine by 0.3 mg/dL or more within 48 hours, an increase to 1.5 times or more than baseline, or urine volume less than 0.5 mL/kg/h for at least 6 hours. KDIGO is the most recent and most commonly used tool for AKI identification. This supports the use of KDIGO criteria for the outcome definition, but the source is a review and does not provide original data or a specific study design.

## Population and temporal design

Use eICU 2.0 adult ICU admissions with at least one systolic and diastolic blood-pressure measurement in the first 24 hours of ICU care. Require adequate measurement coverage (at least 12 hours with BP measurements, or a prespecified density threshold) to reduce recording-density artifacts. Exclude admissions with missing ICU start time, age <18, or no creatinine measurement before or within the first 24 hours.

Define the exposure window as the first 24 hours from ICU admission. Define the outcome window as AKI within 48 hours from ICU admission, using KDIGO creatinine criteria when available. Baseline covariates are measured before or at the start of the exposure window.

## Exposure definitions

1. Time-weighted hypotension burden (primary): For each patient, compute the fraction of the first 24 hours with systolic BP <90 mmHg, weighted by the duration of each hypotensive interval. Use a prespecified interpolation rule (linear interpolation between measurements, with a maximum gap threshold of 2 hours) to estimate continuous BP. Report sensitivity analyses with different interpolation rules and gap thresholds.

2. 24-hour MAP mean (primary baseline): Compute the mean arterial pressure over the first 24 hours using the same interpolation rule. This is the primary baseline comparator.

3. Nadir systolic BP (secondary comparator): Include as a secondary comparator to preserve the parent branch's question.

4. Alternative burden estimands (robustness):
   - MAP-based burden: fraction of the first 24 hours with MAP <65 mmHg, weighted by duration.
   - Systolic-based burden: fraction of the first 24 hours with systolic BP <90 mmHg, weighted by duration (this is the primary burden).
   - Diastolic-based burden: fraction of the first 24 hours with diastolic BP <60 mmHg, weighted by duration.
   - Combined burden: fraction of the first 24 hours with either systolic BP <90 mmHg or MAP <65 mmHg, weighted by duration.

The primary estimand is the systolic-based burden. The alternative estimands are used to test whether the burden signal is robust to the choice of blood-pressure summary. If the signal is robust, all three summaries should show a consistent direction and magnitude of association with AKI, after adjustment for measurement density and baseline severity.

## Baseline illness severity: measurement and conditioning

### Measurement

The primary severity measure is the **APACHE IV score** computed from eICU 2.0 fields available at or before ICU admission. APACHE IV uses the following components, all of which are available in the eICU 2.0 schema:

- **Age** (from `patient.age`)
- **Acute physiological variables** (from `vitalperiodic` and `vitalap` at or before ICU start): temperature, mean arterial pressure, heart rate, respiratory rate, oxygen flow, PaO2/FiO2 ratio (if available), arterial pH, serum sodium, serum potassium, serum creatinine, urine output, white blood cell count, Glasgow Coma Score
- **Chronic health points** (from `stay.admissiontype` and `stay.primarydiagnosis`): chronic organ insufficiency or immunocompromised status

If APACHE IV cannot be computed for a patient (e.g., missing acute physiological variables), the secondary severity measure is the **SOFA score** computed from the same eICU 2.0 fields:
- Respiratory: PaO2/FiO2 ratio (if available) or oxygen flow
- Coagulation: platelet count (from `lab`)
- Liver: bilirubin (from `lab`)
- Cardiovascular: mean arterial pressure and vasopressor use (from `vitalperiodic`, `vitalap`, `infusion`)
- Central nervous system: Glasgow Coma Score
- Renal: creatinine and urine output (from `lab`)

If neither APACHE IV nor SOFA can be computed, the patient is flagged as "severity score unavailable" and included in a prespecified sensitivity analysis (see below).

### Conditioning in the primary analysis

The primary analysis **must** condition on baseline illness severity. Specifically:

1. **Primary model:** Hospital-stratified logistic regression with time-weighted hypotension burden, 24-hour MAP, and baseline severity score (APACHE IV, with SOFA as fallback) as predictors. The severity score is entered as a continuous covariate (standardized to mean 0, SD 1) to avoid arbitrary cut-point choices.

2. **Severity stratum analysis:** Repeat the primary analysis within prespecified severity strata (e.g., APACHE IV <10, 10–19, >=20) to test whether the burden signal is consistent across severity levels. This is a required component of the primary analysis, not an optional sensitivity analysis.

3. **Missing severity score sensitivity analysis:** Repeat the primary analysis excluding patients with unavailable severity scores. Compare the effect estimate and confidence interval to the primary model. If the effect estimate changes by more than 20% (relative) or the confidence interval crosses the null, the result is flagged as sensitive to severity score availability.

4. **Vasopressor adjustment:** Vasopressor exposure in the first 24 hours (any use, maximum dose, duration) is included as a covariate in the primary model, because vasopressor use is both a treatment for hypotension and a marker of severity. This is a required component of the primary analysis.

## Measurement-density controls

Measurement density is a potential confounder of the burden signal. Patients with more frequent BP measurements may have a different distribution of hypotensive intervals, and patients with more frequent measurements may be more severely ill. To control for measurement density, we define the following variables:

1. BP measurement count per hour: the number of BP measurements in the first 24 hours, divided by 24.
2. Time since last measurement: the maximum gap between consecutive BP measurements in the first 24 hours.
3. Measurement coverage: the fraction of the first 24 hours with at least one BP measurement.

These variables are included as covariates in the primary analysis. We also perform a sensitivity analysis excluding patients with measurement density below a prespecified threshold (e.g., <1 measurement per hour).

## Outcome definition

AKI within 48 hours from ICU admission, using KDIGO creatinine criteria:
- Stage 1: Creatinine >=1.5x baseline or >=1.5x baseline with urine output <0.5 mL/kg/h for >=6 hours
- Stage 2: Creatinine >=2x baseline or >=2x baseline with urine output <0.5 mL/kg/h for >=12 hours
- Stage 3: Creatinine >=3x baseline or >=4.0 mg/dL with acute decrease, or urine output <0.3 mL/kg/h for >=24 hours

Use the earliest creatinine measurement before or within the first 24 hours as the baseline. If no baseline creatinine is available, exclude the patient.

## Covariates and adjustment

- Age, sex, race, ethnicity
- ICU admission type (medical, surgical, other)
- Primary diagnosis (if available)
- **Baseline illness severity (APACHE IV, with SOFA as fallback) — required in primary analysis**
- Vasopressor exposure in the first 24 hours (any, dose, duration) — required in primary analysis

- Measurement density (number of BP measurements per hour in the first 24 hours)

## Solver deliverable

The solver must produce the following outputs. Completion is established when all of the following are present and internally consistent:

### Required fitted models

1. **Primary logistic regression model (Model A):** Hospital-stratified logistic regression with predictors: time-weighted hypotension burden (systolic-based, primary), 24-hour MAP, baseline severity score (APACHE IV/SOFA), vasopressor exposure, baseline creatinine, age, sex, race, ethnicity, ICU admission type, primary diagnosis, measurement density (count/hour, max gap, coverage), hospital fixed effects.
   - Output: coefficient estimates with 95% confidence intervals for each predictor, particularly the burden coefficient and the MAP coefficient.

2. **Robustness logistic regression models (Models B1–B4):** Repeat Model A with each alternative burden estimand (MAP-based, systolic-based, diastolic-based, combined) as the burden predictor.
   - Output: coefficient estimates with 95% confidence intervals for each burden estimand.

3. **Severity stratum models (Models S1–S3):** Repeat Model A within each prespecified severity stratum (APACHE IV <10, 10–19, >=20).
   - Output: burden coefficient estimates with 95% confidence intervals within each stratum.

4. **Incremental prediction models:**
   - **Model M0 (baseline):** Hospital-stratified logistic regression with 24-hour MAP and all covariates except burden.
   - **Model M1 (full):** Hospital-stratified logistic regression with 24-hour MAP, time-weighted burden, and all covariates.
   - Output: C-index, Brier score, calibration-in-the-large (intercept), and calibration slope for both models, with 95% confidence intervals. Report the difference in C-index and Brier score between M1 and M0 with 95% confidence intervals.

### Required uncertainty reporting

1. **Confidence intervals:** All point estimates (coefficients, C-index, Brier score, calibration metrics) must be reported with 95% confidence intervals. Use bootstrap confidence intervals (1000 resamples, stratified by hospital) for C-index and Brier score. Use Wald confidence intervals for logistic regression coefficients.

2. **Calibration:** Report calibration-in-the-large (intercept of the recalibration line) and calibration slope for Model M0 and Model M1. A calibration-in-the-large significantly different from 0 or a calibration slope significantly different from 1 indicates miscalibration. Report the difference in calibration metrics between M1 and M0 with 95% confidence intervals.

3. **Hospital-level analysis:** Repeat the primary analysis (Model A) within each hospital with at least 50 patients and at least 10 AKI events. Report:
   - The burden coefficient estimate and 95% confidence interval for each hospital.
   - The proportion of hospitals with a positive burden coefficient (consistent direction).
   - The range of burden coefficient estimates across hospitals.
   - A forest plot of hospital-level burden coefficient estimates with 95% confidence intervals.
   - If fewer than 5 hospitals meet the minimum sample size, report the hospital-level analysis as "insufficient hospital-level data" and do not interpret the hospital-level results.

4. **Sensitivity analyses:** Report the following sensitivity analyses with 95% confidence intervals:
   - Exclusion of patients with unavailable severity scores.
   - Exclusion of patients with vasopressor exposure in the first 24 hours.
   - Exclusion of patients with measurement density <1 measurement per hour.
   - Different hypotension thresholds (systolic <80, <90, <100 mmHg).
   - Different outcome windows (24 hours, 48 hours, 72 hours).
   - Different interpolation rules and gap thresholds.

### Required summary outputs

1. A single table summarizing the primary analysis (Model A) with all coefficient estimates, 95% confidence intervals, and p-values.
2. A single table summarizing the robustness analysis (Models B1–B4) with burden coefficient estimates, 95% confidence intervals, and p-values for each estimand.
3. A single table summarizing the severity stratum analysis (Models S1–S3) with burden coefficient estimates, 95% confidence intervals, and p-values for each stratum.
4. A single table summarizing the incremental prediction analysis (Models M0 and M1) with C-index, Brier score, calibration-in-the-large, and calibration slope, with 95% confidence intervals and the difference between models.
5. A forest plot of hospital-level burden coefficient estimates with 95% confidence intervals.
6. A summary of all sensitivity analyses with effect estimates and 95% confidence intervals.

## Analysis plan

1. Descriptive analysis: Compare time-weighted burden, MAP, and nadir systolic BP across AKI and non-AKI groups. Report measurement density, artifact rates, and severity score distribution.

2. Unadjusted association: Compare AKI incidence across quartiles of time-weighted burden, MAP, and nadir systolic BP. Report risk ratios and 95% confidence intervals.

3. Adjusted association (primary): Fit hospital-stratified logistic regression models with time-weighted burden, MAP, baseline severity score, vasopressor exposure, and all other covariates. Report adjusted odds ratios and 95% confidence intervals.

4. Robustness to summary choice: Repeat the adjusted association analysis for each alternative burden estimand (MAP-based, systolic-based, diastolic-based, combined). Report the direction and magnitude of the association for each summary. If the signal is robust, all summaries should show a consistent direction and magnitude of association with AKI, after adjustment for measurement density and baseline severity.

5. Severity stratum analysis: Repeat the primary analysis within each prespecified severity stratum. Report the burden coefficient estimate and 95% confidence interval within each stratum.

6. Incremental prediction: Fit Model M0 (MAP only) and Model M1 (MAP + burden). Compare discrimination (C-index) and calibration (Brier score, calibration-in-the-large, calibration slope) using prespecified metrics. Report the incremental improvement and its 95% confidence interval.

7. Hospital transportability: Repeat the primary analysis in each hospital and report the range of effect sizes and the proportion of hospitals with consistent direction. Use a prespecified threshold for clinical meaningfulness (e.g., C-index improvement >=0.01).

8. Sensitivity analyses: As specified in the solver deliverable section.

## Falsification criteria

- **Supportive:** Time-weighted burden improves discrimination and calibration beyond MAP in the primary analysis (Model A), with consistent direction across hospitals and sensitivity analyses. The signal is robust to the choice of blood-pressure summary (all summaries show a consistent direction and magnitude of association with AKI, after adjustment for measurement density and baseline severity). The burden coefficient is positive and statistically significant in at least 2 of the 3 severity strata. The incremental C-index improvement is >=0.01 with a 95% confidence interval that does not include 0.

- **Adverse:** No clinically meaningful improvement in discrimination or calibration beyond MAP after conditioning on baseline severity, or inconsistent direction across hospitals. The signal is not robust to the choice of blood-pressure summary (only one summary shows a significant association with AKI, after adjustment for measurement density and baseline severity). The burden coefficient is not statistically significant in any severity stratum. The incremental C-index improvement is <0.01 or the 95% confidence interval includes 0.

- **Inconclusive:** Sparse outcomes, unstable hospital estimates, failure to control recording density, or the effect estimate changes by more than 20% (relative) when excluding patients with unavailable severity scores.

## Method comparison

- Simple baseline: 24-hour MAP mean, adjusted for baseline covariates including severity and measurement density.
- Time-aware alternative: Time-weighted hypotension burden, adjusted for baseline covariates, severity, and measurement density.
- Scientific uncertainty: Whether the added complexity of time-weighted burden provides clinically meaningful incremental prediction beyond MAP after conditioning on baseline severity.
- Resource estimate: CPU-only, ~2–4 hours for the primary analysis on a 16-core machine (increased from the parent's 1–2 hours due to the additional severity stratum analysis and bootstrap confidence intervals).
- Selection reason: The time-aware alternative is scientifically useful because it tests whether duration of hypoperfusion adds information beyond the common MAP summary, after accounting for the strongest rival explanation (baseline severity).

## Limitations

- eICU 2.0 is a retrospective cohort, so causal claims are not possible.
- Measurement density and artifact rates may vary across hospitals, affecting the interpretation of the results.
- The primary outcome is AKI within 48 hours, which may not capture all clinically relevant kidney injury.
- The analysis is limited to patients with adequate BP measurement coverage, which may introduce selection bias.
- APACHE IV and SOFA scores are computed from eICU 2.0 fields and may not be identical to scores computed from the original scoring instruments. Missingness in severity scores is handled by a prespecified sensitivity analysis.
- Hospital-level analysis is limited to hospitals with sufficient sample size, which may reduce the number of hospitals in the transportability analysis.
