# UKB-05: Bone and muscle discordance and fracture risk

## Hypothesis
Participants with low bone mineral density (BMD) AND low grip strength have a
higher fracture risk than participants with low BMD alone, and this excess risk
is partly mediated by higher fall incidence.

## Clinical importance
Fractures are a major cause of morbidity and mortality in aging populations.
Current clinical practice (FRAX tool) focuses on BMD for fracture risk
assessment. If grip strength adds independent predictive information beyond
BMD, this would:
1. Identify a simple, low-cost bedside test (handgrip dynamometry) that could
   augment DXA-based risk stratification.
2. Suggest that bone strength and fall susceptibility are two distinct
   pathways to fracture, with different modifiable targets.
3. Inform clinical decisions about who to refer for additional fracture
   prevention interventions.

## Leading claim vs strongest rival
- **Leading claim**: Low BMD + low grip → higher fracture risk than low BMD
  alone, with part of the excess mediated by falls.
- **Strongest rival**: Frailty confounding — a general state of vulnerability
  that affects BMD, grip, and falls simultaneously.

## Discriminating observation
If the leading claim is correct, the BMD×Grip interaction should be
significant and attenuated (but not eliminated) after adjusting for falls.
If the rival is correct, the interaction should attenuate to null after
adjusting for age, BMI, comorbidities, and physical activity.

## Analysis
1. Cox PH for incident fracture, BMD × Grip interaction, adjusted for age,
   sex, BMI, smoking, walking pace, falls.
2. Mediation: proportion of interaction explained by falls.
3. Site-specific: hip (S72) vs. non-hip.
4. Sensitivity: exclude fractures within 2 years of baseline.
5. Learned alternative: LightGBM + SHAP for non-linear interaction detection.

## Falsification
- **Supportive**: BMD×Grip interaction significant, attenuated but not
  eliminated after falls adjustment.
- **Adverse**: No interaction, or fully explained by falls.
- **Inconclusive**: Borderline interaction, or insufficient fracture events.

## Method comparison
- **Baseline**: Cox PH with prespecified covariates and interaction term.
- **Learned alternative**: LightGBM + SHAP for non-linear interaction detection.
- **What the alternative reveals**: Whether BMD-grip relationship is linear
  (baseline) or has threshold/non-linear structure (learned).
- **Resource**: Both fit on CPU in minutes (~47k rows). No GPU.
