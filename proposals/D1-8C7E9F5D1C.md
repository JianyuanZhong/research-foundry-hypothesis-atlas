# Do laboratory-testing practices make ICU mortality risk models fail across hospitals?

## Scientific deliverable

The future solver must newly construct a leakage-safe six-hour eICU ledger, fit a matched clinical-only model and a testing-process-augmented model, fit the prespecified competing-exit alternative, and evaluate them on hospitals never used for fitting. Completion requires:

1. an auditable cohort flow, source/header/unit audit, accepted analyte-unit rules, exact feature ledger and whole-hospital split;
2. out-of-fold development and held-out-hospital predictions for the clinical-only and process-augmented versions of both model families;
3. the primary paired transport penalty in net benefit at a fixed 10% threshold, with 95% hospital-cluster uncertainty;
4. AUROC, AUPRC, Brier/log loss, calibration intercept/slope, reliability curves and decision-curve net benefit at 5%, 10% and 20%;
5. competing expired-unit-exit and alive-unit-exit CIFs from the ordered model, process-only diagnostics, hospital-wise effects, second-hash stability and uniquepid-cluster sensitivity; and
6. a conclusion labelled supportive, adverse or inconclusive and linked to computed outputs.

No model was fitted in discovery and no clinical result is claimed.

## Seed, unresolved question and clinical importance

This is a dataset-specific adaptation of expert seed [starting question], “Prediction models' dependence on testing practices.” The strongest claim currently supported is only that the frozen eICU snapshot contains ICU-relative laboratory rows, physiologic observations, hospital identifiers and an ICU discharge-status field that can support a bounded transport experiment. The seed itself is an untested hypothesis; it does not establish that testing frequency is a practice signal, that the fields represent orders, or that transport failure occurs.

The unresolved claim is:

> At a six-hour ICU landmark, does adding only observable laboratory-testing behavior (frequency, timing, analyte observation/missingness and result-availability proxies) create a meaningful apparent development advantage that loses calibration or decision utility on hospitals held out, compared with a matched model using the same clinical measurements but no deliberate testing-process features?

This matters because a risk score used to prioritize monitoring, escalation review or scarce critical-care attention should identify patient risk rather than a hospital's propensity to order panels. The proposed result could support excluding process variables, retaining them only with site adaptation, or treating their transport penalty as a deployment warning. It does not claim that a model changes care or that hospital practice is causal.

The substantive advance over the seed and existing generic transport comparisons is a predeclared clinical decision estimand, a fixed leakage-safe landmark, a matched process decomposition, and an ordered competing-exit analysis. It distinguishes a model that predicts a documented hospital-exit process from one that transports a patient-risk signal. The current renal-support evolution asks whether a post-rise support record is clinically consequential and source-concordant. This proposal addresses a different but complementary uncertainty—whether the observation regime itself changes the validity of an ICU mortality decision—and therefore is not a mechanical restatement of renal-support heterogeneity.

## Population, temporal boundary and outcome

Use each eICU unit stay as one observation; do not concatenate later ICU stays. Include adults with patient.age >= 18, parsing age > 89 as 90 with an age-censored flag, and require a valid patient.unitdischargeoffset > 360. The prediction landmark is ICU minute 360. No row with a time field after minute 360 may be used as a predictor.

The primary endpoint is expired ICU-unit exit by minute 2880 (48 hours from ICU admission), not an exact death-time endpoint. Set event time to patient.unitdischargeoffset only for the documented event process; patient.unitdischargestatus=Expired is the event. patient.unitdischargestatus=Alive before minute 2880 is a competing alive unit exit. An alive or expired stay with unitdischargeoffset > 2880 is administratively event-free at the 48-hour boundary. A NULL/other unitdischargestatus is unknown-status censoring and is never recoded as alive. Stays with unitdischargeoffset <= 360 are excluded because the six-hour feature window is not observable. The primary binary logistic target is expired exit by 2880 among known-status stays; the ordered model uses the same target through cause-specific hazards and retains alive exit as a competing state.

This is a documented ICU-unit-exit outcome. It cannot establish exact time of biological death, post-ICU mortality, or a treatment effect. A secondary all-unit-discharge mortality analysis may be reported only as a distinct horizon-free sensitivity, never pooled with the 48-hour endpoint.

## Feature construction and leakage control

Before fitting, print row counts, duplicate-ID counts, invalid offsets, nonnumeric values, unit strings and per-hospital support. Deduplicate exact labid and vitalperiodicid. A lab record is eligible for the primary ledger only if 0 <= labresultoffset <= 360 and, when labresultrevisedoffset is nonblank, labresultrevisedoffset <= 360. A sensitivity ignores revision offset but must be labelled less availability-conservative. This treats labresultoffset as the available result time because no order or turnaround timestamp exists; the assumption is explicitly observational.

Define a development-frozen 12-analyte panel by case-folded exact labname: creatinine, bun, sodium, potassium, chloride, glucose, lactate, wbc, hemoglobin, hematocrit, bilirubin and albumin. Do not add aliases after seeing held-out performance. For each analyte, use numeric labresult only when the development set has a prespecified accepted nonblank labmeasurenamesystem/labmeasurenameinterface pair and the row matches it; no unit conversion is permitted. If no analyte has a stable accepted pair, remove that analyte's value features but retain its observation-process indicators and mark the value branch as unavailable. Conflicting, blank or nonnumeric values are excluded from value summaries and counted in the audit. No labresulttext is modeled.

Clinical values are median, minimum, maximum, interquartile range, last value and least-squares slope over 0–360 for each accepted analyte; development medians impute missing summaries without flags. Vital summaries use the same six statistics for temperature, sao2, heartrate, respiration and systemicmean, with no vital count or explicit missingness indicator in the clinical-only block. For a variable with fewer than two time points, slope is missing. All imputations and scaling parameters are development-only.

The process block contains only observation metadata: total lab rows, numeric lab rows, unique case-folded analytes, number of panel analytes observed, exact panel presence indicators, counts in [0,120] and (120,360], first and last labresultoffset, median gap across distinct offsets, fraction with explicit system/interface, and rows with nonblank labresultrevisedoffset <=360. It does not contain hospitalid, unitdischarge fields, any post-landmark row, or outcome. The process-only diagnostic uses this block alone. A row observed at minute 350 cannot use a later revision or a post-landmark value.

The clinical-only block includes the same accepted laboratory values as B1 but no lab counts, presence indicators, gaps or revision metadata. This is intentionally conservative: it asks whether the process block adds information beyond observed clinical measurements, while acknowledging that imputed missing values do not recover unmeasured physiology.

## Split and estimands

Construct connected components of hospitals linked by any shared uniquepid in patient. Assign each component to 80% development or 20% held-out hospitals using SHA-256 of the fixed string eicu-testing-practice-v1 + NUL + sorted component hospital IDs, modulo 100; buckets 0–79 are development and 80–99 held out. Do this before cohort summaries, accepted units, imputation, model fitting, threshold choice or standardization. Report component sizes and loss. A second fixed namespace is a stability sensitivity. This is internal transport, not external validation; the catalog's reserved participant partition is not claimed as independent validation.

Within development, use five deterministic hospital folds for out-of-fold predictions. No patient, hospital component or uniquepid crosses the outer split. If fewer than 10 held-out hospitals or if any required evaluation has fewer than 25 expired exits, the corresponding transport inference is inconclusive.

Let NB_m^D(q) be the development out-of-fold net benefit at threshold q for model m, and NB_m^H(q) be the aggregate held-out-hospital net benefit from frozen development fitting. For q=0.10, the primary estimand is:

D_NB10 = [NB_B1^D(0.10) - NB_B0^D(0.10)] - [NB_B1^H(0.10) - NB_B0^H(0.10)].

Positive D_NB10 means the testing-process augmentation loses more decision utility on unseen hospitals than it gains in development. Net benefit is TP/N - FP/N * q/(1-q), with no post-hoc threshold or recalibration. Compute the same D_NB10 for the ordered competing-risk model (A1 versus A0). Secondary estimands are the analogous differences for AUROC, AUPRC, Brier score, log loss, calibration slope/intercept and net benefit at q=0.05 and q=0.20. Also report per-held-out-hospital paired contrasts, hospital-weighted and patient-weighted summaries, process-only performance, and observed-versus-predicted CIFs.

The primary clinical-importance rule is supportive of testing-practice dependence only if: (i) B1 has a development NB10 gain of at least 0.005 (five net correctly flagged patients per 1,000 at the 10% threshold); (ii) D_NB10 is at least 0.005 with a two-sided 95% hospital-cluster interval excluding zero; (iii) the direction is reproduced by the ordered model and the second split; and (iv) at least 75% of evaluable held-out hospitals have nonnegative process-model transport loss. This is a decision-utility margin, not a treatment threshold. If B1 does not improve in development, the dependence hypothesis is not supported even if its raw performance differs. Adverse evidence is a process-augmented model with no material development gain and no larger held-out loss, with calibrated held-out performance no worse than B0. Inconclusive evidence includes sparse endpoints, inadequate support/overlap, unstable unit rules, missing accepted value units, nonconvergence, wide intervals crossing the margin, or discordance between model families.

## Matched models

B0 and B1 are regularized logistic regressions with a fixed penalty grid selected only by development hospital-fold log loss. Use identical cohort, feature scaling, imputation, split and thresholds. B0 uses the clinical block; B1 adds the process block. Do not include hospital identity or hospital metadata in either deployable model. Report coefficients and grouped process contributions, but do not interpret coefficients causally.

A0 and A1 are the same clinical-only/process-augmented pair in a cause-specific discrete-time competing-risk model with 30-minute intervals. Fit separate regularized pooled-logistic hazards for expired and alive unit exit, use prespecified elapsed-time indicators, and calculate the 48-hour expired-exit CIF by sequential transition probabilities. A stay can contribute at-risk intervals until its documented unit exit; a documented alive exit is a competing event, an expired exit is the event, unknown status censors, and the horizon truncates at 2880. This alternative preserves the outcome's observed exit process and can show whether process dependence is concentrated in early alive discharge or expired-exit hazard. It must not be judged only by discrimination: report CIF calibration and threshold utility from its expired CIF.

For both families, calibration is assessed without held-out recalibration: calibration intercept and slope, expected calibration error/ integrated calibration index with fixed bins or a prespecified smooth curve, Brier score and reliability plots. Decision curves use q=0.05/0.10/0.20. Use 500 hospital-cluster bootstrap replicates of held-out predictions for the primary interval and all model contrasts; resample whole hospitals, not rows. Add a uniquepid-cluster bootstrap sensitivity. If model refitting bootstrap is attempted, checkpoint it and label the added training uncertainty separately from the fixed-prediction interval.

## Falsification and interpretation

Falsification requires the implementation to pass all of the following predeclared checks:

1. Permuting the outcome within development hospitals before fitting must collapse AUROC toward 0.5 and remove net benefit; any retained signal indicates leakage or split failure.
2. Permuting process feature vectors within hospital while keeping clinical features and outcomes fixed should remove a process-specific incremental gain. Persistence suggests the claimed process block is acting as a proxy for another feature or hospital leakage.
3. A process-only model must not reproduce the clinical-only model's calibration and discrimination unless testing behavior itself carries the putative signal; report this as a diagnostic, not proof of hospital practice.
4. Results must be directionally stable under the revision-conservative primary rule, the revision-ignored sensitivity, and the exact vital deduplication audit. A large change is evidence of uncertain result-availability semantics.
5. A hospital-ID model and hospital metadata model are non-deployable diagnostics only. If they outperform process features in development but fail in holdout, that supports the interpretation that site identity is not transportable; it is not evidence that process variables are causal.
6. No actual* field, unitdischargestatus, unitdischargeoffset, or any row after minute 360 may enter predictors. A deliberate audit that attempts to add these forbidden fields must be rejected by the pipeline.

Supportive results mean the observed testing-process block has a reproducible development utility gain and a larger held-out loss under both matched model families. This supports a transportability warning and a case for process-blinded or site-adapted deployment, not a claim about clinician ordering intent. Adverse results mean the process block adds no meaningful development utility or transports at least as well as clinical features; the seed hypothesis is weakened. Inconclusive results mean data support or uncertainty is inadequate, or models disagree; no deployment recommendation follows.

Computationally checkable claims are source hashes and headers, exact joins, offset filters, duplicate handling, feature ledgers, split integrity, target construction, model fits, predictions, calibration, net benefit, bootstrap intervals and falsification permutations. Clinical adjudication or additional data are required to determine whether lab rows represent orders, clinician intent, test availability, turnaround, assay/instrument/specimen comparability, true physiologic missingness, or actionable mortality risk; the public snapshot lacks these. Expert review and another prospective or multi-database study would be needed before changing hospital policy. This proposal does not infer causation, quality ranking, biological mortality mechanisms or treatment benefit.
