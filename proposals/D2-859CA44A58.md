# Provisional hypothesis: context-dependent MerTK-inhibitor response in the available macrophage–fibroblast assay

## Proposed relationship

In the available E-MTAB-8873 rheumatoid-arthritis macrophage–fibroblast assay, the transcriptional response associated with a MerTK inhibitor will differ between macrophage-context and fibroblast-context samples. The primary estimand is a donor-aware, condition-verified context-by-inhibitor interaction, not a generic mean-expression change. A secondary descriptive test asks whether the interaction separates a fibroblast-linked harmful program from a macrophage-linked resolving/protective program.

This is a hypothesis, not an observed result. No dataset-specific effect, direct target action, causal effect, or clinical benefit has been established yet.

## Supporting observations and evidence boundary

The expert seed and prior campaign work motivate this question from evidence that MerTK-positive synovial macrophage subsets are associated with remission and can influence repair responses in synovial fibroblasts, while macrophage–fibroblast crosstalk and resolution programs are context-, timing-, and niche-dependent. These are biological plausibility observations, not evidence that this assay contains a reproducible treatment interaction or that UNC106238 acts directly on MerTK. The exact public references and their inspected claim mappings must be rechecked and attached before final selection.

## Falsifiable test

Fit a donor-aware baseline estimating the inhibitor-associated response separately by verified cellular context, with matched contrasts and uncertainty. Compare it with a substantive interaction model that tests whether the inhibitor response changes across macrophage versus fibroblast context and, secondarily, whether it separates the proposed harmful and protective programs. Predefine supportive evidence as a reproducible interaction with uncertainty excluding the null and concordant direction across donors/technical replicates; adverse evidence as a null or opposite interaction that fails the prespecified robustness checks; and inconclusive evidence as low precision, insufficient verified context, or unresolved donor/batch dependence. The interaction must be evaluated on held-out or independent contrasts where feasible, with permutation or donor-aware resampling controls. A positive result would support context-dependent transcriptional modulation only; it would not establish mechanism or clinical efficacy.

## Method comparison and next deliverable

The simple baseline is a donor-aware additive model with context and inhibitor indicators plus verified design covariates. The substantive alternative is a mechanistic/interaction model for context-specific program changes, with donor random effects or donor-aware resampling and program-level contrasts. The alternative can reveal whether the same treatment-associated direction is lost or reversed across cell contexts and whether a coordinated program—not a single gene—carries the signal; the additive baseline loses that structure. The future solver must report both fitted contrasts, uncertainty, diagnostics, and whether the interaction survives donor/batch controls. Exact source bindings, assay feature validation, and inspected references remain the immediate next deliverables.
