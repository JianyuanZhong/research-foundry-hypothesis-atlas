# Fixed-capacity enrichment of transported transfusion-risk heterogeneity

## Status and scientific deliverable

This is a substantive successor of `[prior hypothesis]`, itself a developed child of the repairable expert seed `[prior hypothesis]` (expert seed `[starting question]`). It preserves the parent’s population, six-hour recorded-PRBC exposure, non-overlapping 24-hour competing-risk clock, observed-risk (noncausal) estimand, exact source bindings, temporal transport audit, and missing-evidence limits.

The new scientific deliverable is a fixed-capacity trial-enrichment operating characteristic. The future Harbor solver must newly produce, without using test outcomes:

1. the parent’s auditable cohort flow and primary standardized observed-risk heterogeneity estimate (H), including D/E components and the 2008–2010, 2011–2013, 2014–2016 development; 2017–2019 calibration; 2020–2022 locked transport split;
2. a subject-level, development-fitted risk-contrast score (R_i), defined as the model-standardized predicted 24-hour D-or-E risk under recorded PRBC label 1 minus the corresponding risk under label 0, with the sign retained and no causal language;
3. held-out operating characteristics at exactly 5%, 10% (primary), and 20% review/enrollment capacity: event capture among selected episodes, positive predictive value for D/E, capture of the high-versus-low record-defined-stress contrast, calibration of both label-specific risks, and the distribution of (R_i);
4. matched comparisons of (a) hemoglobin-only selection, (b) stress-only selection, (c) the transparent interaction model Bhet, (d) the process-only Bmask, and (e) the chronological GRU J1. All use the same episodes, outcome, split, capacity and uncertainty procedure;
5. a locked later-era transport report evaluating the frozen development-era selector in 2017–2019 and 2020–2022, including event capture, PPV, calibration, overlap, cell support, missingness and process drift;
6. a conclusion ledger linking every conclusion to a computed output, or marking it as requiring adjudicated bleeding/ischemia/intent data or another study.

The output establishes completion only if it is a valid gated estimate, or a complete not-estimable report. It does not establish that transfusion benefits or harms an individual.

## Unresolved question and clinical importance

The strongest claim supported before fitting is only that MIMIC-IV contains temporally indexed hemoglobin, PRBC input-event, physiologic/support, ICU transition and death fields sufficient to attempt an observed-risk heterogeneity analysis. The expert seed and prior literature do not establish effect modification, causal benefit, clinical utility, or transportability.

The unresolved, falsifiable claim is:

> Among adult ICU episodes reaching a six-hour landmark after a hemoglobin of 6.5–8.0 g/dL and no observed recent PRBC, can a prespecified record-defined stress interaction identify, at a fixed 10% capacity, a held-out subgroup with a reproducibly different observed 24-hour D/E risk contrast between recorded PRBC and no recorded PRBC, beyond hemoglobin-only and stress-only selection, while retaining calibration and surviving the locked later-era transport audit?

The selection target is deliberately called an observed-risk contrast, not an individual treatment effect. If successful, it would justify prospective adjudicated validation of a compact phenotype as a transfusion-trial enrichment variable or review-prioritization signal. It would not prescribe transfusion. A null or unstable result would be clinically valuable because it would discourage using a plausible stress phenotype to stratify trials or monitoring without stronger evidence. The substantive advance over the parent is that temporal calibration alone does not show decision relevance: a signal can be calibrated yet fail to concentrate events or distinguish label-specific risk contrasts under a stated capacity.

## Record-defined modifier, outcome and estimand

Within [t0-12h,t0], with clinical and recording time no later than t0, define high stress as at least one supply plus one demand/support component, or at least two independent supply components:

- supply: lactate itemids 50813/52442/53154 >=2; MAP itemids 220052/220181 <65; active pressors in inputevents itemids 221289, 229617, 221653, 221749, 229630–229632, 221906, 221986 or 222315;
- demand/support: HR 220045 >=110; RR 220210 >=24; active invasive/non-invasive ventilation procedureevents 225792/225794.

Low stress requires no positive component plus valid observed supply- and demand-domain coverage. Other episodes are indeterminate and retained in flow. This is not ischemia, tissue hypoxia, oxygen delivery, or measured supply-demand imbalance.

From 	au through 	au+24h assign the first mutually exclusive state: D for admissions.deathtime in the window; E for new ventilation (procedureevents 224385/225792), new pressor starts from the listed inputevents when none is active at 	au, or new renal-replacement procedureevents 225441/225802/225803/225805/225809/225955; X for ICU exit before D/E; N for no event; U for irreconcilable/missing timestamps. Primary Y=D or E, with D/E-specific cumulative incidence and X competing.

For each model and fixed test subject, compute label-specific standardized risks using the measured pre-t0 covariate distribution in the support-eligible target population. The parent interaction is (H=Delta_{high}-Delta_{low}), where (Delta_s=Risk(Y|A=1,S=s)-Risk(Y|A=0,S=s)). The new score is (R_i=hat{risk}_i(A=1)-hat{risk}_i(A=0)), generated from the same fitted model and frozen before test outcomes. Do not interpret (R_i) as a counterfactual treatment effect.

## Models and fixed-capacity evaluation

Use the deterministic subject-level 70/15/15 train/validation/test split from the parent. B0 is penalized six-hour cause-specific hazards for D, E and X with A, S, spline hemoglobin, prespecified pre-t0 physiology/support/context, missingness and observation-intensity variables, without A-by-S. Bhet adds only the locked A-by-S interaction and is the primary scientific model. Bmask uses demographics, care context, masks/counts and A/S labels with physiologic values masked. J1 is a masked chronological GRU over twelve one-hour pre-t0 bins of the same streams, masks and time-since-observation channels, with the same static context and A label, predicting the same hazards. Fit/tune only on train/validation, use five fixed seeds for J1 and one locked test evaluation.

At each capacity q in {0.05,0.10,0.20}, select the top q fraction of test episodes by a prespecified selector score. For hemoglobin-only use the absolute distance from the prespecified hemoglobin target 7.0; for stress-only use the locked high-stress indicator; for Bhet, Bmask and J1 use |R_i| for enrichment of episodes where the recorded label is associated with the largest contrast magnitude, while preserving signed R_i in reports. This is a monitoring/enrollment prioritization comparison, not a treatment policy. Report event capture, PPV, observed event rate, calibration slope/intercept for label-specific risks, and the selected-versus-unselected distribution of stress, A, hemoglobin and R_i. A score that selects high absolute contrast but has no calibration or transport stability is not useful evidence.

Report 1,000 subject bootstrap resamples of the frozen test predictions for capture, PPV, risk contrasts, H and calibration; if the solver envelope permits, report the actual number of train/validation refits separately. Use simultaneous intervals for primary H and capacity comparisons. The future solver may use CPU for B0/Bhet/Bmask; J1 can use one allocated GPU or CPU, but GPU is not a scientific requirement. The future solver must report measured runtime/resources separately from these planning estimates.

## Transport, support and falsification

Fit selectors and model parameters only in development eras 2008–2010, 2011–2013 and 2014–2016. Use 2017–2019 for frozen calibration audit and 2020–2022 as an untouched transport test; do not refit or change q, cutoffs or phenotype definitions. Report all source coverage, cell/event support, U, overlap, effective sample size, weighted covariate balance, observation intensity, PRBC status ambiguity, care-unit mix and stress-component prevalence. If a cell has fewer than 50 subjects or 20 Y events, or any A-by-stress cell has fewer than 50 subjects/20 Y events, common-support/ESS/balance/calibration gates fail, report not estimable rather than pool or retune.

Falsifications and sensitivities are locked before test outcomes:

1. permute A within hemoglobin, stress, care-unit and anchor-year strata; H and R-based capture should lose structure;
2. permute high/low stress within hemoglobin, A, care-unit and anchor-year strata;
3. compare Bmask with Bhet; similar interaction or capture indicates a process explanation;
4. test future A against pre-t0 escalation to quantify residual indication/severity confounding;
5. repeat with blood-gas hemoglobin 50811, hemoglobin 7.0–8.0, item 225168 only, no OR/PACU items, and stress-component leave-one-out;
6. use non-PRBC products as process controls without assuming biological nullness;
7. assess q-operating characteristics by care unit, hospital day, anchor-year group and observation-intensity quintile;
8. scan predictors for post-t0 clinical/store times, terminal/discharge/death fields and post-exposure physiology.

Supportive evidence requires all gates, H’s simultaneous interval outside [-0.05,0.05], compatible Bhet/J1 sign, permutation collapse, no comparable Bmask result, and at q=0.10 a prespecified improvement in event capture/PPV over both hemoglobin-only and stress-only with calibration retained. The same direction and a simultaneous transport difference within +/-0.05 risk points plus acceptable calibration are additionally required in the locked later-era test. This supports only a reproducible observed-risk enrichment signal suitable for prospective adjudicated validation.

Adverse evidence includes reversal of H, failure to beat simple selectors, Bmask parity, permutation persistence, poor calibration, loss of support, or transport sign reversal/drift. It argues against using this phenotype for enrichment and identifies whether physiology, ascertainment or era drift is responsible. Inconclusive evidence includes not-estimable cells, wide intervals crossing the material region, missingness or unit/status ambiguity, unstable J1 seeds, or adequate prediction without stable label-specific contrast. It supports no clinical action.

## Clinical limits and unavailable evidence

MIMIC does not provide adjudicated myocardial ischemia, oxygen delivery/consumption, transfusion intent, complete active-bleeding status, clinician rationale, blood-unit compatibility, bedside appropriateness, rescue completeness, or randomized treatment assignment. Diagnoses, notes and procedures cannot repair these limitations without prespecified validated adjudication, and notes are not used as predictors. The study cannot establish causal transfusion effects, benefit/harm, utility, bedside thresholds, external validity beyond this MIMIC snapshot, or performance in the reserved catalog partition. Those claims require expert adjudication, a prospective or randomized study, and/or external validation.
