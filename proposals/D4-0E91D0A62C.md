# UKB CBC cancer-type contrast: first-CBC estimand with explicit timing and ascertainment falsification

Status: proposed child of [prior hypothesis]; no solver result has been executed here.
Dataset snapshot: ukb-completed-subset-20261002.
Scientific deliverable: newly fit a frozen first-CBC cause-specific competing-risk analysis and its same-input learned comparator; estimate the pre-specified CRC-versus-haematologic CBC contrast in short and delayed windows; quantify repeat-observation selection and calendar/gap sensitivity; produce held-out calibration/uncertainty, negative-control, and sparse-event decisions. A readiness check or model fit alone does not establish the hypothesis.

## Opening, importance, and claim boundary

Hamilton et al. [K1] show that haemoglobin/anaemia carries clinically relevant CRC risk information in UK primary-care records, but their CRC-versus-control case-control design does not establish specificity against haematologic cancer. Sala et al. [K2] report pre-diagnostic CBC trajectories in a CRC-only retrospective cohort, including haemoglobin decline, but explicitly require independent prospective validation. Hampton et al. [K3] document that colorectal prediction studies often omit calibration and have limited validation. None resolves whether a platelet-versus-haemoglobin pattern is cancer-type information or an artefact of when and why a CBC is observed.

The strongest available evidence therefore supports only that haemoglobin and some longitudinal CBC summaries can associate with CRC-related outcomes in selected datasets. It does not support a UKB cancer-type contrast, a stable biological trajectory, clinical test-indication interpretation, or clinical utility.

The unresolved, falsifiable hypothesis is:

> In adults without registered cancer at a complete first UKB CBC, the pre-specified relative phenotype of higher platelets and lower haemoglobin is more associated with CRC than with haematologic cancer in the delayed 2–5-year risk window, and any analogous change-score contrast at a second CBC is not confined to a particular repeat gap, baseline calendar era, or the near-diagnostic 0–2-year window.

This is a descriptive/predictive hypothesis, not a causal or mechanistic claim. It could change the next decision: a reproducible first-CBC signal would justify external clinical validation of single-CBC cancer-type prioritisation; a signal only after selected repeat observation or only near diagnosis would instead support a work-up/timing explanation and argue against a serial-CBC clinical rule.

## Populations and temporal estimands

### First-CBC population — primary

Include a nonmissing `eid`, parseable T0, and complete instance-0 WBC, haemoglobin, RDW, and platelets. Exclude any person with any valid qualifying cancer date on or before T0. Do not require T1 or T2.

Follow from T0 to the first of CRC, haematologic cancer, other primary cancer, death, administrative censoring at 2022-12-31, or the window boundary. Cancer classes are CRC C18/C19/C20; haematologic C81–C96; other primary malignant C00–C97 excluding those families and secondary C77–C79. Death is a competing event. Duplicate rows, invalid dates, impossible date order, and unpaired date/code entries are audited and reported.

The primary estimand is the CRC-versus-haematologic discordance contrast in the delayed risk window 2–5 years after T0. In standardized CBC variables, estimate

`D0,2–5 = (βplatelet,CRC − βHb,CRC) − (βplatelet,HEME − βHb,HEME)`

from separate cause-specific models in the risk set at 2 years, with other cancer and death as competing causes. Positive D means the higher-platelet/lower-haemoglobin contrast is relatively more CRC-associated than haematologic-cancer-associated. This is a conditional delayed-risk association among people alive and free of the target event at 2 years; it is not a causal effect and not a population risk ratio.

The 0–2-year D0 is a pre-specified near-diagnostic secondary estimand. The contrast between D0,0–2 and D0,2–5 is the timing test. Also report cumulative incidence at 2 and 5 years and the 2–5 increment, with denominators and competing events. A model that only shows 0–2 association has not supported a longer-latency CBC signal.

### Repeat-CBC population — conditional stress test, not the primary population

Define a complete research repeat as parseable T1 strictly after T0 with all four instance-1 CBC values present. The main repeat window is 2 ≤ T1−T0 ≤ 8 years, chosen to exclude immediate duplicate-like observations while retaining the realistic UKB repeat-assessment interval. A sensitivity cohort allows any strictly later valid T1 and reports the gap distribution.

For the repeat estimand require no qualifying cancer on/before T1 and no death on/before T1, then follow from T1. Repeat features are baseline CBC plus four changes z(j,1)−z(j,0), the gap in years, and the baseline covariates. The corresponding primary repeat contrast is

`DΔ,0–2 = (βΔplatelet,CRC − βΔHb,CRC) − (βΔplatelet,HEME − βΔHb,HEME)`

over T1 to T1+2; `DΔ,2–5` over T1+2 to T1+5 is a delayed sensitivity/falsification estimand. Instance 2 is sensitivity-only, never dense trajectory evidence.

This repeat cohort cannot answer whether serial CBC testing improves cancer detection in the original first-CBC population. It conditions on return for a complete research assessment, survival, being cancer-free until T1, and measurement completeness. It cannot identify clinical test indication or remove unmeasured healthcare-use, symptom, treatment, or calendar effects. A weighted repeat result is therefore a selection stress test, not a causal correction and not transportability evidence.

## Ascertainment and timing falsification

1. **First-CBC versus repeat-selection check.** Estimate D0,2–5 separately in all first-CBC-eligible people, in R=1 people with a valid complete repeat, and in R=0 people without one. R is defined from T1 availability and complete CBC only, never from future outcomes. A contrast present in R=0 as well as R=1 is less compatible with creation by repeat selection. Differences are descriptive and do not prove absence of indication bias.

2. **Measured repeat-observation weighting.** Fit `P(R=1 | X0)` using only baseline CBC, sex, age, BMI, smoking, CRP, T0 calendar year, and baseline completeness/eligibility indicators. Use a numerator based on sex and T0 year, stabilize, trim at prespecified 1st/99th percentiles, and show covariate balance before/after weighting. Compare unweighted and weighted DΔ estimates. If the point difference exceeds 0.20 log-hazard units or its 90% bootstrap interval is not wholly within ±0.20, classify measured-selection robustness as adverse; otherwise call it only compatible with measured-selection robustness. The weight cannot repair survival-to-T1 or unmeasured indication.

3. **Gap and repeat-era test.** Report DΔ by gap strata 2–4, >4–6, and >6–8 years and by T1 calendar year strata with adequate events. If the direction appears only in one gap/era, or materially changes across these strata, classify the longitudinal claim as timing/ascertainment-sensitive. Do not pool strata to rescue a reversal.

4. **Baseline calendar-era test.** Interact the first-CBC contrast with prespecified T0 calendar-year tertiles and report D0,0–2 and D0,2–5 by era. A large era interaction or a contrast limited to one era is adverse to stable cancer-type information and consistent with calendar/testing or registry changes.

5. **Near-diagnostic displacement.** Repeat each estimand after excluding the first two years after the relevant landmark. Support for a stable phenotype requires the delayed contrast to retain direction and useful precision; a near-diagnostic-only contrast is reported as such, not as long-latency evidence.

6. **Specificity and negative controls.** Compare CRC and haematologic estimates with other primary cancer. Permute cancer type among development event dates while preserving event timing and censoring; the type-discordance interaction should collapse toward zero. A similar pattern for other cancer or persistence after the type permutation is adverse to a cancer-type interpretation.

The first-CBC and repeat analyses are not combined into a single “trajectory” estimate. That would conflate a population estimand with a selected conditional estimand.

## Methods compared before selection

### A. Transparent cause-specific baseline (primary)

Fit separate cause-specific Cox models for CRC and haematologic cancer, with other cancer and death treated as competing causes in the risk sets. First-CBC predictors are the four standardized instance-0 CBC values plus age, sex, BMI, smoking, CRP, and T0 calendar year. Repeat predictors are baseline CBC, four changes, gap, and the same covariates; the weighted repeat fit uses the observation weights above. Add prespecified time-window interactions or separate 0–2 and 2–5 risk-set fits; do not infer persistence from a single proportional-hazards coefficient. Penalization is allowed only for documented separation/convergence failure and must be recorded.

Standardization uses development-fold means/SDs, separately by sex or with sex in the model, and applies the frozen transform to T1/T2. Report D contrasts with robust 95% intervals, time-varying-effect diagnostics, event/person-time tables, and Aalen–Johansen cumulative incidence for CRC, haematologic cancer, other cancer, and death. This model is primary because the contrast is auditable and clinically interpretable. It loses nonlinear thresholds and interactions.

### B. Same-input learned alternative

Fit a CPU gradient-boosted discrete-time multi-task competing-risk model on exactly the same people, predictors, landmarks, outcomes, and windows. Expand to yearly intervals and predict CRC, haematologic cancer, other cancer, and death hazards with interval number. A small fixed grid is tuned only in rolling development folds. The repeat observation head `P(R=1|X0)` is fit within each development fold for weights; future R is never a first-CBC predictor.

B can reveal nonlinear Hb/platelet ranges, interactions and time-varying type-specific hazards that A compresses into additive coefficients. It cannot reveal mechanism, test indication, or causality. Compare A and B on a final temporal holdout using target-specific calibration intercept/slope, calibration plots, 2- and 5-year cumulative-incidence Brier and log scores, and participant-bootstrap intervals. AUC/Uno C-index is secondary. A small discrimination gain without stable calibration and a stable type contrast is not a scientific advance.

### Split, uncertainty, compute

Sort by relevant landmark date; earliest 70% is development and latest 30% is final test, keeping participants intact. All transforms, imputation rules, weights, tuning, and calibration are learned within development folds. The final test is not used to choose the hypothesis or window. Report development descriptive estimates separately from held-out predictive performance; no planned result is observed here.

Use robust model intervals and 500 participant-bootstrap replicates for held-out predictions, weighted estimates, and weighted-minus-unweighted differences, preserving failures and warnings. If held-out haematologic events are fewer than 50 in any primary comparison, label the learned result and that comparison inconclusive rather than switching to a random split or changing outcomes.

## Interpretation and stop rules

Supportive results require all of the following: (a) D0,2–5 has the prespecified positive direction with useful precision; (b) the direction is not confined to R=1, one T0/T1 era, or one repeat-gap stratum; (c) DΔ is directionally compatible in unweighted and weighted analyses, with the weighting difference within the stated margin or explicitly qualified; (d) the delayed signal is not merely 0–2 near-diagnostic amplification; (e) the pattern is more specific to CRC versus haematologic cancer than to other cancer; and (f) B adds stable calibration or time-varying information on the held-out data, not just AUC.

Adverse results are a reasonably precise reversal, a large weighted/unweighted change, an era/gap interaction, disappearance after two years, a similar other-cancer pattern, or collapse under the cancer-type permutation. These would revise or abandon the claim of stable cancer-type CBC information while leaving generic CBC–cancer associations possible.

Inconclusive results include fewer than 50 held-out haematologic events, intervals wider than the 0.20 log-hazard equivalence margin, unstable date/code parsing, weight positivity failure, convergence failure, or a wide delayed null. A wide null is not refutation. Calibration failure with discrimination is not clinical usefulness.

A supportive result establishes only a temporally ordered descriptive association in a selected volunteer cohort and informs external-validation design. It does not establish diagnostic accuracy in symptomatic patients, a referral threshold, stage, mechanism, treatment benefit, or clinical utility. Clinical adjudication, laboratory indication, pathology/stage, symptoms, and external/prospective decision-linked validation are required for stronger conclusions.

## Alternatives not chosen and revisiting evidence

The transparent cause-specific model is primary because it tests the exact contrast with auditable sparse-event behavior. The gradient-boosted model is retained because it addresses a substantive uncertainty—nonlinear and time-varying type-specific information—using the same inputs and outcomes, not because complexity is intrinsically valuable.

A latent Gaussian-process/state-space model or transformer is deferred: only two broadly complete CBC repeats and sparse repeat haematologic events are available, so its extra assumptions would not identify a stable trajectory and would spend compute without improving the central ascertainment test. It should be revisited only if a larger validated longitudinal CBC source, clinical indication fields, or substantially more repeat haematologic events becomes available. A model using future R as a first-CBC predictor is rejected as leakage. Cross-namespace UKB augmentation is rejected because no authorized crosswalk is available.
