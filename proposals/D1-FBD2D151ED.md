# A common-time-zero, two-stage study of recorded peri-TACE2 systemic initiation and subsequent pathway transition

## Clinical question and substantive advance

The unresolved question is not whether a third TACE occurring many weeks later should be treated as if it were contemporaneous with a systemic prescription. It is whether, among adults with HCC already selected for a second TACE, a systemic HCC-directed medication recorded during the immediate post-TACE2 period identifies a different subsequent hepatic-reserve trajectory than a TACE2-only recorded pathway, and how both pathways subsequently transition to later recorded TACE or systemic events.

This child resolves the temporal disagreement between the two parents by separating two scientifically different estimands while retaining one common clinical time zero. The primary estimand is a day-14 landmark-conditional association between two *recorded pathways* defined from TACE2 days 1–14. The secondary stage is a descriptive competing-transition analysis from the same TACE2 origin, reporting later third-TACE and systemic records from days 15–90 (with 120, 180, and 365-day descriptive extensions). A later third TACE is not relabeled as the comparator's baseline treatment, and later events are not interpreted as treatment failure, discontinuation, response, toxicity, or survival.

The strongest claim supported by the available evidence is feasibility of this common-time-zero recorded-pathway design. The untested claim is that immediate systemic initiation is associated with less subsequent hepatic-reserve deterioration, after measured pre-TACE2 adjustment. The study cannot establish administration, adherence, intent, causal benefit, superiority, radiographic response, TACE refractoriness, toxicity, mortality, or survival.

## Hypothesis and estimands

### Primary hypothesis

Among adults with HCC and an earliest adjacent pair of qualifying TACE calendar-day episodes separated by 14–180 days, no ontology-matched systemic record on or before TACE2, and institutional observation through the day-14 landmark, patients with a qualifying systemic medication record on TACE2 days 1–14 will have a different change in exact-assay albumin and total bilirubin over the fixed TACE2 day 30–90 window than patients with no qualifying systemic record and no generic systemic-treatment procedure record during days 1–14. The prespecified favorable direction is a smaller albumin decline (or increase) without a larger bilirubin increase; this directional hypothesis is not a claim of clinical benefit.

The primary estimand is the difference in mean within-assay change, separately for `白蛋白` and `总胆红素`, under the observed, day-14-landmark-conditional pathway definition. For patient i and assay a, use the closest valid pre-TACE2 baseline in days -30 through -1 relative to the TACE2 timestamp and the closest valid follow-up value in TACE2 days 30–90. The estimand is a recorded-pathway association, not an intention-to-treat effect, per-protocol effect, or causal treatment effect.

A secondary fixed window uses TACE2 days 60–120. It is a sensitivity to follow-up timing, not a second primary endpoint and not a day-90 landmark.

### Secondary transition estimand

Starting at the same TACE2 time zero, describe the mutually exclusive first recorded event after the day-14 exposure ascertainment period through day 90: third-TACE first, later systemic first, same-day tie, or neither observed by day 90. The transition stage is not an active-comparator causal contrast. It quantifies the timing and order that motivated the design and reports the frequency of crossover within each initial recorded pathway. Extend cumulative recorded-event proportions to days 120, 180, and 365 when institutional observation permits, explicitly treating missing institutional observation as an observation limitation rather than verified survival.

The primary day-14 contrast must not call the comparator “third TACE.” Third TACE is a later process event, usually occurring well after systemic initiation, and is analyzed only in the secondary transition stage or as a descriptive post-TACE2 event.

## Primary analysis

Describe age, sex, TACE interval, TACE2 year/month, department, pre-TACE2 encounter/procedure counts, prior treatment-history labels, diagnosis proxies, and baseline assay values by recorded pathway. All confounders must be determined before or at TACE2; do not use day-14 medication composition, post-TACE2 imaging, post-TACE2 labs, or later TACE3 as propensity covariates.

Fit a prespecified propensity model for the day-14 recorded systemic pathway using only pre-TACE2 variables: age, sex, TACE1–TACE2 interval, TACE2 year/month (with flexible but prespecified calendar terms), department, prior encounter and procedure counts in 90/180 days, prior resection/ablation/transplant procedure-label indicators, and diagnosis proxies for cirrhosis, portal hypertension, ascites, encephalopathy, variceal bleeding, portal-vein invasion, and metastasis. Add closest pre-TACE2 values of exact albumin, bilirubin, INR, platelets, AFP, creatinine, and sodium when available, with missingness indicators or a prespecified complete-case sensitivity. Diagnosis proxies inherit encounter time and remain coding proxies.

Use unadjusted arm-specific means and mean differences as descriptive results. Primary adjusted analysis uses propensity-score overlap weights followed by weighted linear outcome regression for albumin change and bilirubin change separately, with robust patient-level uncertainty. Report standardized mean differences before and after weighting, propensity distributions, overlap by calendar year, arm effective sample sizes, confidence intervals, and the number of observations contributing to each assay outcome. Do not report a composite ALBI or convert assay values to clinical categories.

Because follow-up laboratory observation is selective, repeat with inverse-probability-of-observation weights estimated from pre-TACE2 variables, using an observation model defined separately for each assay/window. Show complete-case and observation-weighted estimates together. Weight truncation, if needed, must be prespecified and its effect reported; unstable weights downgrade the result to feasibility/inconclusive. A later encounter is not a survival indicator.

The primary contrast is valid only if the following gates are met: both primary arms retain the locked ambiguity exclusions; propensity overlap is adequate overall and within retained years; no retained arm has effective sample size below 100; at least 100 exposed paired outcomes remain in the 2018–2024 primary window; observation weights are stable; and complete-case and observation-weighted directions do not materially conflict. The 2020–2024 era is a prespecified sensitivity, not an unannounced replacement if its exposed paired count is below 100.

## Secondary transition analysis

From TACE2, assign each patient to a first recorded event state over days 1–90 using strict TACE and strict systemic ontologies: (1) immediate systemic first during days 1–14, (2) early third TACE first during days 1–14, (3) later third TACE first during days 15–90, (4) later systemic first during days 15–90, (5) same-day tie, or (6) neither event through day 90. For the day-14 pathway analysis, the at-risk state is defined after excluding events in days 1–14; report the exact rule and do not condition a primary laboratory contrast on a future day-90 event. A same-calendar-day systemic and TACE record is a tie, not an arbitrary ordering.

Report cumulative proportions by days 30, 60, 90, 120, 180, and 365, stratified by initial day-14 recorded pathway, together with observation denominators. Report both-event counts separately from first-event states to show crossover. Do not censor a TACE-only patient at a later systemic record or a systemic patient at a later TACE for the primary laboratory analysis; such censoring would answer a different, time-varying treatment question. A multistate model may be exploratory only, with transition-specific observation assumptions stated; descriptive cumulative incidence is the required secondary analysis.

## Falsification, adverse, null, and inconclusive criteria

1. **Ontology stability.** Repeat the primary pathway construction under broad and strict TACE definitions. A material reversal of the adjusted albumin/bilirubin direction, or a large arm-composition change attributable to ambiguous labels, falsifies the robustness of the proposed recorded-pathway finding and triggers a feasibility-only report.
2. **Pre-exposure negative control.** Repeat the same-assay change using two earlier pre-TACE2 windows, with no post-TACE2 data allowed. A material contrast suggests residual channeling, temporal leakage, or inadequate adjustment; a null negative control does not establish exchangeability.
3. **Positivity.** Require adequate pooled and year-specific propensity overlap and report arm ESS. Lack of overlap in a year cannot be repaired after seeing results by changing the era; pool adjacent years only under a prespecified rule.
4. **Observation.** Compare complete-case with pre-index observation-weighted estimates. Unstable weights or materially discordant directions make the clinical interpretation inconclusive.
5. **Measurement feasibility.** The paired-outcome gate and assay-specific denominators must be met. Fewer than 100 exposed paired outcomes in the designated primary era downgrades the analysis to precision-limited feasibility.
6. **Treatment ontology.** Re-run with placebo exclusion and the bevacizumab-partner rule; materially different conclusions indicate exposure-definition instability.
7. **Era stability.** Report year-specific pathway counts and repeat in 2020–2024. A direction that exists only in a sparse or non-overlapping era is not a stable finding.
8. **Timing placebo.** If a pseudo-index can be constructed without using future information, test a pre-TACE2 placebo contrast. Otherwise state that this falsification is unavailable rather than manufacture it.

A **supportive** result is a directionally compatible, adequately overlapped, observation-weighted association in which albumin declines less (or increases) in the systemic-record arm and bilirubin does not increase more, with no material negative-control signal and compatible strict/broad and era sensitivities. It supports only a recorded-pathway association.

An **adverse** result is greater albumin decline, greater bilirubin increase, or a robustly worse profile in the systemic-record arm. It argues against assuming a liver-preserving association but does not show that the recorded medication caused harm, because indication, severity, response, and administration are incompletely observed.

A **precise null** is a prespecified narrow confidence interval around the clinically relevant assay-change contrasts that excludes a materially meaningful difference on the assay's observed scale, while all overlap, observation, ontology, and negative-control gates pass. Because units and validated clinical thresholds are unavailable, the proposal must not label a numerical null as equivalence in liver function or safety.

A **falsified** design claim occurs when the temporal or ontology premise fails, such as major direction reversal under the locked broad/strict sensitivity, a strong pre-exposure contrast, absent positivity, or inability to distinguish exposure from generic procedure ambiguity. A **scientifically inconclusive** result includes wide intervals, insufficient paired outcomes, unstable observation weighting, discordant sensitivity directions, or missing adjudication needed for the claimed endpoint. Inconclusive is a valid result and must not be resolved by selecting a favorable window.

## Unavailable evidence and claim boundaries

Medication rows are records/orders and cannot establish administration, adherence, dose received, combination delivery, indication, or treatment intent. Diagnosis dates are encounter-time proxies. Examination reports are not clinician-adjudicated response or progression. No validated laboratory units are available. The source lacks reliable Child-Pugh/performance status, complete outside-care capture, vital-status linkage, mortality, and survival follow-up. The source cannot establish hepatic toxicity caused by treatment, treatment response, TACE refractoriness, discontinuation, failure, transplant, or causal superiority. Those claims require medication administration records, clinician review of imaging and intent, validated measurements, adjudicated outcomes, vital-status/out-of-system linkage, or a prospective or multi-institutional study.

## Change from both assessed parents

Relative to the arm-specific 90-day first-event parent, this child rejects the unequal-index comparison because systemic records cluster near day 2 while third TACE occurs around days 51–52; it anchors both primary arms at TACE2, fixes exposure ascertainment to days 1–14, and prevents future third-TACE information from defining the baseline. Relative to the day-14 common-time-zero parent, it makes the comparator's limitation explicit and adds a separate transition/process stage that preserves later third-TACE timing, order, ties, crossover, and observation denominators without relabeling them as an active-comparator treatment effect. It also binds the secondary stage to the same complete-source audit and retains strict/broad ontology, placebo/bevacizumab ambiguity, observation, positivity, negative-control, and assay-feasibility gates. Remaining uncertainty is dominated by confounding by indication/severity, selective laboratory observation, incomplete institutional capture, and absent administration, intent, imaging, validated units, and vital-status evidence.
