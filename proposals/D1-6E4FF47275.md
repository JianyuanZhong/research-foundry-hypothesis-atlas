# Residual Instability Before ICU Discharge: A Multi-Dimensional Trajectory Approach

## Scientific Question

After initial circulatory stabilization in the ICU, patients who exhibit **persistent or fluctuating multi-organ instability** over the 48 hours before discharge have higher risks of ICU readmission and in-hospital death compared to those with sustained stability.

This hypothesis refines the expert seed #10 ([starting question]) by:
1. Moving beyond simple "high oxygen requirements" to a **multi-organ trajectory** approach
2. Testing whether the **pattern** of instability (persistent vs. fluctuating) matters more than the absolute level
3. Examining whether instability in specific organ systems (respiratory, renal, cardiovascular) predicts different downstream outcomes

## Clinical Importance

The resolution of this hypothesis has direct clinical implications:

1. **Discharge Timing Optimization**: Identifying patients with residual instability could enable earlier discharge for stable patients and delay discharge for those with concerning trajectories, reducing unnecessary ICU bed occupancy.

2. **Readmission Prevention**: Patients with persistent multi-organ instability may benefit from extended monitoring, early transfer to step-down units, or post-discharge intensive care planning.

3. **Risk Stratification for Discharge Planning**: Moving beyond single-day discharge assessment to trajectory-based risk models that capture the dynamic nature of recovery.

4. **Resource Allocation**: Hospitals could use trajectory-based metrics to prioritize which patients need extended ICU stays versus safe discharge.

## Hypothesis Statement

After circulatory stabilization in the ICU, patients who show **persistent or fluctuating instability across ≥2 organ systems** in the 48 hours before discharge have:
- **Higher risk** of ICU readmission within 7 days
- **Higher risk** of in-hospital death (within 30 days)

Compared to patients with:
- Sustained stability across all measured organ systems
- Single-system instability (which may have lower risk)

## Study Population

**Inclusion criteria:**
- Patients ≥18 years admitted to ICU in MIMIC-IV
- Surviving ICU admission (alive at discharge)
- ICU stay ≥24 hours (to ensure meaningful stabilization period)
- Have ≥30 minutes of hemodynamic monitoring data (vital signs)
- Have at least 24 hours of ICU stay before discharge

**Exclusion criteria:**
- Patients with immediate transfer to another facility (discharge destination = "transfer")
- Patients with missing vital signs for >50% of ICU stay
- Patients with cardiac arrest before ICU admission

**Sample size considerations:**
- MIMIC-IV ICU stays: ~27,000
- Surviving discharges: ~18,000-20,000
- After exclusions: ~12,000-14,000 eligible patients
- Power to detect HR=1.5 for readmission/death with 80% power: ~3,000-4,000 events

### Derived Variables

#### Organ System Instability Metrics

1. **Respiratory Instability** (from icu/chartevents, itemid=22700 for SpO2, 33000 for PaO2/FiO2)

2. **Cardiovascular Instability** (from icu/chartevents, itemid=1266 for MAP, 1267 for HR)

   - Heart rate variability

3. **Renal Instability** (from hosp/d_labitems, itemid=32520 for creatinine, 32521 for BUN)
   - Creatinine trajectory (slope over last 24 hours)
   - Urine output variability (from icu/outputevents)

4. **Neurological Instability** (from icu/chartevents, itemid=1091 for GCS, 34100 for pupillary response)
   - GCS score trend (decline vs. stability)
   - Pupillary response abnormalities

#### Trajectory Classification

Patients are classified into trajectory groups based on instability patterns in the 48 hours before discharge:

1. **Sustained Stability**: No significant instability in any organ system
2. **Single-System Instability**: Instability in exactly 1 organ system
3. **Multi-System Instability**: Instability in ≥2 organ systems
   - **Persistent**: Instability present throughout the 48-hour window
   - **Fluctuating**: Instability with clear ups and downs

#### Outcome Variables

1. **ICU Readmission** (within 7 days of discharge)
   - Defined as return to ICU within 7 days
   - From hosp/admissions with admit_time within 7 days of discharge_time
   - Need to link via patient identifier

2. **In-Hospital Death** (within 30 days of discharge)
   - Defined as death in hospital within 30 days
   - From hosp/admissions with discharge_status=dead and discharge_time within 30 days
   - Competing risk with ICU readmission

## Statistical Analysis Plan

### Primary Analysis: Cox Proportional Hazards

**Outcome:** Time to first event (ICU readmission or in-hospital death)

**Predictor:** Organ system instability trajectory (categorical)
- Reference: Sustained stability
- Comparison 1: Single-system instability
- Comparison 2: Multi-system persistent instability
- Comparison 3: Multi-system fluctuating instability

**Covariates:**
- Age
- Sex
- Admitting diagnosis (from hosp/d_icd_diagnoses)
- Severity at admission (SOFA score, APACHE II)
- ICU resource use (vasopressor days, ventilator days)
- Comorbidities (from hosp/d_icd_diagnoses)

### Secondary Analysis: Machine Learning Approach

Build a multi-organ trajectory risk score using:
- **Baseline model:** Logistic regression for readmission/death
- **Learned model:** Gradient boosting (XGBoost/LightGBM) with multi-organ features
- **Mechanistic model:** Deep learning with temporal encoding of multi-organ trajectories

**Evaluation:**
- C-index (discrimination)
- Calibration plots
- Decision curve analysis (net benefit)

### Falsification Criteria

The hypothesis will be **supported** if:
- HR ≥ 1.5 for multi-system instability vs. sustained stability
- p-value < 0.05 (two-sided)
- Consistent across single-organ and multi-organ analyses

The hypothesis will be **adverse** if:
- HR < 1.0 for multi-system instability (i.e., more instability = lower risk)
- p-value > 0.05 (no association)
- Single-system instability has higher risk than multi-system

The hypothesis will be **inconclusive** if:
- 1.0 ≤ HR < 1.5 (weak association)
- p-value between 0.05 and 0.10
- Results vary substantially by organ system

## Methodological Alternatives

### Baseline Approach
- **Simple SOFA score at discharge**: Already measures organ dysfunction but doesn't capture trajectory
- **Single-day assessment**: Uses only the day before discharge
- **Limitations**: Loses dynamic information, may miss transient instability

### Learned Alternative
- **Temporal CNN/LSTM**: Models time-series patterns in multi-organ vitals
- **Attention-based models**: Weights importance of different time periods
- **Information gained**: Can detect subtle patterns humans miss, but may lack interpretability

### Mechanistic Alternative
- **Physiological modeling**: Simulates expected recovery trajectories based on organ mechanics
- **Network-based approaches**: Models interactions between organ systems
- **Information gained**: Tests whether specific physiological mechanisms drive outcomes

### Selection Rationale
- **Primary analysis**: Cox regression with clear clinical interpretation
- **ML model**: Secondary validation to assess predictive performance
- **Mechanistic**: Deferred until we understand whether simple trajectory metrics work

## Data Availability and Limitations

### Available Data
- MIMIC-IV ICU data: ~27,000 ICU stays
- Vital signs: Hourly measurements from chartevents
- Laboratory data: Frequent measurements from labevents
- Diagnosis data: ICD-9/10 codes from admissions and procedures

### Limitations
1. **Missing data**: Vital signs and labs may be missing for some patients
2. **Charting variation**: Different units may chart differently
3. **No ground truth**: No known "true" instability metric to validate against
4. **Selection bias**: Only patients with sufficient monitoring are included
5. **Temporal confounding**: Changes in ICU practices over time (2012-2019)

### Temporal Boundaries
- Study period: January 2012 - December 2019 (MIMIC-IV)
- Discharge window: 48 hours before ICU discharge
- Follow-up: 7 days for readmission, 30 days for death
