# Episode 49: Does continuous 24-hour lactate trajectory add decision-relevant mortality-risk information beyond the late lactate?

## Decision, supported claim, and unresolved hypothesis

This is a substantive, noncausal child of `[prior hypothesis]`. It preserves the parent's age-inclusive norepinephrine-entry cohort, exact 24-hour landmark and laboratory availability rules, late-lactate conditioning, leave-one-hospital-out (LOHO) validation, model forms, complete-source bindings, and hospital-refit uncertainty. It replaces the global `+0.02 AUROC` target with a local, clinically interpretable decision-analytic estimand. Binary nonclearance is not a predictor, exposure, estimand, or rescue analysis.

The strongest claim already supported by the executed parent is narrow: in 1,229 selected people from 33 EICU hospitals, adding continuous `T=ln(F/B)` and `T²` to a model already containing the late lactate produced a small, site-stable point improvement in out-of-hospital discrimination (ΔAUROC +0.00479) and proper scores, with acceptable pooled calibration. Its copied-hospital refit-bootstrap interval (−0.00849,+0.02286) did not establish even the direction of ΔAUROC or exclude the old +0.02 target. It did not establish clinical utility.

The unresolved hypothesis for Episode 49 is:

> Among the same adults alive/in hospital at the 24-hour norepinephrine-entry landmark, using the continuous lactate trajectory in addition to the late lactate increases threshold-averaged net benefit for a 24-hour high-mortality-risk review decision by at least **1 net true-positive-equivalent decision per 100 patients**, over fixed predicted hospital-mortality thresholds from 30% through 50%, compared with using late lactate without trajectory.

This asks whether the small global ranking gain is concentrated where a mortality-risk alert could alter who receives a resource-limited senior multidisciplinary review. It does not ask whether lactate should trigger resuscitation, whether review changes treatment, or whether acting on predictions improves survival.

The 30%–50% range is fixed before Episode 49's definitive refit analysis. A threshold `p` assigns an unnecessary review a harm relative to a correctly triggered review of `p/(1−p)`, spanning 0.43 to 1.00 here. The range represents substantial predicted mortality at which a resource-consuming review might be considered. It is not claimed to be a validated clinical preference distribution. It was chosen after the parent result was known, and an outcome-aware feasibility analysis was performed; therefore this same-snapshot study is a post-parent decision analysis, not independent confirmation. The range deliberately includes exploratory thresholds with favorable, null, and unfavorable differences and must not be narrowed to maximize results. Clinician, patient, operational, and ethics review is required before treating any threshold as actionable.

## Why this is an advance over existing knowledge

The inspected full-text Baysan et al. article (2022, DOI `10.1097/CCE.0000000000000750`; frozen XML source `[source checksum]`, [source checksum]) found that adding 24-hour lactate to recalibrated APACHE IV increased the C-statistic from 0.62 to 0.64, and that admission plus 24-hour lactate had the best fit. That supports modest prognostic contribution from serial values, but not decision value of trajectory beyond an already available late lactate in this EICU phenotype.

The inspected 2026 Li et al. full-text HTML (DOI `10.2196/92838`; frozen source `[source checksum]`, [source checksum]) modeled four-window lactate-to-albumin trajectories in sepsis-associated AKI, reported external validation in five Chinese hospitals, and presented calibration and decision curves. It concerns a different biomarker, population, time scale, and comparison against a baseline marker; it does not test continuous lactate change beyond late lactate.

The inspected decision-curve reporting guide (Vickers et al., 2019 publication record for 2018 guide; PMCID `PMC6261531`; frozen HTML source `[source checksum]`, [source checksum]) requires a clinically plausible threshold range, comparison with treat-all/treat-none, and calibration-aware interpretation. It also makes clear that threshold preferences arise from the decision context. Episode 49 applies those principles but labels the result “potential decision value,” because EICU cannot measure the benefit or burden of the proposed review.

The substantive advance is therefore local rather than algorithmic: quantify whether retaining the earlier lactate changes decisions enough to matter at a fixed 24-hour risk boundary, with transport and uncertainty preserved. It avoids the Episode 48 structural positivity failure because `T` remains continuous and no binary clearance-group effect is estimated.

## Primary estimand and secondary descriptions

For fixed thresholds `p ∈ {0.30,0.31,…,0.50}`, let an alert occur when the held-out predicted probability is at least `p`. For model `m`:

`NB_m(p) = TP_m(p)/N − FP_m(p)/N * p/(1−p)`.

The primary estimand is the uniformly threshold-averaged incremental net benefit, numerically integrated by the trapezoid rule over the 21 locked points and expressed per 100:

`ΔINB = 100/(0.50−0.30) * integral_[0.30,0.50] [NB_M1(p)−NB_M0(p)] dp`.

Positive values favor retaining the earlier lactate. The material hypothesis is `ΔINB >= +1.0` per 100. Uniform threshold weighting is an explicit working assumption, not an empirical distribution of patient preferences. Report the full curve so the average cannot hide crossings.

Secondary, non-label-changing outputs are: threshold-specific M0/M1/default NB and ΔNB; minimum/maximum ΔNB and fraction of thresholds positive; pooled Brier score, log loss, AUROC, calibration-in-the-large and calibration slope; a calibration plot with prespecified deciles of held-out M1 predictions (counts, mean prediction, outcome rate); mean absolute `|M1−M0|`; fraction with absolute risk shift at least 0.05; mean probability shift separately in deaths and survivors; and fixed-prediction site deletions of ΔINB. These describe risk revision and model behavior, not causal benefit or a binary exposure effect.

## Uncertainty, gates, and frozen classification

Use 2,000 copied-hospital refit-bootstrap replicates with seed `20260949`. Sample 33 hospitals with replacement. Each sampled original hospital remains one held-out group; all copies are excluded together when held out, training hospitals enter with sampled multiplicity, preprocessing and both models are refit, and each test row receives its sampled hospital multiplicity. Compute weighted TP, FP, defaults, curves, ΔINB, proper scores, and calibration metrics. Use 2.5th/97.5th percentile intervals. The parent bootstrap metrics cannot be algebraically reused because it did not retain threshold-level predictions per refit.

Computability gates:

- exact three source hashes/headers and exact population flow through 1,506/1,229/33;
- at least 25 hospitals, 800 people, 200 deaths, and 200 survivors; every retained site has both outcomes; maximum site share ≤15%;
- all 33 original M0/M1 fits converge and emit finite probabilities;
- at least 1,900/2,000 bootstrap replicates are finite and converged;
- at least 80% of 33 site deletions yield finite ΔINB;
- all 21 thresholds are evaluated exactly, with no post-result deletion or range change.

Supportive guardrails:

- M1 pooled calibration slope in [0.80,1.20] and absolute calibration-in-the-large ≤0.05;
- M1 point net benefit is at least the better of treat-all and treat-none at every locked threshold;
- point ΔNB at no threshold is below −0.5 per 100;
- at least 80% of finite site deletions have positive integrated ΔINB.

Classification hierarchy:

1. If any computability gate fails, label **inconclusive/not computable**.
2. If the 95% upper bound for ΔINB is below 0, label **opposite: trajectory worsens threshold-averaged decisions**.
3. If the 95% lower bound is at least +1.0 and every supportive guardrail passes, label **supportive of a clinically material decision-analytic increment**.
4. If the 95% upper bound is below +1.0, label **adverse to the prespecified material-gain claim**. If its lower bound is above 0, this may also support a smaller-than-material positive increment.
5. Otherwise label **inconclusive**. A nominally supportive interval with a failed guardrail is **inconclusive/mixed**, not supportive.

No AUROC, binary nonclearance, isolated favorable threshold, subgroup, or secondary risk-shift result can change the primary label.

## Interpretation of possible results

A supportive result would mean that under the locked models, selected EICU risk set, and explicitly weighted 30%–50% review preferences, trajectory adds at least one net true-positive-equivalent alert per 100 beyond the late value, with acceptable calibration and site robustness. It would justify external/prospective validation of a trajectory-assisted risk-review tool. It would not justify lactate-guided treatment or claim saved lives.

An adverse result would mean that these data and this model rule out the prespecified +1-per-100 threshold-averaged increment with the stated uncertainty. A precise small positive result would say the increment exists but is below the material target. Neither would prove serial lactate generally useless; the conclusion is conditional on measured patients, feature set, thresholds, outcome, and EICU measurement.

An opposite result would indicate that adding trajectory worsens the locked decision-analytic metric. Threshold crossings or guardrail failure would mean preferences or sites change the preferred model and would be reported as mixed/inconclusive even if the integrated point estimate is favorable.

An inconclusive result would mean the interval crosses +1, zero, or required guardrails fail. It cannot be converted to support by citing the parent AUROC, selecting a favorable threshold, or binarizing clearance.

## Verification fixtures and evidence limits

An automatic verifier can check source hashes and headers; required columns; joins; age mapping; one-person selection; `t0/L24`; collection versus revision deadlines; conflict/tie rules; exact 1,506/1,229/33 flow; frozen hospital list; absence of outcome in site selection; exact M0/M1 matrices; fold-only transformations; estimator settings; one prediction per person; DCA arithmetic at all 21 thresholds; default strategies; trapezoidal integration; copied-hospital multiplicities and refits; calibration/proper-score/deletion metrics; intervals; gates; and label-output consistency.

Required synthetic/result fixtures include:

- lower ΔINB bound ≥1 with all guards passing → accept supportive; reject “treatment improves survival”;
- interval wholly between 0 and 1 → accept adverse to materiality plus “small positive increment”; reject “no increment”;
- interval crossing 0 and 1 → accept inconclusive; reject supportive or adverse;
- upper bound <0 → accept opposite; reject “trajectory helps”;
- supportive-looking interval with failed source, convergence, calibration, default, threshold-harm, or site guard → accept not-computable or mixed/inconclusive as specified; reject supportive;
- correct metrics followed by a causal, mechanism, deployment, or patient-benefit claim → reject the conclusion while accepting computation;
- adverse or inconclusive metrics with appropriately bounded interpretation → accept; the verifier must not reward hypothesis confirmation.

A verifier cannot establish that an alert causes beneficial review, that false-positive and true-positive consequences match the threshold odds, that 30%–50% reflects patient/clinician preferences, or that mortality is preventable. Those require clinical, patient, operational, and ethics adjudication. EICU also cannot establish actual norepinephrine administration/dose, arterial versus venous lactate, assay indication, fluids, beta-agonists, source control, liver/renal handling, tissue perfusion, limitations of treatment, cause of death, what clinicians knew, or what actions they took. External temporal/geographic validation is required for transport; a prospective impact study, ideally randomized if a trajectory-triggered action is proposed, is required to claim changed decisions or outcomes.
