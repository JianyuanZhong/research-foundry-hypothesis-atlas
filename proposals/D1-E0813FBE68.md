# Provisional repaired hypothesis: discordant AKI recovery in MIMIC-IV

Status: provisional checkpoint for the assigned repair. This is a hypothesis and data contract, not a result. No cohort counts, model estimates, or clinical conclusions are claimed.

## Proposed relationship

Among adults in a first MIMIC-IV ICU stay with a documented AKI episode who are alive and still under observation at a prespecified 48-hour ICU landmark, a phenotype of improved creatinine but impaired urine output will have worse subsequent in-hospital death and/or persistent or worsening kidney dysfunction than a concordant creatinine-and-urine-output recovery phenotype. This is a prognostic association, not a causal or mechanistic claim.

The strongest rival is measurement/monitoring discordance: creatinine is sampled at clinician-selected times, urine output is recorded only when collection is present, and fluid balance is a treatment/monitoring summary rather than direct organ state. A second rival is residual severity and treatment selection: more ill patients may receive more invasive monitoring, diuresis, fluid administration, or renal replacement therapy. The experiment must test incremental prognostic information beyond creatinine recovery and monitoring intensity, without interpreting association as tubular injury, fluid overload, or a treatment indication.

Population: adults in their first ICU stay, with a prespecified AKI episode definition using only information available before the landmark. Time origin is ICU admission; primary landmark is 48 hours after ICU admission. Exposure uses pre-landmark creatinine trajectory/value, urine output over a fixed pre-landmark window, and cumulative fluid balance. The primary comparison is creatinine-improved/urine-output-impaired versus concordant recovery; fluid balance is secondary/stratified, not a silent replacement.

Outcomes: in-hospital death and a prespecified post-landmark kidney outcome such as persistent/worsening creatinine or renal replacement therapy, with follow-up through discharge or a fixed 7-day window and explicit competing-risk handling. Fix thresholds and outcome definitions before exploratory outcome analysis.

Baseline: prespecified logistic or cause-specific Cox model using age, sex, admission type, pre-landmark severity, creatinine value/trajectory, urine-output category, fluid-balance category, and monitoring intensity. Substantive alternative: a time-aware landmark model using ordered pre-landmark trajectories (for example, discrete-time survival or regularized recurrent/functional model), with the same patient split, outcome, calibration/uncertainty evaluation, and bootstrap intervals. The alternative can reveal whether sequence and slope add information beyond a single landmark category; it cannot establish mechanism. CPU is appropriate for the baseline; a GPU is optional only if trajectory fitting is materially slower.

Supportive results require a prespecified incremental association with uncertainty, persistence after monitoring-intensity adjustment, and no material reversal in fixed sensitivity analyses. Adverse results include no incremental association, disappearance after monitoring adjustment, or reversal. An imprecise null is inconclusive. AKI adjudication, true recovery, fluid overload, and treatment indication require clinical adjudication or another study.
