# Is laboratory surveillance actionable after recorded vital state is measured?

Status: Episode-19 substantive child of `[prior hypothesis]`. This is an observational prognostic, transportability, and measurement-process experiment. It is not a causal test-ordering study, a hospital-quality ranking, a treatment recommendation, or evidence that a monitoring alert benefits patients.

## Unresolved question and falsifiable hypothesis

The parent establishes the right unresolved association: laboratory observation timing and intensity may carry short-horizon ICU-risk information, but may instead be a fingerprint of clinician concern, local interfaces, or physiologic deterioration. Its next scientific weakness is decision translation. A favorable incremental Brier score at 1,800 minutes does not by itself say whether a monitoring team would receive a useful review candidate at the six-hour landmark.

The question is therefore: among adults at the fixed six-hour ICU-relative landmark, after adjustment for the physiology that was actually recorded by that time, does patient-linked laboratory observation history produce calibrated, transportable review candidates beyond a state/process baseline, at fixed prespecified risk thresholds and acceptable observed burden?

Primary hypothesis: laboratory process features added to the recorded-state model (Bp versus Bv) improve macro-hospital held-out calibration and integrated competing-risk Brier score for recorded ICU death through minute 1,800, and identify additional threshold-crossing review candidates whose held-out observed death risk is above the corresponding fixed threshold. The effect should attenuate under state- and hospital-stratified process permutation and remain directionally present under outcome-free target-hospital normalization. If the gain disappears after recorded vital-state adjustment, is reproduced by site descriptors, or survives state-matched process permutation, the patient-linked surveillance interpretation is refuted in favor of omitted physiology or workflow encoding.

This is a stronger and more clinically interpretable claim than “testing frequency predicts death”: it asks whether the signal changes a prespecified review decision after a recognizable physiologic state has been measured. “Actionable” here means evidence of transportable risk reclassification at a fixed review threshold, with reported burden and uncertainty—not proof that a clinician should act or that acting changes outcome.

## Evidence boundary

The strongest available evidence is an outcome-blind parent audit: 135,680 eligible adult first-stay episodes, 2,470 recorded ICU deaths by minute 1,800, 208 hospitals, 175 event-bearing hospitals, 195 hospitals with at least 20 eligible episodes, and 2,209,118 revision-aware laboratory rows available by the landmark. These are support counts, not fitted performance or clinical benefit. The vital scan is a separate bounded feasibility job and its coverage, overlap, and event-bearing support remain unverified until its output is available.

The tested claim is limited to this eICU snapshot and its recorded fields: an incremental recorded-data prognostic/measurement-process signal that transports to held-out hospitals and changes a retrospective threshold classification. It cannot establish that a test was ordered because of concern, that a vital value is physiologically correct, that unrecorded instability was absent, that a review would occur, or that changing surveillance improves survival. Those stronger claims require expert adjudication/chart review, external hospitals, and prospective impact evaluation.

## Models and the decision analysis

Derive lab state summaries (first/last/median/range/slope where at least two values exist), threshold flags, analyte availability, counts, distinct result times, first/last time, recency to 360, inter-result gaps, revision-known status, interface/system indicators, total rows, distinct analytes, and total span. Derive vital recorded-state summaries (first/last/median/range/slope where supported) and a missingness indicator for every retained vital. Keep vital observation process separate: row count, distinct observation times, recency, and duration.

* `B0`: parent diagnostic, pooled elastic-net discrete-time cause-specific logistic death and alive-discharge heads over hourly rows `[360,1800]`, using demographics/admission context, lab state and lab availability only.
* `Bv`: transparent state/process baseline equal to B0 plus vital recorded-state and vital availability blocks. It is the minimum comparison for “after recorded vital state is measured.”
* `Bp`: exactly Bv plus patient-level laboratory-process features, excluding `hospitalid` and hospital descriptors. The primary estimand remains the paired macro-hospital held-out difference in integrated competing-risk Brier score and calibration; CIF at 1,800 is secondary.
* `Br`: secondary relative-process diagnostic replacing continuous Bp process features with empirical percentiles computed from a fixed 10% outcome-free calibration subset of eligible predictor-window episodes within each target hospital. Use no target outcomes, discharge status, or post-landmark data. Require at least 50 calibration candidates and 20 evaluation episodes per target hospital.
* `T`: one-layer GRU or temporal convolution over six one-hour bins in `[0,360]`, with the exact Bp lab/vital state, process, and mask channels, no hospital ID, and competing death/discharge heads. Fit only in development hospitals; use development validation hospitals for stopping and hyperparameters. It is a substantive alternative only if ordering, persistence, or co-movement improves held-out calibration/Brier beyond Bp. The next-bin observation auxiliary task, if used, is descriptive and cannot enter outcome evaluation.

At minute 360, translate each held-out prediction into a fixed review rule using prespecified absolute cumulative-risk thresholds `q ∈ {0.01, 0.02, 0.05, 0.10}` for recorded death by 1,800. For each q, report: Bv alert rate and Bp alert rate; incremental candidates `(Bv < q, Bp >= q)`; observed competing-event risk and calibration in each alert cell; death capture, PPV, false-review burden, and number needed to review; and the paired difference in hypothetical net benefit across q. These are retrospective label-relative diagnostics, not utility or treatment effects. A threshold cell is interpretable only if it contains at least 200 held-out episodes, 20 recorded deaths, and 20 hospitals; otherwise mark it unsupported. “Dynamic risk” is deliberately not a post-landmark rolling model: adding post-360 measurements would change the fixed-landmark estimand. The temporal T tests within-window dynamics, while the review rule remains frozen at minute 360.

## Splits, uncertainty, support, and falsification

Use five grouped outer folds, each holding out approximately 20% of hospitals once, with `uniquepid` intact. Stratify hospital assignment by eligible episodes and events where feasible. Fit codebooks, unit decisions, imputation, scaling, penalties, temporal stopping, and any calibration only in development hospitals. Primary uncertainty is a hospital-clustered bootstrap or hierarchical interval with at least 1,000 resamples if feasible; use simultaneous intervals for the four threshold cells. Report every hospital’s eligible episodes, recorded deaths, competing discharges, and support.

Hard gates before interpreting the primary contrast are: at least 100 hospitals with eligible evaluation support, at least 50 event-bearing hospitals, at least 1,000 recorded deaths and 1,000 competing discharges overall, at least 20 hospitals per outer fold with both event types where feasible, vital-state coverage of at least 30% of eligible stays for every primary vital variable and at least two observations in at least 20% (otherwise downgrade that variable to audit-only), and overlapping predictor support in at least 80% of held-out hospitals. These are readiness criteria, not results; failure makes the relevant claim inconclusive.

Primary falsifications are: (a) permute lab-process channels within hospital and Bv-risk strata while holding state fixed; (b) repeat conditional on vital observation density; (c) strict common-panel and revision-known lab codebooks; (d) compare with hospital-descriptor-only predictions from `hospital`; (e) inspect concentration by lab family, interface, and hospital; and (f) repeat the fixed-q reclassification after this matched process permutation. A gain that survives permutation, is reproduced by site descriptors, or is confined to one interface/hospital is evidence against a patient-level interpretation.

Supportive evidence requires all hard gates, leakage-free held-out predictions, directionally favorable Bp-minus-Bv calibration/Brier results with uncertainty excluding no improvement, at least one supported q cell with above-threshold observed risk and reduced/acceptable false-review burden, attenuation after matched permutation, and no single-hospital domination. Adverse evidence is no transport improvement, calibration harm, reversal under strict revision/unit handling, loss after vital-state adjustment, or persistence under state-matched permutation/site controls. Inconclusive evidence includes failed vital coverage/overlap, sparse threshold cells, unstable fits, fold reversals, unsupported alert burden, or target leakage. All three interpretations concern recorded-data prognostic/process behavior only.

## Alternatives and revisit record

The transparent Bv/Bp contrast is selected because it isolates the clinically meaningful state-adjusted process increment and is auditable. T is retained at equal inputs because it can reveal temporal ordering, persistence, and co-movement lost by summaries; it is deferred if support or convergence fails, not rejected because it is neural or GPU-capable. Br tests outcome-free target-site normalization but cannot establish causal benefit.

An EHR-only latent Gaussian-process model inspired by ALADYNOULLI is deferred: it adds representation without a sharper identification contrast and lacks the demonstrated genetic inputs. A multimodal Oncoformer reproduction is unavailable because images and the main article/STAR Methods are unavailable; a lab-only version would duplicate T without improving this decision test. A full Delphi reproduction is unavailable because eICU lacks its UKB trajectory/genetic/lifestyle scope; only the narrow equal-input temporal adaptation is useful. Vasopressor, hypotension, hyperoxia, ventilation, nighttime discharge, transfer, hypoglycemia, and recovery-speed seed branches remain deferred because their exposure/administration or adjudicated clinical outcomes are not bound here. The renal/nutrition branch is not revived: the parent audit found only three conservative renal-worsening/documented-non-delivery episodes and zero detected lows.
