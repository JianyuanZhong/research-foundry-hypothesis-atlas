# UKB 29: Interaction between air pollution and genetic susceptibility to lung function

Admission: feasibility_required. Verify participant overlap, measurement dates, missingness and event support before committing this design.

## Hypotheses to Be Tested

At similar smoking exposure, those with greater genetic susceptibility to asthma have a stronger association between particulate-matter exposure and lung-function decline.

## Study population and key data

Individuals with estimates of address contamination, a standard asthma PRS, and repeat lung function measurements.

## Basic Approach

Use pulmonary function measurements from years after the corresponding pollution exposure as baseline, analyze subsequent changes and interactions, and control for smoking, region, and social factors.

## Significance of the Research

Test the combination of external exposure and intrinsic respiratory susceptibility.

## Main Challenges

Address-based estimates cannot represent individual doses; residential moves and year mismatches need to be addressed, and asthma PRS is not a general pulmonary function PRS.

## Life Sciences

Environment, behavior, and biological susceptibility

## Competing Explanations

Regional deprivation, occupational exposures, or population structure may create spurious interactions.

## Minimal validation and falsification criteria

First verify pollution years and at least two pulmonary function tests; do not analyze decline if the correct temporal order is missing.
