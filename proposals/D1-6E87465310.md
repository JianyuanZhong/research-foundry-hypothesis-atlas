# Final-window physiologic trajectories and failed transition after ICU discharge

## Status and provenance

This is an episode-1 adaptation of repairable parent `[prior hypothesis]`, the imported expert seed `[starting question]` (“Residual instability before ICU discharge”). The seed is an untested question, not evidence of the hypothesis. The MIMIC-IV snapshot is `[source checksum]`; the catalog is `[internal dataset path]` ([source checksum]).

## Unresolved clinical question

At the point an adult leaves the ICU alive, does the *trajectory and persistence* of physiologic instability during the preceding 24 hours identify patients at higher risk of a failed transition—ICU readmission or in-hospital death within 48 hours—beyond the conventional last-observation summary? This matters because an ICU discharge decision allocates monitoring and bed capacity, but clinicians currently have no validated rule in this snapshot for whether a single near-discharge measurement is enough or whether persistence, variability, and support trajectory carry clinically relevant residual information.

### Strongest claim supported by existing evidence

The imported expert seed and the general ICU-readmission literature motivate the plausibility that high oxygen requirement or fluctuating vital signs near ICU discharge are associated with subsequent adverse events. The local data can measure those proxies and test out-of-sample prognostic performance. The natural-history demonstration shows that ordered structured histories can contain information lost by isolated events; its results do not establish this ICU phenotype. The accessible evidence does not establish that changing discharge timing or post-ICU monitoring based on these variables improves outcomes.

### Hypothesis to test

Among adults in their first ICU stay within a hospitalization who leave the ICU alive after at least 24 hours, a model using four 6-hour bins from the final 24 hours—levels, within-window trends, persistence, variability, support status, and observation/missingness—will improve 48-hour post-ICU failure prediction over a prespecified last-observation-plus-clinical-summary baseline. The primary incremental estimand is held-out integrated Brier score (learned sequence model minus baseline); a clinically meaningful result requires a point improvement of at least 0.005 with a subject-bootstrap 95% CI excluding 0, plus acceptable calibration. The hypothesis is falsified if the learned model does not meet this criterion, if the baseline is better, or if any apparent gain remains after temporal-order permutation.

This is a prognostic hypothesis. A positive result does not show that ICU discharge caused the event, that the event was preventable, or that delaying discharge or adding monitoring would help.

## Exact population and temporal design

Use the participant-level discovery partition only; the reserved hash buckets 80–99 are inaccessible and are not an external validation set.

1. Join `hosp/icustays` to `hosp/admissions` on `subject_id, hadm_id`, and to `hosp/patients` on `subject_id`.
2. Keep adults (`hosp/patients.anchor_age >= 18`), ICU stays with non-null `intime` and `outtime`, and ICU length of stay at least 24 hours.
3. Keep the first ICU stay per `hadm_id` (ordered by `intime`) to avoid correlated discharge episodes and leakage from an earlier ICU stay. Keep only ICU discharges alive: `admissions.deathtime` is null or strictly later than `icustays.outtime`. The hospitalization must have a valid `dischtime` or `deathtime`.
4. Define time zero as the recorded `icustays.outtime`. Exposure data are restricted to the half-open interval [outtime - 24 hours, outtime); no post-discharge field may enter a feature.
5. Primary follow-up is [outtime, outtime + 48 hours], administratively censored at `admissions.dischtime` if discharge occurs first. A secondary horizon is 7 days, with the same censoring rule.
6. A 48-hour composite failure is either: (a) a later `icu/icustays.intime` in the same `hadm_id` at or after the index `outtime` and no more than 48 hours after it, or (b) `admissions.deathtime` strictly after `outtime` and no more than 48 hours after it. Count death and ICU readmission separately as cause-specific outcomes. If both occur in the window, death is the terminal event and the composite is positive. Overlapping or inconsistent stays are retained in a flow/quality report and excluded from the primary event-time analysis unless a deterministic rule can resolve them.
7. Report the number excluded for missing timestamps, age, ICU death, first-stay restriction, absent final-window observations, and outcome ambiguity. The primary cohort needs at least 200 composite failures and at least 50 events of each separate type; otherwise report the analysis as infeasible/inconclusive rather than changing the hypothesis.

## Baseline versus substantive alternative

The comparison tests a scientific uncertainty: whether temporal persistence/order and measurement patterns contain clinically useful information beyond a near-discharge snapshot, not whether a more complex model wins by a tiny metric change.

**Simple baseline.** Fit a ridge logistic model for the composite using only the last valid value in the final 6-hour bin for each core numeric feature, the binary oxygen/hypotension/vasopressor markers, ICU LOS, age, sex, first/last careunit, and a small count of the four predefined instability domains. Fit separate cause-specific logistic heads for readmission and death. This is an interpretable approximation to a conventional last-observation discharge summary.

**Learned longitudinal alternative.** Fit a small gated recurrent model (one GRU layer, hidden size 32) on the four ordered 6-hour vectors. Each vector includes the same numeric features, support indicators, category flags, slopes/within-bin variability, and missingness/count indicators. Use a shared representation with composite, readmission, and death heads, binary cross-entropy with inverse-frequency weighting, early stopping on validation Brier score, and no future-window variables. This is a bounded adaptation of the date-ordered structured-history idea in the natural-history demonstration; it does not reproduce Delphi or claim a foundation model. Its scientific output is an estimate of whether persistence/trend/order and informative observation patterns improve calibrated risk, plus an audit of which trajectory patterns drive the difference.

Use deterministic participant hashing consistent with the catalog namespace `ehr-hypothesis-discovery-v1` and dataset `mimic`: discovery buckets 0–59 for fitting, 60–69 for model selection, and 70–79 for the locked test. All encounters and modalities for a `subject_id` stay in one split. No model tuning uses the locked test. Estimate 95% CIs with 1,000 subject-level bootstrap resamples of locked-test predictions; report AUROC/AUPRC only as secondary summaries, and report Brier score, calibration intercept/slope, reliability plots, sensitivity/specificity at an exploratory 10% risk threshold, and decision curves. Repeat the learned fit with at least five seeds and report between-seed variability.

The learned model’s temporal-order falsification test shuffles the order of the four bins within each subject while preserving values and missingness. A real trajectory contribution should attenuate toward the baseline; if it does not, the claimed temporal mechanism is unsupported and possible leakage or a static-pattern explanation must be investigated. Additional negative controls are a post-`outtime` feature audit and a label-permutation fit on training data.

## Interpretation and falsification

- **Supportive:** The learned model meets the prespecified Brier improvement and calibration criteria on the locked participant-held-out test; its five-seed interval is stable; bin-order permutation removes the incremental gain; and the high-risk group has a reproducible excess cumulative incidence. This supports that final-window trajectories contain prognostic information beyond last values. It does not establish a safe discharge threshold, treatment effect, preventability, or benefit of extra monitoring.
- **Adverse:** The baseline is as good or better; the learned gain is below 0.005 or CI includes zero; or order permutation preserves the gain. This falsifies the claim of clinically relevant temporal-order information, or indicates the gain is static/missingness information or a computation problem. If the model predicts well but calibration is poor, it is not evidence for bedside risk stratification.
- **Inconclusive:** Fewer than the event minimum, poor coverage of the final window, unresolved timestamp/stay contradictions, large between-seed variability, unstable calibration, or insufficient observations in key subgroups. This does not support or refute the biological hypothesis.
- Computationally checkable claims are cohort counts, feature availability, leakage exclusion, split integrity, fitted model outputs, uncertainty intervals, calibration, permutation behavior, and the stated prognostic comparison. Whether an ICU readmission was preventable, whether discharge was clinically appropriate, why support was continued, whether a charted oxygen value reflects a device reliably, and whether changing monitoring changes outcomes require clinical adjudication, richer bedside/device data, or a prospective/interventional study. MIMIC has no randomization, reliable treatment-limitation adjudication, post-ICU monitoring assignment, or external validation here.

## Demonstration dispositions and deferred alternatives

- **Natural-history generative transformer:** concrete, bounded adaptation used here—the GRU receives ordered MIMIC ICU measurements and tests a specific discharge-transition estimand. The MIMIC ICU horizon and available fields do not support the paper’s broad disease natural-history or external-cohort claims.
- **Bayesian longitudinal EHR/genetic framework:** its full genetic discovery claim requires genetic inputs not established in this MIMIC snapshot. An EHR-only latent-trajectory model is scientifically possible, but would answer a different representation question and is deferred in favor of the directly actionable discharge-transition comparison.
- **Cancer multimodal longitudinal model:** the local bundle documents that the main paper/full STAR Methods are unavailable, and MIMIC does not include the required chest-X-ray image files. A lab-only adaptation could be scoped, but it would not test the multimodal claim and is deferred. No unavailable article text or image result is being used.

The actual scientific deliverable is newly fitted, locked-test-evaluated baseline and GRU models, not merely an input audit. Completion requires: a cohort/flow file; deterministic split and feature-coverage report; fitted baseline and five-seed GRU prediction files; locked-test Brier/calibration/discrimination estimates with subject-bootstrap intervals; cause-specific cumulative-incidence estimates; the order-permutation and label-permutation falsification outputs; and a written interpretation that stops at prognostic evidence.
