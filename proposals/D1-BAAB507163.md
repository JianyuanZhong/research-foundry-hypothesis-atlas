# Time-weighted hypotension burden versus mean arterial pressure for early AKI in eICU

## Scientific question

Among adult ICU patients with adequate systolic and diastolic blood-pressure measurements in the first 24 hours of ICU care, does a prespecified time-weighted hypotension burden measure improve prediction of acute kidney injury (AKI) within 48 hours beyond the 24-hour mean arterial pressure (MAP)?

The unresolved claim is that duration and time-weighting of hypotension add clinically meaningful prognostic information beyond MAP, after accounting for baseline illness severity, vasopressor exposure, baseline kidney function, and hospital. A null result would weaken the claim that a more complex burden measure is useful; a positive result would support further validation and prospective study.

## Why it matters

MAP is the common bedside summary and a natural baseline for hemodynamic risk. Minimum systolic blood pressure alone can overstate a single low reading, while MAP may understate sustained hypoperfusion. If time-weighted burden improves early AKI risk stratification, it may inform monitoring and resuscitation decisions. If it does not, clinicians and researchers should not add a more complex measure without evidence.

## Prior evidence

- [K1] The INSPIRE database study by Liu et al. (2026) demonstrates that sustained intraoperative MAP <65 mmHg, particularly for >=30 min, is associated with higher odds of postoperative ICU admission after adjustment for measured clinical and surgical factors. Time-weighted area below MAP 65 mmHg and duration within the strict operation window are associated with ICU admission. This supports the concept that time-weighted hypotension burden is a clinically relevant measure, but the study is limited to gastrointestinal cancer surgery patients and does not address AKI prediction.
- [K2] The Cleveland Clinic study by Tarbaghia et al. (2026) demonstrates that early hemodynamic response to dobutamine in septic shock with myocardial dysfunction is associated with more ICU-free days and lower adjusted 28-day ICU mortality. Baseline echocardiographic parameters did not identify responders. This supports the clinical importance of hemodynamic measures in ICU patients, but the study is limited to septic shock patients and does not address hypotension burden or AKI prediction.
- [K3] The StatPearls review by Goyal et al. (2026) provides a summary of KDIGO AKI criteria, including an increase in serum creatinine by 0.3 mg/dL or more within 48 hours, an increase to 1.5 times or more than baseline, or urine volume less than 0.5 mL/kg/h for at least 6 hours. KDIGO is the most recent and most commonly used tool for AKI identification. This supports the use of KDIGO criteria for the outcome definition, but the source is a review and does not provide original data or a specific study design.

## Population and temporal design

Use eICU 2.0 adult ICU admissions with at least one systolic and diastolic blood-pressure measurement in the first 24 hours of ICU care. Require adequate measurement coverage (e.g., at least 12 hours with BP measurements, or a prespecified density threshold) to reduce recording-density artifacts. Exclude admissions with missing ICU start time, age <18, or no creatinine measurement before or within the first 24 hours.

Define the exposure window as the first 24 hours from ICU admission. Define the outcome window as AKI within 48 hours from ICU admission, using KDIGO creatinine criteria when available. Baseline covariates are measured before or at the start of the exposure window.

## Exposure definitions

1. **Time-weighted hypotension burden**: For each patient, compute the fraction of the first 24 hours with systolic BP <90 mmHg, weighted by the duration of each hypotensive interval. Use a prespecified interpolation rule (e.g., linear interpolation between measurements, with a maximum gap threshold) to estimate continuous BP. Report sensitivity analyses with different interpolation rules and gap thresholds.

2. **24-hour MAP mean**: Compute the mean arterial pressure over the first 24 hours using the same interpolation rule. This is the primary baseline comparator.

3. **Nadir systolic BP**: Include as a secondary comparator to preserve the parent branch's question.

## Outcome definition

AKI within 48 hours from ICU admission, using KDIGO creatinine criteria:
- Stage 1: Creatinine >=2x baseline or >=1.5x baseline with urine output <0.5 mL/kg/h for >=6 hours
- Stage 2: Creatinine >=2x baseline or >=2x baseline with urine output <0.5 mL/kg/h for >=12 hours
- Stage 3: Creatinine >=3x baseline or >=4.0 mg/dL with acute decrease, or urine output <0.3 mL/kg/h for >=24 hours

Use the earliest creatinine measurement before or within the first 24 hours as the baseline. If no baseline creatinine is available, exclude the patient.

## Covariates and adjustment

- Age, sex, race, ethnicity
- ICU admission type (medical, surgical, other)
- Primary diagnosis (if available)
- Baseline illness severity (e.g., APACHE II score if available)
- Vasopressor exposure in the first 24 hours (any, dose, duration)
- Baseline kidney function (creatinine, eGFR if available)
- Hospital (fixed effects or stratification)
- Measurement density (number of BP measurements per hour in the first 24 hours)

## Analysis plan

1. **Descriptive analysis**: Compare time-weighted burden, MAP, and nadir systolic BP across AKI and non-AKI groups. Report measurement density and artifact rates.

2. **Unadjusted association**: Compare AKI incidence across quartiles of time-weighted burden, MAP, and nadir systolic BP. Report risk ratios and 95% confidence intervals.

3. **Adjusted association**: Fit hospital-stratified logistic regression models with time-weighted burden, MAP, and nadir systolic BP as predictors. Report adjusted odds ratios and 95% confidence intervals.

4. **Incremental prediction**: Fit time-aware models (e.g., Cox regression or gradient boosting) with MAP as the baseline, then add time-weighted burden. Compare discrimination (C-index, AUC) and calibration (Brier score, calibration-in-the-large) using prespecified metrics. Report the incremental improvement and its 95% confidence interval.

5. **Hospital transportability**: Repeat the analysis in each hospital and report the range of effect sizes and the proportion of hospitals with consistent direction. Use a prespecified threshold for clinical meaningfulness (e.g., C-index improvement >=0.01).

6. **Sensitivity analyses**:
   - Different interpolation rules and gap thresholds
   - Different hypotension thresholds (systolic <80, <90, <100 mmHg)
   - Different outcome windows (24 hours, 48 hours, 72 hours)
   - Exclusion of patients with vasopressor exposure in the first 24 hours
   - Exclusion of patients with measurement density below a prespecified threshold

## Falsification criteria

- **Supportive**: Time-weighted burden improves discrimination and calibration beyond MAP in the primary analysis, with consistent direction across hospitals and sensitivity analyses.
- **Adverse**: No clinically meaningful improvement in discrimination or calibration beyond MAP, or inconsistent direction across hospitals.
- **Inconclusive**: Sparse outcomes, unstable hospital estimates, or failure to control recording density.

## Method comparison

- **Simple baseline**: 24-hour MAP mean, adjusted for baseline covariates.
- **Time-aware alternative**: Time-weighted hypotension burden, adjusted for baseline covariates and measurement density.
- **Scientific uncertainty**: Whether the added complexity of time-weighted burden provides clinically meaningful incremental prediction beyond MAP.
- **Resource estimate**: CPU-only, ~1-2 hours for the primary analysis on a 16-core machine.
- **Selection reason**: The time-aware alternative is scientifically useful because it tests whether duration of hypoperfusion adds information beyond the common MAP summary.

## Limitations

- eICU 2.0 is a retrospective cohort, so causal claims are not possible.
- Measurement density and artifact rates may vary across hospitals, affecting the interpretation of the results.
- The primary outcome is AKI within 48 hours, which may not capture all clinically relevant kidney injury.
- The analysis is limited to patients with adequate BP measurement coverage, which may introduce selection bias.
