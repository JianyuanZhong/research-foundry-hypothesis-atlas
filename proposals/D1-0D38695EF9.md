# Does hypotension duration add information beyond severity and recording density for 48-hour AKI?

## Scientific opening and unresolved question

The clinically important unresolved question is whether the amount of time an ICU patient spends below a prespecified MAP threshold adds information about subsequent acute kidney injury (AKI) beyond the patient's lowest MAP, baseline severity, and the intensity of MAP recording. The strongest remaining threat is that a duration signal may be a proxy for severity, treatment, or more frequent measurement rather than a distinct hypotension process.

The hypothesis is:

> Among adult ICU stays with a minimum MAP of at least 50 mmHg in the first 48 hours, cumulative time below 60 mmHg adds clinically meaningful information about incident AKI beyond the minimum MAP, baseline severity, and a prespecified recording-density measure. The incremental information is not confined to a small subset of hospitals and persists in a prespecified risk-adjusted comparison.

This is an observational association, not a treatment target. The database does not identify an appropriate hypotension threshold or randomize hypotension.

## Evidence already supported and the claim being tested

Lehman et al. (2010, PMID 22158679) reported in MIMIC II that minimum MAP and duration of MAP below 70, 60, and 50 mmHg in a target 48-hour window were associated with AKI risk. The abstract states that duration was included as a predictor and that sampling duration was controlled in the duration analysis. It does not establish causality, a treatment target, or transportability to this eICU snapshot.

Wesselink et al. (2019, DOI 10.1016/j.bja.2018.04.036) found that prolonged exposure to low mean arterial pressure is associated with end-organ injury, but emphasized that retrospective observational designs and heterogeneous definitions limit causal conclusions. That work supports clinical importance and the need for a careful comparator, not the eICU threshold or effect.

The eICU metadata and catalog support a bounded adaptation: eICU has longitudinal MAP summaries, laboratory measurements, ICU-stay identifiers, discharge offsets, and hospital identifiers. The data are not raw waveforms and do not provide a validated treatment assignment or clinical adjudication of AKI.

The unresolved claim is whether duration of hypotension below 60 mmHg contributes information about incident AKI after accounting for the minimum MAP, baseline severity, and the recording process. The experiment will test this directly in eICU with patient-level temporal splitting and hospital-level validation.

## Population and temporal boundaries

The primary population is adult ICU stays with `age >= 18`, a nonmissing `systemicmean`, and `unitdischargeoffset > 0`. The analysis window is `[0, 480]` minutes after `unitadmittime24`, truncated to `unitdischargeoffset`. The primary exposure is cumulative time below 60 mmHg in that window. The primary estimand is the incremental association of duration with incident AKI during the first 48 hours, conditional on baseline severity, minimum MAP, and recording density.

The primary analysis excludes stays with less than 200 valid MAP observations in the window, stays with more than 20% missing MAP, and stays with more than 20% missing creatinine in the 48-hour window. This is a data-availability rule, not a claim that missingness is ignorable.

A secondary analysis uses a fixed 24-hour window and a secondary outcome of creatinine increase from baseline to 48 hours. The primary outcome is incident AKI, defined as a nonmissing `labresult` for creatinine (using a prespecified label dictionary on `labname` or `labmeasurenameinterface`) with `labresultrevisedoffset` no later than 48 hours after admission and a minimum of 24 hours between admission and the creatinine measurement. The primary creatinine value is the latest valid measurement in the window, with exact label and value rules frozen before outcome inspection. A sensitivity analysis uses the earliest valid creatinine and excludes any stay with a creatinine measurement in the first 24 hours.

The primary analysis excludes stays with a recorded vasopressor infusion after the 48-hour window and with a recorded vasopressor infusion during the window if the primary model does not include treatment as a covariate. If treatment is included, the analysis is labeled treatment-exposed and cannot be interpreted as an effect of hypotension. A prespecified sensitivity excludes stays with vasopressor exposure during the window. The primary model does not adjust for treatment because the data do not support a causal interpretation and would change the estimand.

## Outcomes, variables, and estimand

The primary outcome is a binary indicator of incident AKI by 48 hours. The primary exposure is the cumulative hours below 60 mmHg in the window. The primary baseline model includes age, gender, ethnicity, admission height, admission weight, admission source, unit stay type, unit discharge offset, acute physiology score, APACHE score, predicted in-hospital mortality, and minimum MAP. The primary incremental model adds the duration below 60 mmHg. The primary comparison is the change in AUROC, calibration intercept/slope, Brier score, and the absolute 48-hour AKI-risk difference for a patient with the same minimum MAP but 12 additional hours below 60 mmHg, reported with uncertainty.

The primary model is a regularized logistic regression with prespecified threshold tuning. A secondary comparison is a duration-only model with the same covariates, to show whether duration adds information beyond severity and minimum MAP. A hospital-level random-effect logistic regression is a sensitivity analysis, with all hospital-specific coefficients frozen before outcome inspection and with whole-hospital leave-one-hospital-out validation. The primary split is patient-level; no patient may appear in both training and validation hospitals.

The primary analysis is exploratory and observational. It does not estimate the effect of reducing hypotension duration. The estimand is the incremental predictive association of duration with incident AKI after adjustment for baseline severity and minimum MAP, conditional on the observed recording and treatment process.

## Baseline and substantive alternative

The simple baseline is the minimum-MAP model described above: it uses only the lowest observed MAP and baseline severity covariates. This is the clinically interpretable comparator and the primary baseline.

The substantive alternative is a duration model that represents the cumulative time below the prespecified MAP threshold. It can reveal whether the risk is associated with sustained exposure, and it allows a prespecified comparison with a duration-only model and a hospital-level sensitivity. This is a mechanistic-style representation of exposure burden, not a mechanistic model of kidney physiology; it does not establish that hypotension causes AKI.

A competing alternative is a recording-density model that uses the number of valid MAP observations, the fraction of the window with observations, the inter-observation interval, and the number of observations below each threshold. This alternative can reveal whether the duration signal is driven by more frequent measurement or by the MAP trajectory itself. The primary model includes a prespecified, non-linear recording-density term (or a prespecified spline) so that the duration contrast is not conflated with recording intensity. A duration-only model is also fitted to show whether duration adds information beyond severity and recording density.

A learned alternative is deferred. A sequence model such as a transformer or recurrent model could learn nonlinear interactions between MAP, time, and covariates, but it would not resolve the central confounding question and would add complexity without a clinically necessary comparison. The configured solver envelope is 16 CPUs, 262,144 MiB, and 28,800 seconds; a CPU regularized logistic model and hospital-level random effects are expected to fit within this envelope. No GPU is required. The discovery budget is 3,600 seconds and the full study is a future solver task, not a discovery result.

## Falsification and interpretation

The experiment is falsified for the proposed incremental information if the duration model has no statistically significant improvement in AUROC, calibration, or Brier score over the minimum-MAP model, or if the improvement is confined to a small subset of hospitals. The hypothesis is supported if the duration model improves discrimination and calibration, the incremental association is consistent across the 48-hour and 24-hour windows, and the hospital-level analysis shows no clinically large heterogeneity.

An adverse result is a precise null or a result that reverses the direction, or a duration signal that disappears after controlling for minimum MAP, baseline severity, or recording density. This would argue against duration being a useful standalone risk measure in this snapshot, not against the biological association in general.

An inconclusive result is a wide confidence interval, inadequate event support, substantial missingness or recording-density heterogeneity, or a result that changes materially between the fixed 24-hour and 48-hour windows. This would justify narrowing the question or collecting more complete monitoring data; it would not establish that duration is safe or ineffective.

The strongest claim the experiment can support is a data-bound association and a comparison of predictive information. It cannot establish that hypotension caused AKI, that a duration threshold is a treatment target, or that the finding transports to other hospitals or populations. Those claims require clinical adjudication of AKI, treatment assignment and timing, missingness and recording-density validation, and external validation.
