# Phenotype or patient state? Bounding the donor-state dependence of the fibroblast-repair-instructing capacity of MerTK/CD206-positive synovial macrophages

Alternative (complementary) branch of episode 1, domain 3 (Disease Mechanisms & Pathway Hypotheses).
Branch directory: `work/intrinsic/`. The Lead's primary branch (`working-proposal.md`) asks a different
question on the same deposit — the *combination rule* when both macrophage subsets act together, using
the 1Mpos:3Mneg / 3Mpos:1Mneg mixture samples. This branch does not use the mixtures and does not
re-ask the combination rule. It asks whether the resolving *phenotype* or the donor's *disease state*
carries the instructive function, and symmetrically whether the recipient fibroblast's donor state
modifies the response.

## 1. The scientific opening

The frozen ArrayExpress experiment description of the source deposit contains a quantitative claim
hidden inside a qualifier. Verbatim **[OBSERVED**, `E-MTAB-8873.idf.txt`, `Experiment Description`**]**:

> "In contrast to inflammatory effect of the MerTK/CD206neg STMs on FLS, the MerTK/CD206pos
> population, **especially that isolated from biopsies of RA patients in sustained disease remission**,
> induced repair response of FLS as manifested by increased expression of collagens (e.g. COL1A) and
> TGFb response genes (e.g. TGFBI)"

The same paragraph also records the claim that *is* clearly supported **[OBSERVED**, same file**]**:
"MerTK/CD206neg and MerTK/CD206pos STMs isolated from the biopsies of the same patient (either with
active RA or RA in remission) elicited these divergent FLS responses."

So two different things are asserted in one sentence. The second is a *within-patient, between-subset*
claim: sorting on MerTK/CD206 separates two functionally opposite macrophage populations inside one
individual, including inside an active-RA individual. The first — the word "especially" — is a
*between-patient-state* claim: the resolving capacity of the MerTK/CD206-positive population is said to
be greater when the donor is in sustained remission. No effect size, no per-donor numbers and no
uncertainty accompany it in any material I could inspect: not in the frozen deposit text, and not in
the inspected abstract and Extended Data legend of the primary publication [K1], which states only that
MerTK-positive subsets "induced the repair response of synovial fibroblasts in vitro" and that a low
*proportion* of MerTK-positive macrophages predicts flare. The review that frames the translational
reading [K3] likewise speaks of "capitalizing on the joint-protective ... biology of newly identified
STM clusters" and of "relative proportions of STM clusters" predicting flare, while separately noting
that RA progression involves "phenotypic changes in resident STMs" — the two statements are in tension
and the review does not resolve it.

This is a boundary-of-explanation opening, not a manufactured anomaly. Two explanations of the same
published observation differ in what they predict:

- **H-phen (the sorted phenotype carries the function).** MerTK/CD206 positivity identifies a cell
  state whose fibroblast-repair-instructing program is intact in active disease; remission tissue
  differs mainly by having *more* of these cells. Prediction: MerTK/CD206pos macrophages sorted from an
  active-disease donor induce the fibroblast repair program about as strongly as those from a remission
  donor, when applied to the same fibroblasts in the same experiment.
- **H-state (the donor's disease state carries the function).** The resolving program is degraded in
  active disease while the surface markers persist, so the sorted phenotype is a partially empty
  label. Prediction: the repair induction is substantially or wholly confined to remission-derived
  MerTK/CD206pos macrophages.

A third, recipient-side explanation competes with both and is supported by independent evidence [K2]:
expanded synovial fibroblasts retain stable, epigenetically encoded donor-specific programs and show
*differential responsiveness* to an inflammatory stimulus. Hence:

- **H-fls (recipient imprinting).** The magnitude of the response is set partly by the fibroblast
  donor's own disease state, so a "macrophage state effect" measured across experiments with different
  fibroblast donors can be partly or wholly a fibroblast effect.

These are not stylistic variants. They imply different clinical actions (section 3) and they make
different predictions on observations that exist in the deposit (sections 5-7).

## 2. Strongest already-supported claim versus the claim under test

**Already supported, not to be re-sold** (anchor, not result): sorted MerTK/CD206pos and
MerTK/CD206neg synovial macrophages exert opposite effects on RA fibroblasts, including when sorted
from the same patient; MerTK-positive subsets carry remission-enriched regulatory signatures, produce
pro-resolving lipid mediators, and their *proportion* in remission tissue is associated with flare risk
after treatment cessation [K1]. Nothing in this proposal is valuable if it merely restates this.

**Under test (unresolved):** the *magnitude and donor-level consistency* of the donor-disease-state
contribution to that instructive capacity, i.e. the quantity the word "especially" asserts without
quantifying — and, symmetrically, whether the recipient fibroblast's disease state modifies the
response. Formally, the estimand is the fraction of the within-fibroblast-donor repair induction
attributable to the macrophage donor's disease state (defined as rho in section 6).

**Honest boundary of what this deposit can ever answer:** because each fibroblast donor was exposed to
exactly one active-derived and one remission-derived macrophage donor, a donor-state effect here is
confounded with the identity of the particular individuals sampled. The deliverable is therefore a
**bound on rho with sign-concordance evidence across three disjoint donor pairs**, not an identified
causal state effect. I say this up front because the design cannot be rescued by a better model.

## 3. Why it matters clinically

The two explanations license opposite next moves, and the difference is not rhetorical:

- If **H-phen** holds, MerTK/CD206 positivity is a usable functional surrogate. Autologous
  phenotype-based strategies (sort/expand the patient's own resolving macrophages) and MerTK-directed
  agonism remain plausible in *active* disease, and subset-proportion biomarkers [K1, K3] can be read
  as capacity measures.
- If **H-state** holds, the same strategies are predicted to underperform exactly where they are
  needed. A patient in active disease would not have functionally competent resolving macrophages to
  expand or agonise; restoring resolution would require reprogramming the cells, not relabelling or
  enriching them; and a subset-proportion biomarker would be measuring availability while the
  therapeutic question is capacity. The clinical development path (cell therapy vs small-molecule
  reprogramming vs suppressing the inflammatory compartment) depends on which holds.
- If **H-fls** contributes materially, then trial-level heterogeneity in any resolution-restoring
  therapy may be driven by the stromal recipient rather than the myeloid donor, which argues for
  fibroblast-state stratification rather than macrophage-state stratification.

A *bound* is already decision-relevant: showing that donor state accounts for at most a small fraction
of the repair effect in the deposited pairs would weaken the case for abandoning phenotype-based
strategies; showing it accounts for most of it would make a donor-state-stratified functional assay a
prerequisite for any such programme.

## 6. Variables, outcome and estimand

### 6.1 Unit, exposure, covariates

Unit of analysis: the fibroblast cell, nested in co-culture sample (= one sample tag = three pooled
technical replicate wells), nested in fibroblast donor x run. Primary exposure: macrophage donor
disease state (active vs sustained remission) at fixed subset, fixed fibroblast donor, fixed run.
Secondary exposure: fibroblast donor disease state at fixed macrophage donor, subset and run.
Recorded covariates: fibroblast donor age and sex (both available), run, sequencing depth per cell,
per-arm cell yield. Unavailable covariates: macrophage donor age, sex, treatment, disease duration,
remission duration **[UNRESOLVED, verified absent]**.

### 6.2 Pre-processing (fixed before any contrast is computed)

1. Sum the two `COL1A1` probe columns; match `TNFRSF11B` by exact column name; keep all other columns
   as-is, preserving transcript identifiers.
2. Drop cells with <100 total molecules (verified to remove zero cells; retained for transparency).
3. Macrophage/doublet gate: drop cells whose summed macrophage-marker counts (`CD14`, `MRC1`, `MERTK`,
   `ITGB2`, `FCGR3A`, `C1QA`, `C1QB`, `CD163`) exceed their summed fibroblast-program counts
   (`COL1A1`, `COL1A2`, `FN1`, `TIMP1`, `TGFBI`). Report the gated fraction per arm; run the primary
   analysis both gated and ungated. The Lead observed 0.4-1.2% macrophage-dominant cells in five R2
   files; I did **not** extend that measurement to my contrast arms, because a differential residual
   fraction between the two arms of a C-contrast is itself part of the open question's composition rival
   and I did not want to look at it before the analysis is registered.
4. Normalise within cell to counts per 10,000 molecules and take `log1p`.

### 6.3 Outcome: per-cell repair and inflammatory scores

Gene sets are **fixed a priori** from the deposit's own cluster descriptions and the published repair
readout [K1], not learned from the contrast under test (this avoids selecting genes on the comparison
being tested):

- **Repair score `Rep`** = mean of the per-cell standardised values of `COL1A1` (summed probes),
  `COL1A2`, `TGFBI`, `TGFB3`, `TGFBR1`, `FN1`, `TIMP1`.
- **Inflammatory score `Infl`** = mean of `IL6`, `IL1B`, `CXCL8`, `CCL2`, `MMP1`, `MMP3`, `MMP9`,
  `MMP13`, `ICAM1`, `LIF`, `IL11`, `SPP1`.
- **Polarity** `P = Infl - Rep` (secondary). Additional secondary readouts: `TNFSF11`/`TNFRSF11B`
  (RANKL/OPG, bone-erosion relevant), `CMKLR1` and `TGFBR1` (resolvin/TGF-beta receptor arm),
  `MERTK`/`AXL` on the fibroblast side, `PCNA`/`LDHA` (proliferation/glycolysis, to check that score
  changes are not a proliferation artifact).

Standardisation is per gene using the mean and SD **among the FLS-only cells of the same fibroblast
donor in the same run**, so each donor's own unstimulated fibroblasts set the scale. `Rep` is therefore
in units of that donor's baseline fibroblast variability, which is what makes magnitudes comparable
across donors with different baselines.

**Primary outcome is `Rep`**, because the claim under test is specifically about *inducing repair*.
`P` and `Infl` are secondary; reporting all of them is pre-specified, so a `Rep`-null with an `Infl`
effect must be reported as such and not relabelled.

### 6.4 Estimands

For each C-contrast with fibroblast donor d:

- Anchors (does the arm induce repair at all?):
  `A_act(d) = E[Rep | Mpos from active donor] - E[Rep | FLS-only]`,
  `A_rem(d) = E[Rep | Mpos from remission donor] - E[Rep | FLS-only]`.
- State difference: `Delta(d) = A_rem(d) - A_act(d)` (= the difference of the two co-culture means,
  since they share the FLS-only reference).
- **Primary quantity: the donor-state fraction** `rho(d) = Delta(d) / max(A_act(d), A_rem(d))`,
  defined only when the denominator is positive and bounded away from 0 (pre-specified minimum anchor
  0.15 baseline SD; otherwise `rho` is reported as undefined and the contrast is listed as
  anchor-failed). `rho ~ 0` means the sorted phenotype carries the function (H-phen); `rho ~ 1` means
  the repair effect is essentially confined to remission-derived macrophages (H-state); intermediate
  values quantify partial dependence.
- Scale-free companion: `delta_C(d)` = Cliff's delta between the per-cell `Rep` distributions of the
  two co-culture arms (probability that a cell from the remission-derived arm exceeds a cell from the
  active-derived arm, minus the converse). This is immune to the standardisation choice and to
  baseline-level differences.

For each D-contrast with macrophage donor m: `Resp(d, m)` = Cliff's delta of `Rep` between the
co-culture cells and that donor's own FLS-only cells in the same run — a **responsiveness** measure
rather than a level — and `Delta_FLS(m) = Resp(remission FLS, m) - Resp(active FLS, m)`. Cliff's delta
is primary here precisely because remission and active fibroblasts may differ in baseline `Rep` level
and in scale, which would make a raw mean difference uninterpretable (a ceiling artifact).

### 6.5 Uncertainty: what the registry allows and what the design actually supports

The registry disables an ordinary donor t-test: every 8873 sample has multiple contributing donors and
`donor_status = "requires_contributing_donor_model"`. Accordingly:

- **No pooling across fibroblast donors** into a two-sample test, and no treatment of cells as
  independent donors.
- Cell-level resampling (stratified bootstrap within arm, 2000 resamples) gives intervals that describe
  the sampling error of the cell population *within that pooled well set*. Because the protocol pooled
  three technical replicate wells under one sample tag **[OBSERVED, IDF]**, well-to-well variance is
  unrecoverable and these intervals are an explicit **lower bound on total uncertainty**. They will be
  labelled as such in every table; they are not evidence of donor-level replicability.
- **Donor-level inference is concordance, not pooling.** Report the sign of `Delta(d)` for C1, C2, C3
  separately. Three agreeing signs give a one-sided exact binomial p = 0.125 under a symmetric null —
  which is *not* significance and is reported as such. Adding C4 (a different question) would give 4
  signs and p = 0.0625; this is pre-specified as a secondary, clearly-labelled combination, not the
  primary test.
- A hierarchical model (section 7.2) provides a partially pooled estimate with a sample-level random
  intercept, which is the closest the deposit allows to an honest dependence model; with 3 samples per
  state the random-effect variance is itself weakly identified and will be reported with its prior
  sensitivity.

## 7. Analyses: a simple baseline and a substantive alternative on the same question

### 7.1 Baseline (pre-specified primary): sample-level pseudobulk displacement

Compute `Rep` at the sample level from `prepared/E-MTAB-8873.counts.tsv.gz` (or equivalently as the
mean of per-cell scores), then form `A_act`, `A_rem`, `Delta` and `rho` per contrast. Cost: seconds.
Transparent, auditable, and adequate to detect a large donor-state effect. What it loses: it collapses
~350-1500 cells per arm into one number, so it cannot distinguish a uniform graded shift from a change
in the *fraction* of fibroblasts in a repair-high state; it cannot see dispersion or bimodality; it
gives no internal uncertainty (one observation per arm); and it is maximally vulnerable to the
residual-macrophage composition rival, because a few macrophage-transcript-rich cells move a pseudobulk
mean much more than they move a median per-cell score.

### 7.2 Alternative (substantive, same question): per-cell hierarchical model with a mixture extension

Model the raw counts of the score genes jointly rather than a pre-averaged score:

`y_{g,i} ~ NegBinomial(mu_{g,i}, phi_g)`,
`log mu_{g,i} = log(depth_i) + a_g + b_{g,run} + beta_{g,arm(i)} + u_{sample(i)}`,

with `arm` in {FLS-only, Mpos-active, Mpos-remission, Mneg-active, Mneg-remission}, a gene-level
hierarchy on `beta_{g,arm}` (partial pooling toward a program-level effect, so the "repair program"
state effect is an estimated shared component rather than a hard-coded average of 7 genes), and a
sample-level random intercept `u_{sample}` that prevents cell-level pseudo-replication from inflating
precision. Fit per fibroblast donor x run (i.e. per C-contrast) and then jointly with donor as a
grouping factor.

Mixture extension (the scientifically distinctive part): model the per-cell `Rep` distribution in each
arm as a two-component mixture anchored on the FLS-only and best-responding arms, with a free weight
`w_arm`. This separates two readings of the same mean shift that have different therapeutic meanings:

- **graded**: every fibroblast is pushed further toward repair, and `w` is unchanged while the
  component locations move — consistent with a soluble mediator field and with dose-dependent benefit;
- **fractional/bistable**: the same fibroblast repair state is entered by a larger *fraction* of cells,
  `w` moves and the component locations do not — consistent with a switch, implying threshold-like
  rather than proportional responses to any intervention.

This is also the direct test of the composition-vs-within-state-regulation rival carried from seed
D3-M1, applied here to the *donor-state* question rather than to the subset question.

**What the alternative recovers that the baseline loses:** per-gene heterogeneity of the state effect
(is the entire "especially remission" signal just `COL1A1`?), honest uncertainty under within-sample
cell dependence, the graded-vs-fractional distinction, robustness to residual macrophage cells (they
enter as a small mixture component instead of shifting a mean), and a shrunken `rho` that is not
dominated by whichever arm happens to have the fewest cells (relevant given the 1060-vs-341 asymmetry
in C3).

**Approximate CPU budget [estimated, not measured].** The data are ~13,000 cells x ~25 score genes =
~3.3e5 count observations with <10^3 parameters. NUTS or Laplace/variational fitting in
numpyro/statsmodels on 8-16 CPU cores: minutes per model, under ~2 CPU-hours including 2000 bootstrap
replicates and prior-sensitivity runs; the EM mixture fits are seconds each even with 200 restarts.
Total well under 4 CPU-hours. Timing is an estimate from the verified file sizes and cell counts; it is
a readiness item, not an executed measurement.

**GPU: justified non-use, not a ban.** For this fit the matrices are tiny (hundreds of parameters,
~1e4 rows); gradient evaluation is dominated by kernel-launch and transfer overhead, so an A100 would
not reduce wall-clock time and would not change feasibility. The reason is workload size, not a policy
against GPUs. **Revisit condition:** if the analysis is extended to all 445 features x ~32,000 cells
with an amortised latent-program model (scVI-style VAE) — which becomes attractive if more runs or
whole-transcriptome co-culture data are admitted — then `gpus: 1` is appropriate and should be
requested through managed compute. I explicitly considered and **did not select** that learned-latent
model now: with three macrophage-donor pairs the limiting quantity is donor-level replication, not model
capacity, and a higher-capacity model would deliver a sharper-looking estimate of a quantity that
remains confounded with donor identity. That trade, not interpretability preference, is the selection
reason.

Both analyses are run. Disagreement between them is itself a result: it implies a composition or
dispersion effect invisible to pseudobulk, and the per-cell model's answer is then preferred with the
reason stated.

## 8. The discriminating observable and pre-specified decision rules

**Discriminating observable:** the sign and magnitude of `Delta(d)`, normalised as `rho(d)`, across
C1-C3 — three paired comparisons in which the fibroblast donor, run, subset, culture duration and
nominal cell dose are held fixed and only the macrophage donor's disease state (and that donor's
identity) varies.

**Gate F0 (anchor; evaluated first).** In E1-E4, the MerTK/CD206pos and MerTK/CD206neg arms must
separate on `P` in the published direction, and at least one MerTK/CD206pos arm per C-contrast must
have an anchor `A >= 0.15` baseline SD. If F0 fails, the result is reported as a failed measurement
anchor in this deposit — an honest negative about the assay as deposited, not about the biology — and
no `rho` is claimed.

**Supportive for H-state** (the "especially" clause is real and large): `Delta(d) > 0` in C1, C2 and
C3; each cell-level bootstrap interval excludes 0; median `rho >= 0.5`. *Strong form:* in addition
`A_act(d) < 0.15` baseline SD in all three (active-derived MerTK/CD206pos macrophages induce no
detectable repair), which is the sharp prediction that distinguishes H-state from a mere quantitative
gradient. Licensed conclusion: in these three donor pairs, most of the measured repair-instructing
capacity co-varies with the macrophage donor's disease state, so the sorted surface phenotype is an
incomplete functional surrogate. **Not licensed:** that disease state *causes* it (donor identity is
confounded), that the mediator is MerTK signalling, that the deficit is reversible, or that the same
holds in tissue or in remission-derived fibroblasts (that cell of the design is empty).

**Supportive for H-phen** (phenotype sufficiency): `|rho(d)| <= 0.2` with bootstrap intervals inside
that band in all three contrasts, while `A > 0.15` baseline SD in at least two. Licensed conclusion: in
these pairs the sorted MerTK/CD206pos phenotype carries most of the repair-instructing capacity
irrespective of donor state, and the deposit's "especially" qualifier corresponds to a small effect in
the deposited pairs. **Not licensed:** that phenotype-based therapy will work; ex vivo instruction of
expanded fibroblasts at 48 h is not clinical resolution.

**Adverse / discordant:** signs of `Delta` disagree across C1-C3; or F0 fails; or the effect tracks the
per-arm cell yield or the gated residual-macrophage fraction rather than donor state; or the
sensitivity analysis that drops run R1 and the SA258R arm reverses the conclusion. Then the honest
report is that three donor pairs in this deposit cannot settle the question, with the discordance
pattern shown.

**Inconclusive (the most likely outcome, stated in advance):** the interval for `rho` spans both the
+-0.2 equivalence band and 0.5. With three paired contrasts this is the expected result unless the
effect is near-total. What it licenses — and the only thing it licenses — is a **bound**: the point
estimate and interval for `rho`, the sign-concordance count with its exact binomial p (0.125 for three
agreeing signs, explicitly not significance), the per-donor anchors, and a design calculation of how
many macrophage donors per state would be needed to resolve `rho` to +-0.2 given the observed
between-pair spread. An imprecise null must not be converted into "the phenotype is sufficient", and a
consistent-but-wide positive must not be converted into "remission state is required". I commit to
reporting the bound as the headline result if that is what the data give.

**Recipient-side (D-contrasts) rules.** Report `Delta_FLS(m)` for D1-D4 with sign concordance within
subset (D1 vs D2 for MerTK/CD206pos; D3 vs D4 for MerTK/CD206neg) and give the age-matched R5 pair
(D2, D4) separately. Two concordant pairs are weak evidence (symmetric-null p = 0.25); the pre-specified
framing is descriptive-with-bound. A material recipient-state effect (|`Delta_FLS`| above 0.15 in
Cliff's delta units, concordant in sign, including in the age-matched pair) would mean that fibroblast
imprinting must be modelled in any future macrophage-state comparison and would partially explain
heterogeneity in published co-culture results. The fibroblast-state x macrophage-state interaction
cannot be estimated at all (two empty cells), and I will not report one.

**What would make me abandon versus continue this direction.** Continue to a dedicated experiment if
H-state is supported, or if `rho` is bounded away from 0 with concordant signs. Revise toward the
recipient-side question if the D-contrasts show a larger and more consistent effect than the
C-contrasts. Stop (and record the bound) if F0 fails or if `rho` is tightly bounded inside +-0.2, since
then the phenotype-sufficiency assumption underlying current biomarker and therapeutic framing [K1, K3]
is not challenged by this deposit.

## 9. Competing explanations, stated with their specific consequences

1. **Macrophage-donor individual identity (the central confound).** One active and one remission
   macrophage donor per fibroblast donor means "state" and "individual" are not separable. Three
   disjoint pairs give sign replication, not identification. A consistent positive `Delta` is
   compatible with "remission macrophages are better" and with "these three remission individuals
   happened to be better". Mitigation available: none within this deposit. Fix: section 10.
2. **Recipient fibroblast imprinting [K2].** Addressed as a first-class arm (D1-D4) including an
   age-matched pair, not as a caveat.
3. **Sorting purity and residual macrophage content.** A differential residual-macrophage or doublet
   fraction between the two arms of a C-contrast could produce an apparent state effect by composition.
   Pre-specified marker gate, per-arm gated fraction reported, analysis run gated and ungated, and the
   mixture model absorbs a small off-target component. Deliberately not measured before registration.
4. **Sort yield and effective dose.** The C3 arms differ 3.1-fold in recovered cells at equal depth.
   Nominal dose was 3000 macrophages/well, but post-sort yield and viability per donor are unreported
   **[UNRESOLVED, expert question]**; a lower effective macrophage dose in one state mimics a state
   effect. Reported per arm and probed descriptively across the three pairs.
5. **Ex vivo culture selection.** Fibroblasts were expanded ~1 week; macrophages were used fresh. The
   two sides are not comparably conditioned, and expansion can both erase donor-state differences in
   fibroblasts and select adherent survivors. This bounds generalisation to tissue for both H-state and
   H-fls.
6. **Age and sex.** All donors female. Fibroblast donor age is held fixed in C1-C3 by design.
   Macrophage donor age/sex are unrecorded and unrecoverable **[OBSERVED]** — the single largest
   uncontrolled covariate for the primary contrast. For D-contrasts, age and state are partially
   collinear except in the age-matched R5 pair.
7. **Composition vs within-state regulation.** Handled by the mixture extension; a fractional result
   and a graded result have different interventional readings and must not be merged.
8. **Limited panel.** 445 targeted features; `NOTCH3`, `PDPN`, `THY1`, `CXCL12`, `HBEGF`, `PTGS2`
   verified absent. No pathway claim may extend to them. In particular the known NOTCH3 and HBEGF
   fibroblast axes cannot be examined here and nothing in this proposal rebrands them.
9. **Run, depth and multiplexing.** Depth differs by run; all contrasts within run; tags unique within
   run **[OBSERVED]**; hashing misassignment and doublets remain possible and are partly absorbed by
   the gate and the mixture component. Technical replicate wells are pooled per tag, so cell-level
   intervals understate uncertainty (stated in every table).
10. **Annotation inconsistencies** in R1 and in the SA258R pure-Mpos arm **[OBSERVED]**: sensitivity
    analysis dropping both, plus expert questions.
11. **Deposit incompleteness**: 3 of 5 described biopsy pairs deposited, no R3/R4 **[OBSERVED]**. A
    null here cannot refute the authors' statement, which may rest on the undeposited pairs.
12. **Treatment confounding on the macrophage side [UNRESOLVED].** Remission patients may have been on
    or off DMARDs/biologics; drug exposure rather than remission state could explain a macrophage
    functional difference. Unrecorded in the deposit; an expert question.

## 10. What would require another study or expert adjudication

**The experiment that fixes the identified defect.** The confound between donor disease state and donor
identity cannot be removed by analysis. The decisive design is: >=5-6 macrophage donors per disease
state crossed against >=4 fibroblast donors of each state (filling both currently empty cells), paired
same-patient MerTK-positive and MerTK-negative subsets in every donor, replicate wells kept separately
taggable so well-level variance is estimable, nominal and realised macrophage dose recorded per well,
and — the strongest version — **longitudinal sampling of the same patients before and after achieving
sustained remission**, which converts a between-individual comparison into a within-individual one and
is the only configuration that can show the capacity is *acquired with* remission rather than
characteristic of the individuals who achieve it.

**Also requiring other data or wet work:** identification of the mediator (conditioned-medium and
transwell arms, lipid-mediator measurement); whether any measured state difference is reversible ex
vivo (e.g. by pro-resolving mediator or MerTK-agonist treatment of active-derived MerTK-positive
macrophages — the therapeutically decisive question that no observational contrast can answer); tissue
validity (spatial transcriptomics/imaging of MerTK-positive neighbourhoods around fibroblast states);
whole-transcriptome confirmation beyond the 445-feature panel.

**Requiring expert/clinical adjudication, which no automatic verifier can supply:** whether an ex vivo
repair-score difference of the estimated size should change the choice between expanding/agonising the
resolving compartment and suppressing the inflammatory compartment; the clinical definition and
duration of "sustained remission" for each donor and the drug exposure at biopsy; and resolution of the
three SDRF annotation inconsistencies and the missing R3/R4 runs.

**Computationally checkable vs not.** Checkable from the bound files: existence, cell counts, depth,
gene presence (already verified); the contrast table; the score definitions; `A`, `Delta`, `rho`,
Cliff's deltas, the hierarchical and mixture fits, the gated/ungated and drop-R1 sensitivities, the
sign-concordance counts and all intervals. Not checkable here: causal attribution of `Delta` to disease
state rather than donor identity; tissue relevance; reversibility; any therapeutic inference.
