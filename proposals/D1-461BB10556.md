# When does ICU mortality prediction learn patient state versus testing practice?

Status: substantive, evidence-bound child of [prior hypothesis]. This redirects the parent after an outcome-blind support audit; it does not claim that the parent hypothesis is false. It tests an observational transportability and measurement-process hypothesis, not a causal effect of testing, a mortality treatment effect, or clinical benefit.

## Scientific deliverable

The future solver must construct a frozen six-hour landmark cohort and newly estimate whether explicit laboratory measurement-process features improve calibrated whole-hospital transport of 24-hour ICU-death risk beyond a state-only model. Completion requires:

- a source-bound cohort/data dictionary and flow counts;
- a frozen laboratory value/unit, revision-time, and missingness codebook;
- paired patient-state and process-aware model fits on identical episodes and hospital folds;
- held-out-hospital cumulative-incidence predictions, calibration, Brier scores, uncertainty, and process-channel ablations;
- a secondary temporal model with ordering/missingness channels if its inputs pass support checks; and
- an interpretation file linking every conclusion to computed outputs and labeling claims that require external or clinical validation.

The actual scientific output is not the best AUROC. It is an estimate of whether workflow/measurement information explains a reproducible gap between within-hospital and whole-hospital transport, and whether retaining that information improves or harms clinically relevant calibration at a fixed alert burden.

## Unresolved question and clinical importance

Existing evidence supports two narrower claims: EHR laboratory collection can be informative rather than random, and models can lose transportability when data-generating and workflow processes differ. The accessible Sun et al. study (DOI 10.1186/s12911-024-02612-1; PMCID PMC11270887) compared missing-data strategies for deterioration prediction in its setting. Those findings do not establish how large the workflow contribution is in eICU, whether it survives held-out-hospital evaluation, or whether a model that uses it is safer to deploy.

The unresolved claim is:

> In adult first ICU stays, a six-hour model for the next 24 hours of recorded ICU death will show a larger whole-hospital calibration/accuracy degradation when laboratory measurement-process channels are omitted than when they are retained; a compact temporal representation will reveal additional transportable ordering or persistence only if it improves calibrated risk beyond the matched transparent process-aware model.

This matters because an ICU warning model that learns who gets tested may appear accurate in development and become miscalibrated at a hospital with different testing intensity. Conversely, removing all observation information can discard clinically useful signals about active surveillance. Separating these possibilities informs model deployment, site-specific monitoring, and whether a new hospital needs recalibration rather than a new physiological model. It does not by itself justify changing test-ordering practice.

## Feasibility evidence and its boundary

The exact eICU snapshot is [source checksum]. The bounded outcome-blind audit found 135,680 adult first-stay episodes eligible at 360 minutes, 2,470 recorded ICU deaths by 1,800 minutes, 208 hospitals, 175 hospitals with events, 195 hospitals with at least 20 eligible episodes, 2,209,118 lab rows known by the six-hour landmark after revision filtering, and 111,655 episodes with at least one such lab row. These are support counts only; no association, model performance, or clinical benefit was computed.

The parent’s assigned renal/nutrition direction was audited on its exact source paths and failed its own stability gate: only 3 conservative rise-plus-documented-non-delivery episodes and no detected low in that cell. Therefore this child does not relax the parent’s gate or recast missing nutrition as feed interruption. The parent remains a valid deferred question for chart-adjudicated or larger data.

## Population, time and estimand

Use the first adult ICU stay per person: age >=18, treating age >89 as 90; positive numeric unitdischargeoffset; smallest valid numeric unitvisitnumber per uniquepid, breaking ties by patientunitstayid. Set the landmark at t=360 ICU-relative minutes. Include only stays with unitdischargeoffset >360.

Primary outcome: first recorded ICU death in (360,1800], defined as patient.unitdischargestatus exactly normalized to Expired and patient.unitdischargeoffset <=1800. The estimand is the 24-hour cumulative incidence of recorded ICU death after the landmark, with alive ICU discharge before 1,800 minutes treated as a competing event. A sensitivity outcome is a cause-specific discrete-time event among episodes remaining in the ICU at each hour. This is not all-hospital mortality and does not recover deaths after an alive ICU discharge.

Primary prediction target: individual risk/CIF of recorded ICU death by 24 hours after t=360. Evaluate calibration-in-the-large, calibration slope, integrated/cause-specific Brier score and uncertainty; discrimination and fixed-alert-burden net-benefit-like summaries are secondary. Do not report a causal effect of testing or a claim about unrecorded death.

## Exact available data bindings

All source files below are read-only ordinary files inside the eICU 2.0 source directory listed in datasets/eicu/README.md; there are no nested archive members beyond each CSV.gz ordinary file. The snapshot catalog and schema IDs are part of the frozen provenance.

- patient.csv.gz, table patient, schema datasets/eicu/table-ab037c09d7df9a3c.json. Required columns: patientunitstayid, uniquepid, age, hospitalid, unitvisitnumber, unitdischargeoffset, unitdischargestatus, hospitaladmitoffset, hospitaladmitsource, admissionweight, gender, ethnicity and unitstaytype. Use patientunitstayid as the stay key and uniquepid only for first-stay grouping and clustered uncertainty. ICU-relative time is unitdischargeoffset; there is no invented unitadmitoffset.
- hospital.csv.gz, table hospital, schema datasets/eicu/table-811df7b2ef435e12.json. Required columns: hospitalid, numbedscategory, teachingstatus, region. Join patient.hospitalid = hospital.hospitalid. Hospital ID is used for fold assignment and descriptive drift summaries, never as a predictor in held-out inference.
- lab.csv.gz, table lab, schema datasets/eicu/table-79bdb33275339b1a.json. Required columns: labid, patientunitstayid, labresultoffset, labname, labresult, labresulttext, labmeasurenamesystem, labmeasurenameinterface and labresultrevisedoffset. Use labresultoffset as clinical measurement time and require labresultrevisedoffset <= 360 or null for a value to be known at the landmark. Join on patientunitstayid. Fit a frozen panel from observed exact names, initially including potassium, sodium, hgb, hct, glucose, creatinine, bun, calcium, wbc x 1000, platelets x 1000, bicarbonate, fio2, pao2, paco2, ph, anion gap, hco3, o2 sat (%), magnesium and base excess; retain only names and units passing the solver’s pre-outcome audit.

For every panel test, derive last valid value, first value, count, range/slope where at least two values exist, time since last valid result, indicator of any result, indicator of revision-known result, and an interface/system stratum only where its values are stable and interpretable. Do not silently convert conflicting units; record rejected/ambiguous rows and run a strict-codebook sensitivity. The measurement-process block consists of test-specific missingness, counts, time gaps, total distinct tests, total lab rows and hospital-level testing-rate summaries that do not use the outcome.

Optional extension, only if a pre-specified support audit passes: vitalPeriodic.csv.gz, table vitalPeriodic, schema datasets/eicu/table-a22c6d6981a32279.json, with patientunitstayid, observationoffset, temperature, sao2, heartrate, respiration, systemicmean and related columns; and vitalAperiodic.csv.gz, table vitalAperiodic, schema datasets/eicu/table-72ace5b89971196b2.json, with patientunitstayid, observationoffset, noninvasivesystolic, noninvasivediastolic and noninvasivemean. These add physiological measurement-process channels, but the primary claim must remain reproducible with the audited patient/lab/hospital set.

Do not use apachePatientResult actual outcomes or any post-landmark field as a predictor. Its schema is available but its actual mortality columns would leak the target. No images, raw waveforms, medication administration record, reliable narrative note, clinician intent or complete test-order table is available.

## Analysis and alternatives

Freeze eligibility, value/unit rules, panel, outcome, competing-event rules and hospital folds before fitting.

B_state, the simple baseline, is an elastic-net pooled discrete-time cause-specific logistic model for death and alive discharge over hourly rows from 360 through 1,800 minutes. It uses age, sex/ethnicity where valid, weight, admission source, stay type and the panel’s last values, early slopes/ranges and clinically defined threshold flags. Missing panel values receive training-fold medians plus only a single global availability flag required to distinguish no usable panel; it excludes test counts, detailed missingness, time-since-test and hospital ID. Fit penalties and any splines only in the development hospitals.

B_state+process is the primary substantive comparison. It uses exactly the same episode-hour rows, value features, target and folds and adds test-specific masks, result counts, elapsed time since each test, total testing density, interface/revision channels and pre-landmark hospital attributes. Compare it with B_state within hospitals and across whole-hospital folds. Estimate the change in held-out calibration slope/intercept, Brier score and CIF risk at fixed alert burdens with patient-clustered bootstrap intervals. An improvement that appears only in random within-hospital splits is not transport evidence.

T_temporal is a secondary one-layer GRU or temporal convolution over six one-hour pre-landmark bins. Each bin contains the same panel values, explicit missingness masks, elapsed-time channels, counts and interface/documentation channels; the heads predict death, alive discharge and next-bin lab observation. Use the same episode set and hospital folds, with no future outcome or future measurement. Compare against B_state+process using calibration and Brier first, then discrimination. The temporal model is informative only if ordering, persistence or co-movement improves transport beyond the transparent process-aware model and survives value-only versus process-only ablations. A small predictive gain without calibration or clinical decision value is not sufficient.

Use three whole-hospital folds (development 60%, validation 20%, transport 20%) stratified by eligible episode count and event count, keeping all stays of each uniquepid together. If a fold lacks enough event and competing-discharge support, do not make a transport claim; report the fold as unsupported. A secondary patient-random split quantifies the optimism caused by ignoring hospital workflow.

## Falsification, interpretation and gates

Before fitting, require at least 100 total death events, at least 10 event-bearing hospitals in each evaluation fold, at least 20 eligible episodes in each retained hospital, positive value/missingness overlap across folds, and no feature whose revision availability depends on post-landmark information. These are computational stability rules, not clinical thresholds.

Supportive evidence requires: (1) the process-aware model reduces held-out-hospital integrated Brier score and calibration error relative to B_state in at least two transport folds, with a prespecified relative Brier improvement of at least 10% and bootstrap uncertainty excluding no improvement; (2) the direction persists after strict unit/revision codebooks and after removing hospital attributes; (3) process-channel ablation shows the improvement is attributable to observation features rather than an accidental value change; (4) calibration at a fixed alert burden improves without an unacceptable increase in alerts; and (5) the effect is not dominated by one hospital or one lab family. This supports the narrower conclusion that measurement process carries transport-relevant predictive information in this snapshot.

Adverse evidence is no transport gap, no process-aware improvement, a reversal after revision/strict-codebook handling, deterioration in calibration when process features are included, or process-only performance that collapses under within-hospital permutation. That would refute the proposed process advantage for this setting, not prove that testing is clinically irrelevant.

Inconclusive evidence includes insufficient event-bearing hospitals after exclusions, no common support in testing intensity, unstable weights/calibration, fold-specific reversals, target leakage, or failure to distinguish alive discharge from death. In that case report support counts and descriptive distributions only; do not select the better model by AUROC or confirm the hypothesis.

Harbor can check source bindings, leakage control, cohort construction, feature derivation, fold assignment, fits, calibration, uncertainty, ablations and whether conclusions follow from outputs. It cannot determine whether a test was ordered for concern, whether a recorded result is physiologically correct, whether an alert changes care, or whether the model benefits patients. Those require chart review, expert adjudication, external hospitals and prospective impact evaluation.

## Why this supersedes or defers the parent

The parent asks an important safety question but its clinically appealing renal-worsening/documented-nutrition contrast is not supported by the available episode cells: the exact outcome-blind audit found only three conservative rise-plus-non-delivery episodes and zero detected lows, below its own gate. Relaxing that definition would turn missing or ambiguous nutrition documentation into a result. The child preserves the parent’s strongest insight—that observation is part of the estimand—but moves it to a directly supported outcome and a hospital-transport question with 2,470 recorded events and 208 hospitals. It should supersede the parent for the next executable study, while the insulin/nutrition hypothesis should be revisited only with adjudicated feeding, administration and renal outcomes or a larger cohort.
