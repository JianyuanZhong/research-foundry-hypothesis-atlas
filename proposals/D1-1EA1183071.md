# eICU monitoring adequacy after a recorded insulin-rate update

## Decision and scientific deliverable

The inherited renal-worsening × documented-nutrition-gap hypothesis is not ready to use as a primary 24-hour hypoglycemia study. The bounded audit confirms that the exposure ingredients exist, but the nutrition field is a heterogeneous intake-output stream and a missing row is not evidence that feeding stopped. The inherited broad screen also has only 26 detected <70 mg/dL values across 1,565 episodes with any 24-hour glucose, and the final covered-cell count, competing-event handling, and observation-weight positivity were not established. I therefore redirect the primary question to a more directly supported and actionable endpoint: whether high-risk recorded insulin episodes receive a glucose measurement in the next six hours.

The future solver must newly construct a frozen, leakage-safe episode/hour dataset and estimate the renal-worsening × explicitly observed nutrition-documentation contrast for next-glucose observation. It must produce: a source/header/codebook audit; cohort flow and coverage table; the four exposure-cell counts; event and competing-censoring counts; B0/B1 coefficients and standardized risk differences with patient-clustered uncertainty; observation-process diagnostics; held-out-hospital predictions and calibration; ablations separating physiology from documentation; and a prespecified interpretation file linked to those outputs. Detected low glucose <70 mg/dL in the subsequent 6 and 24 hours is secondary and descriptive unless its event/support gate passes. No clinical result is claimed here.

## Unresolved question and hypothesis

The strongest claim supported before fitting is only that eICU contains recorded insulin-rate updates, creatinine results, nutrition-labelled intake-output rows, and glucose results with ICU-relative timestamps. The available data do not establish insulin administration, feed interruption, AKI, physiologic hypoglycemia, symptoms, or benefit from intensified monitoring.

Primary falsifiable hypothesis:

> Among adults in their first eligible ICU stay with a positive recorded `Insulin (units/hr)` update and a recent valid glucose, a recorded creatinine rise is associated with a lower probability of a glucose result being recorded in the following six hours; this association differs between episodes with an explicitly recorded positive or zero nutrition entry and episodes with no such nutrition documentation.

The primary outcome is deliberately a measurement-process outcome: a glucose result observed in the next six hours. It tests whether monitoring is aligned with a readily identifiable risk context and could motivate chart review or prospective monitoring protocols. It does not claim that an unmeasured low occurred. The renal/nutrition contrast is an interaction in observed documentation, not a causal effect of renal failure or feeding.

Secondary hypothesis, only if supported: the same cells differ in first detected glucose <70 mg/dL during six hours and, separately, during a 24-hour horizon. This is an ascertainment-dependent outcome. The inherited 24-hour detected-low endpoint is challenged because its broad feasibility count is sparse and because follow-up is cut by discharge, death, and end-of-life documentation.

## Population and temporal design

Use eICU snapshot `[source checksum]`. Time is minutes from ICU admission; the catalog has no `unitadmitoffset`, so ICU time zero is the native origin. Keep adults (`age >=18`; encode `> 89` as 90), positive finite `unitdischargeoffset`, and the smallest valid numeric `unitvisitnumber` per `uniquepid`, breaking ties by `patientunitstayid`. A stay can contribute one index episode only.

An index is the earliest `infusionDrug.infusionoffset` t with `1,440 <= t < unitdischargeoffset`, exact `drugname = 'Insulin (units/hr)'`, finite `drugrate > 0`, and at least one audited glucose in `[t-360,t]`. A recorded rate update is not administration. Do not carry a rate forward, use zero/negative rates, or silently substitute `infusionrate`; retain that field only for a unit-sensitivity audit.

At t, define renal history using audited creatinine in `lab`: latest result in `[t-360,t]` and latest result in `[t-1,800,t-1,080]`, both required. Primary recorded renal rise is current minus baseline `>=0.3 mg/dL`; sensitivities are `>=1.5` times baseline and continuous change. Call this “recorded creatinine rise,” never KDIGO AKI. Use `labresultoffset` as clinical time and require `labresultrevisedoffset <= t` for values used at index; deduplicate by `labid` under a frozen revision rule.

Define explicitly observed nutrition documentation from `intakeOutput` in `(t-720,t]`, requiring finite `cellvaluenumeric` and the exact path prefix `flowsheet|Flowsheet Cell Labels|I&O|Intake (ml)|Nutrition (ml)|`. Positive delivery entries are labels containing a prespecified case-folded whitelist of oral/PO intake, enteral feeds/formula/tube intake, TPN, PPN, or fat emulsion. Exclude `Enteral meds`, medication labels, crystalloids, dextrose-only fluids, flushes, weights, and output paths. A positive nutrition entry is `N=1`; an explicit finite zero in the same audited nutrition channel is `N=0` only if there is no positive nutrition entry in the window. Otherwise label `N=unknown`, not non-delivery. The solver must print the frozen label list and counts before fitting. This is documented intake status, not calories, feed interruption, or clinician intent.

The primary four cells are recorded renal rise/no rise crossed with observed positive/zero nutrition documentation, excluding N=unknown. A sensitivity uses a stricter six-hour nutrition window and `intakeoutputentryoffset` in place of `intakeoutputoffset`. A no-interface/ambiguous-label stratum must remain separate. `nurseCharting` may describe interface coverage but cannot convert missing `intakeOutput` into no feeding; no unvalidated nursing whitelist is permitted.

Build one row per at-risk hour h=0,...,5 after t. The row is censored at the earliest of ICU discharge, expired `unitdischargestatus`, or a prespecified EOL save/discussion time from `carePlanEOL`; censoring is not a low. The primary hour outcome is whether a valid glucose is recorded in `[t+60h,t+60(h+1))`, with a six-hour episode-level outcome of any observed glucose in `(t,t+360]`. The secondary detected-low outcomes use `<70 mg/dL` and `<54 mg/dL` only among valid, observable glucose results and are reported separately from missingness. Results after the applicable censoring time are excluded.

## Baseline and substantive alternative

B0 is a transparent pooled discrete-time logistic model for hourly glucose observation with restricted splines for time since index, prior glucose level/slope and gap, recorded insulin rate and prior rate updates, age, weight when available, admission source, early severity, hospital attributes, and pre-index testing density. It excludes the renal/nutrition terms. B1 adds recorded renal rise, nutrition documentation cell, their prespecified interaction, and missingness/interface strata. Standardize B0/B1 over the eligible episode distribution to report the joint-versus-no-rise/observed-positive contrast for six-hour observation probability and its difference/ratio with patient-clustered bootstrap intervals. Use a competing-risk sensitivity for discharge/death/EOL.

The substantive alternative is a compact one-layer GRU or temporal convolution over the preceding 24 one-hour bins. Inputs are the same clinical variables plus explicitly masked glucose values, time since last glucose, testing/interface indicators, recorded insulin-rate history, creatinine trajectory, and nutrition-documentation channels. It has an observation head for each next hour and a secondary detected-low head. Fit with the identical episode set and whole-hospital split; no future labs, discharge status, EOL fields, or post-index observations enter the inputs. This alternative can reveal whether ordering, persistence, and co-movement of renal/glucose/testing/nutrition signals carry monitoring information lost by fixed summaries. A performance gain alone is not a clinical advance: require calibrated improvement at fixed alert/ review burden and a reproducible renal/nutrition effect pattern in held-out hospitals.

Use 60/20/20 whole-hospital development/validation/transport folds, keeping `uniquepid` in one fold. Hospital IDs are unavailable as learner inputs in transport; only `hospital` attributes may be used. Report Brier score, calibration slope/intercept, reliability, and fixed-review-burden curves; discrimination is secondary. If there is no support in a fold, transport is unestimable rather than imputed.

B0/B1 are selected as the primary analysis because the scientific target is an interpretable monitoring contrast, not a leaderboard. The temporal alternative is retained because the relevant uncertainty is whether observation gaps and physiologic ordering add information beyond summaries. A mechanistic insulin-glucose-renal model is deferred: the data lack confirmed insulin administration, delivered calories, insulin sensitivity, symptoms, and adjudicated kidney injury, so its parameters would not be identifiable.

## Support gates and falsification

The primary observation study proceeds only if the final audited cohort has at least 500 eligible index episodes, at least 100 in each primary four-cell combination, at least 10 hospitals with both renal states and at least 10 hospitals with observed nutrition positive/zero states, and at least 70% with a non-censored six-hour opportunity. These are design stability gates, not clinical thresholds. Every B1 cell must have at least 20 observed six-hour outcomes and no near-zero observation probability after prespecified trimming. If any gate fails, report model-free counts and uncertainty, do not fit an unstable interaction, and classify the contrast inconclusive.

The secondary six/24-hour detected-low analysis additionally requires at least 20 detected <70 events overall, at least 5 per primary cell, stable estimates under strict/broad glucose labels and revision rules, and adequate competing-event support. If this fails, a low cannot be used to refute or support the monitoring hypothesis; it is descriptive/inconclusive. The previously observed 26-event broad screen does not satisfy these final gates because it did not apply the paired creatinine, exact nutrition, revision, and censoring codebook.

Falsifications are preregistered: shuffle index times within stay; shuffle renal/nutrition histories within hospital; use pre-index placebo observation windows; compare strict versus broad glucose/nutrition codebooks; compare clinical versus entry times; stratify laboratory and bedside/interface glucose; use an insulin-free analogous episode cohort; and use newly observed non-glucose lab testing as a negative-control outcome. The renal/nutrition pattern should not reproduce in placebo windows or the negative-control testing outcome. A strong association confined to one hospital/interface or erased by entry-time/revision sensitivity is adverse to transportable interpretation.

Supportive results are a stable, calibrated, held-out-hospital reduction in next-glucose observation for the prespecified renal/nutrition contrast, with failed falsifications; a detected-low signal may strengthen the case for chart review only if its separate gate passes. Adverse results are a null, reversal, loss after strict label/revision handling, or a documentation-only pattern shared by negative controls; this refutes the proposed marker in this snapshot, not insulin safety. Inconclusive results include sparse cells/events, non-positivity, unstable weights, censorship/interface ambiguity, nonconvergence, or unsupported hospital transport.

## Clinical limits and resource plan

The audit measured, without using post-index low counts to choose this redesign, 138,433 first adult stays, 99,784 qualifying positive exact-label insulin rows, and 2,376 stays with at least one qualifying row before recent-glucose restriction. It also found many structured nutrition rows, including explicit positive/zero rows, but mixed labels such as medication infusions, crystalloids, feeds, and oral entries. These are availability checks, not study results. The final paired-cell and outcome counts remain solver outputs; `support_audit.py` is retained as the bounded outcome-blind audit script.

B0/B1 and observation diagnostics fit on CPU within the future envelope of 16 CPUs, 262,144 MiB RAM, and 28,800 seconds. The temporal alternative is a small task-specific model; estimate one allocated A100 (`cuda:0`) only if the CPU pilot shows repeated fits exceed the envelope, otherwise use CPU. The hardware inventory documents A100-SXM4 80 GB devices, but ordinary shells have no CUDA allocation; no GPU is scientifically required. These are planning estimates, not a completed fit.

The eICU-09 expert seed supplied the clinical idea and explicitly warned that absent nutrition records must not be treated as cessation; this proposal preserves that caution. The eICU-02/eICU-08 seeds motivate the monitoring-process endpoint. The three research demonstrations were inspected only as method/ambition references under their availability notes; no unavailable paper text, supplements, external validation, or reproduction claim is used here. A future claim about physiologic hypoglycemia, feed interruption, AKI, insulin administration, causal effect, alert response, or clinical benefit requires adjudicated chart evidence, expert review, external validation, or a prospective/emulated-intervention study.
