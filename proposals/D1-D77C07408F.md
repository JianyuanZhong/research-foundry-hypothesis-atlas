# Asynchronous kidney–respiratory recovery after a fixed stabilization landmark

## Scientific deliverable

The future Harbor solver must newly estimate whether an interpretable, paired organ-recovery state predicts near-term ICU deterioration after an observable stabilization landmark. Completion requires:

1. an auditable cohort flow from all valid adult ICU stays through landmark, paired-measurement coverage, phenotype categories, competing endpoints, and unknown labels;
2. a fixed landmark at ICU admission time plus six hours, not a first qualifying time selected from the data;
3. renal and respiratory recovery components measured only in the subsequent six-hour window, with row-level clinical-time and recording-time audits;
4. held-out cause-specific cumulative-incidence estimates for concordant and discordant recovery phenotypes, compared with a transparent latest-state/context baseline using identical source domains;
5. a same-input masked chronological joint model, with uncertainty, calibration, and a clear account of what temporal coupling reveals beyond the fixed phenotype rule;
6. observation-process, treatment-process, timestamp, label-permutation, and time-shuffle falsifications; and
7. a conclusion ledger linking each claim to computed outputs and separating prognostic association from treatment effect, discharge appropriateness, or clinical diagnosis.

No model has been fitted and no clinical result is claimed in this proposal.

This is a substantive child of the assigned expert seed `[prior hypothesis]`. It is also deliberately outside the mature live-ICU-discharge branch represented by `[prior hypothesis]`: that branch asks whether late history among observed live exits survives discharge-selection and observation-process audits. This child asks an earlier, different question about cross-organ recovery after stabilization, before an ICU-discharge decision exists. Its scientific advance is an estimand about asynchronous physiology, not another discharge-selection safeguard.

## Unresolved question and evidence boundary

The seed proposes that, after circulatory stabilization, persistent kidney or respiratory impairment signals renewed deterioration. The strongest evidence presently available for this proposal is narrower: MIMIC-IV contains dated ICU vital signs, support records, laboratory values, urine output, ICU boundaries, and hospital death fields from which an observational recovery trajectory and subsequent recorded events can be reconstructed. The seed card and its related reference do not establish that this local phenotype is valid, that it is distinct from observation or treatment intensity, or that acting on it improves care.

The unresolved prognostic claim is:

> Among adults who reach a fixed, observable circulatory-stabilization landmark, does a kidney-lag or respiratory-lag recovery pattern during the next six hours carry a higher 24-hour risk of a first ICU rescue/escalation or timed in-hospital death than concordant recovery, after accounting for terminal organ state, admission/stay context, active support, and observation intensity?

The paired distinction is clinically important because a single SOFA-like snapshot can hide different directions of recovery: the kidney may be improving while respiratory support worsens, or the reverse. Those states could imply different surveillance needs, but an association would not show that either organ is the causal source of deterioration or that a particular intervention should be changed.

The primary estimand is the standardized held-out 24-hour cumulative-incidence contrast for first rescue/death between each discordant phenotype and concordant recovery, with live ICU exit treated as a competing observation boundary. A secondary estimand is the incremental predictive value of chronological cross-organ organization over the same-input terminal-state and context baseline. These are prognostic operating characteristics, not causal effects.

A targeted Europe PMC search was used as a novelty check (frozen search sources `[source checksum]` and `[source checksum]`). It is not a systematic review and does not prove novelty. It did not establish a validated MIMIC-IV estimand matching this fixed-landmark, paired kidney–respiratory recovery contrast.

The fixed landmark is:

```
h = icustays.intime + 6 hours
```

There is no search over candidate hours and no outcome-dependent selection of `h`. Landmark eligibility requires:

- at least four valid MAP observations in the six hours before `h), spanning at least three distinct clock hours;
- pre-landmark median MAP >=65 mmHg using the prespecified MAP priority rule below;
- no vasoactive infusion start or restart in [`h-2h,h`] among the prespecified input item IDs;
- no timed death at or before `h`; and
- `h+6h <= icustays.outtime`, so that the paired recovery window can be observed in the index ICU stay.

Ongoing support at `h` is not silently treated as absence: active vasoactive status, invasive/non-invasive ventilation status, care unit, and observation counts are baseline covariates. A sensitivity restricts to no active vasoactive interval at `h`; another allows a new infusion in the last two hours and treats the stabilization rule as a covariate-defined subgroup. The operational rule is an observable landmark, not adjudication of true circulatory stabilization.

## Paired recovery phenotype

Construct the phenotype from the fixed window [`h,h+6h`], split into early [`h,h+3h`) and late [`h+3h,h+6h`) halves. A row is eligible for a half only if its clinical timestamp is within that half and its recording timestamp is no later than the end of the half; the primary real-time matrix additionally requires the row to have been stored by the prediction cutoff `h+6h`. The solver must report a chart-time-only sensitivity and the fraction of rows changed by the store-time rule.

Require at least one valid renal observation in each half and at least one valid respiratory-support domain in each half. Report coverage separately for creatinine, urine, FiO2/flow/PEEP, and ventilation procedures. Complete phenotype analysis excludes unknown coverage rather than labeling it as non-recovery; inverse-coverage weighting and complete-case bounds are secondary, not replacements for the flow.

### Kidney component

Use creatinine `labevents.itemid=50912) and Foley output `outputevents.itemid=226559). For each half, use the median creatinine and total Foley mL divided by observed hours with output records. The primary renal direction is deliberately transparent:

- renal improving/stable: late creatinine median is no higher than early median and late urine rate is no lower than early rate;
- renal non-improving: creatinine rises or urine rate falls;
- renal unknown: either half lacks the required measurement or the unit/value fails validation.

The primary rule does not claim that a short-term creatinine slope diagnoses AKI or that Foley volume is complete urine output. Sensitivities use creatinine tolerance ±0.3 mg/dL, output observed-only versus output-required, and a weight-normalized rate when a valid pre-cutoff `inputevents.patientweight) is available. Continuous creatinine and output changes are also retained for the learned model.

### Respiratory component

Use FiO2 `223835), oxygen flow `223834/227287), PEEP `220339/224700), SpO2 `220277), respiratory rate `220210), ventilator mode `223849), and procedure intervals `225792/225794). Build a support-direction vector in each half:

- FiO2 and oxygen flow are lower-support when their validated medians decrease;
- PEEP is lower-support when its validated median decreases;
- invasive/NIV procedure presence is lower-support when the procedure ends and is not present in the late half;
- SpO2 must not fall by more than 2 percentage points in the primary respiratory-improvement call; this is a guard against calling a worsening oxygenation state recovery.

Respiratory improving/stable requires lower support in at least two available support domains with no guarded SpO2 fall. Respiratory non-improving means no such improvement or a worsening support direction. Respiratory unknown means no valid support domain in both halves. The two-domain rule is frozen before outcome inspection. Sensitivities use FiO2/flow only, PEEP/procedure only, arterial-support strata, and continuous standardized support slopes.

The four primary labels are:

1. concordant recovery: renal improving/stable and respiratory improving/stable;
2. kidney-lag: respiratory improving/stable, renal non-improving;
3. respiratory-lag: renal improving/stable, respiratory non-improving;
4. dual non-recovery: both non-improving.

Unknown renal or respiratory coverage is reported separately and excluded from the primary complete-phenotype contrast. These are operational prognostic phenotypes, not diagnoses of organ injury, microcirculatory failure, or irreversible organ dysfunction.

## Competing outcomes and temporal boundaries

Prediction begins at `h+6h), immediately after the phenotype window. Follow through `h+30h), or until a first competing transition:

- **R (ICU rescue/escalation):** the first of (a) a new or restarted prespecified vasoactive input interval whose `starttime > h+6h), (b) a new invasive or non-invasive ventilation procedure interval (`procedureevents.itemid=225792/225794)) beginning after `h+6h), or (c) a later ICU stay in the same admission with `intime > h+6h). A support interval already active at `h+6h) is not a new event. Report vasoactive, ventilation, and return-to-ICU components separately.
- **D (timed death):** `admissions.deathtime) in (`h+6h,h+30h`], ordered before R if timestamps tie.
- **S (live ICU exit):** `icustays.outtime) in (`h+6h,h+30h`] before R or D, when the patient is not known dead at the exit. S is an observation boundary, not a good outcome.
- **N:** no R, D or S by `h+30h) while the admission and index ICU stay remain ascertainable.
- **U:** `hospital_expire_flag=1) with missing `deathtime), ambiguous event ordering, or otherwise unresolved timed-death ascertainment. U is never silently recoded as N.

The primary clinical contrast is the first-event 24-hour cumulative incidence of R-or-D, with R and D also reported separately and S retained as a competing state. If a patient exits the ICU and later returns, S is first; this preserves the estimand as “deterioration or death before the observed ICU-exit boundary,” rather than pretending that later ward data are complete. A 7-day hospital-death/ICU-return sensitivity is descriptive only. No `dod), discharge note, radiology text, post-landmark measurement, future transfer, `dischtime), `discharge_location), or `last_careunit) is a predictor.

## Baseline, phenotype model and learned joint alternative

Freeze a deterministic subject-level 70/15/15 train/validation/test partition, approximately stratified by endpoint and anchor-year group. No subject crosses partitions. All imputation, scaling, clipping, availability rules, phenotype thresholds, feature selection, calibration, and review thresholds are learned or frozen without test outcomes.

### Transparent baseline B0

B0 is a penalized discrete-time cause-specific hazard model with separate R, D and S hazards in one-hour bins after `h+6h). It uses:

- age, gender, anchor-year group, admission type and admission location;
- ICU elapsed time at `h`, first care unit, and prior ICU-stay count in the admission;
- active vasoactive and ventilation status at `h+6h);
- the latest available creatinine, urine, respiratory-support values and MAP at `h+6h);
- measurement masks, counts, time since last observation, and treatment/observation-process indicators from the prespecified source whitelist.

B0 is transparent because coefficients, direction, missingness, and cause-specific hazards are inspectable. It tests whether a paired recovery state adds clinically interpretable information over the terminal state and context that a bedside snapshot would retain.

### Fixed phenotype model B1

B1 is B0 plus the two renal/respiratory directions and their four-level phenotype contrast. The primary scientific output is the standardized 24-hour CIF difference for kidney-lag and respiratory-lag versus concordant recovery, with dual non-recovery reported separately. B1 is not allowed to use extra variables unavailable to B0.

### Learned joint trajectory model J1

J1 is a small masked chronological GRU with a three-head discrete-time output for R, D and S. It receives exactly the same source whitelist, subject split, context vector, observation masks and treatment-process indicators as B1, plus the six one-hour bins of raw values and continuous changes used to construct the phenotype: creatinine, Foley output, MAP, FiO2, O2 flow, PEEP, SpO2, respiratory rate, ventilation-procedure status, and vasoactive status. Missingness masks are explicit; missing output is not zero. No text, notes, future transfers, discharge fields, or post-`h+6h) rows enter J1. Hidden size is 32 or 64, selected once on validation data; five fixed seeds and validation-only early stopping are required.

J1 is scientifically useful rather than merely more complex: it can reveal persistence, reversal, within-window timing, and cross-organ coupling that the binary kidney/respiratory rule and B1’s terminal state discard. If J1 improves prediction but the phenotype contrast is null, the specific asynchronous phenotype hypothesis is not supported; the result only motivates a different, less interpretable trajectory hypothesis. If B1 supports the phenotype and J1 adds nothing, the fixed paired summary may be sufficient for this question. A learned gain without calibration or without superiority to the process-only control is not evidence of biology.

A mechanistic state-space model with latent renal and respiratory recovery states was considered. It could provide smoothed latent states and transition uncertainty, but would require unvalidated observation and support-response assumptions and could turn missingness into model-imposed physiology. It is deferred, not rejected; revisit it if repeated paired coverage and external clinical adjudication become available. A large transformer is also deferred because this question is about a prespecified cross-organ estimand, not generic sequence scale. Neither GPU use nor model complexity is a merit criterion.

## Falsification and interpretation criteria

Required checks are:

1. **Availability audit:** every predictor row must satisfy clinical and recording-time rules; every interval must be valid and clipped; prohibited columns and future rows are detected by an automated whitelist.
2. **Process-only control:** fit a model from masks, counts, test/order intensity, Foley charting, care unit, and treatment-process fields with physiologic values removed. If it matches B1/J1 or removes the phenotype contrast after adjustment, a physiology-specific interpretation is weakened.
3. **Chronology shuffle:** swap early and late bins within each stay while preserving values, coverage and counts. The directional phenotype contrast and J1’s temporal increment should attenuate; persistence indicates an order-insensitive rule or leakage.
4. **Outcome permutation:** permute endpoint labels within training/validation/test partitions and refit. Any comparable held-out gain is a pipeline failure.
5. **Definition sensitivities:** repeat arterial-only versus all-MAP rules, creatinine-only versus creatinine-plus-output kidney rules, respiratory support-only versus procedure-supported rules, alternative valid-bin requirements, and chart-time-only versus available-by-cutoff matrices.
6. **Treatment-process audit:** report active support, starts, duration and treatment density by phenotype; repeat analyses stratified by support status. Do not interpret treatment-associated differences as treatment effects.
7. **Competing-state audit:** require the claim to survive separate R and D analyses and explicit S handling. A gain confined to S or N is not evidence for adverse organ recovery.
8. **Selection and ascertainment stress:** report all-eligible versus complete-phenotype flow, unknown U bounds, coverage-weight overlap/ESS, care-unit strata, and an adverse assignment of U to D. Instability is adverse or inconclusive, not a favorable result.

### Prespecified result categories

A **supportive** result requires adequate paired coverage and event support, a higher adjusted R-or-D CIF for at least one lag phenotype than concordant recovery in the prespecified direction, a 95% interval excluding zero and extending beyond a 2-percentage-point absolute-risk null region, acceptable calibration, and stability across the timestamp, coverage, endpoint and support-process sensitivities. B1 must add information over B0; B1 exceeding the process-only model is required for a physiology-specific interpretation. J1 may corroborate or expose additional timing information but is not required to establish the fixed-rule phenotype contrast.

A **null** result is adequate support with all lag-versus-concordant absolute CIF intervals contained within ±2 percentage points and no reproducible B1 increment beyond B0. This supports no clinically material association detectable by this operational phenotype; it does not prove that asynchronous recovery does not exist.

An **adverse** result is a reliably reversed contrast beyond the null region, a B1 signal explained by the process-only model, a result that disappears under available-by-landmark timing or competing-state handling, or a falsification failure. An adverse computational result challenges the proposed operational explanation; it does not establish that the opposite physiology is protective.

An **inconclusive** result is sparse paired measurements, fewer than 100 patients in a required phenotype, fewer than 30 first R-or-D events in the complete-phenotype analysis, high U/unknown coverage, incompatible units, poor overlap/ESS, unstable calibration, or intervals too wide to distinguish the null region. These sample-size thresholds are feasibility flags, not evidence of absence; the solver must report the actual flow even if the thresholds fail.

## Clinical limits and claims requiring another study

Computationally checkable claims include source/archive/member provenance, schema and item labels, joins, time ordering, cohort flow, phenotype construction, split integrity, feature whitelist, endpoint labeling, model fitting, held-out predictions, CIF/Brier/calibration, uncertainty, overlap, and falsification outputs.

MIMIC-IV does not provide adjudicated circulatory stabilization, complete urine collection, bedside microcirculatory state, clinician intent, treatment indication, fluid balance reliability, radiographic lung status, a definitive organ-failure diagnosis, or external validation. This study cannot establish:

- that a lag phenotype is true kidney or respiratory pathophysiology rather than a measurement/support proxy;
- that a fluid, vasopressor, diuretic, ventilator, or monitoring intervention would change the outcome;
- that ICU rescue was appropriate, preventable, or beneficial;
- that an ICU-discharge or ward-monitoring policy should be changed;
- net clinical utility, causal treatment effect, or transportability.

Those claims require expert chart adjudication, richer physiologic and treatment-intent data, an external cohort, or a prospective/interventional study.
