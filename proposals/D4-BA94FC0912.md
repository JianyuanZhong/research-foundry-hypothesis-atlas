# UKB CBC specificity versus generic pre-diagnostic observation

Status: proposed substantive child of `[prior hypothesis]`. No hypothesis test or full solver fit has been executed. The bounded audits below are availability checks only.

## Scientific opening and clinical decision

The parent proposal asks whether a higher-platelet/lower-haemoglobin CBC profile is relatively more associated with colorectal cancer (CRC) than haematologic cancer after a two-year delay. Its unresolved interpretive problem is broader: the contrast could be a CBC-related cancer-type pattern, or a generic pre-diagnostic observation signal caused by illness, care-seeking, repeated testing, registry ascertainment or coding. A CRC-only retrospective study reports pre-diagnostic CBC trajectories but calls the result investigational and in need of external validation [K1]. A recent review finds that blood-trend evidence is fragmented and explicitly identifies informative observation as a recurring bias [K2]. The clinically meaningful next decision is whether to prioritize cancer-type-specific external validation and measurement work, or to abandon that interpretation and study generic observation/indication bias first.

The strongest claim supported by inspected evidence is descriptive: CBC values or trajectories can precede and associate with some cancer outcomes in selected retrospective populations. The unresolved claim tested here is narrower and falsifiable:

> In UKB adults with a complete first CBC and no registry-recorded primary cancer at T0, the delayed 2–5-year higher-platelet/lower-haemoglobin profile will show a larger CRC-versus-haematologic contrast than its contrast for other primary cancers, while showing no comparable contrast for a prespecified inpatient injury negative-control outcome. If the profile behaves similarly for other primary cancers or for injury, the apparent CBC cancer-type contrast is more consistent with a generic pre-diagnostic observation/health-status signal than with CRC/haematologic specificity.

This is a temporally ordered descriptive association. It is not a causal, mechanistic, diagnostic-accuracy, screening, stage, treatment, referral-threshold or individual clinical-utility claim.

[K1] supports the 24-month pre-diagnostic CBC question but is CRC-only, Spanish and retrospective. [K2] bounds the field as exploratory and highlights informative observation. [K3] motivates explicit longitudinal/selection handling and a transparent-versus-learned comparison, but its EHR/genetic signatures are not a CBC cancer-specific result. This child adds a same-table cancer-family comparator and a prespecified non-neoplastic negative control, not cosmetic model complexity.

## Population and temporal boundaries

The primary population is each unique `eid` with parseable T0 and nonmissing instance-0 WBC, haemoglobin, RDW and platelets. Exclude any valid primary-cancer registry event on or before T0. Do not require T1 or T2 for the primary analysis.

Normalize ICD-10 strings without inventing missing codes. Define:

- CRC: C18–C20.
- Haematologic cancer: C81–C96.
- Other primary cancer comparator: C00–C97 excluding C18–C20, C81–C96 and secondary C77–C79.
- Secondary C77–C79 is excluded from the primary cancer classes and reported separately as an audit category.
- Negative control: inpatient S00–T88, from same-index `41270/`41280` pairs.

For the near-diagnostic secondary analysis, follow from T0 until the first CRC, haematologic cancer, other primary cancer, injury, death or administrative censoring at 2022-12-31, with a maximum of T0+2 years.

For the primary delayed analysis, enter risk at T0+2 only if alive and free of primary cancer through T0+2. Follow to T0+5, the first outcome, death, or 2022-12-31, whichever comes first. Events before or at T0+2 are reported and do not become delayed events. This is a conditional delayed-risk estimand, not an unconditional five-year risk. Registry cancer outcomes and inpatient injury outcomes are fitted separately; registry and inpatient dates are never pooled.

## Exposure profiles and estimands

Standardize T0 CBC variables using development-only means and scales. The fixed profiles are:

- g_CRC: platelets +1 SD, haemoglobin -1 SD, WBC and RDW at development means.
- g_HEME: platelets -1 SD, haemoglobin +1 SD, WBC and RDW at development means.

For each outcome c in {CRC, HEME, OTHER}, estimate cause-specific CBC contrasts q_c(w) = beta_platelet,c,w - beta_Hb,c,w in each window. Preserve the parent estimand D_CH(w) = q_CRC(w) - q_HEME(w); positive D_CH means the platelet/haemoglobin ordering is relatively more CRC-associated than haematologic-cancer-associated.

The new primary specificity estimand is profile-standardized:

`R_c(w) = P(c, g_CRC, w) - P(c, g_HEME, w)`

where P is the model-standardized cumulative incidence over the same risk set with observed age, sex, BMI, smoking, CRP and T0 year retained and only platelets/haemoglobin replaced. Report R_CRC, R_HEME, R_OTHER and all component risks. The primary specificity contrast is:

`I_CBC(w) = R_CRC(w) - R_OTHER(w)`.

A positive I_CBC means the fixed CBC ordering shifts absolute incidence toward CRC more than toward the comparator cancer family; a near-zero value means CRC and other primary cancers show the same CBC ordering, consistent with a generic cancer/observation signal. R_HEME and D_CH retain the parent’s class contrast.

For injury, use the same profiles and covariates with the same risk-set and delayed-entry rules in an inpatient injury-versus-death model. The negative-control contrast is `R_INJ(w) = P(injury, g_CRC, w) - P(injury, g_HEME, w)`. Treat ±0.5 percentage points as a descriptive negative-control tolerance: an interval wholly within this band is compatible with no measured profile contrast; an interval wholly outside it is a negative-control failure; a broad interval is inconclusive. This margin is not a clinical threshold.

For the delayed primary specificity result, report 95% participant-bootstrap intervals for I_CBC and each R_c. Use the parent’s descriptive ±1 percentage-point boundary for I_CBC only: above +1 pp supports a materially CRC-specific absolute contrast, below -1 pp is adverse, and an interval crossing that band is relative-only/inconclusive. This is a reporting boundary, not a referral or treatment rule.

## Prespecified comparator and falsification design

The other-primary-cancer family is the primary same-table comparator, not an optional post hoc subgroup. Fit the exact same cohort, predictors, time windows, competing-event definitions, fixed profiles, temporal split and uncertainty procedure for CRC, haematologic and OTHER. Do not select a cancer family after inspecting CBC coefficients.

The inpatient S00–T88 outcome is the prespecified negative-control outcome. It uses the same table, same participant key, same baseline CBC and covariates, same T0/T0+2/T0+5 boundaries and same temporal holdout, but its paired outcome comes from `41270/`41280`. A profile contrast for injury would indicate a generic health-state, healthcare-contact or observation component; it would not prove that cancer associations are entirely non-biological. A null injury contrast does not prove clinical indication is absent.

Additional locked falsifications are:

1. A 0–2-year versus 2–5-year comparison. A contrast confined to 0–2 years is near-diagnostic timing evidence, not delayed cancer-type specificity.
2. CRC/HEME/OTHER class-label permutation within T0-calendar-year and event-status strata, preserving dates and censoring. It should collapse I_CBC and D_CH toward zero; failure is an implementation or label-specificity warning.
3. The repeat-attendance indicator R=1: a complete T1 assessment strictly after T0 and within 2–8 years with all four instance-1 CBC fields observed. Report R=1 and R=0 estimates, fixed gap strata and a baseline-only stabilized inverse-probability weighting stress test. This is not a causal correction because symptoms, test indication and healthcare use are unavailable.
4. Registry versus inpatient source sensitivity for the three cancer families, using paired `41270/`41280` cancer codes restricted to C00–C97 as an ascertainment proxy. Keep sources side by side, never average them. Report paired-date displacement and crossing of T0+2/T0+5. Source disagreement is not proof that either source is correct.
5. T0 calendar-era interactions and missing/invalid-pair denominators. No post hoc favorable era or gap selection is allowed.

## Transparent baseline versus learned alternative

The alternatives answer the same scientific question; the learned model is not included merely to raise discrimination.

A. Transparent cause-specific Cox baseline. Fit separate cause-specific Cox models for CRC, HEME, OTHER and death, with the four T0 CBC values, age, sex, BMI, smoking, CRP and T0 year. Use other cancers and death as competing causes. Derive q_c, D_CH, model-standardized R_c and I_CBC. Advantages are auditable coefficients, an explicit class contrast and straightforward profile standardization. It can lose nonlinear platelet/haemoglobin effects, interactions and changing type-specific hazards.

B. Same-input learned alternative. Fit a CPU gradient-boosted discrete-time multi-task competing-risk model with yearly intervals and separate hazards for CRC, HEME, OTHER and death; fit the analogous injury/death outcome model for the negative control. Use exactly the same people, covariates, source-specific outcomes, delayed entry, windows, profiles, split and censoring rules as A. Only the functional form changes. This model can reveal nonlinear CBC ranges, CBC-by-age/sex interactions and time-varying hazards that an additive Cox model hides; it cannot reveal symptoms, indication, pathology, mechanism or source correctness.

Sort by T0 date: earliest 70% for development and latest 30% for locked temporal holdout. Fit standardization, missingness handling, tuning, calibration and selection weights only in development. On the holdout, compare target-specific calibration intercept/slope, 2- and 5-year cumulative-incidence Brier and log scores, fixed-profile I_CBC/R_c/R_INJ and participant-bootstrap uncertainty. C-index/AUC is secondary and cannot establish specificity. Use 500 participant bootstrap replicates, preserving failed fits and reporting the seed.

Sparse-event rules are fixed before fitting: if an outcome has fewer than 50 events in the temporal holdout, do not claim learned-model superiority or a stable outcome-specific calibration result; if fewer than 20 events are available in development, report the contrast as unstable/inconclusive and do not tune that outcome’s learned head. If any primary CRC/HEME/OTHER outcome or injury negative-control outcome fails the relevant event-support rule, report the overall specificity conclusion as inconclusive rather than dropping the outcome or changing the window.

## Supportive, adverse and inconclusive results

Supportive results require all of the following in the delayed registry analysis: I_CBC is positive with its 95% interval above 0 and preferably above +1 pp for the decision-facing label; D_CH retains the positive parent direction; R_CRC exceeds R_OTHER rather than both showing the same contrast; the signal is not confined to 0–2 years, R=1, one gap or one era; the injury negative control is compatible with its tolerance; label permutation collapses; and registry/inpatient source comparisons do not reverse the interpretation. This supports a cancer-family-specific descriptive association worthy of external, indication-rich validation. It does not justify clinical action.

Adverse results are a delayed I_CBC at or near zero with a similarly positive R_OTHER, a clear injury contrast, permutation failure, disappearance after excluding 0–2 years, a repeat-attendance/weighting shift, or a registry/inpatient reversal. These favor a generic pre-diagnostic observation or health-state interpretation and should redirect work toward order dates, indication, symptoms and care pathways; they do not refute any general CBC-cancer association.

Inconclusive results include fewer than 50 holdout events, fewer than 20 development events, nonconvergence, invalid same-index pairing, positivity failure, source overlap too sparse for displacement, or intervals broad enough to include both no meaningful specificity and the ±1 pp boundary. An imprecise null does not refute specificity.

Computationally checkable outputs are cohort counts, exact paired-field integrity, code-family assignment, event timing, D_CH, q_c, R_c, I_CBC, R_INJ, permutation behavior, source displacement, selection sensitivity, calibration, Brier/log scores and bootstrap intervals. Clinical adjudication remains required for whether cancer codes/dates reflect true incident cancer, symptoms, test indication, primary-care contact, pathology, stage, treatment, transfusion/iron context, healthcare utilization, and whether any absolute-risk contrast is useful in symptomatic practice. A prospective external cohort with lab order dates and indications, expert incident-cancer adjudication, and a decision-impact study would be required for diagnostic or clinical-utility claims.

## Deferred alternatives and revisit evidence

- Deferred state-space/GP or transformer CBC trajectory models. The specificity question can be answered with first-CBC inputs and exact cancer-family controls; only a small number of broadly complete repeated CBC landmarks are available, and a latent trajectory adds assumptions without recovering symptoms or indication. Revisit only if T1/T2 completeness and delayed event support are audited and the scientific question becomes trajectory-specific.
- Deferred genetic or multimodal models. The required genetic/imaging inputs are absent or in separate namespaces/payloads; adding them would be a different question. Revisit with a documented crosswalk and clinically adjudicated outcomes.
- Deferred ApoB/albumin/HCC and pancreatic directions from the parent. They do not test CBC cancer-type specificity; the pancreatic branch is sparse in the parent audit. Revisit only if the CBC specificity direction is abandoned or a separate liver/pancreas question is selected with its own outcome support.
- Rejected future-attendance predictors, pooled registry/inpatient dates and cross-namespace joins. These violate temporal or measurement validity rather than offering useful alternatives.

The learned alternative is selected because it can reveal nonlinear and time-varying information that the transparent model loses while preserving the same clinical estimand. It will not be preferred for a small ranking gain: if it improves ranking without calibrated absolute risks or without changing I_CBC/R_INJ interpretation, the scientific result remains the baseline descriptive conclusion.
