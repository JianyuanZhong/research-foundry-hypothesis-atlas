# Observation-qualified estimands for strict-prior assay content and HCC recorded return

## Decision-relevant question and substantive advance

This is a substantive child of `[prior hypothesis]`. The incumbent correctly preserves the strict completed-prior comparison of assay content against capture intensity and the discharge-landmarked 30-day subsequent local admission endpoint. Its remaining observation-process problem is not merely that return timing is coarse: a 30-day zero is a mixture of (i) a patient who is locally observed again after the window and has no recorded return in the window and (ii) a patient with no later local encounter, for whom the release cannot distinguish continued absence, outside care, or death. The incumbent reports that mixture descriptively, but does not make it an estimand with a model-based decomposition.

This child makes that ambiguity operational while retaining the incumbent primary endpoint. It asks whether the apparent assay-content increment is information about a subsequent local return, information about the hospital's later ability to observe the patient, or both. The new scientific deliverable is a frozen administrative observation ledger and a held-out decomposition of the same 30-day label into three exhaustive, explicitly nonclinical states:

- **R:** a same-patient local encounter begins in [td, td+30 days);
- **N+_L:** no such local encounter, followed by at least one same-patient local encounter in [td+30 days, td+L);
- **N-_L:** no local encounter in either interval through td+L.

Here td is the native discharge time and L is 90 days for the primary observation-process analysis, with 60 and 180 days as prespecified nested sensitivities when administratively eligible. N+_L means later local re-observation, not complete follow-up or proof of a true no-return. N-_L is an explicitly unresolved local-observation state, not survival, cure, outside-care absence, or a competing clinical event.

The primary estimand remains the incumbent-compatible 30-day recorded-return risk and the held-out E-versus-Q proper-score increment for R versus not-R. The new estimands are:

1. the held-out proper-score increment for the three-state \\(Z_L\\in\\{R,N+_L,N-_L\\}\\);
2. the state-specific E-versus-Q increments for R and N+_L, with calibration;
3. the observation-process interaction, namely whether the E-versus-Q increment for R differs from the increment for N+_L and whether the 30-day binary result changes materially after the zero is decomposed;
4. the nested-window curve \\(L\\in\\{60,90,180\\}\\), reported only in the corresponding administratively eligible population.

These quantities are directly identifiable from native local encounter rows. They do not identify unrecorded outside returns or mortality. The advance is therefore a valid observation-process decomposition of the local recorded-care estimand, not a claim to recover a clinical readmission endpoint.

## Supported evidence, unresolved claim and falsification

The available evidence supports only that the HCC release contains reproducible native encounter admission/discharge times, that a local recorded-return label can be defined, and that strict completed-prior assay content can be separated from source-capture intensity. The incumbent support audit found 42,681 administratively complete 30-day anchors and 4,913 recorded returns; among 30-day nonreturns, 6,325 had a later local encounter in [30,90) and 8,257 in [30,180). It also found 82.41% of recorded returns in [14,30). These are feasibility observations, not model results, and the incumbent's 42,688/4,916 ledger must still be reconciled.

The unresolved claim tested here is:

> Among first locally recorded adult HCC-keyed inpatient discharges, strict prior assay content improves prediction of the 30-day local recorded-return state beyond strict prior capture intensity; after decomposing 30-day nonreturns by later local re-observation, a genuine return-information increment will be larger for R than for N+_L and will remain directionally stable across L=60,90,180, whereas an observation-intensity explanation will concentrate the increment in N+_L or make the binary E-versus-Q increment unstable across L.

This hypothesis is falsifiable. A process-only Q model matching E on R and N+_L, an E increment confined to N+_L, a reversal across observation windows, or a material loss of calibration is adverse to the assay-content interpretation. A stable R-specific increment with attenuation of the N+_L increment supports the narrower claim that assay content contains information about this local recorded-return distribution beyond capture intensity. A stable N+_L increment is still scientifically informative: it says the assay history predicts future local observability, not necessarily return risk.

## Population, landmark and exact outcome rules

Use HCC snapshot `[source checksum]` and catalog [source checksum]. All source files are ordinary read-only CSVs; no archive member is used.

Normalize Unicode and whitespace in the two key fields. Join `diagnoses` to `encounters` exactly on (`Patient Master Index`, `Encounter Number`). A record-coded HCC key is a normalized `Diagnosis Name` containing `hepatocellular carcinoma` or case-insensitive `hepatocellular carcinoma`. Publish the exact regex, matched diagnosis strings, diagnosis-type distribution, duplicate keys and unmatched keys. This is not proof of active HCC, and diagnosis rows have no native time and are never predictors.

Require age >=18, parseable native `Admission time` and `Discharge time`, nonnegative stay, and anchor admission in [2011-01-01, 2026-01-01). For each patient retain the earliest eligible HCC-keyed encounter sorted by native `Admission time`, then `Encounter ID); do not replace an invalid earliest record with a later encounter. Set t0=`Admission time` and td=`Discharge time`. The primary prediction landmark is td, but all assay/capture history remains frozen before t0. The only discharge-context sensitivity may add td-t0 and discharge calendar/time-of-day; it may not add anchor-linked rows because release/availability timestamps are absent.

For every anchor with td+30 days <= 2026-01-01 00:00, define R=1 if any other encounter for the same normalized `Patient Master Index` has parseable native `Admission Time` in [td,td+30 days), requiring candidate admission >=td. Overlapping index rows are not returns. Use the earliest qualifying admission for timing; order equal timestamps by `Encounter Number`, report ties, and retain the deterministic rule. R=0 otherwise. The primary zero means no qualifying local row, not survival or complete follow-up.

For L in {60,90,180}, require td+L <= 2026-01-01 00:00 for the corresponding observation-process risk set. Define O_L=1 if any other same-patient encounter has native admission in [td+30 days,td+L), regardless of R; otherwise O_L=0. Define Z_L=R if R=1, N+_L if R=0 and O_L=1, and N-_L if R=0 and O_L=0. A later encounter after day 30 is never used as a predictor, never relabels R, and never proves that the patient was under complete observation. If multiple later encounters exist, retain the earliest for the ledger and report multiplicity. Exact boundaries are half-open: day 30 belongs to the observation interval, day L does not.

The primary observation-process target is L=90 because the existing audit indicates adequate administrative support; L=60 and L=180 are nested sensitivities, not replacements for the primary 30-day estimand. Report risk-set counts, exclusions, follow-up boundary dates, R/O cross-tabulations, and the three-state prevalence for every L. The 180-day population is expected to be smaller and may be inconclusive if sparse.

Define three explicit estimands on the L-eligible population. Let \\(p^M_{i,Y}\\) be a model's binary R probability and \\(p^M_{i,Z_L}\\) its three-state vector, with M in {Q,E}. With lower-is-better loss \\(\\ell\\):

- \\(\\Delta_Y=E[\\ell(p^E_Y,R)-\\ell(p^Q_Y,R)]\\) on all 30-day eligible anchors;
- \\(\\Delta_{Z,L}=E[\\ell(p^E_{Z_L},Z_L)-\\ell(p^Q_{Z_L},Z_L)]\\) on L-eligible anchors;
- \\(\\Delta_{s,L}=E[\\ell(p^E_s,1\{Z_L=s\})-\\ell(p^Q_s,1\{Z_L=s\})]\\) for s=R and N+_L, using one-vs-state log loss/Brier only as a decomposition, not as a replacement for the proper vector score.

Also report the observation-qualified conditional contrasts \\(\\Delta_{Z,L}^{O=1}\\) and \\(\\Delta_{Z,L}^{O=0}\\) by evaluating locked predictions in the O_L strata. These are descriptive conditional estimands, not causal effects, because O_L is future and can be affected by the same latent process as R. Standardize the two stratum results back to the L-eligible population using the observed O_L proportions; the standardized result must equal \\(\\Delta_{Z,L}\\) up to numerical tolerance. This equality is an auditable decomposition, not an adjustment for unobserved care.

## Strict information sets and source-specific availability

All models use identical people, labels, feature manifest, preprocessing, split and calibration protocol. Exclude direct identifiers `Name`, `ID number`, `Mobile phone number`, `Medical insurance/encounter card number` and `Hospitalization number`.

A contains admission age, sex, department, calendar era, native admission time-of-day and missingness. A_d additionally contains only native LOS td-t0 and discharge calendar/time-of-day.

Q contains no assay identity or result. It contains strictly prior capture/process summaries: completed prior-encounter count and duration; active days; time since last completed encounter; source-specific row counts and distinct linked-encounter counts for labs, procedures, orders, medications and examinations; observed source-type count; missing/excluded-time counts; left-truncation and no-history indicators. No medication name/dose/route/frequency/status, procedure name, order name, or examination text enters Q.

E adds to Q assay-specific laboratory content: `Test` identity, `Qualitative Result`, valid assay-specific `Quantitative Result`, `Specimen Type`, recency, span, count, missingness and within-assay change/slope only when at least two distinct valid `Test Time` values exist. Numeric values are never pooled across assays because HCC has no lab unit column.

The primary history window is [t0-730 days,t0). A source row is eligible only if its (`Patient Master Index`,`Encounter Number`) links to a non-anchor encounter for the same patient, that encounter has parseable native admission/discharge with linked discharge <t0, and the source-specific native availability time is parseable and in the window:

- labs: `Test time`;
- procedures: `Start time`;
- orders: `order time`, never the later `start time`;
- medications: `Start time`;
- examinations: `start time`.

Exclude anchor-linked rows even when their event time precedes t0. Publish counts of missing/invalid availability, missing linked discharge, open/overlapping linked stays, anchor-linked rows, post-t0 rows, duplicates and row inflation. Timestamp-only and anchor-linked histories remain sensitivity branches, never primary evidence.

## Matched baseline and substantive alternative

The transparent baseline is fit-only standardized regularized multinomial logistic regression:

- B_Q^Y/B_E^Y for binary R;
- B_Q^Z/B_E^Z for the three states Z_L;
- B_Q^T/B_E^T for the incumbent four-state timing endpoint [0,7), [7,14), [14,30), no recorded return, retained for direct continuity.

Use the same Q or E feature matrix, patient split, regularization selection and calibration in each paired comparison. The primary question is the E-versus-Q increment, with A and A_d reported as context-only reference models.

The substantive alternative is a matched discrete-time multistate observation model H_Q/H_E. It creates person-state intervals from td:

1. no return at risk for [0,7), [7,14), [14,30);
2. if no R by day 30, at risk for first later local observation in [30,L).

Fit complementary-log-log cause-specific hazards with interval offsets, an absorbing R state, and a later-observation transition after day 30. Use the same Q/E vectors, same fit/selection/evaluation patients, same preprocessing and same calibration data as B. Predeclare only interval intercepts, E-by-interval terms and a small ridge/weakly-informative penalty; select penalty on fit/selection buckets, never on locked outcomes. Convert H hazards into coherent probabilities for R, N+_L and N-_L. The model is scientifically useful because it can distinguish an E increment in the return hazard from an increment in the later-observation hazard; the single multinomial baseline cannot show that temporal transition decomposition. It is not selected to chase AUROC.

Completion requires reporting B and H for Q and E on identical splits and feature rows, with both the 30-day binary output and Z_L output. The H-derived R probability must be scored against the same R label and the H-derived three-state probabilities against the same Z_L label. A noncoherent probability sum, interval hazard outside [0,1], or mismatch between derived R and the sum of R states is a computational failure.

An irregular latent sequence model is retained as a deferrable alternative only if the primary multistate analysis finishes within the solver envelope and history support is adequate. It is not selected now: the unresolved question is separation of return from later observation, which H exposes directly with native encounter transitions; a latent encoder would add representation assumptions before resolving the observation estimand. A neural/document/pathology branch is deferred because clinical documents and pathology have no native time and no validated extraction, not because neural methods are categorically excluded. Revisit it only if timestamped release logs or validated semantic labels become available.

## Falsification, observation audit and gates

Mandatory falsifications and audits are:

1. Shuffle complete strict E histories across patients within each split while preserving Q, source counts and outcome labels; E-Q return and Z_L increments should attenuate.
2. Permute lab payload within assay and partition while preserving assay identity, times and counts; measured-content increments should attenuate.
3. Permute assay identity while preserving values, times and counts; assay-specific signal should attenuate.
4. Shuffle strict event times within patient/source while preserving payload and counts; timing-sensitive increments should attenuate.
5. Fit H_Q. If H_Q reproduces the E result for both R and Z_L, the assay-content interpretation is rejected even if prediction is good.
6. Fit labels permuted within calendar era and department; persistent performance indicates leakage or label artifact.
7. Re-run every state under exact 30-day and L-day boundaries, equal admission timestamps, overlapping stays, earliest-anchor choice and invalid-time exclusions. Material changes are inconclusive.
8. Verify no row at/after t0, anchor-linked primary-history row, current-anchor row, post-discharge row, future-window feature, outcome-derived O_L feature, or patient split leakage.
9. Publish duplicate-key, multiplicity, unmatched-key and row-inflation audits for each two-column join. Reconcile the incumbent 42,688/4,916 ledger with the independent 42,681/4,913 audit before confirmatory interpretation.
10. Verify the algebraic decomposition: the all-state Z_L score, O_L-stratum scores weighted by observed O_L proportions, and H-derived state probability sums agree within declared numerical tolerance.
11. Repeat the observation state with L=60,90,180. A conclusion that changes sign or is driven by one window is observation-process-sensitive and not confirmatory.
12. Keep O_L out of all predictors. Any model using later local encounters to predict R is leakage.

Supportive evidence requires all of: a favorable, calibrated E-Q increment for the primary R outcome; favorable Z_90 proper-score evidence or a reproducible R-versus-N+_L state contrast; attenuation under content/history permutation; no process-only reproduction; no leakage or ledger discrepancy; and directionally stable decomposition across at least two administratively supported L values. This supports only an incremental-information claim about local recorded care and, if the state contrast is present, that the increment is more return-specific than later-observation-specific.

Adverse evidence is a null or harmful R increment, calibration deterioration, Q-only reproduction, E signal confined to N+_L, permutation persistence, or any leakage. Inconclusive evidence includes unresolved anchor counts, sparse N+ or N- states, dominant invalid-time burden, wide clustered intervals, unstable L-window results, major boundary sensitivity, or inability to reconcile the ledger. A positive Z_L result with a null binary result means the assay history predicts the observation-qualified composition without improving any-return prediction; report it as observation-process information, not as a clinical return-risk result.

The HCC files cannot establish active disease, cirrhosis, stage, tumor burden, liver reserve, indication, planned versus unplanned status, admission cause, treatment receipt/completion, death, outside-hospital care, complete follow-up, benefit, safety, clinical utility or external validity. The N-_L state is explicitly unresolved. Clinical adjudication, timestamp/release audit logs, death and outside-care linkage, imaging/pathology with native dates, stage/resectability and treatment-intent labels, prospective external validation, and a causal or utility study would be required for stronger clinical decisions. MIMIC, eICU and UKB remain directly accessible but are not external validation cohorts for this HCC design.
