# Provisional successor hypothesis: does hypotension duration add information beyond nadir for subsequent AKI?

## Proposed relationship
Among adults in a qualifying eICU ICU stay with at least one valid systolic blood-pressure measurement below 65 mmHg during ICU time 0–24 hours, greater cumulative time below 65 mmHg is associated with higher risk of new or worsening acute kidney injury during the subsequent 24–72 hours, after accounting for baseline illness severity, prior vasopressor exposure, and hospital. The duration signal is expected to add reproducible information beyond the admission nadir alone.

This is an association hypothesis, not a causal claim that lowering blood pressure causes AKI or that 65 mmHg is a treatment target.

## Unresolved question and clinical importance
A single nadir can be transient or artifact-prone, whereas sustained hypotension may represent a different exposure burden. The unresolved claim is whether duration and trajectory add clinically meaningful information about subsequent AKI beyond the nadir, rather than merely reflecting severity, treatment response, or measurement intensity. Resolving this could inform whether early AKI risk assessment should use duration/trajectory rather than a single minimum value.

## Evidence currently supporting the opening
A perioperative study used time-weighted MAP <65 mmHg as a reference hypotension burden and found that waveform morphology did not improve postoperative AKI prediction beyond that burden [K1]. An ICU AKI review describes AKI as heterogeneous and dynamic, with creatinine-centered diagnosis necessary but insufficient and urine-output and hemodynamic context important [K2]. A longitudinal EHR prediction study shows that temporal information can improve prediction while variable sampling rates and heterogeneous data types create a methodological challenge [K3]. These works motivate the proposed test but do not establish the eICU duration-versus-nadir association. No computed eICU effect estimate is claimed here.

## Population, timing, exposure, and outcome
Qualifying population: adults (`age >=18`) in a single qualifying ICU admission with valid ICU start time and at least one valid systolic BP <65 mmHg in the first 24 hours. Exclude records lacking the required admission identifier or with impossible/ambiguous timestamps.

Primary exposure window: ICU time 0–24 hours. Derive valid systolic BP from both vital tables, cumulative minutes below 65 mmHg, nadir, and measurement counts. Use piecewise-linear interpolation between valid measurements, with no extrapolation beyond the first/last observed measurement. Flag or exclude physiologically implausible values using a prespecified range. Sensitivity analyses use no interpolation, a shorter interpolation gap, and adjustment for measurement count/observation intensity.

Primary outcome window: 24–72 hours, after the exposure window is complete. Define baseline as the last valid creatinine before or at ICU time 0 when available; otherwise use the first creatinine in a prespecified pre-exposure window, with missingness reported rather than silently imputed as normal. Primary outcome is new or worsening AKI by KDIGO-compatible creatinine criteria: creatinine increase >=0.3 mg/dL within 48 hours from baseline, or >=1.5 times baseline if the rise is documented within the outcome window. Use the earliest qualifying event and retain time to event. Secondary urine-output AKI is included only if item semantics, weight, and timing are verified; otherwise report it as unavailable rather than substitute a proxy.

## Baseline and substantive alternative
The simple baseline is a prespecified hospital-stratified logistic model using nadir systolic BP in 0–24 hours, age, sex, baseline creatinine, and available baseline severity/vasopressor context. The substantive time-aware alternative adds cumulative hypotension minutes, time below threshold in prespecified intervals, last BP before window end, and a simple recovery/trajectory feature, while retaining nadir. The alternative can reveal whether sustained exposure, recovery after the nadir, or observation density carries information that a single minimum discards. It is scientifically useful only if those features are stable and not merely proxies for measurement intensity or treatment; otherwise the baseline remains preferred.

Evaluate both models on the same rows, with a hospital-held-out split and the same outcome definition and uncertainty procedure. Report duration effect estimates and confidence intervals, calibration, and hospital-held-out discrimination as secondary descriptive outputs. Compare direction and uncertainty across hospitals. A complex sequence model is deferred because the available data cannot distinguish hypotension-mediated kidney injury from severity, treatment, and observation artifacts; revisit it only if a bounded diagnostic shows that trajectory information is materially unavailable to the simpler representation.

## Falsification and interpretation
The hypothesis is supported if duration adds reproducible information beyond nadir with a prespecified direction and uncertainty, and the result is not explained solely by observation density or a single hospital. It is adverse if duration adds no information, reverses after prespecified adjustment, or is confined to artifact-prone measurements. It is inconclusive if AKI ascertainment, timestamp validity, or sampling is too sparse to estimate the comparison reliably. Any causal interpretation, treatment threshold, or mechanism requires additional clinical adjudication, richer treatment/etiology data, and prospective or quasi-experimental evidence.

## Missing evidence and limitations
The exact creatinine item semantics, urine-output semantics, and clinical adjudication of AKI are not assumed established by this provisional draft. The parent’s source-scan evidence supports field availability, not clinical validity. No full study result has been computed. Interface interruptions, discharge/transfer, end-of-life treatment limitations, reasons for missing measurements, and treatment/etiology details require explicit handling or additional evidence.

## Scientific deliverable
The actual deliverable is a newly fitted baseline and time-aware analysis with a frozen cohort, exposure/outcome timing, uncertainty, hospital-held-out evaluation, and interpretation tied to computed outputs. This provisional draft records the design and its remaining evidence gaps; it is not a claim of completion.
