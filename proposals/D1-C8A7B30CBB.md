# Early lactate trajectory and short-term mortality across ICU admission types

## Why this question, and what it is not

Recent MIMIC candidates repeatedly turn a reasonable documentation question into a large retrospective routing protocol: many masked reader panels, compatible latent states, bespoke workload ledgers, and release gates. Those designs are often repairable as software specifications but do not yet answer a clinical question, and they repeatedly risk treating stored text agreement as clinical truth or a retrospective route as a safe intervention. The present proposal deliberately uses a smaller, directly computable observational experiment.

The clinical question is whether the *change in blood lactate during the first six ICU hours* adds reproducible prognostic information beyond the initial lactate and readily available admission covariates, and whether that incremental signal is consistent across ICU admission types. This matters because clinicians must decide whether a persistently abnormal early trajectory should prompt closer reassessment, escalation of monitoring, or search for reversible causes. A useful answer would distinguish a marker that merely restates initial severity from a time-updated marker that identifies a different-risk subgroup.

The hypothesis is prespecified and falsifiable:

> Among adult MIMIC-IV admissions with an ICU stay and a first eligible blood lactate of 2.0 mmol/L or greater, a lactate measured 2–6 hours later is associated with 28-day mortality after adjustment for the first lactate, baseline age/sex/admission characteristics, initial creatinine, initial mean arterial pressure, and early mechanical ventilation/vasopressor indicators; the incremental discrimination and calibration are present in both medical and surgical/other ICU strata, but the magnitude is not assumed to be identical.

This is a prognostic association, not a treatment effect. It does not test whether clearing lactate causes survival, whether a lactate-guided protocol improves care, or whether any alert should be deployed. Existing research and the demonstrations establish that longitudinal EHR trajectories can be predictive; they do not establish that a simple six-hour lactate change is incrementally useful under this exact MIMIC population, measurement process, and ICU-type heterogeneity. The substantive advance is an interpretable, transportability-oriented test of incremental value with a fixed missingness estimand, rather than another high-capacity trajectory model.

## Population and temporal construction

1. Start with all admissions in `hosp/admissions` having a matching ICU stay in `icu/icustays`.
2. Keep adults with `anchor_age >= 18` and a first ICU stay with nonmissing `intime` and `outtime`.
3. The index episode is the first ICU stay chronologically within each `(subject_id, hadm_id)`, with ties broken by smallest `stay_id`. Do not combine separate hospital admissions.
4. Define eligible lactate `L0` as the first blood-lactate result with `charttime` in `[intime, intime+6h]`. To make the hypothesis clinically interpretable, require `L0 >= 2.0 mmol/L`; this threshold is fixed before any outcome analysis and is a cohort definition, not a treatment threshold.
5. Define `L1` as the first eligible blood lactate with `charttime` in `(L0_time+2h, L0_time+6h]` and before `outtime`. If several results share the earliest eligible time, use their median. The primary complete-case estimand requires L1; the missing-L1 group remains in the denominator for a prespecified availability analysis and is never coded as normal.
6. Define the exposure as `delta6 = L1 - L0` and a categorical trajectory: decrease by at least 20%, decrease less than 20%/stable, or increase by at least 20%, where the percent change is `(L1-L0)/L0`. The primary model uses continuous `delta6` per 1 mmol/L and a restricted cubic spline with knots fixed at the 5th, 50th, and 95th percentiles of the training sample only. The categorical analysis is secondary and descriptive.
7. Baseline covariates are age, sex, admission type, admission location, first lactate, first creatinine in `[intime,intime+6h]` from `hosp/labevents` item 50912 or 52546 with blood/chemistry dictionary confirmation, first mean arterial pressure from the frozen `chartevents` item list, and early vasopressor exposure if computable as above. Each covariate has an explicit missing category or missingness indicator; no imputation may use data after `L1` except the exposure itself.
8. The outcome is all-cause death by 28 days after `intime`, using the earliest of `patients.dod` and `admissions.deathtime` when present, bounded by an administrative end-of-follow-up defined as `intime + 28 days`. If death date is unavailable, `hospital_expire_flag` is not treated as 28-day death; it is used only as a separate in-hospital mortality sensitivity outcome. A death occurring on the boundary is counted; date-only deidentification uncertainty is reported.

## Analysis and baselines

The primary analysis is an age/sex/admission-type/initial-severity-adjusted logistic regression for 28-day mortality among complete L0/L1 cases. Report adjusted odds ratio per 1 mmol/L increase in `delta6`, 95% confidence interval, calibration slope/intercept, Brier score, and area under the ROC curve. Confidence intervals use bootstrap resampling at the subject level (1,000 fixed-seed resamples) because a subject may have multiple admissions, while the primary population keeps the admission as the unit. A sensitivity analysis keeps one admission per subject (earliest eligible) to test dependence.

Compare, using identical splits and missingness handling:

* Baseline model: age, sex, admission type/location, first lactate, creatinine, first MAP, and computable vasopressor indicator.
* Incremental model: baseline model plus `delta6`.
* Null temporal control: baseline model plus the time interval between L0 and L1, with delta6 permuted within ICU type and baseline-lactate decile using a predeclared random seed. This tests whether apparent gain is merely a measurement-intensity or timing artifact.
* Simple clinical baseline: first lactate alone, to quantify how much of the association is already present at presentation.

Use a chronological split by ICU `intime`: earliest 70% for model development, next 15% for threshold/calibration selection, latest 15% as locked test data. No tuning, item selection, unit pooling, or threshold adjustment may use locked-test outcomes. Report complete-case results and inverse-probability-of-observation sensitivity results where observation probability is estimated from baseline variables only. Do not claim missingness is random.

The primary incremental criterion is prespecified as all three of the following on the locked test set: (a) likelihood-ratio test for adding delta6 with two-sided alpha 0.05, (b) absolute AUROC increase of at least 0.02 with bootstrap interval, and (c) improved or non-inferior calibration-in-the-large, defined as absolute change no worse than 0.05. These are utility gates for this experiment, not validated safety thresholds. Heterogeneity across exact first-careunit strata is summarized with an interaction and stratum-specific estimates; no claim of universal transportability follows from one MIMIC center.

## Falsification and interpretation

The experiment is falsified or failed computationally if the lactate item inventory changes after outcome access, a timestamp leaks beyond `L1`, duplicate handling is nondeterministic, the chronological split overlaps, the outcome uses post-28-day information, or the null temporal control gains as much as the true delta6 model. A negative control using the change in a prespecified non-lactate chemistry analyte with comparable measurement frequency (creatinine, when available) is not expected to be null clinically; it is included only as a measurement-process comparison and is not interpreted as a causal falsification test.

*Supportive result:* delta6 has a directionally coherent association, the incremental model meets all three locked-test criteria, calibration is not degraded, and the gain is not confined to one exact ICU unit. This supports the narrow claim that early lactate trajectory contains incremental prognostic information in this MIMIC population. It does not show that lactate-guided treatment improves outcomes.
*Adverse result:* delta6 is null, unstable across ICU units, fails calibration, or the null timing permutation performs similarly. This supports the clinically useful conclusion that an observed six-hour change should not be assumed to add reliable prognostic value beyond the initial lactate and baseline data in this setting; it does not prove lactate is useless or that a different interval has no value.
*Inconclusive result:* L1 availability is low or strongly selected, confidence intervals are wide, unit strata are sparse, or the complete-case and observation-weighted estimates disagree materially. The result is then a measurement-process limitation, not evidence for or against the hypothesis; external multicenter data with protocolized serial lactates are required.

MIMIC cannot adjudicate clinician intent, treatment indication, lactate assay comparability across time, fluid resuscitation, source control, actual bedside reassessment, outpatient death after missing linkage, or clinical benefit. The experiment therefore cannot establish causality, treatment efficacy, safety, workflow feasibility, or a live alert threshold. Those claims require a prospective protocolized validation with treatment, fluid/vasopressor dose, assay timing, clinician action, and patient-centered outcomes.
