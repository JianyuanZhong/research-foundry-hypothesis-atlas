# Endpoint-sharpened urine-output trajectory proposal

## Scientific opening and substantive repair

The parent asks whether a prespecified 12-hour urine-output trajectory adds held-out information for a first observed ICU deterioration event beyond current state, treatment exposure, and documentation intensity. The available evidence supports only that urine output is an imperfect, treatment- and measurement-sensitive observable: creatinine-based estimates can overestimate kidney function during prolonged critical illness [K1], urine output can predict observed CRRT initiation without establishing indication or benefit [K2], and AKI classification can depend on measurement strategy and observation duration [K3]. It does not establish that a urine trajectory is physiologic deterioration.

The substantive child repair is endpoint validity. The parent’s “first mutually exclusive event” and censoring at ICU outtime do not make two consequences explicit: death must be a competing event rather than an event that may be confused with discharge/transfer censoring, and a procedure already ongoing at the landmark is not a new initiation. This child defines those boundaries using fields already present in MIMIC-IV 3.1. The repair changes the estimand and can change cumulative-incidence estimates; it is not a cosmetic mapping edit.

## Falsifiable hypothesis and rival explanations

Among adults at ICU-hour 12, a prespecified 12-hour urine-output trajectory adds held-out information for a first observed deterioration event in the next 12 hours beyond current state, explicit treatment exposure, and documentation intensity. The leading rival is that the apparent trajectory signal is generated by diuretic/fluid/vasopressor response, selective charting, or initiation timing rather than a physiologic process. The experiment is predictive association, not a causal treatment effect or mechanism.

The primary estimand is the 12-hour cumulative incidence of the first event in (L,L+12 hours], where L = icustays.intime + 12 hours, among new RRT initiation, new invasive-ventilation initiation, or death. These are competing observed events, not adjudicated deterioration. A stay ending before the first event is administratively censored only for non-death discharge/transfer; death is retained as a competing event when its time is known. If multiple event types occur at the same timestamp, retain the first by the prespecified order RRT, invasive ventilation, death, and report the tie count.

## Population, temporal boundaries, and exact endpoint construction

Use MIMIC-IV 3.1 adult ICU stays with valid age >=18 (patients.anchor_age + anchor_year_group, with the documented age-91 convention), ICU duration >=24 hours, and no qualifying event at or before L. Predictors use only information available at or before L. The outcome window is (L,L+12 hours].

For procedure initiation, use procedureevents.starttime as the event time, require a valid stay/event interval, and count only a row whose starttime is strictly after L and no later than L+12 hours. If a procedure interval has starttime <= L and endtime > L, it is ongoing at the landmark and is not a new event; it may be used as a baseline treatment/state feature but cannot be counted as initiation. Repeated rows for the same procedure are collapsed to the earliest qualifying starttime per stay and event type. Exclude procedure rows with invalid or contradictory intervals and report the exclusion rule and count.

Use RRT procedure itemids 225441, 225802, 225803, 225809, and 225955, and invasive ventilation itemid 225792. Exclude non-invasive ventilation itemid 225794. Use admissions.deathtime and hospital_expire_flag for death; do not infer death from a missing procedure row. If deathtime is present in the outcome window, death is the competing event at that timestamp. If ICU outtime occurs before L+12 hours and there is no death or qualifying procedure initiation, censor at outtime as administrative non-death discharge/transfer. Report component-specific risks and the competing-risk cumulative incidence; do not treat repeated procedure rows as independent observations.

This endpoint is observed initiation. Clinical indication, physiologic deterioration, fluid-balance validity, and treatment benefit require expert adjudication or another study.

## Baseline and substantive alternative

B0 is regularized logistic prediction using current/last urine value, current vital signs, demographics, admission type, care unit, and preceding treatment summaries. B1 adds documentation intensity. B2 adds explicit fluid, diuretic, vasopressor exposure summaries and timing. T1 is a prespecified hourly urine trajectory/persistence model with slope, recovery, and explicit missingness, compared with B2 on the same landmark, split, endpoint, and uncertainty procedure. T1 can reveal ordered decline/recovery and persistence that latest-value summaries lose; this is the substantive scientific contrast, not a small predictive-score contest. A neural sequence encoder is deferred unless T1 is feasible and the artifact-adjusted contrast remains unresolved.

Use a subject-level train/validation/test split, with no subject appearing in more than one split. Fit preprocessing, imputation, regularization, and any model selection inside training folds only. Use repeated subject-level bootstrap or repeated splits for uncertainty. Report AUROC, AUPRC, calibration intercept/slope, Brier score, and decision-curve/net benefit at prespecified alert thresholds. Report component-specific cumulative incidence and the competing-risk estimand, not only binary discrimination.

Run trajectory-shuffling and pre-landmark charting-gap negative controls. Supportive results require held-out incremental value beyond B2, stable calibration, persistence in high-coverage strata, and no negative-control reproduction. Adverse results include no incremental gain, gain confined to low coverage, disappearance after treatment/documentation adjustment, or negative-control reproduction. Inconclusive results include inadequate eligible landmarks, sparse urine coverage, unstable mapping, or wide uncertainty; they do not refute the hypothesis.
