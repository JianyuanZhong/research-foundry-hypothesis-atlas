# Time-weighted early hypotension beyond minimum MAP for later observed kidney injury

## Episode-2 successor and decision question

This is a substantive successor to the assessed eICU early-hypotension candidate `[prior hypothesis]`. It retains that candidate's clinically important question but makes the estimand more explicit: exposure is completed before a six-hour landmark, follow-up begins only after that landmark, the primary outcome is explicitly an observed creatinine-rise phenotype, and post-exposure laboratory observation is modeled as an ascertainment process rather than silently treated as normal. The design also requires a transparent density-adjusted and hospital-held-out test.

The clinical decision is whether an early risk summary for deciding who merits intensified kidney surveillance should include time-weighted hypotension burden in addition to the lowest recorded MAP. This is a risk-stratification question, not a recommendation to target MAP 65 or to give a particular vasopressor.

### Falsifiable hypothesis

Among adult eICU unit stays that remain in the ICU through minute 360, have adequate noninvasive MAP observation in minutes 0–360, and have a valid baseline creatinine in that window, greater time-weighted MAP-below-65 burden in minutes 0–360 is associated with a higher probability of a first later observed creatinine rise of at least 0.3 mg/dL in minutes 360–2,880, conditional on minimum MAP and prespecified baseline clinical information. Adding duration to a minimum-MAP model will improve calibration and discrimination in hospitals held out from model fitting.

The falsifiable null is no residual association and no reproducible held-out improvement after the prespecified observation-density, hospital, and temporal-leakage checks. The claim being tested is not that hypotension causes AKI, that correcting it prevents injury, or that 65 mmHg is a treatment threshold.

## What is established and what is unresolved

The strongest available evidence here is structural: the frozen eICU snapshot contains ICU-relative patient-stay offsets, noninvasive MAP observations, creatinine-labeled laboratory observations, discharge status/offsets, hospital identifiers, and infusion descriptors. The local schema audit in `work/episode2_eicu_schema_audit.md` verifies the headers and join/time fields. The eICU seed proposes the clinical idea, and current literature makes dynamic ICU kidney-risk prediction plausible, but neither establishes this conditional association in this snapshot nor establishes a causal treatment effect.

The unresolved claim is narrower and clinically useful: whether duration/depth of early recorded hypotension contains stable information about a later recorded creatinine rise that minimum MAP alone misses, or whether any apparent advantage is mainly a consequence of recording density, hospital practice, treatment feedback, or leakage. Resolving this would inform whether a duration feature is worth carrying into monitoring-risk tools or trial-enrichment designs; a negative result would argue against replacing a simpler minimum-MAP summary.

The substantive advance over a basic association is the separation of minimum from duration/depth, strict temporal ordering, competing discharge/death, an explicit unknown-outcome state, hospital transport testing, and falsification of measurement-process artifacts.

- At least one valid baseline serum creatinine in `0 <= labresultoffset <= 360`.

Report exclusions and the numbers discharged/dead before the landmark; these are not controls.

Exposure is fully defined in minutes 0–360. Outcome follow-up begins at minute 360 and ends at the earliest of ICU discharge, ICU death, minute 2,880, or the last usable creatinine observation. For every eligible stay, classify the follow-up state as first observed creatinine rise, death/discharge before a qualifying observed test, follow-up creatinine without the rise, or no post-landmark creatinine (unknown ascertainment). No missing follow-up creatinine is coded as no injury.

## Exposure construction

Within each stay, sort valid primary `vitalAperiodic.noninvasivemean` in minutes 0–360.

- Minimum MAP is the minimum valid value.
- An observation at (t_i) carries forward to the next observed time only if the gap is at most 30 minutes; never bridge a longer gap. Clip the accepted interval to 0–360.
- Duration is the sum of accepted minutes with carried MAP <65 mmHg.
- Depth burden is (sum_i max(0,65-MAP_i)Delta t_i), in mmHg-minutes, as a secondary exposure.
- Retain MAP count, covered minutes, fraction covered, median and maximum accepted gap, fraction covered below 65, and minimum MAP. These are both diagnostics and candidate measurement-process covariates.
- Primary inference uses minimum MAP and duration as continuous restricted cubic splines with knots fixed before outcome inspection. A prespecified linear-logistic version in clinically interpretable units is the transparent baseline. Do not select threshold or functional form from the best result.

Sensitivity analyses use thresholds 60 and 70 mmHg, accepted gaps of 15 and 60 minutes, at least six observations, robust minimum MAP (e.g., lower quantile rather than one extreme), and `vitalPeriodic.pamean` or `systemicmean` alone when available. If the duration signal appears only with sparse long-gap carry-forward or one interface, classify it as measurement dependence.

## Outcome construction and clinical-label limits

Baseline creatinine is the earliest valid creatinine in minutes 0–360, tie-broken by `labid). The primary outcome is the first later creatinine in (360 < labresultoffset <= 2880) with value at least 0.3 mg/dL above that baseline. Report maximum rise, number/timing of tests, and whether the follow-up test was before discharge/death.

This is an observed creatinine-rise phenotype, not complete KDIGO AKI. The eICU snapshot does not reliably supply pre-ICU baseline kidney function, complete urine-output semantics, dialysis indications, nephrotoxin/contrast exposure, or clinician adjudication. Baseline AKI may already be present and a creatinine rise may have multiple causes.

The primary predictive estimand is the conditional risk of this observed event among eligible stays, with a missing-observation state retained. Fit a discrete-time cause-specific model for observed event over 6–48 hours; ICU discharge and death before a qualifying test are competing absorbing events, and absence of a post-landmark test is explicitly censored/unknown. Report a complete-observation estimate among stays with a post-landmark creatinine and an inverse-probability-of-observation weighted estimate using only pre-landmark predictors: baseline creatinine, early MAP summaries/density, demographics, admission source, unit type, hospital, and early lab count. Report weight positivity and diagnostics. Weighting addresses observed ascertainment only; it cannot repair unmeasured clinical reasons for testing.

## Baselines, analysis, and uncertainty

The prespecified nested comparison is:

- B0: age, gender, ethnicity, admission source, unit type, baseline creatinine, pre-landmark heart-rate/temperature/respiration summaries when present, and hospital fixed effect or hospital-stratified intercept.
- B1: B0 plus minimum MAP.
- B2: B1 plus early hypotension duration. The hypothesis is B2 versus B1.
- B3: B1 plus depth burden as a separate secondary exposure, not a post-hoc replacement.

Do not include future labs, discharge variables as predictors, APACHE first-24-hour aggregates, or post-exposure vasopressor rates. A second covariate set adds only pre-landmark diagnosis/admission-diagnosis indicators. Report infusion presence and pre-exposure vasoactive status descriptively and as a prespecified interaction/sensitivity, without causal treatment interpretation.

Use five grouped hospital folds, all stays from one hospital in one fold, with model fitting and spline/weight estimation confined to training hospitals. Report held-out-fold distributions of AUROC, area under precision-recall, Brier score, calibration intercept/slope, and the incremental differences B2–B1. Report adjusted duration association and 95% confidence intervals using hospital-cluster bootstrap or cluster-robust uncertainty; also report hospital-specific directions and event/unknown counts. Repeated stays must not cross a fold in the person-cluster sensitivity.

The result is supportive only if the duration association is directionally consistent across most held-out folds, the interval is compatible with a positive clinically interpretable association, and B2 retains calibration with a reproducible improvement over B1 after density adjustment and observation weighting. A small predictive improvement is not by itself clinical utility; an external decision threshold, net benefit/cost analysis, clinician review, and prospective impact study would still be needed.

## Falsification criteria

The computational hypothesis is falsified or downgraded to unsupported if:

1. The adjusted duration association is null or reverses across prespecified gap, threshold, MAP-modality, density-adjusted, and hospital-held-out analyses, with uncertainty excluding the prespecified positive direction.
2. B2 fails to improve held-out calibration/Brier/discrimination over B1, or improvement disappears outside resubstitution.
3. The apparent signal is limited to one hospital, one MAP interface, sparse observations, long-gap carry-forward, or the complete-follow-up subset and disappears under observation weighting.
4. Permuting duration within hospital, minimum-MAP strata, and coverage/density strata does not eliminate the incremental signal; this indicates leakage, residual structure, or a coding error.
5. A temporal negative control in which post-landmark burden is used to predict a creatinine change wholly within minutes 0–360 is comparably strong; that would indicate temporal leakage or selection/confounding.
6. Inclusion of the explicitly labeled APACHE leakage variables creates the signal while the clean design does not; the claim is then not supported.
7. There is inadequate overlap of duration at a given minimum MAP, unstable observation weights, too few events, or too few hospitals for grouped validation; the correct conclusion is inconclusive, not a relaxed design.

## Meaning of results

Supportive results establish only that time-weighted early recorded hypotension is a reproducible prognostic marker for a later observed creatinine-rise phenotype beyond minimum recorded MAP in this eICU snapshot. They justify external validation or enrichment research, not a treatment target or causal kidney-injury claim.

Adverse results provide useful negative evidence: minimum MAP may be sufficient for this operational outcome, or the added duration measure may not transport once density, hospital, and ascertainment are controlled. They should not be converted into evidence that hypotension is harmless.

Inconclusive results arise from sparse MAP, missing follow-up creatinine, poor duration/minimum-MAP overlap, weight nonpositivity, ambiguous creatinine units/labels, or unstable hospital-fold estimates. These motivate a better cohort or prospective data collection, not selective exclusion or threshold/model changes.

The computation can check source integrity, cohort counts, time ordering, exposure formulas, missingness/competing-event accounting, nested-model performance, uncertainty, and falsification tests. It cannot establish complete KDIGO AKI, chronic kidney status, blood-pressure artifact validity, treatment intent, causal effects, or clinical benefit. Stronger conclusions require expert adjudication of BP artifacts and kidney injury, pre-ICU kidney data, richer medication/fluid/urine/RRT records, external validation, and ultimately a prospective or quasi-experimental study.
