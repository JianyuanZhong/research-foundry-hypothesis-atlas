# Provisional hypothesis: reduced laboratory monitoring during persistent instability

Status: provisional and untested. This is a design draft, not a result.

## Proposed relationship

Among adults in a continuous ICU stay who remain physiologically unstable at a prespecified index, a reduction in laboratory-testing intensity during the preceding 12 hours is associated with a higher risk of a clinically defined deterioration in the following 12 hours, beyond contemporaneous measured severity and recent support. The association is expected to attenuate when end-of-life care-plan documentation and discharge/transfer context are handled explicitly; a residual association in held-out hospitals would be a monitoring-process signal, not proof that changing laboratory orders causes deterioration.

## Evidence-supported versus unresolved claims

The available eICU catalog and table metadata support only that the dataset contains longitudinal, timestamped laboratory, vital, respiratory, infusion, patient, and hospital records, with `patientunitstayid` as the within-dataset join key and `hospitalid` for hospital-level stratification. The expert seed supplies the opening that testing frequency and missingness may encode hospital practice and may transport poorly. It does not establish the proposed association. No source-row audit or analysis has been completed in this draft.

The unresolved claim is whether a time-local reduction in testing identifies a monitoring-process signal for subsequent deterioration among patients who are still unstable, rather than merely reflecting recovery, impending transition, end-of-life care, documentation interruption, or unmeasured severity.

## Clinical importance

If supported, this could identify a subgroup and time window in which a monitoring-gap review might be clinically informative. It could also show that the apparent signal is explained by care transitions or end-of-life decisions, which would redirect interpretation and avoid an unsafe treatment implication. The study is observational and cannot establish that ordering more tests prevents deterioration.

## Preliminary falsifiable test

Restrict to adult continuous stays with valid 18-hour intervals and an index time after admission. Define exposure as a prespecified reduction in the number of distinct laboratory result timestamps over the prior 12 hours relative to the preceding 12 hours, with an explicit minimum observation requirement. Define outcome as a prespecified deterioration in the next 12 hours using time-local respiratory, hemodynamic, and infusion-support changes; treat death, discharge, and transfer as competing events rather than ordinary non-events. Compare a contemporaneous-state baseline using current severity, recent support, and hospital against a longitudinal model using the same inputs plus prior trajectories and monitoring history. Use hospital-held-out evaluation, negative-control time windows, and sensitivity analyses for sparse end-of-life documentation and discharge/transfer context.

Support would require a prespecified direction and uncertainty interval for the monitoring signal, attenuation under transition/end-of-life adjustment, and consistency in held-out hospitals. Adverse evidence would be no association, reversal, or a result fully explained by the rival process variables. Inconclusive evidence would include insufficient valid intervals, unstable outcome ascertainment, or wide intervals; it would not be interpreted as refutation.

## Exact data bindings still to verify

The draft intends to use eICU source tables identified in `datasets/eicu/README.md` and the table metadata JSON files, joined only within eICU by `patientunitstayid`, with `hospitalid` retained for stratification and held-out validation. Required fields include admission/discharge/transfer times, age/adult eligibility, timestamped laboratory results and analyte identifiers, vital signs, respiratory support, infusion/vasopressor records, and available care-plan or end-of-life documentation if present. The exact table IDs, archive members, field names, units, timestamp semantics, and row counts must be audited before analysis. No cross-dataset joins are permitted.

## Missing evidence and limitations

The three inspected key-reference receipts required for final delivery are not yet attached. Clinical adjudication of deterioration, intent-to-limit care, and whether a monitoring gap reflects clinical judgment rather than true absence of testing is not available automatically. The source-row audit, final outcome definition, and feasibility counts are pending. A future solver must report the fitted baseline and longitudinal estimates, uncertainty, held-out-hospital performance, negative controls, and conclusions tied to computed outputs; it must not claim causal benefit or clinical truth from QA alone.
