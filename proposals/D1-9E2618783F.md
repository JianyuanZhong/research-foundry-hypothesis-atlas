## 1. Scientific question (unchanged from v1)

**Hypothesis.** In ICU patients who develop AKI, a fall in serum creatinine without a
matching recovery of urine output ("discordant recovery") identifies patients who remain
at higher risk of (a) in-hospital death and (b) persistent AKI at ICU discharge than
patients with concordant recovery (creatinine fall AND urine-output recovery).

**Why this matters.** Creatinine-based recovery is the default bedside signal that "the
kidneys have recovered." Haines et al. [K1] show that creatinine systematically
overestimates true GFR after prolonged critical illness because muscle wasting lowers
creatinine generation independent of filtration. If a falling creatinine can reflect
muscle loss rather than filtration, then creatinine-only recovery is a potentially
misleading marker, and urine output — a direct readout of filtration — should carry
independent prognostic information. The unresolved question is whether, in a large
real-world ICU cohort, the creatinine/urine-output discordance at a fixed post-peak
window predicts worse downstream outcomes after adjustment for severity.

**Leading explanation vs. rival.**
- Leading: discordant recovery reflects persistent low GFR (true kidney dysfunction)
  that creatinine masks → higher mortality/persistent AKI.
- Rival 1 (severity confounding): discordant patients are simply sicker (older, higher
  illness severity, more sepsis) and the association is fully explained by baseline
  severity.
- Rival 2 (measurement artifact): urine output recording is sparse/incomplete, so "no
  UO recovery" partly means "no UO recorded," and the association is a documentation
  artifact.
- Rival 3 (muscle-wasting direction): creatinine falls because of muscle loss (Haines
  mechanism), so the association is mediated by catabolism rather than kidney function.

Design features that separate these: severity adjustment tests Rival 1; a
documentation-sensitivity analysis (require ≥12h of UO entries in the window) tests
Rival 2; the persistent-AKI-at-discharge outcome (also creatinine-based) partially
cross-checks Rival 3 (if discordance were pure muscle wasting, discharge creatinine
should be *low*, not high — a discordant group with high discharge creatinine supports
the kidney-dysfunction reading).

**What answering would change.** If discordant recovery is an independent risk marker, a
simple bedside rule (creatinine trend + hourly UO in the 48–72h post-peak window) could
flag patients for closer renal monitoring, earlier nephrology involvement, or more
cautious nephrotoxin use — without any new biomarker. If the association vanishes after
severity adjustment or under documentation sensitivity, the seed's premise is refuted
and creatinine-based recovery remains adequate.

## 3. Population (v2, with measured feasibility)

**Inclusion (primary, lab-defined AKI):**
1. Adult ICU stay: `icustays.los >= 2` (46,337 of 94,458 stays — measured).
2. ≥1 serum creatinine in `chartevents` (itemid ∈ {220615, 229761}) with non-null
   `valuenum`, charttime within [intime, outtime]. (91,250 stays have ≥1 creatinine —
   measured.)
3. **Baseline creatinine** available: a creatinine value with charttime in
   [intime, intime + 48h]. If no pre-ICU baseline is available in this admission, use
   the earliest in-stay value as baseline and flag `baseline_source='in_stay'`
   (sensitivity S7 restricts to `baseline_source='early_window'`).
4. **AKI event** (KDIGO-style, creatinine criterion only — UO is reserved for the
   recovery phenotype, so the AKI definition is creatinine-based to keep the phenotype
   orthogonal to the index event):
   - `sCr_peak >= baseline + 0.3 mg/dL` at some charttime within 48h of baseline, OR
   - `sCr_peak >= 1.5 × baseline` within 7 days of baseline.
   - `sCr_peak` = maximum valuenum in [baseline_time, baseline_time + 7d].
   - Require `baseline > 0.4 mg/dL` (physiologic floor).
5. **Fallback AKI definition** (if early-window baseline availability is poor, to be
   measured in the solver's feasibility step): `sCr_peak >= 1.5 mg/dL` with in-stay
   baseline = earliest value. Primary definition is preferred if ≥60% of AKI-eligible
   stays have an early-window baseline — **unverified**.
6. **Recovery window must fit in the stay:** `outtime >= t0 + 72h` (t0 = peak time).
   Stays ending before the window closes are excluded from the primary analysis and
   reported in a sensitivity with last-available values.
7. **UO data in window:** ≥1 urine-output entry (itemid ∈ {226559, 226560, 226561,
   226567} in **outputevents**) with charttime in [t0+24h, t0+72h] and value ≥ 0.
   Stays with no UO entries in the window go to a separate "no UO data" group and are
   excluded from the primary 3-group analysis (documentation gap); tested in S3.

**Exclusions:**
- `outtime − intime < 48h`.
- No creatinine (rule 2).
- No weight (itemid 226512) in chartevents for the stay → default weight 70 kg with a
  `weight_defaulted` flag. Weight availability: 87,392/94,458 stays overall (measured);
  fill rate within AKI stays **unverified**.
- Pediatric (anchor_age < 18 at admission).
- One row per (subject_id, hadm_id, stay_id); primary analysis restricted to the
  **first ICU stay per subject_id** (by icustays.intime); sensitivity S4 includes all
  stays.

**Expected N (v2, measured anchors):** 91,250 stays have creatinine; 91,510 stays have
≥1 outputevents row (measured). The creatinine×UO overlap count is therefore ≈91k
(upper bound, exact intersection **unverified** — the v1 job that measured it was
cancelled before completion). AKI prevalence in ICU 20–30% of stays with creatinine
(literature prior, not measured here) → expect ~6–9k AKI stays, of which the
recovery-window + UO-in-window filters may leave ~2–5k classifiable stays.
**Feasibility risk:** if the classifiable N is < 1,500, the 3-group comparison is
underpowered and the design should be simplified (drop the 3rd group, widen the window).

## 4. Anchor time and windows (unchanged)

- **t0 = time of creatinine peak** (charttime of the maximum valuenum in
  [baseline_time, baseline_time + 7d]; ties broken by earliest charttime).
- **Recovery window:** W = [t0 + 24h, t0 + 72h] (48h). Rationale: 24h after peak allows
  the nadir to form; 72h matches the "day 3" recovery assessment used in the
  renal-recovery literature [K2, K3].
- **UO window:** same W, aligned with the creatinine window.
- **Follow-up:** persistent-AKI outcome starts at ICU discharge (icustays.outtime);
  in-hospital death from index admission (deathtime within the same hadm_id). The
  recovery phenotype is fully measured before outtime (rule 6), so no follow-up time is
  required to be classified (avoids immortal-time bias).

## 5. Phenotype definitions (exact formulas; unchanged)

Let `W = [t0+24h, t0+72h]`.

**Creatinine recovery (CR):**
- `sCr_end` = last non-null creatinine valuenum with charttime in W; if none, last
  value before outtime (flag `cre_end_imputed`).
- `CR_pct = (sCr_peak − sCr_end) / sCr_peak × 100`.
- **Creatinine-recovered** if `CR_pct >= 25` (sCr_end ≤ 0.75 × peak). Sensitivity S1
  varies the threshold over {20, 25, 30, 50} and "return to baseline"
  (sCr_end ≤ baseline × 1.25).

**Urine-output recovery (UOR):**
- Aggregate all UO entries in W (outputevents, itemid ∈ {226559, 226560, 226561,
  226567}, value ≥ 0): total_mL = Σ value.
- `window_hours` = number of distinct hours in W with ≥1 UO entry (documentation
  coverage).
- `weight_kg` = median of chartevents itemid 226512 values in the stay; if absent,
  70 kg (flag `weight_defaulted`).
- **Hourly UO rate** = total_mL / window_hours (mean hourly output over hours actually
  documented, not over the full 48h — avoids penalizing sparse documentation as
  anuria; Rival 2).
- **UO-recovered** if hourly rate ≥ 0.5 mL/kg/h (KDIGO oliguria threshold). Sensitivity
  S2 varies {0.3, 0.5, 0.7} mL/kg/h and drops the `window_hours >= 12` requirement.
  The `window_hours >= 12` guard (≥25% of the 48h window documented) is in the primary
  analysis.

**Three groups (primary):**
1. **Concordant recovery:** creatinine-recovered AND UO-recovered.
2. **Discordant recovery (creatinine-only):** creatinine-recovered AND NOT
   UO-recovered. *(the hypothesis group)*
3. **Non-recovery:** NOT creatinine-recovered (regardless of UO).

UO-recovered but NOT creatinine-recovered stays go to group 3 and are reported
separately (the "reverse discordant" group; the Haines muscle-wasting direction would
predict this group to have *better* outcomes — a directional cross-check).

**Edge cases:** sCr_end missing → non-recovery, flag `cre_end_missing`; all UO = 0 in W
→ UO-recovered = false; window_hours = 0 → excluded from primary (rule 7).

## 6. Outcomes (unchanged)

**Primary:** in-hospital death — `admissions.deathtime` non-null for the index hadm_id
(equivalently `hospital_expire_flag = 1`).

**Secondary:** persistent AKI at ICU discharge — creatinine in the last 24h of the ICU
stay (max valuenum with charttime in [outtime − 24h, outtime]) satisfies
`sCr_last24h >= 1.5 × baseline` OR `sCr_last24h >= baseline + 0.3`. Creatinine-based by
necessity (no measured GFR); secondary cross-check, not the primary endpoint.

**Tertiary (descriptive):** ICU LOS (outtime − t0), hospital LOS (dischtime −
admittime), discharge to non-home location (`discharge_location` category list to be

## 7. Analysis plan (v2)

**(a) Baseline comparison.** 3-group (4-row incl. reverse-discordant) table: age, sex,
computed severity score (below), AKI stage (peak/baseline ratio), baseline sCr, peak
sCr, t0 − intime, weight, UO window_hours, ICD comorbidities (diabetes E10–E14, heart
failure I50, sepsis A41.9/R65.2*, chronic kidney disease N18) from diagnoses_icd for
the index hadm_id. Continuous: mean±SD + Kruskal-Wallis; categorical: % + chi-square.

**(b) Primary model — unadjusted (baseline analysis).**
- Death: 3-group crude death rates; discordant vs concordant risk ratio with 95% CI
  (Wald).
- Persistent AKI: same, group rates.

**(c) Adjusted model (v2 change: computed severity score).**
Charted APACHE II (itemid 226743) and SOFA (itemid 227428) are present in d_items but
charted in only 14 and 6 of 94,458 stays respectively (measured this episode), so they
cannot serve as covariates. Instead, compute a **SAPS-lite / severity proxy** from
variables that are fully available:
- `age` (patients.anchor_age at admission),
- `baseline_sCr` and `AKI stage` (peak/baseline ratio binned 1–3),
- `t0 − intime` (hours to peak),
- `mechanical ventilation at t0` (procedureevents ventilator itemids — to be enumerated
  in the solver step; **unverified**),
- `vasopressor use before t0` (inputevents vasopressor itemids — to be enumerated;
  **unverified**),
- `ICD comorbidity load` (count of a fixed list from diagnoses_icd: diabetes, heart
  failure, CKD, sepsis, malignancy).
- Cox proportional hazards for in-hospital death (groups categorical, reference =
  concordant) with the above covariates; check PH assumption (Schoenfeld residuals); if
  violated, logistic model with time-to-discharge or landmark at 7 days.
- Logistic regression for persistent AKI at discharge, same covariates.
- Report HR/OR with 95% CI for discordant vs concordant and non-recovery vs concordant.
- **Interpretation note:** the computed score is a severity *proxy*, not a validated
  ICU severity score. Residual severity confounding (Rival 1) is therefore only partly
  controlled; the unadjusted-vs-adjusted gap (d) is a lower bound on confounding. This
  limitation is stated in the results rather than hidden.

**(d) Baseline-vs-adjusted comparison.** Report (b) and (c) side by side. The gap
quantifies how much of the raw association is explained by the measured severity
proxy. If the unadjusted HR is large but the adjusted HR collapses toward 1.0, the
hypothesis is refuted in its independent-risk form (Rival 1).

**(e) Sensitivity analyses (unchanged):**
- S1: creatinine-recovery threshold {20, 25, 30, 50}% and "return to baseline."
- S2: UO threshold {0.3, 0.5, 0.7} mL/kg/h; drop the `window_hours >= 12` requirement.
- S3: include "no UO data in window" stays as a 4th group (tests Rival 2).
- S4: all ICU stays (not just first per patient).
- S5: AKI defined by ICD codes (N17*, 585*) instead of lab-based.
- S6: exclude stays with RRT (procedureevents / ICD 39.95, 54.98* — itemids to be
  enumerated in solver step; **unverified**). RRT confounds UO (anuria on dialysis).
- S7: restrict to `baseline_source = 'early_window'`.

**(f) Multiplicity:** primary hypothesis is a single discordant-vs-concordant
comparison for the primary outcome; sensitivities are exploratory, labeled as such,
no multiplicity correction.

## 8. Falsification criteria (unchanged)

The hypothesis is **refuted** if, in the primary analysis (first ICU stay, lab-based
AKI, 48h recovery window, 0.5 mL/kg/h UO threshold, ≥12h UO documentation):
- **F1 (death):** adjusted HR for in-hospital death, discordant vs concordant, ≤ 1.0
  with 95% CI upper bound < 1.2, OR unadjusted and adjusted HRs both ≤ 1.2.
- **F2 (persistent AKI):** adjusted OR for persistent AKI at discharge, discordant vs
  concordant, ≤ 1.0 with 95% CI upper bound < 1.5.
- **F3 (confounding):** unadjusted HR/OR ≥ 1.5 but adjusted estimate crosses 1.0 →
  the raw association is explained by severity (Rival 1 confirmed; hypothesis refuted
  in its independent-risk form).
- **F4 (documentation):** in S3, the "no UO data" group has outcome rates intermediate
  between concordant and discordant, or indistinguishable from discordant → the
  association is plausibly a documentation artifact (Rival 2).

**Supportive result:** adjusted HR/OR for discordant vs concordant remains elevated
beyond the F1/F2 bounds with CIs excluding the refutation thresholds, stable across
S1–S7, and the reverse-discordant group shows the predicted (better) direction.
**Inconclusive result:** CIs spanning the refutation thresholds, or classifiable N
< 1,500 (underpowered); report as such without claiming support or refutation.

## 10. Method alternatives (recorded reasoning; unchanged)

- **Baseline (unadjusted group comparison)** vs **severity-adjusted Cox/logistic
  model**: the adjusted model is the substantive alternative — it tests Rival 1
  (severity confounding), which the unadjusted comparison cannot. The gap between them
  is itself a scientific output.
- **ML/trajectory alternatives (deferred, with reason):** a black-box model or a
  creatinine-trajectory mixed model would add no mechanistic insight for this
  3-group prognostic question, would be hard to falsify against F1–F4, and the
  tabular N (expected 2–5k) does not justify the complexity. A future extension could
  fit a creatinine-trajectory model to test whether the *shape* of the creatinine fall
  (not just the endpoint) carries additional information — deferred because it is a
  separate scientific question requiring a longer creatinine series.
