# Baseline cystatin-C/creatinine discordance as a protocol for confirmatory repeat kidney-function assessment

## Independent successor and unresolved clinical question

This is an independent successor to `[prior hypothesis]`, with that candidate as the required parent for lineage. The audit asks whether the UKB rectangular export can support a more decision-relevant endpoint than repeat biomarker status. It cannot presently do so: the catalog exposes many candidate health-outcome, follow-up, diagnosis, medication, mortality, and hospital-related field IDs, but the local metadata does not certify their field identities, coding, units, event-date semantics, ascertainment completeness, linkage provenance, or clinical adjudication. Field presence is therefore not endpoint identifiability.

The clinically consequential question remains testable at a protocol level: among adults with baseline creatinine-equation eGFR at least 60 who actually return for a complete qualifying repeat assessment, does baseline cystatin-C/creatinine discordance add reproducible information for prioritizing a confirmatory kidney-function assessment beyond age, sex, and baseline creatinine-equation eGFR? The observable target is repeat equation status and repeat equation disagreement, not CKD, kidney failure, mortality, a hospital event, treatment response, or benefit from testing. This is a falsifiable decision-support surrogate, not an action-effect estimate.

Existing evidence supports use of creatinine and, when appropriate, cystatin C or a combined equation to estimate filtration. It does not establish that this export's discordance measure improves a testing-prioritization rule, nor that such a rule improves patient outcomes. The substantive advance is an exact, source-bound test of incremental baseline-only ranking plus an explicit audit showing why an apparently available clinical-event extension is not currently identifiable. The proposal fails closed rather than converting unverified field IDs into diagnoses or outcomes.

## Decision interpretation and evidence limits

- **Supportive:** supports only baseline conditional ranking for a confirmatory repeat assessment in the selected complete-repeat cohort. It does not show that ordering a test changes treatment or outcomes.
- **Adverse:** reproducible worsening argues against incremental prioritization in this schedule boundary; it does not disprove cystatin C in other clinical uses.
- **Inconclusive:** uncertainty crossing the null, interval/repeat heterogeneity, or isolated equation/capacity improvement yields no threshold recommendation.
- **Neutral technical stop:** missing/contradictory field certificate, source/schema mismatch, unresolved units, coding, dates, assay comparability, equation test failure, or incomplete null refit; this is not biological evidence.

The available four-dataset export cannot support a decision-effect, CKD, mortality, hospital-event, diagnosis, medication-response, utility, cost-effectiveness, measured-GFR, or treatment-benefit claim without additional authoritative dictionary and linkage documentation, clinical adjudication, repeated clinical records, and—where relevant—prospective observation of testing decisions, harms, costs, preferences, and downstream outcomes. The verifier can check computation, provenance, leakage, uncertainty, privacy, and whether conclusions match outputs. It cannot establish clinical validity, adjudication, causal benefit, utility, or transportability.
