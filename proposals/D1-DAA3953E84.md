# Does a within-patient drop in clinical-panel lab-draw intensity predict ICU deterioration because it marks abandonment of a worsening patient, or because it marks recovery of an improving one?

## Unresolved question and clinical importance

ICU patients are monitored by repeated laboratory draws, and clinicians decide how often to draw.
[K1] Agniel 2018 showed, across 272 lab tests in 669,452 patients, that the *presence and timing* of a
test order — not only its value — carries survival signal (order presence significant in 233/272
tests; order timing beat the value in 118/174). Lab-draw intensity therefore encodes a care process,
not only physiology. [K3] the WICH-ICU stepped-wedge cluster randomised trial (NCT07013175) is now
testing whether *reducing* routine ICU laboratory testing and arterial blood gas sampling is safe,
with 30-day mortality as the primary outcome and measured testing intensity per patient-day. The
decision is live: if a fall in a patient's own draw intensity prospectively signals deterioration,
de-implementation that lowers testing could delay recognition in the patients who most need it; if the
association is only a severity/indication/recovery fingerprint, reducing testing should be safe.
[K2] a scoping review of de-implementation of low-value care documents that such strategies are widely
promoted while patient-level safety evidence remains thin and outcome reporting is inconsistent —
so the direction of the safety question is genuinely unsettled. The discriminating piece currently
missing is a **within-person test that separates the competing explanations at the patient level**
rather than only adjusting for current severity.

The parent's own probe found a **non-monotone (inverted-U)** relation between `logR` and (24,48]h
mortality and made the shape the tested claim (RCS 3 df). But an exposure-response shape alone does
not distinguish the leading explanation (H1, a patient-level monitoring signal) from its strongest
rival: **confounding by indication** — clinicians draw less when the patient is clearly *improving*
(a low-mortality state) and less when the patient is clearly *dying / comfort-care* (a high-mortality
state). Episode 18 converts that rival from an adjustment into a **positive, directional test**.

## What episode 18 changes (the scientific revision)

- Retain the volume-normalised clinical-panel draw-rate ratio
  `logR_panel = log((d2_panel + 0.5)/(d1_panel + 0.5))`, where `d1_panel` counts pre-specified
  clinical-panel lab rows with `0 < labresultoffset <= 720` and `d2_panel` counts
  `720 < labresultoffset <= 1440` (panel families resolved from the exact `lab.csv.labname` strings).
- **New primary estimand**: the interaction between `logR_panel` and a pre-specified
  **physiological-trajectory stratum** (`improving` vs `not_improving`), where the stratum is defined
  by the sign of a standardised composite of hour-12-minus-admission changes in available
  physiological variables (MAP=`systemicmean`, heart rate, respiration, sao2, and the panel lab
  values). The interaction is estimated inside a hospital random-intercept + logR random-slope
  multilevel logistic model, alongside the RCS(3 df) main-effect term.
- This is a genuine scientific revision: it changes the estimand from "is the shape non-monotone" to
  "in *which* patients does the drop carry hazard", which the closest inspected work does not resolve
  for this multi-hospital cohort.

## Leading claim vs strongest rival (directional predictions)

- **H1 (patient-level monitoring/abandonment signal)**: the harmful association of de-escalation
  concentrates in the `not_improving` stratum; interaction OR > 1 with a CI excluding 1, main-effect
  OR > 1 in `not_improving`, and an `improving`-stratum OR whose CI includes 1.
- **R1 (confounding by indication / recovery)**: de-escalation simply tracks improvement; the
  association is confined to the `improving` stratum, or the interaction is null/≤1.
- **R3 (disposition/EOL artifact)**: the association persists only among stays with a comfort-care
  plan or pre-outcome ICU discharge (`carePlanEOL`, `unitdischargelocation`) and disappears once those
  are excluded.

## Design (frozen)

- **Data**: eICU-CRD 2.0, read-only, under `datasets/eicu/`. Join key `patientunitstayid` in every
  table; hospital via `patient.hospitalid`. All offsets in **MINUTES**.
- **Population**: `patient.csv.gz` rows with numeric `age >= 16`, `unitdischargeoffset >= 1440`
  (in ICU at the hour-24 landmark), and decisive `unitdischargestatus in {Alive, Expired}`. Restrict
  the between-hospital component to `hospitalid` with >= 200 eligible stays. Parent-probe baseline:
  132,774 eligible stays, 208 hospitals, 1,860 events; the solver re-derives these.
- **Temporal boundaries**: exposure windows (0,720] and (720,1440]; landmark t = 1440 min; outcome
  window 1440 < `unitdischargeoffset` <= 2880.
- **Outcome (primary)**: `(24,48]h in-ICU mortality` = `unitdischargestatus == 'Expired'` AND
  `1440 < unitdischargeoffset <= 2880`. Observability rule: stays discharged alive before hour 24 are
  not in the risk set; stays discharged alive at/after hour 24 without an in-ICU death are censored
  non-events.
- **Adjustment**: `apachePatientResult.apachescore`/`acutephysiologyscore` and
  `predictedicumortality` for `apacheversion == 'IVa'`; hour-12 `vitalPeriodic` vitals
  (`systemicmean`, `systemicsystolic`, `heartrate`, `respiration`, `sao2`) and hour-12 panel-lab values.
  Binding correction inherited from parent: `vitalPeriodic` has no `meanbp`; the MAP field is
  `systemicmean` (`meanbp` exists only in `apacheApsVar`).

## Baseline vs substantive alternative (method exploration)

- **Baseline**: fixed-effect logistic regression on hour-24 severity (APACHE IVa score / predicted ICU
  mortality) + linear `logR_panel`, no hospital structure, no interaction.
- **Substantive alternative**: a **within-patient draw-time point process** (inhomogeneous marked
  Poisson) whose time-varying intensity depends on the evolving physiology and hospital, yielding a
  *draw deficit* (observed minus expected draws given physiology) as an alternative exposure. This
  captures *when* draws stopped (the gap/hazard), which the count-ratio RCS discards, and tests
  whether the ordering **process** adds prospective signal beyond measured values.
- **Comparison**: ΔAUROC, calibration (slope, O:E), Brier score and decision-curve net benefit, with
  **leave-one-hospital-out (LOHO)** transport for both. Both must be reported; the alternative is
  chosen only if it changes the discriminating conclusion, not for a small AUROC gain.

## Falsification criteria

- **Supportive of H1**: interaction OR > 1 with 95% CI excluding 1, AND non-improving-stratum
  main-effect OR > 1 with CI excluding 1, AND improving-stratum OR CI including 1, AND the pattern
  surviving R3 exclusion.
- **Adverse to H1**: interaction ≤ 1, OR association confined to the `improving` stratum, or signal
  fully explained by R3.
- **Inconclusive**: interaction CI wide/covering 1, LOHO transport flips the sign, or calibration is
  inadequate — reported as inconclusive, not as confirmation.
