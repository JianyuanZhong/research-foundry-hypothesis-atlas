# Residual instability and nighttime ICU discharge: a transportable safety signal in eICU

## Proposal status, parentage and clinical decision

This episode-8 candidate is a substantive successor to the leading valid eICU transportability proposal \`[prior hypothesis]\`, with the assessed eICU seed-06 import \`[prior hypothesis]\` and seed-10 import \`[prior hypothesis]\` as scientific foundations. It preserves the prior line's strict time ordering, hospital-held-out validation, ascertainment accounting and noncausal interpretation, but asks a distinct ICU-disposition question.

The consequential decision is whether ICU discharge timing should be treated as potentially risky mainly for patients who still show residual physiologic instability, or whether an apparent nighttime-discharge association is similar across observed states. A finding that the association is concentrated in a measurable high-instability subgroup could motivate prospective review of discharge-readiness criteria and overnight monitoring. It would not by itself justify delaying discharge, changing staffing, or ranking hospitals.

The primary hypothesis is:

> Among adults discharged alive from an ICU, the association between nighttime ICU discharge and hospital death within the subsequent 48 hours is larger among stays with high recorded physiologic instability in the final six ICU hours than among stays with low recorded instability, and this interaction is directionally reproducible across hospitals after adjustment for observed discharge-state and admission context.

The null is no interaction, or an interaction that disappears after accounting for observation density, ICU length, hospital composition and held-out-hospital validation. This is an observational conditional-association and transportability hypothesis. It is not a causal effect of the clock time, an effect of staffing or bed pressure, or proof that delaying discharge would improve survival.

## What is supported before computation, what is unresolved, and the advance

The strongest evidence-supported claim before execution is structural and feasibility-related. The frozen eICU snapshot and full catalog expose ICU-stay identifiers, hospital identifiers, ICU-relative offsets, ICU and hospital discharge times/statuses, and timestamped structured vital observations. Raw headers inspected from the read-only files agree with the catalog. The relevant files have 200,859 patient data rows, 297,064 Apache-result rows, 25,075,074 aperiodic-vital rows and 146,671,642 periodic-vital rows (the preceding shell line counts included one header line). These facts establish data availability and scale; they do not establish a nighttime effect, a valid causal estimand, or complete clinical stability ascertainment.

The unresolved claim is exactly the interaction above: whether the recorded association between nighttime ICU discharge and later recorded hospital death differs by a prespecified, proximal, physiologic state measure and remains visible in hospitals held out from model fitting. The substantive advance over the prior renal/testing line is that the principal estimand is now a disposition-timing interaction with a fixed post-discharge outcome, rather than incremental prediction of a laboratory-observation phenotype. The advance over the untested seed is to replace vague “residual instability” with timestamped, density-qualified rules and to separate association from unavailable operational mechanisms.

## Population and temporal boundaries

Use eICU snapshot \`[source checksum]\` and catalog \`[internal dataset path]` (catalog SHA-256 \`[source checksum]\`). The eICU files are ordinary gzip CSV files; the archive member is the ordinary file and no nested member is assumed. Sources remain read-only and all derived audit/results files belong in the workspace.

The analytic unit is one \`patientunitstayid\`. Repeated ICU stays are retained; \`uniquepid\` is used only for a person-clustered sensitivity. Include:

- numeric adult age \`patient.age >= 18\`; report missing age and the eICU \`> 89\` convention;
- an ICU stay with \`unitdischargestatus = Alive\`;
- a valid \`unitdischargeoffset > 360\`, so the preceding six-hour state window exists;
- a parseable \`unitdischargetime24\`, with a discharge clock category defined below;
- valid coverage from at least two of the four primary vital streams during the final 360 ICU minutes. Require at least 60 minutes of carried-forward coverage and at least three raw observations per contributing stream. Low-density stays remain in the flow and are classified as unknown for the primary instability exposure rather than stable.

The exposure time is the recorded ICU discharge time. The pre-discharge state window is the half-open interval [\`unitdischargeoffset - 360\`, \`unitdischargeoffset\`) in ICU-relative minutes. No observation at or after \`unitdischargeoffset\` is permitted in an exposure. The outcome window begins strictly after ICU discharge and ends at ICU-discharge offset + 2,880 minutes, or earlier hospital discharge if that occurs first. The hospital calendar date and timezone are unavailable; \`unitdischargetime24\` is treated only as the hospital-local recorded clock time.

Define nighttime a priori as \`23:00:00\` through \`06:59:59\`, and daytime as \`07:00:00\` through \`22:59:59\`. A missing or malformed clock value is an unknown-exposure state. A sensitivity uses 22:00–06:59 versus 07:00–21:59; it is not allowed to replace the primary definition after inspecting results.

## Recorded outcome and ascertainment states

The primary outcome is hospital death within 48 hours after ICU discharge among ICU-alive discharges. Use \`patient.hospitaldischargestatus\` and \`patient.hospitaldischargeoffset\`, not an Apache predicted probability. An event requires \`hospitaldischargestatus = Expired\` and

\`0 < hospitaldischargeoffset - unitdischargeoffset <= 2880\`.

For the main cohort, assign each eligible stay one outcome state:

1. hospital death within 48 hours after ICU discharge;
2. documented hospital discharge alive by 48 hours;
3. documented hospital death after 48 hours, which is a known 48-hour non-event;
4. documented hospital discharge alive after 48 hours, which is also a known 48-hour non-event;
5. hospital disposition, status, or offset unavailable, hence unknown follow-up.

The analysis must report state counts by hospital, nighttime category and instability category. ICU discharge status is restricted to Alive, so an ICU death is outside the primary decision population and is reported in the eligibility flow. A hospital death after ICU discharge is a recorded administrative outcome, not a cause-of-death adjudication. Hospital-discharge location and status are useful descriptive strata; discharge location is not silently treated as a causal covariate.

## Residual-instability exposure

For each primary vital variable, retain finite values only within these audit rules: noninvasive/systemic/P.A. mean pressure 20–200 mmHg, heart rate 20–250/min, respiration 3–80/min and SpO2 50–100%. These are data-quality bounds, not treatment targets. Within the final 360-minute window, divide time into 30-minute bins. For each stream, carry its latest valid value forward for at most 30 minutes; no value is carried across a longer gap.

A bin is physiologically flagged if at least one available stream is abnormal: mean pressure <65 mmHg, heart rate >120/min, respiration >30/min or <8/min, or SpO2 <90%. The thresholds define an auditable recorded-state screen and must not be described as safe treatment targets. The primary high-instability exposure is at least 60 flagged covered minutes across the final six hours, with at least two flagged bins and at least 60 total covered minutes from at least two streams. Low instability is fewer than 60 flagged covered minutes under the same coverage requirement. If the coverage requirement is not met, instability is unknown; missing observations are never imputed as normal.

Report continuous flagged minutes, number of streams, raw observation counts, maximum gap, covered minutes and last-observation recency. Prespecified sensitivities use 15 and 120 flagged minutes, and a stream-specific definition requiring abnormality in two distinct streams. These sensitivities test measurement dependence; they do not select the most favorable cut point. A secondary model uses the separate vital summaries (minimum, maximum, last, median, interquartile range, slope and flagged duration) rather than a single composite, with all preprocessing learned inside training folds.

Laboratory values are not part of the primary exposure because exact label/unit coverage must be established first and testing is informative. If the audit resolves \`labname = creatinine\` and \`labname = lactate\` with consistent numeric \`labmeasurenamesystem\`/interface units, their final-six-hour last/value-change indicators are a secondary state block. Their absence does not change the primary cohort.

## Analysis plan and baselines

The primary association analysis estimates the hospital-standardized 48-hour hospital-death risk difference between nighttime and daytime discharge separately at low and high recorded instability, with the difference of those contrasts as the interaction estimand. Fit a prespecified logistic model with nighttime, instability, and their interaction; age, gender, ethnicity, hospital-admission source, unit type, unit-stay type, ICU length at discharge, pre-discharge vital coverage, and hospital fixed effects are adjustment variables. ICU length is known at the discharge decision but is a selection variable, so report a model without it as a sensitivity rather than hiding this choice. No hospital ID is used as a prediction feature.

The transparent baseline is an adjusted additive model with nighttime and the pre-discharge state summaries but no interaction; the primary model adds the prespecified interaction. The model-learning alternative is a bounded ridge logistic model (and, only if the event/overlap gate passes, a shallow gradient-boosted tree with a fixed depth and feature budget) using the same pre-discharge blocks. Compare:

- M0: admission context + nighttime indicator;
- Mstate: M0 + prespecified six-hour vital summaries, coverage and missingness;
- Minteraction: Mstate + nighttime × high-instability interaction;
- Mlearn: the bounded nonlinear model with the same allowed inputs.

Use five grouped hospital folds from a fixed hash of \`hospitalid\`; all stays from a hospital remain in one fold. Preprocessing, imputation, feature selection and tuning occur in training hospitals only. Hospital-held-out predictions are required for M0, Mstate and Minteraction; Mlearn is secondary and must not displace the transparent estimand. Evaluate Brier score, calibration intercept/slope, AUROC, precision-recall area, alert rate and sensitivity/false-negative rate at a sensitivity target selected from training-hospital predictions only. If a threshold cannot be transported because the target is infeasible or sparse, report gate failure instead of changing the target.

Report the interaction estimate and 95% intervals using a hospital-cluster bootstrap, not a row bootstrap. Report per-hospital low/high-stratum counts, risk differences, overlap, held-out calibration and direction. Add a leave-one-hospital-out analysis where event and exposure counts permit it, and a \`uniquepid\`-clustered sensitivity. Unknown-outcome states are retained in the flow. A complete-case sensitivity is labeled an ascertainable-record analysis, not the primary population analysis.

- adult, discharge-status, discharge-offset, discharge-follow-up and outcome-state flow by hospital;
- counts and event rates in each night × instability cell, the number of hospitals contributing to every cell, and hospital-held-out overlap;
- laboratory label/system/interface inventory and parse/plausibility exclusions before any optional lab predictor is enabled;
- the proportion of EOL records by timing and the proportion of unknown hospital follow-up;
- model convergence, regularization/tuning choices, effective sample size for any observation weighting, and held-out threshold feasibility.

Stop scientific interpretation as inconclusive if essential columns are absent, nighttime or instability cells have inadequate overlap, fewer than five hospitals contribute usable events, the high-instability exposure is dominated by one stream or one hospital, unknown follow-up dominates, or event counts cannot support an interaction interval. Do not rescue a failed gate by converting unknown to survival, broadening the night window, using post-discharge observations, adding Apache aggregates, or changing the endpoint.

## Falsification and interpretation

The primary interaction is weakened or falsified computationally if:

- the interaction estimate changes sign across hospitals or is driven by one hospital;
- the interaction disappears after coverage/gap adjustment, stream-specific definitions, the 15/120-minute sensitivities, or leave-one-hospital-out validation;
- nighttime appears harmful only in a random patient split but not in hospital-held-out predictions;
- the same result remains after permuting nighttime labels within hospital and ICU-length/seasonal proxy strata, or after permuting instability labels within coverage strata;
- a model using only observation density, last-observation recency and discharge timing performs as well as the physiologic-state model, indicating a measurement/selection explanation rather than a state-specific signal;
- using a deliberately earlier 12-hour state window produces an equal or stronger interaction while the proximal final-six-hour signal is absent;
- a label-permuted outcome or a pre-discharge pseudo-outcome produces the claimed interaction;
- post-discharge records enter the feature table, hospital outcome is used to define eligibility, or the result cannot be reproduced from the declared computed outputs;
- calibration, Brier score and hospital-specific risk differences do not support the claimed direction or intervals are implausibly narrow relative to hospital clustering.

Supportive results require all of the following: the prespecified high-instability × nighttime interaction has a consistent direction in several hospitals; its hospital-cluster interval excludes no interaction or the predeclared clinically meaningful margin if one is specified before fitting; low- and high-instability cell overlap is adequate; the direction persists in hospital-held-out predictions and measurement-density sensitivities; and the conclusion is tied to the computed risk-difference table and uncertainty. This would support a reproducible recorded association useful for prospective safety review, not a recommendation to delay ICU discharge.

Adverse results—no interaction, a reversed interaction, or a higher risk in daytime—are scientifically valuable and should be reported as evidence against the stated subgroup hypothesis. If Mlearn improves discrimination but worsens calibration, held-out false-negative rate or cross-hospital stability, that is a transport/safety trade-off, not a clinical improvement. If a signal is seen only in the density-only model or only after excluding unknowns, the likely conclusion is ascertainment/selection dependence.

Results are inconclusive when observation density, missing outcome, hospital overlap, or event scarcity prevents a stable interaction estimate; when EOL documentation, destination and discharge selection plausibly dominate and cannot be separated; or when a laboratory extension cannot pass its label/unit audit. Inconclusive does not mean the hypothesis is false.

## What this dataset cannot establish

Computationally checkable claims are: exact cohort flow; the recorded clock-time exposure; the recorded pre-discharge vital-state definition; the 48-hour hospital-disposition state; the adjusted interaction estimate; held-out calibration/discrimination; missingness sensitivity; hospital heterogeneity; and whether conclusions match the computed outputs and uncertainty.

This dataset cannot establish that nighttime discharge caused death, that delaying discharge would reduce death, that staffing, bed capacity, handoff quality, treatment limitation, or clinician intent caused the association, or that residual recorded instability equals expert-adjudicated physiologic instability. It lacks bed-capacity and staffing records, complete goals-of-care context, validated cause of death, raw waveforms, a reliable calendar date/timezone linkage, and prospective discharge-readiness adjudication. Expert ICU review is required to validate the instability screen and outcome interpretation; a prospective multi-hospital study or target-trial analysis with operational data is required for a causal discharge-timing claim. Hospital estimates must not be used as quality rankings.
