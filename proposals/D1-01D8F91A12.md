## Unresolved question and clinical importance

ICU clinicians decide how often to draw laboratory tests. [K1] showed the presence and timing of a test order
carries survival signal beyond the value (233/272 tests; timing better than the result in 118/174), so ordering
behaviour encodes the care process. The decision-facing, unresolved question is dynamic: when a patient is still
physiologically unstable at 24 h but the laboratory draw rate suddenly falls over the next day, does that mark a
true change in the patient's trajectory (deterioration going under-monitored, or recovery being de-escalated), or
is it mostly the treating hospital's ordering convention? [K3] is the sharpest rival: in a surgical ICU, ordering
was driven by order sets, consultant expectations and junior inexperience, and changing tests per patient-day left
ICU mortality and LOS stable. Resolving which explanation holds changes a consequential decision - whether a
drop in monitoring is a deployable patient-level warning signal, and whether an observation-process model trained
at one hospital can be trusted at another without recalibration.

## Hypotheses (falsifiable)

- H1 (patient-signal): in still-unstable stays, a >=50% fall in panel draw rate from (0,24] h to (24,48] h is
  associated with higher 3-day deterioration (death or new organ-support escalation) after adjustment for
  instability at 24 h, and the association is directionally consistent under leave-one-hospital-out (LOHO).
- R1 (severity/improvement confounding): the association is explained by residual physiology at 24 h (patients
  improving get less testing); after full adjustment for 24 h physiology and its change it is null.
- R2 (hospital fingerprint): the pooled association is a between-hospital artefact; after hospital fixed effects
  and random slope, the within-hospital effect is null and between-hospital variation in draw-rate change dominates.

## Population, temporal boundaries, variables

- Population: adult eICU stays, `patient.age` >= 16 (age is a string, '> 89' handled as 90), `unitdischargeoffset`
  >= 1440 min (P1: 132,900 of 200,859 stays, 208 hospitals; 138 hospitals with >=200 eligible stays covering
  127,253). Restrict the transport analysis to those 138 hospitals. Exclude stays ending (death/discharge) at or
  before 48 h so the exposure and outcome windows are observable.
- index time t0 = 1440 min after unit admission.
- Instability at t0 (entry): >=2 distinct analytes from the 8-analyte panel measured in (0,1440] AND at least one of
  {vasopressor infusion in (0,1440] using verified `infusionDrug.drugname` strings, mechanical ventilation,
  APACHE IVa predicted ICU mortality in the top tertile}. Exact drugname strings from P3.
- Exposure (primary): panel draw rate in (0,24] h vs (24,48] h, where panel = {creatinine, BUN, WBC x 1000, Hgb,
  platelets x 1000, sodium, potassium, chloride} (exact `labname` strings confirmed, P2); de-escalation = relative
  fall >= 50%. Pre-specified sensitivity grid: {33%, 50%, 66%} relative falls and absolute drop >=2 draws.
- Outcomes: primary = deterioration by 72 h = in-ICU death (`hospitaldischargestatus` == 'Expired' with
  `unitdischargeoffset` <= 4320) OR escalation of organ support in (48,72] (new vasopressor start or new
  ventilation). Secondary = in-hospital death. Both from `patient.hospitaldischargestatus`/`hospitaldischargeoffset`.

## Estimand, baselines, alternative, transport

- Estimand: hospital-clustered adjusted risk difference and OR for the 3-day deterioration outcome comparing
  de-escalation vs sustained monitoring, with 95% CI; plus a random-slope logistic (hospital intercept and slope)
  and the LOHO distribution of the effect size across the 138 hospitals.
- Baseline M1: instability + APACHE IVa (`apachePatientResult.predictedicumortality`) logistic, no monitoring
  features. Baseline M2 (value-only): last 24 h analyte values, no draw-count features.
- Learned alternative M3: gradient-boosted trees on value features + observation-process features (panel draw
  counts and their timing), same split, to test whether process information adds transportable discrimination -
  and what M2 loses (draw-rate dynamics carry no information in a value-only model by construction).
- Falsification: supportive if adjusted OR>1 with CI excluding 1 AND LOHO sign consistent in a pre-specified
  majority of hospitals with between-hospital slope variance small relative to the pooled effect. Refuted (R2) if
  the pooled association attenuates to null under hospital fixed effects / random slope or the LOHO distribution
  includes 0 with between-hospital variance dominating. Refuted (R1) if adjustment for 24 h physiology removes it.
  Inconclusive if events per hospital are too few (wide CIs / sign flips without a dominant hospital).

## Clinical adjudication / missing evidence (must not be papered over)

eICU has no order sets, staffing or bed-capacity data, so R2 cannot be fully separated from unmeasured hospital
conventions; escalation is a proxy (new vasopressor/ventilation codes), not an adjudicated clinical deterioration
event. Threshold sensitivity and per-analyte totals are not yet probed. Association/transport only - no causal claim.
