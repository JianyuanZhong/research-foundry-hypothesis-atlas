# Are laboratory workflow features clinically unsafe to transport at a fixed alert burden?

Status: Episode-6 substantive successor of [prior hypothesis]; adaptation of expert seed [starting question], not a reproduction of any demonstration or paper.

## Unresolved question and clinical importance

The strongest claim supported before this experiment is limited to data availability and feasibility: this frozen eICU snapshot contains multi-hospital ICU stays, ICU-relative vital and laboratory observations, and laboratory revision offsets. Earlier episode audits reported broad early-lab coverage and hospital heterogeneity, but no fitted result, transport failure, workflow cause, clinical utility, or causal effect.

The unresolved claim is:

> In adults alive and observable six hours after an initial ICU admission, does adding early laboratory observation-process information to the same measured clinical values create a transport gap in a fixed-sensitivity alert—after separating alert behavior from hospital prevalence differences—while a values-only alert remains more stable across held-out hospitals?

This matters because escalation and monitoring lists are acted on at a threshold, not at AUROC. A model that uses local test-ordering or interface patterns can preserve ranking while changing who is alerted, PPV, and calibration in another ICU. The substantive advance is a prespecified decision-facing transport estimand that (i) selects an 80%-sensitivity threshold only in development data, (ii) reports alert burden and safety, (iii) reports prevalence-standardized PPV alongside observed PPV, and (iv) tests threshold stability across development resamples. It remains an observational portability study: it cannot show that testing practice causes harm or that changing a hospital's testing policy improves outcomes.

## Baseline and substantive alternative

The primary transparent baseline is paired L2 logistic regression (C=1, deterministic solver), fitted separately for V and V+P on identical folds. Numeric imputation/scaling, categorical encoding, threshold selection and any residualization are fit inside the relevant development data. Logistic coefficients and the incremental P block make the scientific comparison auditable, but static summaries lose trajectory shape and irregular temporal ordering.

The substantive learned alternative is a one-layer width-64 GRU, dropout 0.1, Adam 1e-3, batch size 256, weighted binary cross-entropy, at most 30 epochs and patience 5, using twelve fixed 30-minute bins. V sequences contain within-bin mean and last value for each measured channel only; empty values use outer-training medians, with no mask/count/timestamp/time-since-last feature. V+P adds laboratory observation masks, within-bin counts, capped time-since-latest-result and fixed early process totals, with context appended to the final hidden state. This alternative can reveal whether temporal physiology and the timing of recorded observations contain information that first/last/mean/min/max summaries lose. It is not included to chase a small predictive gain. If CPU fitting exceeds the 7,200-second science budget, preserve the checkpoint/log and label the GRU branch deferred; request at most one GPU only after a bounded CPU diagnostic demonstrates necessity, never alter the scientific question.

A mechanistic workflow model is deferred because these sources do not reliably identify order intent, cancellation, interface failure, turnaround, staffing/capacity or treatment indication. That missing dependency blocks causal workflow interpretation, not this bounded portability comparison.

## Analysis, uncertainty and falsification

Report pooled and hospital-macro internal/external metrics, with one-class hospitals omitted from AUROC only and retained for Brier, calibration and alert metrics. Use 2,000 hospital-cluster bootstrap resamples of complete paired hospital prediction sets, preserving folds/repetitions, for percentile 95% intervals. Report prevalence and p_ref for every standardized PPV estimate.

Primary support for the transport hypothesis requires a positive V+P excess external-minus-internal loss relative to V in at least one alert-safety metric (prevalence-standardized PPV, sensitivity, alert fraction, calibration or Brier), interval excluding zero, a nonnegative internal P increment, and no explanation solely by observed prevalence. A lower interval bound >0.02 is a non-triviality check, not a clinical-benefit threshold. Robust support requires concordant direction in logistic and GRU and stable threshold target attainment. Threshold instability, family disagreement, or a result present only for observed PPV but absent for prevalence-standardized PPV weakens the claim.

Falsifications and integrity checks:
1. Permute D within hospital and refit; AUROC should approach 0.5 and alert metrics follow permuted prevalence.
2. Compare valid revision-safe features with revision-ignorant availability as diagnostic only; a large valid/permissive difference flags revision leakage sensitivity.
3. Permute each patient's P vector within hospital while preserving V and outcomes.
4. Shuffle within-stay offsets preserving values/counts.
5. Compare raw P with training-only V-risk residualized P, V-risk strata, and general vital-monitoring process controls.
6. Check no patient overlap across folds, no post-360/revision-after-360 predictors, no identifier predictors, no outer-test preprocessing, and exact threshold isolation.
7. Recalculate external observed PPV after prevalence standardization; if only observed PPV changes with prevalence, do not call it transport failure.

Supportive results support only that recorded laboratory process can make this early alert less portable beyond measured state at the tested operating point. Adverse results—no excess loss, stable/improved external behavior for V+P, disappearance after acuity/monitoring controls, or standardized rather than observed PPV stability—falsify or weaken the hypothesis for this panel/cohort but do not prove universal portability. Inconclusive results include missing canonical fields, <20 supported hospitals, threshold failure, unstable site metrics, intervals spanning zero and the 0.02 check, family disagreement, GRU deferral when a temporal claim is made, or integrity failure.

Computationally checkable are cohort, joins, cutoffs, features, folds, fits, predictions, threshold isolation, standardization formula, metrics, bootstrap intervals and falsification behavior. Clinical adjudication or another study is required to determine whether a missing row means no order, unrecorded order, interface loss, delayed result or de-escalation; whether residualization removes acuity; whether an alert changes care or outcomes; and whether a policy is beneficial. Missing are clinician intent, staffing/interface logs, turnaround, post-discharge mortality, prospective alert actions and external validation outside this eICU snapshot.
