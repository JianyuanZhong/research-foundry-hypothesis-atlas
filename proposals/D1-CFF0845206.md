# Fully observed alive index-hospital discharge after post-shock IV-loop initiation

Parent: [prior hypothesis]

## Episode-12 endpoint repair

This successor preserves the parent's namespaced subject partition, first-eligible-stay rule, hour-48 decision point, two-clock event/measurement contract, unit-safe cumulative-fluid phenotype and weight hierarchy, actually delivered 24-hour IV-loop strategies, clone-censor analysis, measured-overlap target population, pre-treatment trajectory adjustment, adherence/positivity/ESS gates, ALDL7 bounds, mortality/RRT safety gates, exact MIMIC bindings, and observational evidence limits. It changes the primary endpoint and its estimand because the parent's ALDL7 is unknown whenever ICU observation ends before durable day-7 liberation can be established.

The repaired primary outcome is discharge alive from the linked index hospitalization within 28 days after t0. It remains observed after ward transfer, is patient-important, and does not infer anything after discharge. It is not “alive at day 28”: a patient discharged alive on day 4 is a success even if later survival is unknown. Death before discharge is a competing event; remaining hospitalized at day 28 is an observed failure. ALDL7 remains a key secondary mechanistic endpoint with the parent's lower/upper bounds so the experiment directly reports what resolution was gained and whether a discharge signal is compatible with respiratory recovery.

A proposed “alive ICU discharge without readmission by day 7” was stress-tested and rejected as primary. ICU discharge is observed, but absence of another MIMIC admission cannot exclude readmission elsewhere, and day 7 is close to the 24-hour grace period. It may be reported only as “alive ICU discharge with no recorded MIMIC readmission by day 7,” a sensitivity that cannot establish freedom from readmission.

## Evidence, unresolved claim, and advance

The strongest evidence inspected supports a narrower conclusion than the hypothesis. McMullan et al., Critical Care Explorations 2026;8:e1404, DOI 10.1097/CCE.0000000000001404, frozen source [source checksum], reported similar lactate and no statistically detectable between-group differences in AKIRisk or urinary cystatin-C in a modest secondary analysis of the randomized RADAR-2 protocolized conservative-fluid/active-deresuscitation strategy; clinically important effects were not excluded. That is not evidence that a single delivered IV loop at this hour-48 decision improves discharge.

Boyle et al., “Optimizing endpoints in early phase clinical trials of acute respiratory distress syndrome,” Am J Respir Crit Care Med 2026;212:1702–1709, DOI 10.1093/ajrccm/aamag238, full-text XML inspected from frozen source [source checksum], explains that critical-care endpoints have important tradeoffs and must handle competing death/discharge explicitly. It does not validate this endpoint, threshold, or observational design.

The unresolved falsifiable claim is:

> Among invasively ventilated adults at ICU hour 48 who have recovered from vasopressor-treated shock, have unit-safe cumulative balance at least +50 mL/kg, retain urine output and potassium at least 3.0 mmol/L, and lie in measured treatment overlap after adjustment for recent respiratory, hemodynamic, renal and fluid trajectories, initiating an actually delivered ICU IV loop within the next 24 hours increases discharge alive from the index hospitalization by day 28 by more than 5 percentage points versus withholding all loops for that window, without excess observed 28-day death or new observed RRT.

The advance over the parent is resolution, not a stronger causal claim. The new endpoint is fully classifiable from the index admission even after ICU transfer. It tests whether a plausible respiratory/fluid benefit reaches a consequential whole-hospital milestone, while retaining ALDL7 as a bounded mechanistic outcome. Discharge is influenced by placement, practice and goals of care; therefore supportive evidence prioritizes external validation and a pragmatic trial, not treatment.

Hard feasibility gates remain: more than 10% of eligible person-hours with treatment probability below .01 or above .99, fewer than 100 adherent subjects in either arm, ESS below 75 in either arm, or severe trajectory-stratum nonoverlap makes the decision estimate inconclusive. ## Falsification and decision rules

Before outcomes, emit sequential attrition, treatment/deviation events, units/status exclusions, route concordance, overlap, standardized differences, calibration, weights, ESS and adherence. Eventual A/B assignment must not predict pre-t0 six-hour creatinine, urine or MAP slopes after trajectory adjustment; standardized difference >0.10 or multiplicity-controlled P<.05 blocks causal interpretation. Prespecified post-weighting trajectory covariates must all have standardized differences <=0.10. Expected 24-hour fluid/urine direction is an exposure-process check only.

Repeat the parent's trajectory restriction and quantitative residual-bias grid for an unmeasured binary readiness/trajectory factor needed to move the Y28 RD to 0 and +0.05. Repeat washout 6/12h, grace 6/12/24h and t0-only, never-treated, balance +30/+100 and continuous, complete-hour capture, drain/irrigant exclusions, FinishedRunning-only input, planned-only extubation, durability 24/48/72h, cardiac-surgery/heart-failure exclusions, KDIGO strata, no albumin co-initiation, calendar era, first ICU per admission, ICU/eMAR concordance and overlap .05/.95.

Supportive: in the adherent measured-overlap population, Y28 RD point estimate >+.05 and 95% lower limit >0; the upper 95% excess in both index-hospital and any-recorded 28-day death is <+.03; upper 95% excess observed RRT is <+.05 under both date brackets; overlap/ESS/balance/negative controls pass; expected process direction is present; endpoint, trajectory, grace and exposure sensitivities do not materially conflict; and ALDL7 does not show a well-identified adverse effect. This supports trial prioritization only.

Adverse: the 95% upper limit for Y28 RD is <=0 in a well-overlapped analysis, or the 95% lower limit of death harm is >=+.03, or the lower limit of observed RRT harm is >=+.05 under both brackets. Faster fluid removal or an ALDL7 possibility interval cannot override observed discharge failure or harm.

Inconclusive: the point estimate fails +.05 while uncertainty includes meaningful benefit; arm events/ESS/positivity fail; early-discharge censoring is unstable; endpoint consistency failures are material or differential; death/RRT gates are not passed; grace, era, destination or trajectory sensitivities conflict; residual confounding of modest strength reverses the conclusion; negative-control or two-clock/unit checks fail; or Y28 and an informative ALDL7 analysis conflict. Nonsignificance is not equivalence, and a supportive discharge estimate is not survival benefit.

## Computable versus adjudicated claims

The verifier can check hashes/headers, joins, partition, temporal boundaries, fluid units, weight hierarchy, delivered treatment, cloning/censoring, overlap/weights/ESS, endpoint arithmetic, competing-death classes, ALDL7 bounds, RRT brackets, bootstraps, falsification and whether the branch label follows computed outputs. Every numerical conclusion must link to an output.

It must reject correct computation paired with unsupported claims: “alive discharge” called “alive at day 28,” no recorded readmission called no readmission, no recorded death called survival, shorter stay called respiratory recovery, null RRT called renal safety, or an observational estimate called causal treatment benefit.

An automatic verifier cannot establish bedside intent/readiness, goals of care, discharge appropriateness, unmeasured confounding/exchangeability, completeness of out-of-hospital death/readmission/dialysis, renal safety, patient preferences, transportability or a recommendation. These require blinded clinical review, external linked follow-up and preferably randomization.
