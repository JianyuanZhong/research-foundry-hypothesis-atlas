# Does reduced laboratory surveillance during recorded instability identify near-term ICU risk?

Status: Episode-27 substantive successor to `[prior hypothesis]` and `[prior hypothesis]`. This is an observational prognostic and measurement-process experiment. It is not a causal test-ordering study, a hospital-quality ranking, a treatment recommendation, or evidence that increasing testing or an alert improves survival. No fitted result is claimed.

## Unresolved question and clinical importance

The strongest evidence already supported by the parent line is an outcome-blind feasibility design for adults reaching ICU-relative minute 360: laboratory result/revision history and recorded vital observations are available at scale across hospitals, and a transparent/temporal competing-risk experiment can evaluate recorded ICU death versus alive ICU discharge. The prior designs address absolute laboratory surveillance, six-bin temporal ordering, general vital-observation intensity, sparse carePlanEOL timing, death-versus-discharge specificity, and result-versus-revision clocks. None isolates the clinically different claim that a *patient-relative fall* in laboratory surveillance while the patient remains physiologically unstable is informative.

The unresolved hypothesis is:

> Among adults alive and still in the ICU at minute 360, an abrupt reduction in patient-linked laboratory observation relative to that patient’s preceding surveillance rate, occurring while recorded vital physiology remains unstable, is associated with higher subsequent recorded ICU-death risk over the next six hours, beyond recorded physiologic state, absolute laboratory/vital observation intensity, and admission context. The increment should be death-specific, transportable across held-out hospitals, and attenuated by within-hospital process permutation.

This matters because a reduced-testing-during-instability pattern could represent an actionable retrospective review signal, or could instead be discharge preparation, transfer, end-of-life workflow, interface interruption, or an artifact of missing physiology. Resolving that distinction is more clinically useful than reporting that “more missingness predicts death.” A robust observational signal would justify a prospective silent-mode/order-and-collection audit; a null or workflow-reproduced signal would discourage treating reduced testing as hidden deterioration.

The substantive advance is a patient-relative, state-conditioned change-point estimand. It asks whether the *change in surveillance conditional on contemporaneous instability* adds information beyond absolute counts and temporal summaries already proposed. It does not infer why testing changed.

Estimate cause-specific discrete-time hazards in one-hour bins; derive death and alive-discharge cumulative incidence. The primary estimand is the macro-hospital paired difference in integrated competing-risk Brier score for the death CIF over (360,720] between the state/absolute-surveillance baseline and the conditional-change model. Report the secondary 24-hour horizon separately, not as a replacement for the primary.

## Baseline and substantive alternative

All codebook, missingness, range, scaling, regularization, threshold and feature choices are learned only in development hospitals.

### Simple baseline B0

Fit pooled elastic-net discrete-time cause-specific logistic hazards for the two outcomes using admission context, recorded late-bin vital state and masks, aggregate laboratory state/availability, absolute laboratory and vital observation intensity, and carePlanEOL presence/timing. B0 includes a prespecified main effect for instability but excludes the patient-relative surveillance-change interaction. It uses the same cohort, labels, feature-support gates, folds and outcome horizons as every comparator.

A matched process-only control Bproc adds the surveillance drop without the instability interaction. A matched interaction model Bint adds the drop, instability and their interaction to B0. The primary scientific contrast is Bint-B0 for death; Bproc-B0 and the alive-discharge contrast test whether the finding is merely generic workflow.

### Scientifically informative alternative

Fit a compact mechanistic change-point model M on the same six bins. First estimate each patient’s expected laboratory observation rate from bins 1–4 using an outcome-blind hierarchical negative-binomial (or Poisson with overdispersion check) model with exposure time and supported analyte availability; then represent bins 5–6 by the patient-level log rate residual and its uncertainty. Combine this residual with the late vital-instability state in the same competing-risk hazard model. M can reveal an individual departure from that patient’s own prior surveillance regime and distinguish a stable low-testing patient from an abrupt fall after intensive testing—information lost by B0 aggregate counts. It is not claimed to identify the clinician’s intended surveillance plan.

As a conditional learned sensitivity, fit a one-layer GRU (hidden size 32, dropout 0.1, development-only early stopping) over the same six bins and channels, with separate death and alive-discharge heads. Compare it only if support passes; its purpose is to test non-linear persistence and joint state/process co-movement, not to reward complexity. Use an order-shuffled GRU and parameter-count-matched non-temporal model. M is the primary substantive alternative because its target is the patient-relative surveillance change; the GRU is deferred if it adds no uncertainty-supported information over M.

The baseline loses patient-specific change-point information and the alternative loses some transparency and depends on rate-support assumptions. The planned comparison is scientific: whether a within-patient surveillance departure under instability is informative, not whether one model has a small discrimination gain.

## Split, uncertainty and decision-oriented outputs

Use five outer folds holding out approximately 20% of hospitals, keeping all stays for a `uniquepid` together and stratifying event counts when feasible. Within each development fold use a 75/25 fit/validation split. Learn the codebook, rate model, ranges, scaling, calibration and any thresholds only in development hospitals. Never use hospital ID as a patient predictor.

Primary metric: macro-hospital paired difference in integrated competing-risk Brier score for recorded death over (360,720], with 95% hospital-clustered bootstrap or hierarchical intervals (at least 1,000 resamples if feasible). Secondary metrics are death CIF calibration at 720 and 1,800, death/discharge CIF Brier scores, fold direction, pooled results alongside macro-hospital values, and calibration intercept/slope. Report the interaction estimate only with uncertainty and do not use coefficient significance as the main transport criterion.

For a label-relative review diagnostic, within each held-out hospital select the B0 top-q episodes at q in {0.01, 0.02, 0.05, 0.10}, then select exactly the same number of episodes by Bint and M scores without held-out outcomes. Report review burden, recorded-death capture, PPV, calibration among reviewed/non-reviewed episodes, and incremental membership. Do not call these clinical utility or net benefit. The q gate is at least 20 evaluation hospitals, 10 alerts per hospital on average, 100 alerted episodes and 20 recorded deaths pooled across supported cells. If unsupported, report only model performance.

## Support gates and falsification

Before outcome evaluation, save a cohort flow, outcome-blind codebook, rate/instability definition, feature-support table and fold manifest. Primary interpretation requires at least 1,000 recorded deaths, 50 hospitals with at least 20 eligible episodes, at least 20 evaluation hospitals per reported contrast, adequate early and late laboratory support, vital-field support in at least 80% of evaluation hospitals, overlap of drop/instability strata, finite converged predictions, and complete timestamp/patient/site leakage audits. A primary interaction requires at least 10 episodes in each populated hospital/stratum used for permutation. Failed gates make the affected claim inconclusive, not positive.

Lock these before examining outcome labels:

1. Permute the patient-level surveillance-change features within each evaluation hospital, stratified by B0 risk quintile, late-instability stratum, vital coverage and carePlanEOL-observation status, while holding state fixed. The patient-linked interaction should attenuate if it is not just site/risk workflow.
2. Compare Bint-B0 with Bproc-B0, the vital-process-only increment, and the death-versus-alive-discharge increment. A matching discharge or vital-process gain is adverse to the targeted interpretation.
3. Repeat on a strict common analyte panel and revision-known-only rows; repeat with result-time and conservative availability clocks. A signal requiring post-360 revisions or an unresolved assay/interface is adverse.
4. Mask the final 60-minute bin and, separately, earlier bins, to test whether the result is a near-landmark artifact.
5. Stratify by hospital surveillance regime, interface/system and carePlanEOL presence/timing. Concentration in one interface, hospital or documented limitation regime is adverse.
6. Recompute with outcome-free within-hospital percentile normalization and leave-one-hospital-out influence summaries. A single hospital or site descriptor reproducing the effect falsifies general patient-linked interpretation.
7. For M and GRU, permute bin order while preserving each patient’s counts/state and compare the parameter-matched non-temporal model. Unchanged performance rejects a temporal/change-point interpretation.
8. Prohibit post-360 predictors, Apache mortality fields, hospital IDs in patient models, target-fold preprocessing and outcome-dependent codebook/range choices.

Supportive results require all applicable gates, an uncertainty-compatible Bint-B0 or M-B0 improvement for recorded death at the primary horizon, no material death calibration harm, a smaller or non-positive competing-discharge increment, attenuation under patient-process permutation and temporal order permutation, no comparable vital-process or site/interface reproduction, and stability under common-panel/revision/care-limitation restrictions. This supports only an observational recorded-surveillance review proxy.

Adverse results include a null/reversed death contrast, equal or larger discharge gain, no attenuation under permutation, reproduction by vital/process/site descriptors, disappearance under defensible revision/common-panel rules, or dependence on one hospital/interface or care-limitation stratum. These results favor workflow, discharge preparation, interface interruption, missing-physiology or end-of-life explanations.

Inconclusive results include failed event/overlap/temporal support, wide intervals spanning meaningful favorable and adverse values, unstable fold directions, nonconvergence, rate-model overdispersion/zero-inflation that cannot be handled prespecifically, or inability to establish revision-safe analyte identity. Inconclusive is neither confirmation nor refutation.

Completion is this newly fitted, uncertainty-bearing experiment—not confirmation of the hypothesis. Harbor can verify source paths, schema and column availability, joins, offsets, leakage, model arithmetic, uncertainty, falsification execution and whether conclusions follow from computed outputs. It cannot establish that an observed gap reflects an order decision, specimen collection, clinician intent, true physiology, complete goals-of-care documentation, that a clinician would review the pattern, or that changing surveillance improves survival. Those claims require source-system/order-and-collection audit, clinical adjudication, external validation, prospective silent-mode evaluation, and ultimately an impact or randomized study.
