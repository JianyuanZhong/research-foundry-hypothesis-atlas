# Lagged component-specific AKI discordance: support-ordered competing risks with a matched temporal comparison

Status: proposed design only. No cohort scan, model fit, Harbor execution, or clinical result was run in this branch.

## Scientific opening, question, and advance

Creatinine recovery accompanied by low urine output or positive recorded balance may identify a clinically important discordance, but the observed pattern can also be produced or revealed by administered support and surveillance. The parent design therefore asks a lagged, noncausal question rather than treating support as a treatment confounder:

> Among adults with a creatinine-defined AKI episode who reach the same prespecified 24-, 48-, or 72-hour landmark in their first eligible ICU stay, does a lagged recovery/urine/recorded-balance discordance have a component-specific seven-day hospital-death or ICU-recorded-RRT risk difference versus concordant recovery that remains directionally present in adequately supported pre-landmark strata and is not confined to support-first or high-surveillance patients?

L=48 hours is primary; L=24 and L=72 are prespecified replication and boundary tests. The exposure ends six hours before each landmark. The strongest available evidence supports only bounded premises: creatinine can overestimate measured kidney function during critical illness and muscle loss [K1]; urine-output windows and persistence thresholds alter AKI classification and mortality prediction [K2]; positive recorded balance and support intensity track ICU AKI/mortality while fluid capture and kidney-support initiation are incomplete or discretionary [K3]. None establishes this MIMIC phenotype, true GFR, true volume status, treatment intent, complete RRT capture, or causality.

The leading explanation is a reproducible renal/recorded-balance discordance that persists outside support-first and dense-documentation strata. The strongest rival is support/measurement selection: input, organ support, and surveillance precede or generate the observed contrast, so its risk difference attenuates in renal-first or observation-complete comparisons. Creatinine/muscle measurement error is a second rival. A persistent association would be prognostic/descriptive evidence within observed risk sets, not a renal mechanism or treatment effect. A support-first-only, process-density-equivalent, current-window-only, or component-incoherent signal would favor the rival. These comparisons cannot identify unmeasured biology, intent, or causal effects.

The substantive child advance is a matched methods comparison that makes this timing uncertainty visible. A transparent fixed-summary competing-risk model is the primary auditable analysis. A small temporal model receives exactly the same pre-landmark six-hour channels and patient split, but can retain order, persistence, intermittency, and support-response trajectories that fixed summaries collapse. Its value is scientific only if held-out component/order calibration or risk contrasts change the support-selection interpretation; a small AUROC gain is not sufficient.

## Actual scientific deliverable

For each L in {24,48,72}, the solver must newly produce:

2. A row-auditable exposure manifest: three six-hour bins, creatinine and urine construction, input precedence and units, accepted/zero/no-row/unresolved statuses, state labels, support fields, and missingness.
3. Separate seven-day Aalen–Johansen cumulative incidences and discordant-minus-concordant risk differences for hospital death and ICU-recorded RRT, overall and within prespecified support-intensity/order cells, with competing-event counts and 1,000 subject-cluster bootstrap intervals.
4. Support-first versus renal-first/simultaneous comparisons, input-excluded organ-support ordering, current-window and measurement-error sensitivities, and the process-only surveillance diagnostic.
5. Fixed-summary and temporal-model held-out predictions on identical subjects, including component CIF calibration, time-dependent Brier score, cause-specific discrimination, event counts, and 1,000 subject-cluster bootstrap intervals for reported risk contrasts/metrics.
6. A manifest linking every output to archive member, fields, joins, clinical time, boundary rule, risk set, outcome precedence, model, split, counts, uncertainty, and interpretation.

Completion requires computed outputs, uncertainty, sparse-cell decisions, supportive/adverse/inconclusive classification, and conclusions explicitly linked to those outputs. Readiness or a favorable estimate is not completion.

## Population and time construction

Use MIMIC-IV adults with `hosp/patients.anchor_age >= 18`. Join `icu/icustays` to `hosp/admissions` on `(subject_id, hadm_id)`, then to `hosp/patients` on `subject_id`. Retain finite `intime < outtime) stays satisfying `admittime <= intime`, `intime < dischtime`, and `outtime > admittime`; flag `outtime > dischtime` and clip observable ICU follow-up at valid `dischtime`. Within each `hadm_id`, sort by `intime), then `stay_id), and retain exactly the first eligible stay. Do not replace a first stay lacking t0 with a later stay. A subject can contribute multiple admissions, but all admissions for one subject remain in one split.

Clinical time is hours from retained `icustays.intime`. Primary creatinine is `hosp/labevents.itemid=50912`; 52546 is sensitivity only. Require finite `valuenum) and compatible `valueuom), use `charttime) rather than `storetime), and median-collapse valid same-time primary creatinine duplicates. The earliest candidate in `[intime,intime+72h)` is t0 if it is at least 0.3 mg/dL above a valid same-admission value with earlier `charttime) in the preceding 48 hours, or at least 1.5 times the valid minimum in the preceding seven days. Candidate and comparator must share `(subject_id,hadm_id)`; a same-time comparator is invalid.

A blank-`hadm_id` lab is assigned only when `subject_id) and `charttime) lie inside the retained admission/ICU interval and no overlapping admission can receive it; otherwise exclude and count it. This rule never joins an ICU table. Use an admission-baseline sensitivity from `[admittime,t0)` only when neither preceding window supplies a comparator. Exclude established pre-t0 RRT using the exact procedure set below and finite `starttime). Keep separate reasons for invalid time, no baseline, no t0, ambiguous blank-hadm, and pre-t0 RRT.

For L, define `Ltime=t0+L), primary exposure `A_L=[max(t0,Ltime-24h), Ltime-6h)`, divided into three consecutive six-hour bins. `W_L=[Ltime-6h,Ltime)` is a strict washout and contributes to neither primary exposure nor support-order timing. A boundary-contaminated current-window sensitivity uses `[max(t0,Ltime-24h),Ltime)`. Use the most recent valid creatinine in `A_L), peak creatinine from t0 through the end of `A_L), and recovery when peak minus lagged creatinine is at least 0.3 mg/dL; peak/landmark ratio <=0.8 is a sensitivity. In each six-hour bin, low urine is <0.5 mL/kg/h; primary urine classifiability requires at least two of three bins, with all-three as sensitivity. Unknown is never imputed.

## Exposure, support, and ordering

Primary urine uses `icu/outputevents` itemids {226559,226560,226561,226563,226567}; parse finite numeric `value), not `valuenum), and compatible `valueuom). Numeric zero is observed zero. Excluded itemids {226564,226565,226627,226631,226566,227489,226713,227519} are used only in a measurement-error sensitivity. For each `icu/inputevents` row overlapping `A_L), clip to `A_L) and choose exactly one quantity by this fixed precedence: finite `totalamount` with compatible `totalamountuom` in mL; else finite `amount` with compatible `amountuom` in mL; else finite `rate` with compatible `rateuom` in mL/hour integrated over its clipped positive interval. Never add fields. For amount/totalamount rows with finite positive `starttime<endtime`, allocate volume by overlap/duration. For point rows, include the quantity only when `starttime` lies in `A_L`; do not infer duration for null/nonpositive intervals. Reject negative/nonfinite quantities as unresolved. Retain row-level accepted, zero, no-row, unresolved/non-mL, and mixed-validity statuses, all rejected-row counts, item/category composition, and unit provenance. Recorded balance is accepted administered input minus direct primary urine, not total-body balance. A renal state requires both input and direct-urine opportunity.

The six mutually exclusive state labels are concordant recovery; low-urine-only; positive-recorded-balance-only (balance >0); both; unknown/insufficient opportunity; and no recovery. The binary discordance is low urine OR positive recorded balance, but component indicators and the six-level state must be published.

Define support interval `S_L=[t0,Ltime-6h)). Summarize accepted input in `S_L) as total compatible mL, weight-normalized mL when available, row counts, accepted-positive/accepted-zero counts, unresolved/non-mL counts, input status (NO_ROW, ZERO_ONLY, POSITIVE_ACCEPTED, UNRESOLVED_ONLY, or MIXED_VALID_UNRESOLVED), first/last accepted times, and exact `itemid, ordercategoryname, secondaryordercategoryname, ordercomponenttypedescription, ordercategorydescription, amountuom, totalamountuom, statusdescription`. These are recorded administrations, not intent or dose-response.

Pre-L RRT uses `icu/procedureevents` itemids {225441,225802,225803,225805,225809,225955} and finite `starttime`; retain `endtime,value,valueuom,ordercategoryname,statusdescription,storetime`. For the organ-support sensitivity, use `icu/d_items` dictionary labels exactly “Invasive Ventilation” or “Non-invasive Ventilation”; do not substitute undocumented itemids.

The first renal-defining observation `r_L) is the earliest `charttime` in `A_L) of a valid primary creatinine or primary urine row contributing to the state. The first support time `s_L) is the earliest accepted input `starttime) or organ-support `starttime) in `S_L), clipped to S_L. With a prespecified one-minute tie tolerance, classify support-first, renal-first, simultaneous, or neither/unclassifiable. An interval that overlaps the renal observation, missing/nonpositive timing, or invalid-only rows is unorderable rather than forced into an order. Record first support class and timestamps. Repeat ordering with input rows removed from `s_L), leaving only dictionary-verified organ support; this tests whether ordering is driven by non-input support without erasing the input-selection pathway.

Support-intensity cells are fixed separately for each L from positive accepted input in the training partition only: no compatible input, below the training-partition median, and at/above that median; unresolved-only remains unknown. No test-set or outcome-derived cutpoint is permitted. Also report training-frozen quartiles descriptively. Surveillance is separate: primary output-row count and bin coverage, non-creatinine `hosp/labevents` count, and non-weight `icu/chartevents` count in S_L. These are process indicators, not negative-control outcomes.

## Risk sets, endpoints, estimands, and uncertainty

At L, include retained-stay subjects alive at Ltime, still in the clipped ICU interval, without incident RRT before Ltime, and with exposure/state opportunity determined without W_L. Report losses from death, ICU exit, RRT, missing creatinine, insufficient opportunity, invalid timing, and washout contamination.

For hospital death, use the first valid `hosp/admissions.deathtime` in `[Ltime,Ltime+7d)`, no later than valid `dischtime), with `hospital_expire_flag` as an internal consistency check. Death remains observable after ICU exit while admission is active. Alive discharge is a competing event; an alive in-hospital subject at day 7 is administrative censoring. A death equal to dischtime is valid only when time/flag consistency passes; otherwise it is a timing exception.

For ICU-recorded RRT, use the first exact-set procedure row with finite `starttime` in `[Ltime,Ltime+7d)`, strictly before clipped ICU exit and while alive. ICU exit alive and hospital death before RRT compete; earliest valid time wins and death wins an exact tie. A start exactly at ICU exit or death is not an RRT event and is counted as a tie exception. Missing `starttime` is unobservable and counted. Do not backfill ward or post-ICU RRT.

The primary noncausal estimands are:
`RD_D(L,s)=F_D(7d | discordant,s)-F_D(7d | concordant,s)` and
`RD_R(L,s)=F_R(7d | discordant,s)-F_R(7d | concordant,s)`,
where each F is the observed Aalen–Johansen cumulative incidence in the same L risk set and support stratum s. Report empirical and fixed-summary-standardized contrasts, component and competing-event counts, and complete denominators. A six-hour discrete-time cause-specific hazard implementation is allowed only as a computational route to the same estimand. Use 1,000 subject-cluster bootstrap replicates, keeping all admissions for each subject together in every replicate and split.

A cell is estimable only with at least 50 risk-set subjects and at least 10 component events; otherwise publish its denominator and counts as inconclusive/not estimable and never merge after viewing outcomes. A descriptive observation-complete sensitivity may restrict to subjects observable through day 7; it is not a replacement estimate. Stabilized pre-L observation weights are optional secondary sensitivity only, based on t0/S_L data, prespecified 1st/99th percentile truncation, and reported positivity.

## Falsification and interpretation

Predeclare five checks: (1) quartiles of non-renal laboratory plus non-weight chart density as a process-only exposure; (2) support-first, renal-first, simultaneous, and input-excluded organ-support order; (3) strict-washout versus current-window boundary contamination; (4) primary versus excluded urine items and two-of-three versus all-three-bin completeness; (5) separate death and ICU-RRT components with discharge/ICU-exit competition. A result present only after washout violation is leakage adverse. A comparable process-only gradient, support-first-only contrast, excluded-item dependence, or RRT contrast explained by ICU exit weakens the renal interpretation. A result in adequately supported renal-first cells, at L=48 and one other landmark, with a smaller/different process gradient is supportive. Sparse cells, nonpositivity, unstable bootstrap, many unknowns, timing exceptions, or a sequence gain without reproducible component/order contrast or calibration is inconclusive. An imprecise null is not decisive refutation.

## Matched baseline versus learned temporal alternative

Both methods use a single immutable pre-landmark input contract `X_L): all six-hour bins from t0 through `Ltime-6h), plus baseline covariates known by t0. Neither can read W_L, any post-L record, outcome-derived support cells, or future admissions.

The fixed-summary baseline is a transparent cause-specific discrete-time hazard model. Its features are deterministic summaries of `X_L): pre-t0 creatinine comparator/AKI stage; per-bin and aggregate creatinine level, peak, slope, last value and masks; per-bin urine rate, completeness, item composition and masks; canonical accepted input volume, positive/zero/no-row/unresolved status, representation fraction, item/category composition and missingness; recorded balance; weight/CVP when dictionary-supported; pre-L RRT and organ-support indicators/durations; support intensity/order; ICU/admission timing, age, sex; and non-renal density/missingness. Fit separate six-hour cause-specific hazards with the hospital-death versus alive-discharge and ICU-RRT versus alive-ICU-exit/death competing states, with prespecified support-by-state/order interactions. Preprocessing, support cutpoints, regularization and any class weights are training-only. Standardize held-out CIFs to the same test risk set and report calibration, time-dependent Brier, cause-specific discrimination, risk contrasts, counts and clustered uncertainty. This is primary because every feature and event clock is auditable.

The learned alternative is a one-layer GRU (hidden size 64, dropout=0, Adam learning rate 1e-3, maximum 100 epochs, early stopping after 10 validation-loss epochs, fixed seed 20261002) over the same six-hour channels and masks in `X_L`, with separate cause-specific heads for the same two competing-risk targets. It may learn persistence, order, intermittency, and trajectories such as an accepted-input rise preceding sparse urine or creatinine recovery; those relations are lost when the baseline retains only peaks/slopes/last values and counts. The GRU cannot access a richer source table or extra time window. Assign subjects deterministically by the first eight hexadecimal characters of SHA-256(`mimic-akidisc-v1:` + decimal `subject_id`): residues 00–b3 train (70%), b4–d9 validation (15%), and da–ff test (15%); all admissions for a subject follow that assignment. Use training-only scaling/encoding and early stopping on validation loss. Evaluate once on the identical held-out test subjects at every L, with component CIF calibration, time-dependent Brier, cause-specific discrimination, support/order-stratum calibration, and the same discordant-versus-concordant held-out contrasts. Use 1,000 subject-cluster bootstrap replicates for these reported test metrics/contrasts, with failures and usable replicates recorded.

Primary selection is the fixed-summary model: its purpose is to test the clinical comparison transparently, not to maximize prediction. Select or retain the GRU only as an informative secondary if it improves held-out calibration or reveals a reproducible timing/persistence pattern that changes component/order interpretation across L=48 and at least one other landmark. AUROC-only improvement, a small gain without calibration, unstable sparse-cell behavior, or no change in support-selection conclusions defers the GRU; it does not refute the clinical hypothesis. A temporal alternative is scientifically justified here because timing and persistence are the rival explanation, not because it is more complex. A larger transformer, causal treatment model, or mechanistic GFR model is not selected: it would add capacity or assumptions without resolving missing intent, true filtration, total-body balance, complete bedside collection, or ward/post-ICU RRT.
