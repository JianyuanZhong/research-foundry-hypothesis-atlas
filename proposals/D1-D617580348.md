## Frozen bridge contract

### Contract identity and field vocabulary

Before any real label, governance seals a versioned contract schema and a one-to-one mapping from each primary recommendation unit to one contract record. The contract has no free-form completeness categories and is never inferred from author identity, service alone, or clinical intuition. Every field has one of `EXPLICIT`, `ABSENT`, `AMBIGUOUS`, `UNREADABLE`, `NOT_OBSERVABLE`, or `NONRESPONSE`.

Each record contains:

1. `unit_id` and `target_cluster`, copied from the locked primary unit identity;
2. `action_modality`, with the parent’s exact modality vocabulary;
3. `due_interval`, represented as the parent’s interval category rather than an invented calendar date;
4. `polarity`, `recipient`, and `responsibility`, using only explicit text evidence;
5. `replacement/conflict`, copied from the primary unit state;
6. `evidence_pointer`, a stable `(source_table, subject_id, note_id, field_ordinal)` pointer to the text span or detail row supporting each explicit field;
7. `departure_visibility`, which must be `NOT_OBSERVABLE` unless a permitted stored timestamp relationship is present; and
8. `success_event`, which is always `NOT_OBSERVABLE` in MIMIC and is reserved for prospective observation.

`EXPLICIT` means that two blinded readers can point to the same permitted source span or detail row and obtain the same coded value. `ABSENT` means that the permitted artifact explicitly states absence or no action; it is not equivalent to missing text. `AMBIGUOUS`, `UNREADABLE`, `NONRESPONSE`, and `NOT_OBSERVABLE` are adverse-compatible and cannot be converted into `ABSENT`, a default owner, or a favorable route. `recipient` and `responsibility` are not derived from `hosp/services.curr_service`, `admit_provider_id`, author-like text, or ICU unit labels.

The bridge completeness functional is computed only after the primary unit and route labels are append-only sealed. For each primary unit, define:

```text
C_unit = 1 iff target_cluster, action_modality, due_interval,
              polarity, recipient, responsibility, replacement/conflict,
              and evidence_pointer are all EXPLICIT
         and no field is AMBIGUOUS, UNREADABLE, NONRESPONSE,
         or NOT_OBSERVABLE.
```

The admission bridge status is the worst status over all distinct primary units:
`BRIDGE_COMPLETE`, `BRIDGE_RECONCILE`, `BRIDGE_UNRESOLVED`, or `BRIDGE_NOT_OBSERVABLE`. This status is descriptive and secondary. It cannot change an `AUTO` route to another primary route; instead, an `AUTO` atom with any non-complete bridge status is ineligible for silent nomination. A `RECON` route may be considered only as a clinician-reconciliation contract, never as automatic propagation. `TIMING` and `DEFER` remain non-automatic and cannot be rescued by contract completeness.

### Prospective implementation contract

For every nominated atom-route, the protocol must emit a machine-readable contract template before any prospective data are viewed. The template fixes:

* the exact eligible atom signature and its allowed route;
* the exact atomic fields that must be present before a silent suggestion is generated;
* the output state (`NO_OUTPUT`, `SILENT_CANDIDATE`, `SILENT_RECONCILIATION`, or `SILENT_DEFER`);
* an explicit `UNKNOWN_OWNER` and `UNKNOWN_DUE` state rather than defaults;
* the designated prospective responsible role as a governance input, not an inference from MIMIC;
* the observation events required to evaluate the bridge (`viewed`, `accepted/edited`, `signed/finalized`, `transmitted`, `order/referral action`, and, where applicable, patient-facing communication); and
* the data-retention, timestamp, and audit-log requirements for each event.

MIMIC can populate only the source-side fields and stored-text pointers. It cannot populate physical viewing, signature/finalization semantics, transmission, order execution, scheduling, patient preference, outside care, or outcomes. Those fields must remain explicitly `UNAVAILABLE_IN_MIMIC` in the compiled contract and must be collected in a separately approved prospective study. A prospective bridge that omits any required event is `INCONCLUSIVE`, not a pass.

## Blinded documentation-to-contract audit

The existing sampled admissions and challenge reservations are unchanged. No admissions are added and no challenge is selected using route, disagreement, bridge status, or workload. The bridge audit reuses the already selected packets and challenge cases, so its workload is bounded within the parent’s fixed ledger. Every selected admission receives the base bridge coding; only preselected challenge admissions receive duplicate bridge coding.

Two roster-disjoint bridge readers receive the same route-concealed packet permissions as their assigned parent lane, including reciprocal pre-`D` radiology evidence, permitted admission/death/disposition context, canonical exact `DS`, and permitted detail artifacts. They do not see primary routes, component labels, atom identity, weights, challenge type, or each other’s output. They independently code every contract field and its evidence pointer. A third challenge reader reviews only disagreements and pointer reproducibility after both append-only seals; the third reader may mark `UNRESOLVED` but may not choose a favorable value by consensus.

The challenge set contains the parent’s already sealed route-irrelevant, route-decisive, counterfactual omission/addition, and nuisance-feature pairs. Bridge-specific expectations are frozen before review:

* a route-irrelevant or formatting/author-token perturbation must preserve every contract field and `BRIDGE_*` status;
* a route-decisive perturbation may change only the prespecified affected unit field and the route-table consequence;
* masking a required recommendation span must never improve bridge completeness; and
* insertion of a span may improve a field only when the locked parser and evidence-pointer rule identify it as the designated field, otherwise the result is `AMBIGUOUS`.

A pair with no unambiguous transformation is `CONTROL_INFEASIBLE`; it cannot be used as a favorable control and contributes adverse-compatible missingness.

## Secondary estimands and compatible-set treatment

The primary route estimands are unchanged. For the secondary bridge layer, report design-weighted finite-population bounds for:

* `C_unit` and complete-contract prevalence by exact atom and route;
* the fraction of primary `AUTO` admissions that are `BRIDGE_COMPLETE`;
* target/action/due/recipient/responsibility field-specific completeness;
* parent-versus-bridge reader discordance and evidence-pointer reproducibility;
* bridge status changes under each control type; and
* the burden of `UNKNOWN_OWNER`, `UNKNOWN_DUE`, `NOT_OBSERVABLE`, nonresponse, and control infeasibility.

Each bridge field is included in the same admission-level compatible-set `Omega_i` as a secondary contribution, but the parent’s primary route-compatible construction remains authoritative. Shared reader error is allowed without restriction. If an all-reader unanimous field is still compatible with an incorrect field, the adverse endpoint retains that state. Joint numerator/denominator optimization is required for all bridge ratios; independent endpoint substitution is prohibited. The report includes the cardinality and reason codes of each bridge-compatible member and uses the parent’s exact weighted sharp bounds, finite-population inversion, and simultaneous family procedure. A bridge interval is wide or undefined when the corresponding denominator is zero; no imputation or complete-case substitution is allowed.

## Pre-label gates and interpretation

All parent gates must pass first. A cell can be bridge-eligible only when all of the following are true, with thresholds sealed before labels:

1. at least 30 sampled candidate-route admissions and at least 10 non-sentinel challenge admissions when feasible, as inherited;
2. the simultaneous lower bound for complete contracts among the cell’s nominated route is at least `0.90`;
3. the simultaneous upper bound for any field-specific unresolved/ambiguous/nonresponse component is at most `0.05`, separately for target, action, due interval, recipient, responsibility, replacement/conflict, and evidence pointer;
4. the simultaneous lower bound for bridge-reader agreement on each required explicit field is at least `0.90`, and the upper parent-versus-bridge route/field discordance is at most `0.05` for `AUTO` and `0.10` for `RECON`;
5. every route-irrelevant/nuisance control has an upper bridge-status flip bound no greater than `0.02`, and every route-decisive control obeys the locked one-field consequence with an upper violation bound no greater than `0.05`;
6. bridge packet missingness, reader nonresponse, pointer failure, and control infeasibility have a simultaneous upper bound below `0.05`; and
7. the atom-specific additive workload remains within the inherited physician-hour and packet-minute ceilings, with all bridge coding charged to the existing base/challenge rows and no pooled-workload rescue.

These are operational nomination gates, not clinical safety tolerances. A supportive outcome is a fully passing exact atom-route with an explicit contract template and all required prospective event fields declared. It permits only submission to prospective governance for silent validation. An adverse outcome is a gate failure, a route-invariant bridge flip, a shared-error-compatible missing owner/due field for an `AUTO` nomination, or an adverse-compatible `AUTO` state lacking a complete contract; the affected atom is excluded. An inconclusive outcome is sparse challenge support, zero denominator, wide compatible bounds, nonresponse, infeasible controls, broken seal order, or any required field that MIMIC cannot observe. Inconclusive is not evidence of safety or transportability.
