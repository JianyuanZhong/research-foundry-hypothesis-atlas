# Intersectional documentary-M2 retention and subgroup non-loss under incomplete preoperative report bundles

## 1. Unresolved question and substantive advance

This is a targeted child of assessed-valid `[prior hypothesis]` (q_M2 patient retention), with `[prior hypothesis]` as a supporting parent for the subgroup-safety layer.

The clinical decision under study is narrow: allocation of a fixed number of preoperative review offers among adults approaching an eligible HCC resection. The offer is a historical documentary-review flag, not a recommendation for resection extent, transplantation, neoadjuvant/adjuvant therapy, surveillance, or any other treatment.

The parent q_M2 asks whether incomplete stored report bundles can preserve every patient who would be in the same-world maximum-stored-content fixed-K queue and whose eventual same-encounter pathology book is documentary M2. That is stronger than preserving an aggregate M2 count, but it is still an overall contract. An overall-preserved queue can conceal a loss of documentary-M2 captures within a clinically recognizable age/recorded-sex cell if another cell supplies compensating captures. Conversely, a subgroup comparison alone does not protect the identities of oracle-positive patients. The unresolved question is therefore:

> At a nontrivial complete-patient report-bundle coverage level, can the fixed-K semantic queue simultaneously retain every same-world maximum-content documentary-M2 patient and avoid losing documentary-M2 captures to the locked structured/report comparators or capacity-matched random review in each prespecified age-by-recorded-sex cell?

The substantive advance is a single nested, exact frontier, q_guard, that combines the two nonredundant protections:

1. q_req: overall comparator/random capture margins remain positive;
2. q_M2: no maximum-content-queued documentary-M2 patient is lost under incomplete bundles; and
3. q_guard: within each prespecified cell, the incomplete-bundle semantic queue captures at least as many documentary-M2 cases as B, R, Gmask, and capacity-matched random review.

The added cell gate is a finite-corpus distributional non-loss check, not a fairness metric. It does not treat any comparator as clinically entitled to a patient, and it does not call documentary-negative substitutions harmless. It tests whether missing report content creates a concentrated loss that overall patient-retention and capture summaries would hide.

The field `sex` is an administrative recorded value, not self-identified gender. The result cannot be called fair, equitable, safe in practice, or beneficial.

### Confirmatory hypothesis

At rho=0.10 fixed capacity and d=24 hours, in both locked test years (2020 and 2021), every compatible source/operation/accession/onset/radiology-book/pathology-book/deletion state satisfies:

- maximum stored content passes all inherited strict overall G-versus-B, G-versus-R, G-versus-Gmask, and capacity-matched-random margins;
- q_req < 1 and q_M2 < 1; and
- q_guard < 1, where q_guard is the smallest exact patient-complete conditional whole-bundle coverage threshold at which q_M2 passes and, in each evaluable cell, G captures no fewer documentary-M2 cases than B, R, Gmask, or capacity-matched random review.

The q_guard condition is a nested primary claim: q_guard >= q_M2 >= q_req must hold. The exact frontier is falsified by one admissible state, year, cell, comparator, or deletion with a strict loss.

A supportive result establishes only retrospective finite-corpus documentary patient-retention and subgroup non-loss under hypothetical stored-content masks. An adverse result at any layer falsifies only that layer and leaves narrower parent results reportable. A computational or subgroup-feasibility failure is inconclusive, not evidence that the queue is unsafe.

1. computationally inconclusive: any lossless source replay, state construction, whole-accession grouping, interval clock, reader-book, locked-score/tie, deletion, q_req/q_M2, cell join, threshold, or witness check fails;
2. feasibility inconclusive: inherited N/K/event/structural-denominator requirement fails;

4. maximum-content signal adverse: maximum stored content fails any inherited overall or random/deletion gate;
5. aggregate-coverage adverse: q_req is undefined or equals 1;
6. patient-retention adverse: q_M2 is undefined or equals 1;

## 8. Why this is an important falsifiable advance

Existing evidence supports the prognostic importance of MVI and the need for external, calibrated, decision-aware validation, but not that this institution’s stored report text is usable at the decision time or that a fixed review queue changes care. The experiment advances knowledge by exposing a clinically consequential failure mode—distributional loss of documentary-positive review opportunities under incomplete report bundles—while retaining patient-level oracle-positive protection and refusing to turn administrative subgroup counts into claims about fairness or benefit. It can yield a genuinely informative negative result, a narrower q_M2 support with subgroup failure, or a full documentary-support result with a precisely specified external evidence target.
