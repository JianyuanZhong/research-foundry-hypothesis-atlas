# Hypothesis: MAP Trajectory Predicts Clinical Outcomes Better Than Minimum MAP

## Scientific Question

**At comparable minimum mean arterial pressure (MAP) levels, patients whose MAP shows a more favorable trajectory (higher mean, lower variability, less time below target) during the first 24 hours of ICU admission experience improved outcomes (lower mortality and reduced acute kidney injury), after adjusting for shock etiology, vasopressor burden, and comorbidities.**

## Unresolved Question and Clinical Importance

### Current Understanding
Current clinical practice and research have focused on:
- Minimum blood pressure thresholds as a proxy for hemodynamic stability
- Minimum MAP as a risk stratification marker
- Static "best" or "worst" values from admission vitals

### The Gap
These approaches have limitations:
1. **Minimum MAP is temporally arbitrary**: A single minimum value doesn't capture the dynamic nature of hemodynamic instability
2. **Ignores trajectory information**: Patients with the same minimum MAP may have vastly different MAP courses (rapid correction vs. sustained instability)
3. **May miss early warning signals**: Subtle, sustained MAP abnormalities may predict outcomes better than dramatic drops
4. **Don't account for variability**: Hemodynamic variability itself may be a marker of instability

### Clinical Importance
- **Early resuscitation guidance**: Could identify patients needing more aggressive or sustained hemodynamic support
- **Risk stratification**: May improve prediction models beyond current minimum-value approaches
- **Resource allocation**: Could help identify high-risk patients for closer monitoring
- **Treatment optimization**: May suggest that maintaining MAP above a threshold for longer duration is more important than achieving a single high minimum

## Substantive Advance Over Existing Knowledge

### Beyond Expert Seed #3 (Hypotension Duration)
The existing expert seed focuses on "duration of hypotension at the same minimum blood pressure predicting AKI." This proposal extends that by:
1. **Using MAP instead of SBP**: MAP is the more clinically relevant measure for end-organ perfusion
2. **Incorporating trajectory**: Not just duration, but the mean, variability, and shape of the MAP course
3. **Multi-outcome assessment**: Testing both mortality and AKI, not just AKI
4. **Adjusting for confounders**: Including shock etiology and vasopressor burden

### Beyond Minimum MAP Literature
Current literature on MAP in ICU focuses on:
- Optimal MAP targets for specific conditions (e.g., atherosclerotic disease)
- Association between low MAP and mortality
- MAP-guided resuscitation trials

This proposal uniquely addresses:
- **The trajectory aspect**: How the *course* of MAP matters, not just the endpoint
- **Temporal dynamics**: The first 24 hours as a critical window for hemodynamic stabilization
- **Multi-dimensional metrics**: Mean, variability, time below target, and slope

## Falsifiable Hypothesis Statement

**H0 (Null)**: After adjusting for shock etiology, vasopressor burden, age, comorbidities, and hospital characteristics, the trajectory of MAP (mean, variability, time below 70 mmHg, slope) during the first 24 hours of ICU admission is NOT associated with in-hospital mortality or AKI.

**H1 (Alternative)**: After adjusting for the above confounders, patients with more favorable MAP trajectories (higher mean MAP, lower variability, less time below 70 mmHg, less negative slope) during the first 24 hours of ICU admission have significantly lower in-hospital mortality and reduced AKI incidence compared to patients with less favorable trajectories, even when minimum MAP is held constant.

## Statistical Analysis Plan

### Primary Exposure Variables (MAP Trajectory Metrics):
1. **Mean MAP** (0-24h): Average MAP during first 24 hours
2. **MAP Variability**: Standard deviation of MAP in first 24 hours
3. **Time below target**: Proportion of time MAP < 70 mmHg
4. **Slope**: Linear regression slope of MAP over time (mmHg/hour)
5. **Minimum MAP**: (for stratification, not primary variable)

### Primary Outcomes:
1. **In-hospital mortality**: Binary (0/1) at hospital discharge
2. **AKI incidence**: Binary (0/1) using RIFLE criteria (creatinine-based)
3. **AKI severity**: Categorical (R, I, II, III) if AKI develops

### Covariates (Adjustment Variables):
1. **Shock etiology**: Sepsis, cardiogenic, hypovolemic, other
2. **Vasopressor burden**: Number of vasopressor types, total vasopressor hours
3. **Demographics**: Age, gender, ethnicity
4. **Comorbidities**: APACHE II score, SOFA score (if available)
5. **Hospital characteristics**: Teaching status, region

### Analysis Approach:
1. **Descriptive**: Distribution of MAP metrics by outcome status
2. **Unadjusted associations**: Logistic regression for each MAP metric
3. **Multivariable models**: Adjusted logistic regression with covariates
4. **Interaction tests**: MAP trajectory × minimum MAP (to test main hypothesis)
5. **Sensitivity analyses**:
   - Different target MAP (70 vs 65 vs 60 mmHg)
   - Different time windows (first 6h, first 12h, first 24h)
   - Different AKI definitions (RIFLE, AKI-NN)
   - Excluding vasopressor-dependent patients

### Falsification Criteria:
The hypothesis is **falsified** if:
- After multivariable adjustment, MAP trajectory metrics show NO significant association with outcomes (p > 0.05)
- The interaction between MAP trajectory and minimum MAP is non-significant (p > 0.05)
- Results are consistent across sensitivity analyses

The hypothesis is **supported** if:
- MAP trajectory metrics show significant independent association with outcomes
- The interaction suggests trajectory adds information beyond minimum MAP
- Results are consistent across sensitivity analyses

## Method Comparison: Baseline vs. Proposed Approach

### Baseline Approach (Minimum MAP Only)
- **Method**: Logistic regression with minimum MAP as primary predictor
- **Information captured**: Single snapshot of hemodynamic status
- **Limitations**: Ignores temporal dynamics, doesn't capture recovery trajectory

### Proposed Approach (MAP Trajectory)
- **Method**: Logistic regression with multiple trajectory metrics (mean, variability, time below target, slope)
- **Information captured**: Dynamic hemodynamic course, recovery pattern, instability
- **Advantage**: May capture early warning signals and recovery patterns missed by minimum values

### Information Gap Addressed
The trajectory approach can reveal:
1. **Early recovery signals**: Patients whose MAP rises rapidly after a low point may have better outcomes despite a low minimum
2. **Sustained instability**: Patients with prolonged time below target may have worse outcomes even with good minimum values
3. **Variability as a marker**: Hemodynamic variability may indicate instability independent of mean level
4. **Slope as a prognostic marker**: Rate of MAP change may predict outcomes

## Limitations and Unresolved Questions

### Data Limitations:
1. **Vital sign frequency**: 5-minute medians may not capture rapid hemodynamic changes
2. **Missing data**: Some patients may have incomplete vital sign coverage
3. **Vasopressor identification**: Drug name matching may miss some vasopressors

### Clinical Limitations:
1. **Causality**: Observational design cannot establish causation
2. **Target selection**: 70 mmHg is conventional but may not be optimal for all populations
3. **Generalizability**: eICU population may not represent all ICU patients

### Unresolved Questions:
1. What is the optimal MAP target for different shock etiologies?
2. Does MAP trajectory matter more than minimum MAP in specific subpopulations?
3. Can MAP trajectory metrics improve risk prediction beyond current models?
