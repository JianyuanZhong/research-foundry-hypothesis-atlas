TITLE
Day-28 post-TACE landmark with an acute-injury surrogate: does early biochemical perturbation identify the selected patients for whom additional waiting before HCC resection is associated with better day-5 hepatic function?

CLINICAL QUESTION, ADVANCE, AND CLAIM LADDER
For an adult with recorded HCC who has received TACE and has not undergone liver resection by exactly 28 elapsed days, can the routinely dense biochemical response during the first 7 days after TACE—rather than a rarely measured day-14-to-28 “readiness” panel—identify the subset in whom later resection is associated with less postoperative hepatic dysfunction?

The parent correctly fixed eligibility at day 28 and kept nonresected patients visible, but its actionable readiness construct still depends on a current state that the source scarcely observes: only 57 resected-with-prior-TACE patients had a complete bilirubin/INR/albumin triad in days 14–28. This child does not relabel stale values as day-28 readiness and does not pursue a data-hungry dynamic regime. It tests a narrower, feasible and clinically interpretable prognostic hypothesis: the continuous association between TACE-to-resection time and a paired day-5 bilirubin/INR hepatic-dysfunction signal differs according to the magnitude of the acute post-TACE bilirubin/INR perturbation. Acute perturbation is a surrogate marker available by the fixed landmark, not a validated clearance rule and not an instruction to operate.

Strongest evidence-supported claim: routine bilirubin and INR are biologically relevant components of hepatic reserve/PHLF assessment, PHLF is clinically consequential, and the HCC source has dense acute and perioperative measurements sufficient to estimate a selected-resection timing-by-injury association. The 2023 review by Nishio et al. (PMID 37927928; PMCID PMC10623981; abstract and public full-text availability identified) emphasizes that PHLF risk assessment needs liver functional reserve and that dynamic tests, portal hypertension and volumetry add information not captured by routine labs. The ISGLS definition uses increased INR and hyperbilirubinemia on/after postoperative day 5, but clinical grading depends on management; therefore this study calls its endpoint a biochemical hepatic-dysfunction signal, not ISGLS PHLF. Existing observational evidence on preoperative TACE is vulnerable to nonrandom treatment selection and does not establish an optimal interval (Na et al., 2025, PMID 41000227, PMCID PMC12457797, as inspected/cited by the parent).

Unresolved claim tested: among patients who ultimately undergo a recorded resection on days >28–180 after TACE, greater acute biochemical injury modifies the continuous timing–postoperative dysfunction association: additional time is associated with less day-5 dysfunction after a larger acute perturbation, but little or no improvement after a smaller perturbation. The study is associational and prognostic. It cannot show that waiting caused recovery, that an acute laboratory rule should schedule surgery, or that nonresected patients would benefit from resection.

FIXED POPULATION, TEMPORAL BOUNDARIES, AND NONRESECTION
Unit: patient. The population ledger begins with the earliest recorded qualifying TACE, before subsequent resection is known.

Include at the landmark: age >=18 in the index-TACE encounter; an explicit HCC diagnosis (诊断名称 matching 肝细胞癌|原发性肝癌|原发性肝细胞癌|word-bounded HCC) in an encounter dated no later than landmark; valid index-TACE time; and no qualifying liver resection or transplant through t0. Exclude intraoperative TACE, impossible timestamps, donor/transplant procedures, and a resection before or through t0. Landmark t0 = earliest qualifying TACE 开始时间 + exactly 28*24 hours; baseline features stop at t0.

A patient is “observed after landmark” only if a dated encounter, lab, examination or procedure is recorded after t0; this is institutional observation, not proof of being alive or free from outside care. All landmark-eligible patients remain in the selection ledger through day 180, classified as recorded resection on days >28–60, >60–90 or >90–180; no recorded resection; repeat TACE/other treatment; and presence/absence of post-landmark institutional observation. No-resection is not an outcome benefit and not a waiting strategy: it may represent unresectability, progression, death, outside surgery, palliation, emergency indication, preference or loss to care.

The primary postoperative estimand is necessarily restricted to patients with a first qualifying recorded resection on days >28–180 after a TACE and complete paired outcome. For this selected cohort, the exposure clock is the last recorded qualifying TACE before operation, because an intervening TACE resets the biological insult; a sensitivity uses earliest-TACE time to maintain direct alignment with the landmark ledger. This explicit two-clock design avoids pretending that the earliest TACE is the last injury while preserving fixed day-28 eligibility and visibility of all nonresected patients.

EXPOSURE, OUTCOME, AND ESTIMAND
Acute post-TACE injury components, all known before t0:
- bilirubin response = maximum total bilirubin during [0,7] days / latest total bilirubin during [-7,0) before the last preoperative TACE;
- INR response = maximum INR during [0,7] minus latest INR during [-7,0);
- measurement times and assay label (routine versus 急) are retained.

Primary modifier is continuous. Log-transform the bilirubin ratio; robust-standardize log bilirubin ratio and INR change using medians and MADs estimated without postoperative outcomes, then average the two z-scores. Higher values mean greater acute perturbation. Do not choose a “ready/not-ready” threshold from postoperative outcomes. Quartiles are diagnostic displays only. Albumin ratio, creatinine, ALT and platelet response are secondary components after unit/assay validation; a data-derived multivariable score is exploratory and must be cross-fitted.

Primary postoperative outcome is the binary day-5 biochemical hepatic-dysfunction signal from [prior hypothesis]: maximum total bilirubin on postoperative days [5,8) >=1.50 times the latest value in [-7,0) AND maximum INR in [5,8) >= preoperative INR +0.20. It is a prespecified moderate paired change, feasible at 74 events, but not validated ISGLS PHLF. Co-primary continuous components are log(postoperative/preoperative bilirubin ratio) and postoperative-minus-preoperative INR. Report the composite only with both components and their concordance; a threshold-only result is insufficient.

Primary selected-resection estimand: adjusted difference in predicted risk of the joint signal for a 28-day longer last-TACE-to-operation interval at acute-injury-score percentiles 25, 50 and 75, derived from a continuous timing spline multiplied by continuous injury score. Also report standardized risk curves over the empirical 10th–90th timing percentiles. This asks whether acute injury modifies a prognostic timing association; it does not compare assignable treatment strategies.

Selection estimands are separate and descriptive: among all fixed-landmark eligible patients, cumulative recorded incidence by day 180 of resection, repeat locoregional treatment and post-landmark institutional observation, stratified by acute-lab availability and acute-injury quantile where measurable. Acute-lab missingness is itself an observation-process variable; no outcome is assigned to those without surgery.

ANALYSIS, BASELINES, AND GATES
Fit (1) unadjusted continuous timing spline; (2) timing + continuous acute-injury score + interaction; (3) baseline-adjusted model; and (4) fully case-mix-adjusted model only after validation. Use restricted cubic spline knots at empirical 10th/35th/65th/90th percentiles, not a 60-day threshold. Bootstrap patients for 95% CIs; penalized logistic regression is prespecified if separation/interaction instability occurs. Compare the interaction model against timing-only and no-interaction baselines by likelihood-ratio test, calibration slope/intercept and bootstrap optimism; predictive improvement alone is not the clinical claim.

Covariates available before the last TACE: age, sex, calendar year, service, number and spacing of prior TACE/other HCC treatments, pre-TACE bilirubin/INR/albumin/creatinine/platelets/ALT/hemoglobin, cirrhosis/viral hepatitis/portal-hypertension/ascites/renal/diabetes/infection diagnoses, utilization and measurement intensity, and tumor features from reports already available. For the selected-resection association, separately add the latest preoperative liver tests and imaging response plus validated operation extent/approach. Present operation extent both included and omitted because it is chosen after the waiting interval and may mediate/channel the association. Never use postoperative information as a covariate.

Observation gate: tabulate inclusion and paired-outcome completeness by interval, acute score, calendar year, assay label and service; use inverse-probability-of-outcome-observation weighting as sensitivity, never impute postoperative outcomes. Stop clinical interpretation if weighted ESS <100 in either half of timing support, >10% of weight is carried by <=10 patients, or the acute-score-by-time support has an empty decile cell.

Unit/assay gate: a laboratory director must verify bilirubin/INR comparability and routine/急 handling; inspect paired same-time routine/急 agreement and calendar discontinuities. Repeat routine-only and 2018–2025 analyses. Failure makes the composite unavailable, not merely less significant.

Case-mix gate: dual reviewers blinded to postoperative labs validate >=200 stratified imaging reports and >=200 procedure names for diameter, multiplicity, macrovascular invasion, extrahepatic disease, rupture, response, major/minor and approach. Require sensitivity and PPV >=0.90 for automated use; otherwise fully adjudicate or call residual case-mix confounding unresolved.

Selection/intent gate: two clinicians (hepatobiliary surgeon and interventional radiologist), blinded to outcomes, classify index and last TACE as planned preoperative, conversion/downstaging, bridge/definitive/palliative, emergency rupture/hemostatic, or indeterminate; and classify potential resectability at t0 where documented. Primary quantitative analysis does not condition on sparse intent wording, but planned/conversion and emergency strata must be reported. If intent/potential resectability cannot be reliably adjudicated (kappa <0.70 or >30% indeterminate), no scheduling or resectability claim is allowed. An inverse-odds-of-observed-resection sensitivity may reveal selection sensitivity but cannot recover postoperative outcomes for nonresected patients.

FALSIFICATION AND NEGATIVE CONTROLS
1. Primary falsification: interaction evidence requires the global timing-by-injury interaction p<0.05 and a clinically coherent pattern in both continuous bilirubin and INR components. An isolated composite or arbitrary 60-day contrast falsifies the proposed interpretation.
2. Clinical magnitude: supportive effect modification requires that the adjusted 28-day timing risk difference at acute-injury P75 is <=-5 percentage points with 95% CI excluding 0, while at P25 it lies within -5 to +5 points; the P75-minus-P25 interaction contrast must differ by >=5 points with CI excluding 0. These are prespecified relevance thresholds, not treatment cutoffs.
3. Preoperative pseudo-outcome: repeat the paired bilirubin/INR construction entirely before operation (-7 to -4 versus -3 to <0). A timing-by-injury effect >=5 points or standardized interaction >=0.20 in the same direction indicates readiness-trend/channeling and invalidates a postoperative recovery interpretation.
4. Hemoglobin negative-control outcome: postoperative day-1-to-3 nadir/change, adjusted for preoperative hemoglobin and validated operation extent. A standardized timing-by-injury interaction >=0.20 or p<0.01 flags operative-complexity selection.
5. Negative-control exposure: TACE day of week after year/service adjustment. Omnibus p<0.01 or standardized effect >=0.20 on the hepatic endpoint flags scheduling/service bias.
6. Repeat-TACE checks: compare last-TACE and earliest-TACE clocks; restrict to one recorded TACE. Reversal or a result present only under an inappropriate clock is adverse to the biological-injury interpretation.
7. Landmark/leakage checks: no feature after t0 enters the landmark ledger or acute modifier; no postoperative value enters exposure/covariates. Any violation is computational failure. Display 55/60/65-day cuts only as stability checks; significance isolated at 60 days is falsifying.

PRESPECIFIED RESULT BRANCHES
Supportive: all gates pass; the continuous interaction is significant and stable; the P75 28-day risk difference is <=-5 points with CI excluding 0; P25 lies inside the equivalence band; bilirubin and INR continuous outcomes agree; and controls do not fail. This supports acute perturbation as a prognostic effect modifier worth prospectively validating to tailor timing assessment. It does not support operating immediately in “low injury” patients or delaying “high injury” patients.

Adverse/opposite: greater acute injury is associated with no improvement or >=5-point worse risk per 28 later days with CI excluding 0, or low-injury patients alone improve. This rejects the hypothesized recovery pattern and suggests progression, repeated treatment, case mix or selection dominates; no timing rule follows.

Precise null: interaction contrasts and stratum-specific 28-day risk differences have 95% CIs wholly within -5 to +5 points, components concur, and gates pass. This argues against clinically material modification in observed resected complete cases, not equivalence of surgery dates for all TACE recipients.

Inconclusive/falsified: CI includes both clinically important benefit and harm; only 57 events cannot support stable interaction after adjustment; positivity/observation/unit/case-mix/intent gates fail; components conflict; negative controls/pseudo-outcome fail; or results depend on one clock/threshold. Report cohort and selection structure without a timing recommendation.

CLINICAL AND COMPUTATIONAL LIMITS
Missing essential evidence includes explicit surgical intent for most patients, longitudinal proof of resectability, outside TACE/resection/death, mortality after loss to institutional care, ECOG/BCLC, standardized Child-Pugh/ALBI without units, ICG clearance, portal pressure, future-liver-remnant volume, raw imaging/mRECIST, embolization selectivity, scheduling rationale, blood loss/transfusion/anesthesia detail and management needed for ISGLS grade. Blinded clinicians must adjudicate intent, rupture/emergency status, potential resectability, tumor response, operation extent, complications and true ISGLS PHLF/grade. A prospective multicenter study with scheduled laboratory assessment at day 28 and these missing measures is required before testing a decision rule.

A computational verifier can establish hashes, row counts, exact windows, joins, duplicate collapse, two clocks, no look-ahead, endpoint arithmetic, event counts, spline/interaction implementation, bootstrap uncertainty, controls and whether conclusions follow the prespecified branches. It cannot verify resectability, treatment intent, outside survival/care, true PHLF, causal benefit or a scheduling recommendation.
