# UKB 33: Do low testosterone and low SHBG represent different male metabolic states?

Admission: feasibility_required. Verify participant overlap, measurement dates, missingness and event support before committing this design.

## Hypotheses to Be Tested

Among men, combinations of low total testosterone but different SHBG levels show different patterns of association with muscle-strength decline and diabetes.

## Study population and key data

Men with repeated biochemical measurements and grip strength; testosterone, SHBG, albumin, body fat, and follow-up.

## Basic Approach

Jointly model total testosterone and SHBG as continuous variables, distinguishing strength and metabolic outcomes; estimate free testosterone only for formula-based sensitivity analyses.

## Significance of the Research

Avoid interpreting all low total testosterone as the same endocrine abnormality.

## Main Challenges

Blood collection timing and disease affect hormone levels; estimated free values are not equivalent to measured values.

## References

Relevant background or data description, not proof of hypothesis validity or novelty: https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-025-60058-z; field definitions: https://biobank.ndph.ox.ac.uk/ukb/field.cgi?id=31; https://biobank.ndph.ox.ac.uk/ukb/field.cgi?id=30850; https://biobank.ndph.ox.ac.uk/ukb/field.cgi?id=30830

## Life Sciences

Sex, hormones, and metabolic redistribution

## Competing Explanations

Obesity and chronic diseases simultaneously lower SHBG and total testosterone and increase risk.

## Minimal validation and falsification criteria

First check consistency across repeated measurements and blood-draw timing; if the difference disappears after controlling for fat and excluding early illness, limit the interpretation.
