## 1. Unresolved question and rival

After an intubated ICU patient first meets basic stabilization criteria (t\*), does **continued deep
sedation causally delay successful liberation** from mechanical ventilation?

- **Leading claim (C):** sedation depth is on the causal path — sustained deep sedation in the window
  just after t\* prolongs time to successful extubation.
- **Strongest rival (R):** *confounding by indication / reverse causation* — patients who stay deeply
  sedated are systematically sicker and more encephalopathic, and the RASS depth that looks like
  "treatment" partly **is** the severity/arousal state it is credited with causing. [K3] shows deep
  RASS strongly tracks mortality; [K1] is itself an *observational* claim, so it cannot separate C from R.
- **Measurement rival (R2):** apparent "deep sedation" may reflect charting practice rather than
  delivered sedation (documentation completeness).

**Separating observation:** re-anchor the cohort at t\* (not ICU admission); compare *sustained deep*
vs *light* sedation for the hazard of 7-day successful liberation, sequentially adjusting (M0 severity,
M1 +neuro/GCS, M2 +drug composition), with a **negative-control exposure** (analgesia dose) and a
permuted-label control. Under C the effect survives M0–M2 and the negative-control exposure is null;
under R it attenuates at M1 and the analgesia dose shows a parallel spurious effect.

## 2. Clinical importance

Time to liberation drives ICU length of stay, VAP and delirium. If depth is **causal** (C), a
light-sedation target is a lever that changes outcome; if depth is mainly a **severity marker** (R),
depth-only protocols will not help and may mislead. Resolving C vs R is consequential either way.

## 4. Design

- **Population:** adults (≥18) with an ICU stay containing invasive ventilation (**225792**) that
  crosses a stabilization window; not restricted to sepsis. Exclude stays ended (extubated or death)
  before t\*. Unplanned extubation is not "successful liberation".
- **Landmark t\*:** first time with sustained MAP≥65 mmHg for ≥2 h **and** vasopressor-free for ≥2 h
  **and** still invasively ventilated (structured query over chartevents/inputevents/procedureevents).
- **Predictor window:** [t\*, t\*+12 h]. Depth = fraction of RASS observations in [−5,−3].
  Exposure = *sustained deep* if ≥50 % of observations are deep, else *light/other* (X prespecified).
- **Outcome:** successful liberation = extubation (**227194**) without re-intubation within 48 h and
  alive, within 7 days of t\*.
- **Covariate ladder:** M0 = age, sex, admission type, pre-t\* LOS, MAP min, lactate, vasopressor use,
  PEEP, PaO2/FiO2. M1 = M0 + GCS at t\*. M2 = M1 + sedative drug class.
- **Split:** earliest 70 % / latest 30 % of stays by `icustays.intime` (locked before fitting).

## 5. Estimand, baseline vs substantive alternative (same question)

- **Baseline B0 (interpretable):** multivariable Cox / fixed-horizon logistic for 7-day liberation with
  depth as (linear score and deep-vs-light contrast), covariates M0→M2; report adjusted HRs with CIs.
  B0 answers "does observed depth predict liberation after adjustment".
- **Alternative A1 (mechanistic/time-varying):** target-trial emulation with sedation updated in 6 h
  bins, stabilized IPTW / g-computation for "always-light" vs observed sedation; plus a joint latent
  severity+sedation dynamic factor to test whether a single latent severity axis **absorbs** the depth
  coefficient. A1 can reveal residual/latent confounding that B0's observed covariates cannot, i.e.
  it distinguishes "depth is causal" from "depth is a noisy readout of one latent severity axis".
- **Approx budget:** CPU, <1 h, EM/grid, no GPU. **Reason for two arms:** they test the same scientific
  uncertainty (C vs R) with different identifiability assumptions; A1 is selected only if a bounded
  diagnostic shows B0's M1 adjustment leaves meaningful depth signal.

## 6. Falsification criteria (prespecified)

- **Supportive (C):** adjusted HR(deep vs light) <1 with 95 % CI excluding 1, survives M0, M1, M2;
  negative-control analgesia exposure null (CI includes 1); permutation control null.
- **Adverse (R / severity-marker):** deep-vs-light association attenuates to equivalence (HR within
  0.9–1.1) once M1 (neuro/GCS) is included, **or** the negative-control analgesia exposure shows a
  similarly "protective" effect, **or** A1's latent factor absorbs the depth coefficient.
- **Inconclusive:** wide CI, minimum-events power gate failed, deep-sedation class too rare (RASS
  sparsity), or the signal is M2/drug-composition-driven.

## 7. Interpretation of outcomes and limits

Supportive → supports targeting sedation depth as a causal lever (still needs RCT confirmation).
Adverse → RASS depth is largely a severity marker; deprioritize depth-only protocols.
Inconclusive → need better/more sedation documentation before a decision.
**Limits:** single-centre retrospective; RASS documentation gaps (K2 supports scale validity but not
charting completeness); confounding by indication only partly addressed; no causal/mechanistic claim
from association alone; no genetic data; cohort prevalence and power are **not** established in
discovery (the compile-time probe + power gate can legitimately return inconclusive).

## 9. Key references

[K1] Shehabi Y, et al. *Early intensive care sedation predicts long-term mortality in ventilated
critically ill patients.* Am J Respir Crit Care Med 2012 — early deep sedation (RASS −3..−5)
independently predicted delayed extubation (HR 0.90) and higher hospital/180-day mortality. This is
the closest prior evidence **for** the leading claim, and an **observational** result that cannot
separate C from R. [K2] Sessler CN, et al. *The Richmond Agitation-Sedation Scale: validity and
reliability in adult ICU patients.* Am J Respir Crit Care Med 2002 — RASS is a valid, reliable 10-level
scale (r=0.956; κ=0.73), bounding the exposure measurement. [K3] Coşkun Yaş S, Aslaner MA.
