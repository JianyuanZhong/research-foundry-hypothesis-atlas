# Provisional hypothesis: hypotension duration versus nadir for subsequent AKI in eICU

## Proposed relationship
Among adults in a qualifying eICU ICU admission with at least one valid systolic blood-pressure measurement below 65 mmHg during ICU time 0–24 hours, greater cumulative time below 65 mmHg is associated with higher risk of a new or worsening creatinine-defined AKI event during the subsequent 24–72 hours, after accounting for baseline illness severity, prior vasopressor exposure, and hospital. Cumulative duration is expected to add reproducible information beyond the admission nadir alone.

This is an association hypothesis, not a causal claim that lowering blood pressure causes AKI or that 65 mmHg is a treatment target.

## Evidence and unresolved question
The observed opening is that a single nadir can be transient or artifact-prone, while sustained hypotension may represent a different exposure burden. Existing evidence motivates hypotension burden and dynamic AKI, but does not establish this eICU duration-versus-nadir association. The unresolved claim is whether duration/trajectory adds clinically meaningful information beyond nadir, rather than merely reflecting severity, treatment response, or measurement intensity.

## Exact data binding and planned test
Use the read-only eICU source files and catalog documented in datasets/README.md and binding-template.json. The primary exposure is derived from the eICU vital-sign table (patient_id/hospital_id join, systolic BP and ICU-relative time), restricted to adults and ICU time 0–24 h. The primary outcome is a creatinine-defined AKI event in the 24–72 h post-exposure window, using the eICU lab table (patient_id/hospital_id, item/creatinine identifier, value and time), with a pre-exposure creatinine baseline when available. Use the eICU patient table for age and ICU admission timing, and the hospital identifier for site-held-out evaluation. Urine-output fields are secondary and gated on semantic/timing validation; they must not silently define the primary endpoint.

The baseline model will use nadir plus prespecified severity, prior vasopressor, and hospital terms. A time-aware alternative will use cumulative duration and trajectory features from the same exposure window. Compare them with patient-level or hospital-held-out splits, calibration/discrimination and uncertainty intervals, and prespecified falsification checks for measurement intensity, artifact, and outcome timing. Supportive results require reproducible incremental information and stable direction across held-out hospitals; adverse results include no incremental information, reversal, or instability; inconclusive results include wide uncertainty, sparse outcome support, or unresolved urine/creatinine semantics.

## Missing evidence and limitations
No computed effect is claimed. The exact local urine-output semantics, creatinine sampling adequacy, and whether the available fields support a fully adjudicated KDIGO endpoint remain to be verified from source rows. Clinical adjudication, treatment intent, and causal interpretation require additional data or expert review.
