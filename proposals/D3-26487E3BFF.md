# Within-Cell Temporal Feedback of Macrophage-FLS Inflammatory Signaling in RA Synovial Tissue

## Scientific Opening

The parent hypothesis ([prior hypothesis]) proposes that macrophage-FLS inflammatory signaling in RA is bidirectional with macrophage activation preceding FLS response. However, the parent design uses bulk expression profiles from sorted cell populations (RSEC matrices from E-MTAB-8873), which cannot resolve within-cell temporal dynamics. Bulk expression conflates changes in cell-type composition (e.g., increased macrophage infiltration) with changes in the transcriptional state of individual cells.

The D3-M1 expert seed asks precisely this: "Is a signal better explained by measured cell abundance, within-cell state, or both?" This evolution addresses that limitation by using single-cell and paired coculture data to resolve the within-cell vs. composition distinction.

The available datasets provide:
- **E-MTAB-8873**: Paired coculture conditions (Mpos vs Mneg, MerTK inhibitor, LPS pre-treatment) across 5 donors. RSEC matrices are bulk, but the paired design allows donor-aware comparison.
- **GSE95588**: Time-course data (d1-d4) with 70 samples. Donor identity is unresolved.
- **E-MTAB-8322**: Single-cell RNA-seq data from RA synovial tissue (multiple donors: SA220, SA225, SA227, SA139, SA154, SA159, SA149P, RemSA132, RemSA160, RemSA161, RemSA174, ResistantSA144, ResistantSA145, ResistantSA151, ResistantSA166, NaiveSA131, NaiveSA138, NaiveSA146, NaiveSA172, UPASA149, UPASA153, HC0547, HC0572, HC0701, HC0732).
- **E-MTAB-8316**: Paired coculture and inhibitor conditions.

## Hypothesis

**Hypothesis**: In RA synovial tissue, the inflammatory signaling between macrophages and FLS is **bidirectional with macrophage activation preceding FLS response**, and this feedback is a **within-cell phenomenon** (i.e., the transcriptional state of individual FLS cells changes in response to macrophage activation, not merely a change in the proportion of inflammatory cell types).

Specifically:
1. **Within-cell component**: Individual FLS cells in macrophage-positive conditions show higher expression of inflammatory genes (TNF, IL6, IL1B) and FLS-derived signal genes (IL34, CCL2, CXCL12) than FLS cells in macrophage-negative conditions, after accounting for cell-type composition.
2. **Temporal precedence**: Macrophage activation genes (CD68, CD163, TNF) are upregulated before FLS inflammatory genes in the time-course, indicating macrophages as the initiator.
3. **Not composition-driven**: The FLS response is not explained by changes in the proportion of macrophages or other inflammatory cell types.

This hypothesis is falsifiable: if the FLS response is identical regardless of macrophage activation state after accounting for composition, the within-cell component is not supported. If the FLS response is proportional to macrophage number rather than activation state, the composition-driven model is supported.

## Competing Explanations with Specific Observable Predictions

### 1. Within-cell bidirectional feedback (leading claim)
- **Mechanism**: Individual FLS cells change their transcriptional state in response to macrophage activation. The feedback is bidirectional: macrophage-derived signals drive FLS activation, and FLS-derived signals reinforce macrophage activation.
- **Specific observable predictions**:
  - (a) In E-MTAB-8322 single-cell data, FLS cells in macrophage-positive conditions (or in tissue with higher macrophage density) show higher expression of inflammatory genes (TNF, IL6, IL1B) and FLS-derived signal genes (IL34, CCL2, CXCL12) than FLS cells in macrophage-negative conditions, after accounting for cell-type composition.
  - (b) In E-MTAB-8873 paired coculture data, the within-cell component of FLS inflammatory gene expression is significantly higher in Mpos vs Mneg conditions (donor-aware mixed model, p < 0.05 FDR-corrected), after accounting for macrophage number.
  - (c) In GSE95588 time-course, macrophage activation genes are upregulated at d1, FLS inflammatory genes at d2-d4, AND FLS-derived signal genes (IL34, CCL2) are upregulated at d3-d4, indicating a feedback loop with temporal ordering.
  - (d) MerTK inhibitor treatment significantly reduces the within-cell component of FLS inflammatory gene expression (p < 0.05), indicating that the FLS response is dependent on macrophage activation state.

### 2. Composition-driven model (strongest rival)
- **Mechanism**: The apparent "feedback" is an artifact of changes in cell composition (e.g., increased macrophage infiltration) rather than a genuine dynamic interaction between cell types. FLS response is proportional to macrophage number, not activation state.
- **Specific observable predictions**:
  - (a) In E-MTAB-8322 single-cell data, FLS inflammatory gene expression is NOT significantly different between macrophage-positive and macrophage-negative conditions after accounting for cell-type composition (p > 0.1).
  - (b) FLS inflammatory gene expression is proportional to macrophage number (e.g., in conditions with more macrophages, FLS response is higher).
  - (c) In GSE95588 time-course, macrophage and FLS genes are upregulated simultaneously (both at d1), with no temporal lag.
  - (d) MerTK inhibitor treatment does NOT reduce FLS inflammatory gene expression (the FLS response is not dependent on macrophage activation state, only number).

### 3. Unidirectional macrophage-driven model
- **Mechanism**: Macrophage activation precedes and drives the FLS inflammatory response. FLS are passive recipients of macrophage-derived signals. FLS-derived signals do not reinforce macrophage activation.
- **Specific observable predictions**:
  - (a) FLS inflammatory gene expression is significantly higher in Mpos vs Mneg conditions.
  - (b) FLS-derived signal genes (IL34, CCL2, CXCL12) are NOT significantly upregulated in Mpos vs Mneg conditions.
  - (c) In GSE95588 time-course, macrophage activation genes are upregulated at d1, FLS inflammatory genes at d2-d4, with a clear temporal lag.
  - (d) MerTK inhibitor treatment does NOT reduce FLS inflammatory gene expression.

### 4. Donor-specific baseline
- **Mechanism**: FLS inflammatory response varies by donor, independent of macrophage condition. The feedback loop is not a general feature of RA but a donor-specific phenomenon.
- **Specific observable predictions**:
  - (a) The donor-specific baseline explains most of the variance in FLS inflammatory gene expression (R² > 0.5).
  - (b) The macrophage condition effect is NOT significant after accounting for donor (p > 0.1).
  - (c) The direction of the macrophage condition effect varies by donor.

## Falsifiable Test

### Primary test: Within-cell component of FLS response
- **Data**: E-MTAB-8322 single-cell RNA-seq data from RA synovial tissue (multiple donors).
- **Analysis**:
  1. Cell-type annotation (FLS, macrophages, T cells, B cells, endothelial cells).
  2. For each donor, compare FLS cells in macrophage-positive vs macrophage-negative conditions (or tissue with higher vs lower macrophage density).
  3. Donor-aware mixed model comparing FLS inflammatory gene expression (TNF, IL6, IL1B, IL34, CCL2, CXCL12) in macrophage-positive vs macrophage-negative conditions, adjusted for cell-type composition.
- **Estimand**: The fixed effect of macrophage activation state on FLS inflammatory gene expression, adjusted for donor-specific baseline (random intercept) and cell-type composition.
- **Supportive result**: FLS inflammatory gene expression is significantly higher in macrophage-positive vs macrophage-negative conditions (p < 0.05, FDR-corrected). This supports the within-cell bidirectional feedback loop.
- **Adverse result**: FLS inflammatory gene expression is identical in macrophage-positive vs macrophage-negative conditions (p > 0.1). This supports the composition-driven model.
- **Inconclusive result**: FLS inflammatory gene expression is higher in macrophage-positive vs macrophage-negative conditions but not significant (0.05 < p < 0.1). This is inconclusive and requires additional data.

### Secondary test: Paired coculture within-cell component
- **Data**: E-MTAB-8873 paired coculture conditions (Mpos vs Mneg) across 5 donors.
- **Analysis**: Donor-aware mixed model comparing FLS inflammatory gene expression in Mpos vs Mneg conditions, adjusted for macrophage number.
- **Estimand**: The fixed effect of macrophage activation state on FLS inflammatory gene expression, adjusted for donor-specific baseline and macrophage number.
- **Supportive result**: FLS inflammatory gene expression is significantly higher in Mpos vs Mneg (p < 0.05, FDR-corrected). This supports the within-cell bidirectional feedback loop.
- **Adverse result**: FLS inflammatory gene expression is identical in Mpos vs Mneg (p > 0.1). This supports the composition-driven model.

### Tertiary test: Time-course dynamics
- **Data**: GSE95588 d1-d4 time-course data (70 samples).
- **Analysis**: Time-series analysis of macrophage and FLS inflammatory gene expression.
- **Estimand**: The temporal ordering of macrophage and FLS activation.
- **Supportive result**: Macrophage activation precedes FLS response. This supports the bidirectional feedback loop with macrophages as the initiator.
- **Adverse result**: FLS response precedes macrophage activation. This supports the unidirectional FLS-driven model.
- **Inconclusive result**: Macrophage and FLS genes are upregulated simultaneously. This is inconclusive.

**Note**: GSE95588 donor identity is unresolved. The time-course analysis is exploratory.

## Method Alternatives

### Baseline: Donor-aware mixed model (linear regression)
- **Inputs**: E-MTAB-8322 single-cell data, E-MTAB-8873 RSEC matrices.
- **Target**: FLS inflammatory gene expression (TNF, IL6, IL1B, IL34, CCL2, CXCL12).
- **Split**: Donor-aware cross-validation.
- **Outcome**: FLS inflammatory gene expression in macrophage-positive vs macrophage-negative conditions.
- **Uncertainty**: Donor-specific baseline, technical noise, batch effects.
- **Resource**: CPU, ~2 hours.
- **Selection reason**: Simple, interpretable, donor-aware. Suitable for the primary test.

### Learned alternative: Single-cell trajectory inference
- **Inputs**: E-MTAB-8322 single-cell data.
- **Target**: Temporal dynamics of FLS and macrophage inflammatory gene expression.
- **Split**: Donor-aware cross-validation.
- **Outcome**: Trajectory of FLS and macrophage activation.
- **Uncertainty**: Donor-specific baseline, technical noise, batch effects.
- **Resource**: CPU, ~4 hours.
- **Selection reason**: Can capture the temporal dynamics of the feedback loop. However, with limited time points, the model may be underdetermined.

### Mechanistic alternative: ODE model of the feedback loop
- **Inputs**: GSE95588 d1-d4 time-course data.
- **Target**: Temporal dynamics of macrophage and FLS inflammatory gene expression.
- **Split**: Time-course data (70 samples).
- **Outcome**: Temporal ordering of macrophage and FLS activation.
- **Uncertainty**: Donor-specific baseline, technical noise, batch effects.
- **Resource**: CPU, ~4 hours.
- **Selection reason**: Can model the temporal dynamics of the feedback loop. However, with only 4 time points, the model may be underdetermined.

**What information the learned/mechanistic alternative could reveal that the baseline loses**:
- The single-cell trajectory inference can capture the temporal dynamics of the feedback loop, which the linear mixed model cannot.
- The ODE model can model the temporal dynamics of the feedback loop, which the linear mixed model cannot.
- However, with limited time points, the learned/mechanistic alternatives may overfit or be underdetermined. The baseline mixed model is preferred for the primary test.

## Limitations

1. **GSE95588 donor identity is unresolved**: The time-course analysis is exploratory and should be interpreted with caution.
2. **Limited number of donors**: E-MTAB-8873 has 5 donors, which may limit statistical power.
3. **Single-cell data is from RA synovial tissue, not coculture**: E-MTAB-8322 single-cell data is from RA synovial tissue, not from coculture conditions. The within-cell component may differ in coculture conditions.
4. **No direct measurement of macrophage activation state**: The macrophage activation state is inferred from gene expression, not directly measured.
