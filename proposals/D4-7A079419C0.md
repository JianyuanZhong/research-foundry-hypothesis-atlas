# Episode 17 — posterior-thigh MFI and later falls reports

## Unresolved question and clinical importance

Among UK Biobank `ukb672073` participants with visit-2 thigh MRI and a classifiable visit-3 falls response, is higher bilateral posterior-thigh muscle fat infiltration (MFI) at visit 2 positively associated with a fall report for the year before visit 3, after adjustment for visceral adiposity, thigh muscle distribution, prior falls and measured function? The primary visit gap is at least 366 calendar days. The question also asks whether the estimate is directionally robust in the stricter >=730-day subgroup.

Falls can cause injury, fracture, disability and loss of independence. A stable association would justify independent prospective validation of muscle-quality phenotyping; a precise null or adverse association would lower its priority. The advance is not that thigh MRI or falls are novel: it tests whether posterior MFI adds an association beyond muscle distribution/function with an exposure preceding the item’s one-year recall window. This is a selected-returnee association, not an estimate of true-event incidence, causality, screening utility or treatment benefit.

## Evidence, hypothesis and strongest rival

The UKB thigh-MFI VTE cohort supports relevance of MFI to disease outcomes, not falls [K1]. The closest direct overlap reports a cross-sectional posterior/anterior thigh-volume association with “falls in the last year” [K2]; therefore broad thigh-MRI/falls novelty is not claimed. The myosteatosis review describes unresolved aging versus disease/disuse explanations and a need for longitudinal evidence [K3]. None establishes the proposed posterior-MFI association with MFI measured before the recalled falls period.

**Hypothesis being tested:** in the prespecified >=366-day selected-returnee population, one SD higher visit-2 bilateral posterior-thigh MFI has a positive adjusted association with classifiable visit-3 any-fall report, beyond measured VAT, muscle distribution and baseline falls/function.

**Strongest rival:** MFI is a marker of pre-existing frailty, illness, inactivity/disuse, adiposity or prior falls; those factors and reporting/return behavior may generate the association. A 366-day date separation gives at least a one-day gap if the field’s “last year” refers to the preceding 365 days and assessment dates are accurate. This temporal ordering does not remove confounding, time-varying illness, conditioning on MRI/return, recall error or MNAR selection. Persistence after adjustment or weighting does not identify a biological mechanism. The >=730-day sensitivity tests robustness to more conservative timing but changes the selected subset and may be less precise; it is not a separate causal test. The cited review’s etiologic uncertainty is not resolvable with these data.

## Models and analysis alternatives

**Simple baseline:** logistic regression with linear MFI effect and fixed covariate set; report OR, 95% CI and two-sided p-value, plus absolute standardized predicted-risk contrast as a descriptive model-based quantity.

**Substantive learned alternative:** same-row, shrinkage-penalized GAM/logistic model with prespecified smooth for MFI and shrinkage smooths for selected continuous confounders, plus prespecified MFI-by-prior-falls and MFI-by-grip terms. It tests whether association is nonlinear or concentrated in measured frailty/function strata—information a linear log-odds slope loses—not whether prediction improves by itself. Compare on the same cohort using likelihood-based fit and bootstrap uncertainty for MFI curve/contrast; control effective degrees of freedom before outcome inspection and report calibration/influential-observation sensitivity. No post-hoc subgroup search.

Retain measured-response sensitivity as a separate MAR-dependent estimand: among participants with visit-2 MRI/MFI, complete baseline predictors and a valid >=366-day visit-3 date, model classifiable-item response (codes 1/2/3 versus missing/unclassifiable) from visit-2 predictors using cross-fitting; apply stabilized inverse response weights to classifiable respondents. Report propensity calibration, overlap, weight distribution/truncation, effective sample size and balance before/after weighting, then weighted OR/CI alongside unweighted primary estimate. This is conditional on having a timed visit-3 date; it cannot correct MRI attendance, missing visit dates/nonreturn, MNAR, or event/report validity. If positivity/ESS or balance gates fail, label weighting uninterpretable, not a rescue. The response-weighted sensitivity and >=730-day timing comparison answer different uncertainties.

## Falsification and interpretation

Supportive: positive primary OR with CI excluding 1, no influential-data dependence, compatible GAM curve, and directionally compatible >=730-day and weighted estimates with adequate support/overlap. This supports a reproducible association in selected returnees and motivates independent validation with prospective fall surveillance; it does not establish causality, mechanism, true-event risk or utility.

Adverse: precise primary estimate at/below null (CI excludes a positive association), or a positive signal that disappears under prespecified frailty/distribution adjustment or is highly sensitive to measured-response weighting despite adequate overlap. This lowers priority for MFI as an additional falls phenotype in this cohort; it does not prove no association in other populations or exclude measurement error.

Inconclusive: wide intervals spanning null and a potentially useful positive association, insufficient complete-case events, unstable models, failed codebook/quality/positivity gates, or divergence between the primary and nested timing sensitivity without enough precision to explain it. Do not call an imprecise null a falsification. If the primary complete-case cohort is unestimable, return to the Lead rather than silently alter the estimand.

Computationally checkable: exact filters/joins, row-flow counts, fitted models, estimates/intervals, bootstrap/calibration diagnostics, weighting overlap/ESS, and whether narrative claims match outputs. Requires expert review or another study: outcome-code adjudication beyond inspected codes, MRI segmentation/quality, falls-event truth/recall accuracy, residual frailty/disuse/treatment explanations, MNAR selection, transportability and clinical utility. A verifier must reject correct computation paired with unsupported causal/event-truth claims and recognize appropriately limited supportive, adverse and inconclusive interpretations; automation cannot establish clinical truth.

## Alternatives, compute and delivery

The simple logistic baseline is retained because its conditional OR directly tests the question. The GAM is retained for a substantive uncertainty—nonlinearity or measured frailty-stratum concentration—not a marginal predictive gain. A sequence transformer/Delphi-style history model is deferred: the question has a single MRI exposure and a later retrospective endpoint, not a sequence-learning target; added complexity cannot adjudicate events or resolve confounding. No GPU is justified for this tabular fit. A selected-column Parquet scan was measured at 3.6–3.9 seconds on 4 CPUs.
