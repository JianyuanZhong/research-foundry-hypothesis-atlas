# Episode 12 generation alternative: support-aware temporal comparison

Status: design-only repair for `[prior hypothesis]`. No cohort scan, model fit, Harbor execution, or clinical result was run in this branch.

## Decision in one paragraph

Keep the parent question and its lagged exposure/order design unchanged:

> Among adults with a creatinine-defined AKI episode who reach a prespecified landmark in their first eligible ICU stay, does lagged creatinine/urine/recorded-input discordance predict seven-day hospital death or ICU-recorded RRT, component by component, beyond the observed support process?

The executable primary analysis remains the fixed, auditable competing-risk analysis: exact row rules, the six-hour washout, Aalen–Johansen cumulative incidence, support/order strata, and predeclared falsification checks. Add one substantive secondary alternative at the same data and target specificity: a small two-stream temporal competing-risk model. One stream receives renal observations (creatinine and direct urine, with masks and item composition); the other receives support and observation-process measurements (accepted input, organ support, chart density, missingness and ordering). The model has separate cause-specific heads for hospital death and ICU-recorded RRT, with the relevant alive-discharge/ICU-exit competing events. The central learned comparison is not “does a neural network win?” It is whether within-window timing, persistence, and temporal precedence add reproducible component-specific information after the support stream is represented. A support-only model, renal-only model, fixed-summary baseline, and the full two-stream model are all required. This is a predictive/descriptive decomposition, not a causal decomposition and not proof that either stream is a latent physiologic measurement.

## Why this is a substantive alternative

The fixed-summary baseline is scientifically important because it makes the exposure and competing-event estimand inspectable. It compresses the 24-hour exposure window into values such as the latest creatinine, peak creatinine, recovery amount, three urine-bin summaries, recorded input/balance, counts, support intensity, order class and density. That compression loses at least four observations relevant to the rival explanations:

1. Whether support preceded the first renal-defining observation, followed it, or was simultaneous.
2. Whether discordance was persistent across bins or caused by one isolated urine/input record.
3. Whether support escalation and measurement density co-moved with the phenotype.
4. Whether the same summary values arrived through different temporal paths.

A generic sequence model could recover some of this information but could also hide the distinction between renal observations and support/measurement opportunity. The two-stream alternative preserves that scientific distinction in the input architecture. It can therefore answer a bounded question the summary baseline cannot: do temporal renal trajectories provide held-out component-specific information that remains when support and surveillance trajectories are separately available? The output cannot establish mechanism, treatment effect, true kidney function, true volume status, or complete RRT capture.

The alternative is useful only if it changes interpretation of the central support-process rival. A small predictive gain, AUROC increase, or opaque embedding is not a substantive result. If the full model adds no stable component/order calibration or if the signal is carried by the support stream alone, the baseline remains the scientific conclusion and the learned alternative is deferred.

## Matched analysis A: fixed-summary competing-risk baseline

The baseline has two layers.

### Auditable empirical layer

For each L, state, support-intensity stratum and support-order category, compute the observed Aalen–Johansen CIF for hospital death and ICU-recorded RRT with the stated competing events. Report denominators, event/competing-event counts, losses, unknown states, timing exceptions and observation-complete sensitivity. Use the parent’s fixed estimability rule: a support/order cell is not interpreted if it has fewer than 50 risk-set subjects or fewer than 10 events for that component; publish it as inconclusive rather than merging after viewing results.

Use 1,000 subject-cluster bootstrap replicates, preserving all admissions for a subject and preserving the predeclared train/validation/test membership for model metrics. Report percentile intervals, failed/usable replicates and sparse-cell flags. The empirical AJ contrast is the primary scientific result; no prediction model can override it.

### Fixed-summary predictive layer

Construct one row per eligible admission-landmark `(subject_id, hadm_id, stay_id, L)`,  with all features available by `Ltime-6h`. Use the same variables as the two-stream model but summarize each channel with fixed values, slopes, peaks, first/last times, three-bin values/masks, counts, support/order categories and missingness. No sequence order beyond those prespecified summaries is available to this model.

Fit separate six-hour pooled-logistic cause-specific hazards:

- hospital-death hazard versus alive hospital discharge versus continuation;
- ICU-RRT hazard versus alive ICU exit/death versus continuation.

Use a prespecified ridge penalty selected only on the validation subjects. Include the primary discordance/state terms, support/order terms and their prespecified interactions, but no outcome-derived feature selection. Recursively convert cause-specific hazards to seven-day CIFs, retaining the same event precedence, and standardize predictions to the held-out risk-set distribution. Report calibration by component and support/order stratum, integrated/time-dependent Brier score, cause-specific discrimination as secondary, predicted versus empirical CIF, and standardized discordant-minus-concordant contrasts. This gives an executable predictive baseline without treating a regression coefficient as a causal effect.

## Matched analysis B: two-stream temporal competing-risk alternative

### Architecture and target

For each landmark, create an L-specific sequence of six-hour bins from t0 through `Ltime-6h` (3, 7 and 11 bins for L=24, 48 and 72, respectively), with an explicit time-availability mask. Use a small two-stream GRU (one layer, hidden width 32 or 64, dropout fixed before fitting) or a temporal-convolution equivalent only if the declared runtime lacks a stable GRU. The renal and support/observation streams have separate encoders; concatenate their hidden states with shared fixed context at every hazard step. The output has separate cause-specific heads for each endpoint:

- death, alive discharge, continuation for the hospital endpoint;
- ICU-recorded RRT, alive ICU exit, hospital death, continuation for the ICU endpoint.

The target is the first event state in each six-hour interval after `Ltime`, for seven days, with the parent’s exact event precedence. A subject can contribute multiple admissions, but all admissions for a subject are in one split. Landmark rows are not allowed to leak across split membership.

The model is trained with masked multinomial discrete-time likelihood (or equivalent cause-specific binary hazards with mutually exclusive event handling), class weighting fixed from training data only, early stopping on validation component CIF/Brier performance, and no test-set tuning. The model’s predicted CIFs are generated recursively from its hazards and evaluated on the same held-out risk sets as the fixed-summary baseline. The sequence model is not permitted to redefine the discordance exposure or produce an alternative endpoint.

### Required ablations

Fit the following under the same split, preprocessing, epochs/early-stopping rule and target:

1. fixed-summary baseline;
2. support/observation-only temporal stream;
3. renal-only temporal stream;
4. full two-stream temporal model.

The support-only model asks how much of the result is explained by observed support and surveillance. The renal-only model asks whether temporal renal measurements carry component-specific information without the support stream. The full-minus-renal comparison is not a causal mediation effect; it is a predictive stress test of support-process dependence. The full-minus-fixed-summary comparison tests whether within-window order/persistence adds information lost by summaries.

Prespecify model selection before seeing results: retain the temporal alternative for scientific interpretation only if the full model has stable held-out component CIF calibration and Brier performance, and at least one of the following is reproducible at L=48 and directionally present at L=24 or 72: (a) the renal-only/full model preserves a discordant-versus-concordant component contrast in non-support-first strata where support-only does not; (b) the full model changes support-first versus renal-first calibration in a way consistent with the observed order audit; or (c) sequence-versus-summary differences are concentrated in persistence/order features and alter the component-specific, not union-only, interpretation. A small AUROC gain alone fails this criterion.

### Split and uncertainty contract

Use a deterministic subject-level 70/15/15 train/validation/test split, seeded before fitting (seed `20261002`), stratified only on available training-eligible event/support categories if needed and never on future outcomes from another split. All `hadm_id` rows for a `subject_id` remain in one partition. Fit normalization, support intensity cutpoints, class weights, feature vocabularies and early-stopping choices on training data only; validation is for tuning and test is untouched until final evaluation.

For each fitted model, retain the split manifest, training row counts, validation stopping epoch, parameter count, preprocessing statistics and random seed. On the held-out subjects, report:

- component-specific seven-day CIF calibration by decile and support/order stratum;
- time-dependent/integrated Brier score;
- cause-specific discrimination only as secondary context;
- observed versus predicted competing-event counts;
- standardized held-out discordant-minus-concordant CIF contrasts;
- differences from the fixed-summary baseline and from support-only/renal-only models;
- 1,000 subject-cluster bootstrap percentile intervals, with all admissions for a resampled subject retained and split membership fixed.

Bootstrap intervals for test metrics quantify held-out subject sampling variability; they are not a guarantee against training instability. Also report across-seed sensitivity only if the planned budget allows it; otherwise mark it unresolved rather than choosing a favorable seed. If fewer than 50 subjects or 10 component events occur in a support/order cell, label that cell inconclusive for model interpretation even if the model emits a prediction.

## Rival audit and falsification criteria

The strongest rival is that support selection, surveillance, and measurement error create the apparent renal discordance.

Support selection predicts that input, ventilation, RRT recording or other support commonly precedes renal observation and that the contrast is largest in high-input/high-support or support-first patients. The check is the parent’s support-first/renal-first/simultaneous contrast plus input-excluded organ-support order. The temporal ablation checks whether support-only performs as well as or better than renal-only/full for the component contrast.

Surveillance predicts that denser charting makes low urine or creatinine change more observable and therefore creates a process gradient. The check uses non-creatinine `hosp/labevents` counts and non-weight `icu/chartevents` counts in `S_L`, without calling them negative-control outcomes. The process-only density exposure and observation-complete sensitivity are reported separately.

Measurement error predicts dependence on excluded urine items, sparse bin coverage, weight conversion, `storetime`, or current-window contamination. The checks are primary versus excluded urine items, two-of-three versus all-three urine-bin classifiability, weight availability, the strict washout versus current-window sensitivity, and timing-exception counts.

### Supportive pattern

Supportive evidence for a bounded prognostic/measurement claim would require all of the following:

- an empirically estimable L=48 component-specific contrast with a directionally consistent L=24 or L=72 result;
- the contrast is not confined to support-first, high-input, or high-density cells and remains directionally present in a prespecified support-restricted comparison;
- input-excluded organ-support ordering does not eliminate the contrast;
- process-only density produces a smaller or qualitatively different component pattern;
- direct-primary-item and completeness sensitivities do not reverse the direction;
- the full temporal model is calibrated on held-out subjects and reveals persistence/order information beyond fixed summaries, with the renal-only/full comparison not reducible to support-only performance.

This would support a reproducible observed association compatible with a renal-state signal after the measured process audit. It would not establish a renal mechanism, causality, treatment benefit/harm, true GFR, volume status, or complete RRT ascertainment.

### Adverse pattern

Evidence adverse to the renal-recovery interpretation includes:

- attenuation or reversal after support/order restriction;
- a contrast confined to support-first, high-input, high-density, or input-included order;
- input-excluded organ-support ordering removes the contrast;
- process-only density has a comparable or stronger component gradient;
- the result appears only with the current-window exposure, excluded urine items, sparse coverage, or timing exceptions;
- a union-only signal is driven by one outcome component, discharge/ICU-exit selection, or recording opportunity;
- the support-only temporal model matches the full model while the renal-only model does not, or the sequence gain is not reproducible across L or seeds.

These results would favor support/surveillance/measurement explanations within MIMIC, not prove that support caused the phenotype.

### Inconclusive pattern

Declare the result inconclusive when support/order cells are sparse, positivity fails, unknown/unresolved opportunity is substantial, bootstrap failure is material, intervals span clinically meaningful benefit and harm, timing exceptions are numerous, test calibration is unstable, or the model’s sequence-versus-summary difference is not reproducible. An imprecise null does not refute the hypothesis. A sequence model that cannot be calibrated or adds only a small discrimination gain is not evidence for or against renal physiology.

## Clinical limits and completion

MIMIC does not provide measured GFR/cystatin C, muscle mass, tubular injury biomarkers, adjudicated total-body volume or insensible losses, treatment intent, complete bedside collection, reliable post-ICU urine, complete ward/post-ICU RRT, prospective goals of care or external validation. Harbor can check computation, bindings, splits, uncertainty and conclusion-to-output linkage, but not mechanism, causal effects, clinical adjudication or transportability. Completion requires the computed flow, component AJ estimates and intervals, support/order falsifications, baseline/ablation outputs and an evidence-linked supportive/adverse/inconclusive interpretation.
