# Does the remission–resistance macrophage signal travel across donors?

## Hypothesis and clinical/scientific decision

**Falsifiable hypothesis:** In the cross-sectional E-MTAB-8322 comparison of synovial tissue macrophages from RA donors labelled remission (7 donors) versus treatment-resistant (6 donors), a learned, condition-blind macrophage expression-program decomposition will show a reproducible donor-level composition-versus-within-program split in held-out donors that is not erased by equalizing cell detection depth or omitting individual donors. The stricter primary prediction is that program abundance contributes more than two-thirds of the standardized aggregate expression contrast (defined below). A signal that does not transfer to held-out donors, or that tracks capture/run structure, will contradict this claim.

This is not a hypothesis that a state persists, transitions, or causes remission; one biopsy per donor cannot identify transitions, and the groups were not randomized. It also is not an immediate patient treatment rule. The next decision it can change is research prioritization: a stable composition-dominant association supports a matched spatial/clinical study of niche occupancy; a stable within-program component supports an independent perturbation study of a candidate program; an unstable or technically aliased contrast argues against either mechanistic investment until a better-matched cohort is available. The experiment does not decide whether treatment should be stopped, started, or changed.

## Evidence, unresolved question, closest prior work

**Strongest claim supported by inspected evidence:** Synovial macrophages are heterogeneous and differ across clinical groups. Alivernini et al. report differing frequencies of macrophage clusters and remission-associated MerTK-positive populations, plus functional fibroblast responses [K1]. HBEGF-positive macrophages provide an important counterexample to treating macrophage programs as one simple resolution-to-inflammation axis: that work links an inflammatory macrophage subset to fibroblast invasiveness [K2]. A later spatial study ties a LYVE1-positive macrophage network to perivascular niches and response to therapy, showing why cell-intrinsic expression alone can miss tissue context [K3]. These findings support relevance of macrophage state and niche, not causality of the group contrast in this analysis.

**Unresolved claim tested here:** Do the E-MTAB-8322 remission/resistance differences represent a donor-reproducible separation of program abundance and within-program expression, or do they depend on a few donors, cell capture depth, or sequencing-run structure? This is a robustness and decomposition question, not an assertion that macrophage subsets have not been reported. K1 is closely related and likely overlaps the E-MTAB-8322 source cohort; its abstract already reports cluster-frequency differences. Therefore this proposal is not independent biological replication and makes no novelty claim about those clusters. Its incremental value is to define an explicit, donor-held-out estimand and sensitivity test for whether a composition-versus-within-program interpretation is sufficiently stable to justify which independent experiment comes next.

**Leading explanation and rivals:** A reproducible macrophage program shift may be represented partly by changes in its prevalence and partly by expression changes among cells assigned to that program. The strongest rival is selection/measurement: contemporaneous disease severity and medication history may differ between clinical labels, and tissue dissociation, viable-cell yield, capture depth or sequencing runs may alter detected cell/program composition. These effects could create or amplify the same group contrast. Spatial stromal niches are a second rival/context; single-cell macrophage-only counts cannot resolve them. A donor-level association persisting adjustment or holdout is not proof of intrinsic mechanism. If group and technical variables are aliased, this observational dataset cannot identify which caused the pattern.

## Estimand, baseline, and learned alternative

### Estimand and baseline

Target the **captured-cell** difference in normalized macrophage expression between remission and treatment-resistant donors. First reconcile matrix/design/source identities, remove only invalid features/cells using outcome-blind QC, and report per-donor cell counts, total UMIs, detected genes, run counts and distributions. Primary sensitivity includes supplied called cells with valid gene/barcode alignment; repeat after equalizing each donor's cells and detection depth to the minimum support across donors and under frozen low-feature cell filters. Do not interpret cell proportions as intact-tissue abundance.

The simple baseline aggregates each donor's original counts over all retained macrophages (one pseudobulk per donor), applies a count-appropriate normalization and donor-level group contrast, and reports gene-wise effects with donor-label permutation and donor bootstrap intervals. A conventional condition-blind clustering baseline (clusters learned on training donors, held-out cells assigned to cluster centroids) additionally reports each donor's cluster proportions and cluster-stratified pseudobulk effects. This baseline answers whether aggregate expression and discrete cluster fractions reproduce the contrast, but it loses overlapping programs and can conflate a broad state with a within-state shift. Cells do not increase the independent sample size; no cell-level p-values will be treated as donor inference.

### Substantive alternative: cross-fitted count-factor decomposition

Fit an outcome-blind, sparse Poisson/NB low-rank program model to cell-level counts, with donor-specific program weights and gene loadings (candidate program count K=2–6 selected solely within each training split by held-out-donor count reconstruction/stability). To limit overfit and computation, select up to 2,000 variable measured genes using training donors only, retaining the original Ensembl-ID-to-symbol map; project held-out donors onto the frozen training loadings. Donor clinical labels are not used to discover programs. Fit 13 outer leave-one-donor-out folds; align fold programs by loading similarity before comparing weights/loadings. Report held-out reconstruction/deviance and loading/weight stability as adequacy measures, not biological endpoints.

For the donor-level remission-minus-resistant expression vector on the common measured gene set, standardize features by pooled donor-level dispersion and decompose the model-estimated group contrast into program-abundance and within-program terms using a symmetric Oaxaca-style decomposition (split abundance-by-expression interaction equally). Report component vectors and the prespecified relative magnitude

`Q_abundance = ||C_abundance||2 / (||C_abundance||2 + ||C_within||2)`.

This is a relative decomposition magnitude, not a percent of causal effect or variance explained. Define composition/program-abundance dominance as `Q_abundance > 2/3`; within-program dominance as `Q_abundance < 1/3`; intermediate estimates indicate mixed contributions. Compute donor-stratified bootstrap intervals, all 1,716 possible 7-versus-6 donor-label permutations for the aggregate contrast when feasible, leave-one-donor-out estimates, and equal-cell/equal-detection-depth repeats. The alternative can reveal shared co-varying gene programs and whether the same decomposition transfers to unseen donors, which the average pseudobulk signature or non-overlapping hard clusters cannot; it cannot establish a pathway's causality or its function in intact synovium.

**Matched evaluation and uncertainty:** Both approaches use the same remission/resistant donors, held-out donor folds, training-only feature selection, label permutation/bootstrap, and cell-depth sensitivity. Evaluate component direction/magnitude and its fold-to-fold stability; evaluate baseline and learned-model generalization by held-out count deviance and reconstruction. Do not treat better cell-level reconstruction as evidence of clinical importance. Prespecified primary interpretation: support the abundance-dominant hypothesis only if the donor-bootstrap 95% interval for `Q_abundance` lies wholly above 2/3, fold components are reasonably aligned, and equal-depth/leave-one-donor-out analyses do not materially reverse the result. Contradict it if the interval lies wholly below 2/3; a wholly sub-1/3 interval specifically favors within-program dominance. If intervals span 1/3–2/3, the split is mixed/imprecise and is not a falsification of all macrophage biology.

## Alternative selection, split, and compute plan

The baseline and factor model are both donor-aware and directly answer the same decomposition question. The baseline is cheap and transparent; the learned count-factor alternative is selected because correlated, overlapping gene programs and per-donor mixture weights are the substantive uncertainty, not a small classifier score. A scVI-style variational model was considered as a possible way to represent nonlinear cell-level structure, but is deferred: with 13 biological donors, unmeasured clinical/technical confounding and no independent cohort, its extra flexibility would not distinguish those rivals and would complicate program matching. It is not rejected categorically; revisit only if factor-model stability fails for reasons that a held-out learned embedding can resolve and independent donor validation becomes available. Pseudotime/trajectory approaches are not appropriate to these single-time-point data and would not answer the stated question.

**Inputs / target / split / outcome / budget:** Model input is the 13 listed `.matrix.mtx` × `.genes.tsv` × `.barcodes.tsv` triplets plus design/SDRF-derived donor and run mapping; target is held-out count reconstruction and stable factor loadings/weights, then donor-level abundance-vs-within decomposition. Outer split is leave-one-donor-out (13 folds), never cell-random. Inner factor count and HVG selection use training donors only. Evaluate held-out deviance, loading alignment, donor-level component estimate, exact/permutation uncertainty, donor bootstrap, leave-one-donor-out influence and equal-depth sensitivity. Future compute estimate (not measured): CPU 4 cores, 16 GiB RAM, up to 2 hours for sparse 2,000-gene fits, K=2–6 over 13 folds and bootstrap/permutation summaries; no GPU requested because sparse tabular count factorization is the expected workload, not because CUDA is absent. The configured reference timeout is 7,200 seconds; timing remains unverified until solver execution. Do not downscope the cohort or remove folds to meet an unmeasured runtime: if this bound fails, profile sparse fitting and ask the Lead to approve a revised design.

## Falsification, result meanings and next decision

- **Support:** the abundance-dominant estimate passes the pre-registered two-thirds interval rule, learned programs align across held-out donors, and the conclusion survives cell-depth equalization and single-donor removal. This supports a robust cross-sectional captured-macrophage association and justifies a matched, independent spatial cohort to ask whether tissue-level niche occupancy corroborates it; it does not show recruitment, persistence, mechanism, or causality.
- **Adverse to the primary hypothesis:** `Q_abundance` is confidently below 2/3 or the apparent group signal is unstable, reverses with donor holdout/depth sensitivity, or tracks recorded run/cell-yield structure. A confidently sub-1/3 interval specifically supports a within-program rather than abundance-dominant description. If a within-program signature is reproducible, the next useful step is independent matched validation before any perturbation. If technical tracking dominates, revise toward measurement/capture validation and do not nominate a therapeutic target.
- **Inconclusive:** the interval overlaps the 1/3–2/3 region; factors fail to align in held-out folds; there are too few detected cells in one donor; clinical labels/identity cannot be adjudicated; or response and technical factors are aliased. Do not call a wide null/equivalence or refute the biology. Prioritize clinical/run metadata adjudication or a larger matched/spatial cohort before mechanistic claims.

The strongest automatic checks can verify source file/header and matrix dimensions; exact sample–donor–SDRF joins; exclusions; donor-level rather than cell-level inference; train-only feature selection; held-out donor code; reproducible computation of decomposition, intervals and sensitivity outputs; and whether the written conclusion matches those outputs without causal/longitudinal overclaim. An automatic verifier cannot establish clinical remission/resistance validity, unrecorded treatment/severity balance, spatial localization, true independent replication, or a biological mechanism. These require source-clinical adjudication, spatial assay/expert review, or another study.
