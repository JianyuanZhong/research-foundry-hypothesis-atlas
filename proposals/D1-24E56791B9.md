# Residual physiologic instability in the final 12 hours before live ICU discharge

## Scientific deliverable

The future Harbor solver must newly construct an episode-level, leakage-controlled cohort; estimate the association between late ICU instability and short-term post-ICU adverse events; fit a transparent aggregate model and a longitudinal learned model on the same inputs; and produce held-out discrimination, calibration, uncertainty, temporal ablations, subgroup results, and a machine-readable provenance/cohort audit. Completion is established by those computed outputs, not by readiness checks or by confirming the hypothesis.

This is a repaired and data-bound child of `[prior hypothesis]`, the assessed repairable expert seed `[starting question]`.

## Unresolved question and hypothesis

At the ICU discharge decision, does residual physiologic instability during the final 12 hours identify patients at increased risk of ICU readmission or in-hospital death beyond their latest recorded state, baseline severity proxies, and the discharge context? The prespecified directional hypothesis is that greater late-window abnormal burden, persistent respiratory support, and coupled late tachycardia/tachypnea or hypotension are associated with higher risk. A second, substantive hypothesis is that the *ordering and persistence* of these signals contain information missed by an aggregate discharge summary: a longitudinal model should improve calibrated prediction over a same-input aggregate baseline, and shuffling the order while preserving the per-patient measurements should remove that advantage.

What the available evidence supports is narrower: the configured MIMIC snapshot contains timestamped ICU observations, support events, ICU episode boundaries, and hospitalization endpoints. A published 2026 MIMIC-IV post-valve-replacement benchmark (Blattmann et al., DOI `10.3390/diagnostics16030447`, acquired as source `[source checksum]`) reports a longitudinal sequence comparison in a specialized surgical cohort. That does not establish this phenotype, its preventability, its value in general ICU discharges, or that sequence information is useful in this cohort. The unresolved claim tested here is incremental late-trajectory information at a general MIMIC ICU discharge.

“Preventable risk” is a clinical motivation, not an estimand identifiable from these data. Observational association cannot show that delaying discharge or adding monitoring would change outcomes.

## Population, index, and temporal boundaries

Primary population:

1. Start from every row in `icu/icustays` with non-null `subject_id`, `hadm_id`, `stay_id`, `intime`, and `outtime`.
2. Inner join `hosp/admissions` on `(subject_id, hadm_id)`.
3. Retain a live ICU discharge if `outtime < dischtime` and either `deathtime` is null or `outtime < deathtime`. Exclude impossible records where `outtime < intime`, `outtime < admittime`, or the admission join is absent.
4. The primary analysis uses the first eligible live ICU discharge per hospitalization `(subject_id, hadm_id)`, ordered by `outtime`. This prevents a later ICU discharge and its earlier same-hospitalization history from becoming a second independent index. A prespecified secondary analysis includes all eligible discharges, clustered by subject and hospitalization.

Let `t0 = outtime`. The feature window is closed on the right and is exactly `[t0 - 12 hours, t0]`; no event with a clinical timestamp after `t0` may enter a feature. Partition this window into twelve one-hour bins aligned backward from `t0`. Use `charttime` for observations and `starttime/endtime` for intervals; `storetime` is not a clinical measurement time and is not used for temporal ordering. Subject-specific deidentified time shifts mean that cross-subject calendar comparisons are forbidden.

Primary endpoint: a composite of (a) a subsequent ICU stay in the same hospitalization with `icu/icustays.intime > t0` and `intime <= t0+48 hours`, or (b) a recorded `hosp/admissions.deathtime` satisfying `t0 < deathtime <= t0+48 hours`. A later ICU stay is joined by `(subject_id, hadm_id)` and must have a different `stay_id`. The 48-hour death component uses `deathtime`; `hospital_expire_flag=1` without a usable `deathtime` is not assigned an artificial event time.

Secondary endpoints are the analogous 7-day composite; ICU readmission alone by 48 hours and 7 days; death by 48 hours and 7 days; and in-hospital death at any time after `t0` before `dischtime`, identified by `deathtime` when available and `hospital_expire_flag` for the hospitalization-level endpoint. For competing-risk analyses, ICU readmission and death are separate causes, with discharge alive without either event as censoring. Report cause-specific cumulative incidence and, as a sensitivity analysis, Fine–Gray subdistribution estimates. Do not call any endpoint a preventable event.

## Exposure construction

Use the following core `chartevents` item IDs, all verified in `d_items`:

- HR `220045` (“Heart Rate”)
- MAP `220052` (“Arterial Blood Pressure mean”), `220181` (“Non Invasive Blood Pressure mean”), and `225312` (“ART BP Mean”); within a bin use arterial mean first, then non-invasive mean, then ART mean, with one value per source/time and the bin median.
- RR `220210` (“Respiratory Rate”)
- SpO2 `220277` (“O2 saturation pulseoxymetry”)
- temperature `223762` (“Temperature Celsius”)
- FiO2 `223835` (“Inspired O2 Fraction”)
- O2 flow `223834` (“O2 Flow”) and `227287` (“O2 Flow (additional cannula)”)
- device/support `226732` (“O2 Delivery Device(s)”), `223848` (“Ventilator Type”), `223849` (“Ventilator Mode”), `227577` (“BiPap Mode”), and PEEP `220339` (“PEEP set”) or `224700` (“Total PEEP Level”).

For numeric cleaning, use `valuenum` and `valueuom`; reject physiologically impossible values using fixed ranges HR 20–250, MAP 20–200, RR 2–80, SpO2 50–100, temperature 30–43 C, O2 flow 0–100 L/min, and PEEP 0–40 cmH2O. Convert FiO2 values >1.5 by dividing by 100; then retain only 0.21–1.00. These are preprocessing rules, not outcome-derived thresholds. Do not impute an unobserved measure as normal.

For each one-hour bin, calculate:

1. abnormal indicators: HR <50 or >120, MAP <65, RR <8 or >24, SpO2 <92, temperature <36 or >=38.5 C, FiO2 >0.40, O2 flow >=4 L/min, PEEP >5, and support-device evidence of invasive ventilation or non-invasive ventilation after transparent text tokenization;
2. observed-count and missingness for each channel;
3. channel median/last value, within-bin range, and whether the last available value is abnormal;
4. support interval minutes from the clipped pressor/inotrope records and an indicator for any support. Pressor items are `221906` norepinephrine, `222315` vasopressin, `221289/229617` epinephrine, `221749/229630/229631/229632` phenylephrine, `221662` dopamine, `221653` dobutamine, and `221986` milrinone. For the main feature use presence and overlap duration, not incomparable drug doses.
5. optional Foley output in the window and optional last pre-`t0` blood lactate `50813`, creatinine `50912`, bicarbonate `50882`, hemoglobin `51222`, platelet count `51265`, and WBC count `51300`, each with assay and missingness indicators. Do not pool blood-gas and chemistry duplicates without unit checking.

A bin-level abnormal burden is the fraction of observed core channels that are abnormal, requiring at least two observed channels. The primary late-instability exposure is the mean burden over observed bins plus two prespecified clinical indicators: burden >=0.25 in at least two of the last four bins, and abnormal burden in the final observed bin. Estimate its association per IQR and by quintile, with the clinically interpretable persistent indicator as a secondary exposure. The trajectory hypothesis is assessed without selecting cutoffs after seeing outcomes. Sensitivity analyses vary the minimum observed channel count (2 versus 3), use complete-case bins, and replace clinical thresholds with within-training-set standardized continuous values.

## Transparent baseline

Fit a penalized logistic regression (with an unpenalized intercept and penalty selected only in training data) for each endpoint. The primary baseline includes:

- age, sex, anchor-year group, first/last care unit, ICU length of stay, admission type/location, and number of earlier ICU stays in the same hospitalization;
- the final 12-hour features above aggregated as last value, window median, minimum/maximum, within-window SD, linear slope against hours-to-discharge, abnormal fraction, observation count, and missingness;
- presence and duration of each support class, and the explicit persistent-instability indicators.

The baseline is transparent because all transformations, signs, coefficients, calibration, and a ten-row synthetic scoring fixture are exported. It is not a “no model” comparator: it is a strong discharge-summary model using the same information sources and clinical definitions. A second baseline removes all final-window trajectory summaries except the final observed values, testing whether aggregation/trend information matters. A sensitivity baseline adds the optional labs and `discharge_location`; `discharge_location` is excluded from the primary model because it can be a downstream disposition decision and may encode treatment limitations.

## Substantive longitudinal learned alternative

Fit a small time-aware GRU over the same twelve one-hour bins. Each bin contains the cleaned numeric values, categorical support/device tokens, channel-missingness flags, observation count, support minutes, and a time-since-last-observation feature. Numeric scaling, categorical vocabularies, and imputation constants are learned on the training split only. The GRU has a fixed hidden size selected from a small prespecified grid (32, 64), dropout selected on validation data, and a single sigmoid output for each endpoint. The model receives no data after `t0`, discharge note, or outcome-derived feature.

This is scientifically substantive rather than a complexity bonus: it can represent persistence, abrupt deterioration/recovery, coupled channels, and the order in which support and vital abnormalities occur. The aggregate baseline discards those temporal configurations when two episodes have the same summary statistics. The learned model is not used to claim a causal mechanism; post-hoc analysis is descriptive and held out.

The central representation tests are:

- raw chronological bins versus a time-shuffled version preserving each patient’s per-channel values, missingness, and bin count;
- all 12 bins versus only the final bin;
- all channels versus vital-only and respiratory-support-only inputs;
- GRU versus baseline matched for the same patient split and endpoint.

Use permutation/occlusion of each bin and channel on the held-out set to quantify whether the model relies on late persistence, recovery, or missingness. Do not interpret attention weights as clinical explanations.

## Splitting, analysis, and uncertainty

Use a patient-level split by `subject_id` so no person appears in more than one partition; all stays from a patient remain together. Stratify approximately 70/15/15 on the primary composite where feasible. Freeze the split before outcome evaluation and use the same split for every model and ablation. Standardization, imputation, token vocabulary, hyperparameter choice, and threshold selection are training/validation-only.

Report test AUROC, AUPRC against the test prevalence, Brier score, calibration intercept and slope, calibration plots, and decision curves at thresholds selected without test labels. Use five fixed random seeds for model fitting, aggregate predictions before calculating the primary metric, and calculate 95% bootstrap intervals clustered by subject (or repeated patient-level bootstrap). For the exposure analysis, fit a prespecified multivariable logistic model and report odds ratios with robust standard errors clustered by subject/hospitalization; for time-to-event secondary analyses report cause-specific hazard ratios and cumulative incidence. Compare paired test predictions with a paired bootstrap; do not use a small metric difference as evidence of clinical utility.

Primary claims require both:

1. a monotonic association between increasing late abnormal burden and the 48-hour composite, with an uncertainty interval that excludes a clinically trivial association chosen before fitting (for example, OR per IQR above 1.10); and
2. a calibrated incremental result for the learned alternative over the aggregate baseline, with a prespecified minimum absolute AUPRC improvement of 0.02 *and* no clinically important worsening in Brier score or calibration slope.

The numerical thresholds are decision rules for interpretation, not guarantees of clinical importance; report the full continuous estimates and intervals regardless.

## Falsification and interpretation

Supportive evidence would be a reproducible burden-response relationship after adjustment for ICU course and observation density; greater risk for persistent coupled instability; and a sequence model whose held-out calibration and AUPRC improve over the aggregate baseline, with performance falling when temporal order is shuffled. Concordance across 48-hour, 7-day, and competing-risk analyses would strengthen (but not prove) generality.

Adverse evidence would be a flat or reversed burden-response relationship; instability appearing only because of measurement frequency, care unit, or discharge destination; no learned-model advantage over the same-input aggregate baseline; or unchanged performance after time shuffling. An apparent association that vanishes in the first-discharge, vital-only, or missingness-adjusted analysis would falsify the stronger claim that residual physiology itself is informative.

Inconclusive evidence includes wide intervals from low event counts, poor support-channel coverage, severe nonrandom charting, failed calibration, or discordant endpoint definitions. It should be reported as inconclusive, not as confirmation or refutation. A model with better AUROC but worse calibration or only a tiny AUPRC gain does not establish a clinically useful advance.

Computationally checkable claims are cohort construction, temporal leakage exclusion, item/column provenance, endpoint derivation, model fitting, held-out metrics, uncertainty, and whether order-shuffling changes predictions. Claims requiring clinical adjudication or additional data include whether an ICU readmission was preventable, whether discharge should have been delayed, whether a documented oxygen device accurately reflects delivered support, treatment limitations, staffing/bed constraints, patient goals, and external validity. Those require clinician review, prospective implementation, and ideally an external hospital cohort or intervention study.

## Alternatives considered and deferral record

- Transparent aggregate logistic regression is the required baseline because it is auditable and clinically interpretable, but it cannot distinguish different temporal arrangements with identical summaries.
- A time-aware GRU is selected as the learned alternative because the 12-bin sequence is long enough for persistence/order questions, the inputs are structured, and the model can be fit within the planning envelope. Its scientific value is the test of temporal organization, not a small leaderboard gain.
- A tokenized Transformer, motivated by the inspected Delphi methods guide and the related MIMIC sequence benchmark, was deferred as the primary alternative. It could capture longer-range interactions, but a 12-bin, mostly structured discharge window makes the added capacity harder to distinguish from overfitting. It may be a sensitivity model if the GRU/order test is positive and the event count supports it.
- A mechanistic hidden-state recovery model (states such as stable, supported, improving, and deteriorating) was deferred because no validated clinician-adjudicated state labels exist and oxygen-device text is ambiguous. It becomes scientifically useful only after a prespecified state ontology and expert review are available; it is not silently substituted for the GRU.
- Narrative discharge-note modeling was excluded from the primary question because notes can contain the discharge decision and retrospective outcome information, and the primary scientific target is structured residual physiology. It could be a separate study after leakage adjudication.
