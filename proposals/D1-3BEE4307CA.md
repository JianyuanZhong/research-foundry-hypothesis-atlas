# Source-bound successor: dual patient-slot and whole-accession capacity for HCC documentary-M2 prioritization

Parent anchors: `[prior hypothesis]` (patient identity/content retention with explicit as-of visibility bounds) and `[prior hypothesis]` (monotone whole-version onset and identity-robust 24-hour contracts).

## 1. Substantive repair and unresolved clinical question

The parent designs repair two major validity problems: an aggregate coverage curve may cover the wrong patients, and eventual stored report content must not be called available before the decision cutoff. One consequential decision-validity weakness remains. Both still treat a fixed queue of (K) patients as fixed review capacity. That is not a fixed review workload when selected patients have different numbers of eligible examination accessions or multirow report payloads. A model can appear superior at (K) patient positions by preferentially selecting patients whose complete documentary bundle is much larger. Conversely, truncating a bundle or allowing unavailable accessions to consume no capacity can create an artificial advantage.

The source itself shows why this matters. A no-sampling structural audit of the exact examinations file read 419,996 rows and found 392,854 nonblank whole-accession keys across 83,390 patient-encounter groups. The number of accessions per group had median 3, 90th percentile 10, 99th percentile 18, and maximum 197. There were 16,534 multirow accession keys, of which 16,527 had nonidentical component payloads. These counts include all examination types and years and are not cohort prevalence or reading-time estimates; they establish only that a patient-slot budget does not determine a documentary-unit budget.

The repair adds a second, source-computable capacity constraint:

- a fixed maximum of (K) offered patients, retained from the parent design; and
- a fixed maximum of (C) eligible whole-accession review units, frozen before 2020 selection.

The primary queue is the longest ranked prefix satisfying both constraints. Every method, source world, onset state, reader/pathology world, year and deletion uses the same (K,C) budget. A patient’s bundle cost counts all eligible whole accession units reserved for that patient, including units whose content is not exposed at the cutoff. Missing content therefore cannot create free capacity.

The unresolved question is:

> At a fixed patient-slot budget and a fixed whole-accession-unit budget, does report-semantic prioritization retain more documentary-M2 patients than the locked acquisition-only, report-surface, masked-semantic and random baselines, while preserving patient identity/content guarantees across coupled accession, onset, source, reader, pathology and deletion worlds?

This is a clinically consequential bridge question for deciding whether a scarce preoperative documentary-review service merits archive-linked silent-mode validation. It is not a claim that accession count equals minutes, that reports were viewable, that documentary M2 is biological microvascular invasion, or that any treatment should change.

The substantive advance over both parents is a falsifiable resource-validity test. A result that survives identity and onset uncertainty but fails when complete bundles are charged against a common documentary-unit budget is correctly classified as a patient-slot artifact rather than a clinically useful prioritization policy.

## 10. Interpretation

Use one precedence:

1. `computationally_inconclusive`: any replay, state, hash, queue, exact arithmetic, witness, deletion, threshold or prose-linkage failure;
2. `feasibility_inconclusive`: required (N,K,C), event count, accession identity, complete reader/pathology book or nonvacuity condition fails;
3. `2020_selection_adverse`: no valid (q^*<1);
4. `dual_capacity_adverse`: valid computation passes source/identity/onset requirements but a comparator/random/deletion margin or patient-content guarantee fails under (K,C);
5. `temporal_nonreplication_adverse`: locked 2020 (q^*) fails a 2021 identity/content or dual-budget gate;
6. `patient_slot_artifact`: the inherited (K)-only result is supportive but the dual (K,C) result is adverse;
7. `finite_corpus_dual_budget_supportive`: both years pass every dual and inherited gate;
8. append `operational_visibility_unidentified` whenever external lifecycle evidence is absent or its identification width is nonzero;
9. append `workflow_capacity_unidentified` unless local time-motion/RVU/image-burden evidence independently calibrates accession units;
10. `external_bridge_adverse` only in a later archive/workflow study if observed version/readability/action evidence fails; never retroactively relabel the HCC result.

Supportive means only that the locked semantic allocation retains the finite documentary endpoint under equal patient-slot and whole-accession-unit budgets, robust to the registered coupled states. Adverse means a specified patient, accession, state, comparator, budget or temporal witness failed. Inconclusive means the data or computation cannot identify the relevant claim; it is not evidence of failure.

The stronger clinical bridge requires a separate archive/workflow study with immutable report-version IDs and hashes; authored/finalized/released/ingested/retrieved/readable/view timestamps; image-series and report complexity; local expert time-motion or validated work-RVU calibration; staffing and queue measurements; specimen-linked pathology adjudication; and prospective or silent-mode assessment of clinician action and patient outcomes. Until then, no output may say that reports were available before (t_{
m dec}), that documentary M2 is biological MVI, or that deployment is safe, beneficial, fair, causal or transportable.
