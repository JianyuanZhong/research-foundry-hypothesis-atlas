# Decision-time value of remote history before first repeat TACE

## Clinical decision, repair, and falsifiable claim

At the first recorded repeat TACE after TACE2, clinicians already have the contemporaneous pre-procedure assay state. The clinically relevant unresolved question is therefore not whether that latest value predicts another value shortly afterward—ordinary same-assay persistence could make that comparison positive—but whether the patient's earlier state and intervening trajectory add reproducible information once the latest pre-repeat value is known.

This child of `[prior hypothesis]` reverses its primary nesting while preserving its audited population, event clock, assay selectors, exact source bindings, and noncausal scope. For each assay separately, the primary test is whether remote pre-TACE2 baseline `B` adds out-of-fold prediction of the next observed same-assay level `Y` beyond contemporaneous pre-repeat level `P` and decision-time covariates. The confirmatory trajectory representation asks whether elapsed-time-normalized history from `B` to `P` adds beyond `P`; because trajectory is deterministically calculated from `B`, `P`, and their interval, it is a representation/sensitivity of historical information, not a separate independent measurement or biological mechanism.

The strongest claim already supported by evidence is feasibility only: a complete-source, direction-blinded audit found a frozen 319 systemic-record/1,491 comparator cohort, 159/526 first strict repeat-TACE events on TACE2 days 15–90, and complete `B,P,Y` triplets for albumin in 40/118 and total bilirubin in 41/119 patients (systemic/comparator). It also found common-timestamp triplets in 40/118 and only trivial support gain from assay-specific timestamps. No outcome direction, nested-model performance, treatment effect, toxicity, recovery, safety, or clinical threshold is supported by that audit.

The unresolved primary hypothesis is direction-agnostic and falsifiable:

> Among patients selected for a first recorded repeat TACE and an observed same-assay triplet, remote pre-TACE2 history improves held-out prediction of the next observed post-event same-assay level beyond the contemporaneous pre-repeat value and covariates available by the repeat-TACE decision.

The primary estimand is pooled across frozen pathway arms with arm as a covariate. Pathway interaction is descriptive only: approximately 40–41 systemic triplets cannot support a reliable treatment-pathway modification claim. Even a supportive primary result would establish only prognostic information in this selected observation process. It would not show that history should change whether TACE is performed, that repeat TACE causes the subsequent value, or that any medication was administered.

## Decision-time feature ledger and locked nested baselines

All features must be frozen at the repeat-event clock. Features occurring at or after the event are prohibited except the outcome `Y`. The same complete cases, folds, preprocessing, covariate set, and estimator must be used for every nested comparison within an assay.

Let `A` be the frozen pathway indicator. Let compact `Z_dec` contain only variables plausibly available by the decision and supportable in this sparse sample: age, sex, TACE1–TACE2 interval, TACE2-to-repeat interval, TACE2 entry year encoded continuously, latest pre-TACE2 department category collapsed using training-fold frequencies, counts of prior encounters and strict TACE episodes by TACE2, and pre-repeat panel timing (`event−P` hours). For the given assay include `P` itself. Do not include a broad outcome-selected covariate list, post-event treatment, `Y`, or availability indicators defined using `Y`. Missing numeric covariates are median-imputed and categorical covariates mode/other-collapsed within each training fold, with missingness indicators; levels and preprocessing are learned only in training data. If parameter rank fails or events-per-parameter diagnostics are untenable, use the locked minimal core `P + A + age + sex + TACE2-to-repeat interval + event−P hours`; do not choose between compact and minimal models by performance. Report which prespecified rank rule triggered.

For each assay fit ordinary ridge linear regression with the penalty selected inside each training fold from a fixed grid `{0, 0.01, 0.1, 1, 10}` using inner leave-one-out mean squared error; intercept is unpenalized and all continuous predictors are standardized within training data. This nested tuning is part of each outer training fold. As a transparency sensitivity fit unpenalized OLS/HC3 if full rank; coefficients are associational diagnostics, not the primary predictive estimand.

The locked sequence is:

```text
N0 current-state baseline:
Y ~ P + A + Z_dec

N1 remote-history model:
Y ~ P + B + A + Z_dec

N2 trajectory-form sensitivity:
Y ~ P + S + A + Z_dec,
where S = (P-B)/Δt and Δt is exact elapsed days from B timestamp to P timestamp.
```

`N0` is the clinically appropriate baseline: what can be predicted from the current assay state and available decision-time context. `N1−N0` is the primary estimand. `N2−N0` is a locked representation sensitivity; it is not added simultaneously with `B` because, conditional on `P` and `Δt`, it is a deterministic transform of the same two observations. Also fit the parent ordering `Y ~ B + A + Z_dec` versus adding `P` only as a secondary ordinary-persistence benchmark. A large parent-ordering gain with no `N1−N0` gain is explicitly evidence that contemporaneous persistence dominates and historical information is unnecessary.

Trajectory `S` must use exact elapsed time, not number of encounters. Because `B` is selected in a −30-to−1 calendar-day window and repeat TACE is on days 15–90, report the `B-to-P` interval distribution, measurement freshness, and the correlation of `S` with `P`, `B`, and interval. Run a secondary raw-difference representation `P−B`; disagreement between slope and raw difference makes the historical representation unstable. No model includes `Y−P`, avoiding direct mathematical coupling of the outcome to the current predictor.

## Cross-fitting, performance, and uncertainty

Use deterministic five-fold outer cross-fitting, stratified by pathway arm and, when feasible without using continuous outcome values, by assay-availability strata. Generate the fold assignment from a fixed public seed and a privacy-preserving hash of the patient key; freeze it before model fitting. Every imputation, scaling, category collapse, rank decision, ridge penalty choice, and coefficient fit occurs inside outer training data. Evaluate only held-out predictions.

Report for every assay and model:

- pooled out-of-fold RMSE and MAE in native database scale with the no-unit caveat;
- RMSE and MAE divided by held-out pooled `SD(Y)` and cross-fitted `R² = 1−SSE/SST` using one pooled out-of-fold denominator;
- calibration intercept and slope from out-of-fold predictions, with explicit instability if prediction variance is near zero;
- fold-specific sample/arm counts, RMSE, MAE, outcome variance, and selected ridge penalty.

Define improvement so positive favors history: `ΔRMSE_std = RMSE_std(N0)−RMSE_std(N1)`, `ΔMAE_std = MAE_std(N0)−MAE_std(N1)`, and `ΔR² = R²(N1)−R²(N0)`. The primary metric is `ΔRMSE_std`; `ΔMAE_std`, `ΔR²`, and calibration are corroborating metrics. This avoids declaring support from whichever metric happens to be favorable.

Estimate 95% uncertainty for paired out-of-fold performance differences by 2,000 patient-level bootstrap replicates of the fixed out-of-fold prediction rows, stratified by arm. This quantifies test-sample variability conditional on the fitted cross-fitting procedure; it is not a full refit bootstrap and must be labeled accordingly. As a computationally heavier sensitivity, repeat the entire nested cross-fitting pipeline in at least 200 patient-level bootstrap samples if feasible. If fewer than 95% of replicates are estimable, intervals are unstable and the result is inconclusive. Also report corrected repeated-cross-validation sensitivity using 20 fixed seeds; do not select the best seed.

Primary interpretability requires each held-out fold to contain at least five observations from each arm, nonzero `Y` variance, and estimable preprocessing. Otherwise pooled predictions may be displayed but reproducible generalization is inconclusive. Given 40–41 systemic triplets, this is a strict but feasible minimum of roughly eight per fold.

## Selection, timing, regression-to-mean, and placebo gates

Conditioning on repeat TACE and complete panels is a central limitation, not solved by modeling. Report unweighted complete-triplet performance first.

1. **Repeat-event and observation audit.** Among all 1,810 frozen-arm patients, model recorded repeat-TACE selection using only pre-TACE2 covariates. Among 685 selected events, separately model `B`, `P`, and `Y` availability using information available before the corresponding observation; never use assay values or future availability to predict their own observation. Use five-fold cross-fitted logistic models. Report calibration, support, overlap, arm-specific probabilities, final product weights, standardized differences, and ESS. Present overlap/inverse-observation-weighted nested performance only as a sensitivity. It remains a reweighted selected-observation estimand and cannot recover outcomes never measured or remove collider bias.
2. **Weight gates.** Normalize weights to mean one per assay. A weighted sensitivity is reportable as stable only if systemic ESS ≥30, combined ESS ≥120, no probability is outside `[0.01,0.99]`, at least 95% lie in `[0.05,0.95]`, weight coefficient of variation ≤1.5, maximum ≤10, top 5% carry ≤25% of weight, and no row carries >5%. Failure means selection-sensitive/inconclusive, not a reason to trim the primary result. Outcome-blind 1st/99th winsorization is descriptive only.
3. **Regression-to-mean and leverage.** Since current `P` is in every primary nested model, remote history cannot win simply by substituting for the current state. Report performance by training-defined quintiles of `P` and `B`, residual plots, leverage, Cook's distance, DFBETA for `B` in full-sample OLS, leave-one-out changes in `ΔRMSE_std`, and deletion of the union of observations exceeding Cook `4/n` or absolute DFBETA `2/sqrt(n)`. A gain that is confined to extremes or reverses after flagged deletion is not reproducible.
4. **Timing stability.** Repeat the locked 24/48/72/168-hour, first/latest pre, post-selector, calendar-day, and common-timestamp variants. At least six variants must be estimable; at least 75% of estimable variants must have the same sign for `ΔRMSE_std` as primary, and no single variant may account for the conclusion. Report support and interval for every variant.
5. **History-destruction placebo.** Within each outer training fold and separately by arm, permute the paired remote `B` value and its timestamp together across patients, leaving `P`, `Y`, and other covariates fixed; refit/evaluate `N1` for 500 fixed permutations. This preserves marginal baseline distribution and model size but destroys within-patient history. The observed `ΔRMSE_std` must exceed the 95th percentile of placebo improvements for a supportive historical-information claim. This is a predictive randomization diagnostic, not a causal test.
6. **Pseudo-decision pre-event placebo.** Where the event encounter contains enough strictly pre-event same-assay measurements, use an earlier measurement as pseudo-current `P*` and the later strictly pre-event measurement as pseudo-outcome `Y*`, applying the same remote-history-versus-current nesting. Require at least 80 total and 20 systemic pseudo-triplets plus all fold gates; otherwise mark this placebo not estimable. A historical gain of comparable standardized magnitude before the true event shows generic serial measurement structure and blocks any event-specific interpretation, but does not by itself negate general decision-time prognostic value.
7. **Assay coherence.** Albumin and bilirubin are separate co-primary descriptive replications; do not pool raw scales. A broad hepatic-history statement requires same-sign `ΔRMSE_std` and confidence intervals excluding zero for both assays after Holm correction across the two primary tests. A result in one assay only is assay-specific and must be labeled exploratory unless independently replicated.

## Locked decision thresholds and result branches

Because the source lacks laboratory units and a validated minimally important prediction difference, a magnitude threshold here is a study-design convention, not a clinical-benefit threshold. Before outcome fitting, define a small standardized improvement margin `δ=0.05` in RMSE/`SD(Y)` (a 5% outcome-SD reduction). Report the entire confidence interval so readers can apply other margins.

- **Supportive historical prediction for one assay:** `ΔRMSE_std > 0`, its 95% interval excludes 0, and the point estimate is at least `δ`; `ΔMAE_std` and `ΔR²` are non-adverse; calibration does not materially worsen; every fold-support, influence, timing, and history-destruction gate passes; weighted sensitivity is not directionally contradictory; and the gain is not solely a generic pseudo-decision effect. Conclude only that remote history adds reproducible short-horizon prediction beyond the current same-assay state in selected, observed repeat-TACE episodes.
- **Strong cross-assay support:** the supportive branch passes for both assays, Holm-adjusted two-sided primary tests reject no improvement, and standardized gains have the same sign. This permits only a broader prognostic-history statement, not a treatment or safety conclusion.
- **Ordinary-persistence/adverse result:** the secondary parent ordering shows a clear gain from adding `P` to `B`, but `N1` fails to improve on `N0`; or history-destruction performs similarly; or the gain reverses under fixed selectors/influence checks. Conclude that the available predictive signal is adequately explained by contemporaneous persistence and that remote trajectory is not demonstrated to add decision-time information.
- **Precise null:** all computational and selection/timing gates pass and the upper 95% bound for `ΔRMSE_std` is below `δ` (while including or lying below zero). Conclude that this study rules out a standardized historical improvement of 0.05 or larger in this selected cohort under the locked model—not equivalence, no biology, or clinical uselessness.
- **Adverse degradation:** the 95% interval lies below zero or calibration materially worsens with history. Conclude that including remote history harms transportable prediction here and should not be promoted.
- **Selection-sensitive:** unweighted and weighted effects conflict materially, positivity/ESS/weight gates fail, or complete-triplet observation differs strongly by arm/context. Report dependence on the selected observation process; do not generalize to all repeat-TACE candidates.
- **Inconclusive:** reconstruction/count mismatch, fewer than audited triplets, sparse/zero-variance folds, broad interval spanning both degradation and `δ`, unstable bootstrap, rank failure even in the minimal core, selector disagreement, influential-patient dependence, or non-estimable required diagnostics. Do not widen windows, pool assays, change margins, or substitute an in-sample coefficient.

No pathway-modification claim is allowed from this sample. `B×A`, `S×A`, and arm-specific performance may be emitted only descriptively with uncertainty and an explicit sparse-support warning.

## Claim limits, unavailable evidence, and verification

The source does not provide validated laboratory units/analyzer ranges, imaging tumor burden or response, performance status, complete Child-Pugh context, repeat-TACE rationale, technical details/success, complications, acute illness, bleeding, transfusion, albumin/fluid administration, outside care, reliable death, or verified medication administration/adherence/dose. Diagnoses lack native timestamps. The next observed panel is an observation-process endpoint, not adjudicated toxicity, recovery, safety, response, benefit, or a clinical decision outcome. Conditioning on repeat TACE and panel observation can induce collider bias through clinician choice, disease severity, access, survival, and surveillance.

A stronger claim that historical trajectory should alter retreatment requires validated units and clinically meaningful thresholds, clinician adjudication of the decision and intercurrent treatments, imaging/procedure review, complete follow-up, and prospective or external validation. A causal policy claim requires a separate target-trial design with a real decision/action and patient-important outcomes.
