# Provisional HCC trajectory-versus-care-process hypothesis

## Proposed relationship
Among adults with an exact documented HCC diagnosis and a valid inpatient admission time, an ordered 7-day trajectory of liver, coagulation, renal, and tumor-marker laboratory measurements may improve held-out prediction of a subsequent, non-routine inpatient procedure beyond an admission snapshot plus measured treatment indication and documentation intensity. The primary endpoint is a computable procedure-based escalation proxy, not death, ICU admission, decompensation, or clinical harm.

The clinically important unresolved claim is whether temporal laboratory information contains information about later care escalation that is not already encoded by admission severity, routine HCC treatment, care intensity, or documentation practice. A positive result would justify expert review of temporal monitoring; it would not establish a biological mechanism, a treatment effect, or clinical benefit.

## Strongest supported claim and uncertainty
The strongest claim currently supported by the inspected local evidence is that the frozen HCC snapshot contains joinable encounter, diagnosis, laboratory, and procedure records with timed procedure records sufficient for a bounded endpoint audit. The prior audit retained in this branch reports 105,044 encounter rows, 101,457 valid admission times, 104,554 exact HCC patient/admission keys, 338,040 procedure rows, 321,207 timed HCC procedure rows, 15,774 untimed HCC procedure rows, and 20,052 HCC procedure rows after day 7 and before discharge (6,779 keys). These are availability/support counts, not evidence of an effect.

The unresolved dependencies are the frozen routine-versus-non-routine procedure dictionary, assay/unit and missingness rules, event support after the final cohort filter, and clinical adjudication of whether a procedure represents meaningful escalation. No fitted model result is claimed.

## Falsifiable test and interpretations
Compare a prespecified snapshot model with a substantive temporal representation on the same cohort, inputs, split, and endpoint. The temporal model should encode within-assay ordering, change, persistence, and missingness through admission to day 7; the baseline should retain admission severity, demographics, department, treatment/procedure indicators, and documentation intensity. Evaluate held-out discrimination/calibration and incremental value with confidence intervals. A supportive result requires a prespecified non-trivial improvement for the non-routine endpoint, reduced or null improvement for routine procedures, and no improvement in a timing-shifted negative control. An adverse result is no reproducible incremental value or a pattern consistent with care-process/documentation artifacts. An inconclusive result is an imprecise estimate, insufficient endpoint support, or failure of the negative-control/leakage checks.

A positive predictive result does not identify a mechanism or prove that monitoring changes outcomes. Clinical meaning of the endpoint, assay units, treatment indication, and any intervention implication require expert adjudication or additional data.
