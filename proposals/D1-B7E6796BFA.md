# Outcome-blind care-plan vocabulary audit rejects semantic undercoverage as the explanation for exact-label failure

## Clinical question, evidence already supported, and substantive advance

The assigned parents established a target-independent negative result: in an independently selected eICU ventilation-proxy denominator, the exact post-freeze `carePlanGeneral` values `Ventilated - with daily extubation evaluation` and `Ventilated - with no daily extubation trial` are sparse, concentrated by hospital, and do not improve broad hospital-held-out prediction of a later non-peak structured respiratory-setting reduction. Nested resampling, local-intercept recalibration, regularization sensitivity, and component checks did not rescue that exact encoding.

That evidence did not distinguish two explanations. The field might be intrinsically too sparse and workflow-specific, or the analysis might simply have missed alternative Ventilation-group strings used by other hospitals. This child tests the unresolved, falsifiable **semantic-undercoverage hypothesis**: an outcome-blind broader Ventilation vocabulary or an identifiable site-local string vocabulary materially expands post-freeze coverage and improves hospital-held-out prediction beyond both the inherited exact targets and generic care-plan documentation-process controls.

This is clinically consequential because a correct synonym audit could rescue a multicenter phenotyping variable without conditioning denominator selection on the target. Conversely, rejecting semantic undercoverage prevents iterative, outcome-guided relabeling of a documentation field from being mistaken for bedside validation. The strongest supported conclusion remains about structured-documentation predictive fitness only. The untested clinical propositions that any value denotes an actual bedside evaluation, spontaneous breathing trial (SBT), extubation decision, extubation, readiness, quality, benefit, harm, or causal effect are not evaluated.

## Outcome-blind vocabulary and documentation-opportunity controls

Before accessing the outcome, the executable scans all 3,115,018 `carePlanGeneral` rows, normalizes strings by trimming, collapsing whitespace, and case-folding, and enumerates every value for which normalized `cplgroup == "ventilation"`. Exactly eight source-wide values exist:

1. `Spontaneous - adequate` (190,809 rows)
2. `Ventilated - with daily extubation evaluation` (51,862)
3. `Spontaneous - tenuous` (32,587)
4. `Non-invasive ventilation` (26,836)
5. `Ventilated - with no daily extubation trial` (14,907)
6. missing value (7,581)
7. `Ventilated - rapid wean/extubation` (5,705)
8. `Ventilated - chronic dependency` (3,105)

The **primary broader vocabulary** is reproducibly defined as every audited normalized value beginning with the literal prefix `ventilated -`: the two exact targets plus `Ventilated - rapid wean/extubation` and `Ventilated - chronic dependency`. No value or token is chosen by outcome, association, hospital performance, frequency threshold, or model fit. Four binary indicators preserve value identity rather than asserting that their clinical meanings are interchangeable.

The exhaustive audit found no additional spelling variant, synonym, or unrecognized Ventilation-group string. Therefore an empirically distinct site-local lexical encoding does not exist in this snapshot: hospitals vary in use and prevalence of the eight common strings, but do not expose additional local strings in this field. This is itself a falsifiable negative finding, not a reason to invent synonyms from outcomes. The all-eight-value indicator model is retained as a secondary exhaustive sensitivity, but includes spontaneous, non-invasive, and missing-value states and is not interpreted as a ventilated-care phenotype.

Generic documentation opportunity in `[L,L+360]` is represented without value semantics by: any `carePlanGeneral` row; log(1 + all-group row count); distinct group count; any Ventilation-group row; log(1 + Ventilation-row count); and distinct Ventilation-value count. These controls distinguish semantic encoding from the opportunity and intensity of care-plan documentation. They are post-freeze process proxies and are never interpreted as care delivered.

## Computable analysis and prespecified falsification

For each airway definition, the primary broad-deployment hospitals have at least 20 outcome-observed rows and both outcome classes; exact or broader receipt is not required. Every fold trains on all other observed hospitals and evaluates only the held-out hospital. Models use the inherited preprocessing and logistic specification: categorical landmark (and inherited `label3` only for the exact model) is one-hot encoded with unknown levels ignored; numeric variables use median imputation with missing indicators and standardization; logistic regression has `C=1`, no class weighting, and `max_iter=2000`.

Five nested models are compared:

1. inherited pre-landmark baseline;
2. baseline plus generic post-freeze documentation-process controls;
3. process controls plus inherited exact three-state `label3`;
4. process controls plus the four outcome-blind `Ventilated -` value indicators (primary broader encoding);
5. process controls plus all eight audited Ventilation-value indicators (secondary exhaustive sensitivity).

Primary contrasts are broader minus process controls and broader minus exact encoding in both row-weighted micro and equal-hospital macro log loss. Process minus baseline quantifies whether generic care-plan opportunity itself predicts the chart outcome. Brier score, AUROC, calibration, hospital directions, and the all-value sensitivity are descriptive. Coverage reports row, hospital, largest-hospital share, HHI, and exact/broader overlap. Repeated landmarks are clustered by `sid`.

Uncertainty uses 100 nested replicates. Within every observed training hospital and held-out hospital, stays are sampled with replacement, all landmarks for a sampled stay move together, and process, exact, and broader preprocessing/model pipelines are refit for every leave-one-hospital-out fold. Hospitals remain fixed; this does not estimate transport to an unobserved hospital population.

The outcome-blind supportive gate requires: at least a five-percentage-point absolute broader-versus-exact coverage gain; alternatives in at least five unbounded eligible hospitals and at least three hospitals in two bounded definitions; and the broader model beating both process and exact models in micro and macro loss in unbounded and at least two bounded definitions, with every corresponding nested 97.5th percentile below zero. The adverse gate requires all bounded coverage gains below two percentage points, no bounded predictive-support variant, and at least two bounded variants with nested probability at least 0.75 that broader macro loss is worse than process controls. Otherwise the result is inconclusive.

The additional values were exceptionally sparse: chronic dependency occurred in 2, 2, 1, and 1 analysis rows; rapid wean/extubation in 1, 1, 1, and 0 rows. Exact and broader encodings overlapped by construction: there were no exact-only rows; the coverage gain comprised only these 1–3 alternative-only rows per definition. Generic care-plan documentation was more common—860/3,296 unbounded rows had any care-plan row and 161 had any Ventilation-group row—but opportunity remained absent from most rows. Any care-plan documentation spanned 57 unbounded source hospitals; any Ventilation value spanned 34.

All 100 nested replicates completed for every definition with zero failed fold attempts. Broader-minus-process nested macro 95% intervals were `[-0.00250,+0.00593]`, `[-0.00205,+0.00724]`, `[-0.00146,+0.00798]`, and `[-0.00057,+0.03010]`; thus uncertainty does not justify claiming a precisely harmful feature. However, no definition approached the stringent supportive rule, every bounded point estimate was worse under both micro and macro weighting, and all bounded probabilities of worse macro loss exceeded 0.75. The all-eight-value sensitivity was heterogeneous and did not reproducibly rescue prediction: its broader process-adjusted micro loss was worse in all four definitions, while small macro improvements in unbounded and 12-hour analyses were directionally inconsistent and involved non-invasive/spontaneous states outside the primary semantic claim.

The prespecified result is **adverse to the semantic-undercoverage explanation**. Exhaustive vocabulary enumeration increases coverage by only 0.10–0.13 percentage points in bounded cohorts, all additional rows arise in one hospital, and broader encoding worsens rather than improves bounded micro and equal-hospital macro prediction after generic documentation controls. Generic care-plan opportunity itself also worsens point log loss relative to the inherited baseline in every definition, indicating that the result is not rescued by merely controlling for the chance to document.

## Interpretation, limits, and verification boundary

A supportive result would have shown that the exact-label failure was plausibly caused by omitted, multisite Ventilation synonyms and would justify prospective semantic and workflow adjudication. It still would not validate bedside meaning. The observed adverse result supports abandoning **this field's broader string vocabulary as a generic multicenter structured predictor for the frozen chart outcome**, rather than continuing outcome-guided synonym expansion. It does not prove that site workflows are identical, that local interfaces do not map upstream concepts into these eight common exports, or that a site-specific implementation cannot be useful. It specifically shows that no exported site-local lexical alternatives are available to rescue this eICU snapshot.

The values and outcome are chart proxies. No label proves invasive ventilation at the landmark, daily evaluation, an SBT, extubation readiness, extubation, liberation, quality, benefit, harm, or causality. `Spontaneous`, non-invasive, chronic-dependency, rapid-wean, missing, absent, and conflicting records are documentation states, not adjudicated clinical states. Small positive loss deltas are not patient harm. Stronger clinical conclusions require blinded chart review; local interface and data-dictionary review; direct, time-resolved airway/device, SBT, and extubation records; and prospective external multisite validation.

An automatic verifier can check source/input hashes, the all-row scan, normalization, eight-value enumeration, prefix rule, frozen joins and windows, exact-receipt reconstruction, coverage and concentration, process controls, LOHO separation, model features, nested stay resampling/refitting, estimates, and gate logic. It cannot establish bedside semantics, workflow equivalence, care quality, clinical benefit/harm, causal effects, or transport to unseen hospitals.
