# Is laboratory observation history transportable and decision-relevant for early ICU risk monitoring?

Status: episode-16 substantive child of `[prior hypothesis]`. This is an observational prognostic and measurement-process study. It does not estimate a causal effect of ordering tests, monitoring intensity, an alert, or treatment, and it is not a hospital quality ranking.

## Scientific deliverable

The solver must newly freeze a leakage-safe adult first-stay cohort, a revision- and unit-aware laboratory codebook, ICU-relative windows, and grouped hospital folds. It must fit paired competing-risk models and quantify whether patient-level laboratory observation history adds transportable, decision-relevant information beyond measured patient state and simple availability.

Completion requires:

1. cohort flow, exact source/data dictionary, analyte/interface/revision audit and fold manifest;
2. patient-level state, availability and process feature matrices, with all imputation, scaling and category choices learned in development hospitals;
3. held-out-hospital cumulative-incidence predictions for recorded ICU death and alive ICU discharge;
4. macro-hospital and patient-weighted integrated Brier scores, calibration-in-the-large/slope, uncertainty, ablations and process-permutation falsifications;
5. prespecified fixed-alert-burden and decision-curve summaries over a clinically plausible range of thresholds, reported as retrospective workload/risk-ranking quantities;
6. a compact temporal alternative only if event, common-feature and convergence support pass; and
7. an interpretation file linking each conclusion to computed artifacts and labeling supportive, adverse or inconclusive gates.

The primary new output is the paired outer-test difference in macro-hospital integrated Brier score and calibration at fixed alert burdens between a state/availability model and a process-aware model. A model may be statistically better yet fail the decision-relevance gate if its gain does not persist across hospitals or merely increases alerts without improving calibrated enrichment.

## Supported and unresolved claims

The strongest available evidence is feasibility, not a positive scientific result. The prior outcome-blind eICU audit attached to the parent reported 135,680 eligible adult first stays, 2,470 recorded ICU-death events by 1,800 minutes, 208 hospitals, 175 event-bearing hospitals, 195 hospitals with at least 20 eligible episodes, 2,209,118 revision-aware laboratory rows, and 111,655 episodes with at least one landmark-known laboratory row. These counts support a bounded whole-hospital experiment. They do not show that testing history predicts death, improves calibration, transports between hospitals, represents clinical intent, or identifies a beneficial monitoring intervention. This episode also inspected the catalog JSON schemas and raw source headers/sample rows.

The eICU expert seed `[starting question]` proposes that models relying on testing frequency and missingness lose transport performance. The unresolved claim is narrower and falsifiable:

> Among adult first ICU stays with a fixed six-hour early-ICU window, patient-level laboratory observation timing and intensity improve zero-shot, held-out-hospital calibration and risk ranking for 24-hour recorded ICU death beyond measured state and simple analyte availability, with useful enrichment at a fixed alert burden; this incremental information should disappear under process permutation within hospital and baseline-risk/state strata. If it survives permutation or is obtained only from hospital descriptors, it is a workflow/site fingerprint rather than patient-level transportable signal.

A supportive result would be a positive paired macro-hospital Brier/calibration improvement with uncertainty excluding the null, stable enrichment at the prespecified alert burdens, and loss of the process increment under state-matched within-hospital permutation. An adverse result is no improvement, deterioration, poor calibration, or loss of the gain on held-out hospitals; that would support using a state/availability model and auditing workflow shift rather than relying on process features. An inconclusive result is failure of event/common-panel/positivity support, unstable fold estimates, wide uncertainty, or conflicting transport/permutation results; it is not evidence for either clinical strategy.

## Population and temporal boundaries

Use the first adult ICU stay per person from the frozen eICU snapshot:

- age at least 18, treating eICU age values greater than 89 as 90;
- positive numeric `unitdischargeoffset`;
- smallest valid `unitvisitnumber` per `uniquepid`, ties broken by `patientunitstayid`;
- `unitdischargeoffset > 360`, so the landmark is observed.

The primary predictor window is ICU-relative [0, 360] minutes. eICU offsets are already relative to ICU admission; there is no `unitadmitoffset` in the patient table. `hospitaladmitoffset` is retained only as admission context. A secondary sensitivity uses [max(`hospitaladmitoffset`, -360), 360] and labels pre-ICU features separately; it is not pooled with the primary analysis.

A laboratory result is landmark-known only when `labresultoffset` is in [0,360] and `labresultrevisedoffset <= 360` or null under a prespecified, reported null-revision convention. Non-numeric results, unresolved units/interfaces and conflicting mappings are excluded from numeric state features but retained in the audit. No result or revision after minute 360 enters a predictor.

The primary outcome is first `patient.unitdischargestatus` normalized exactly to `Expired` with `unitdischargeoffset` in (360,1800]. Alive ICU discharge before 1,800 minutes is a competing event. A stay still in ICU at 1,800 without recorded death is administratively censored. This is recorded ICU death, not all-hospital mortality, latent death, post-ICU death, or symptomatic clinical deterioration.

## Features and estimands

From each retained analyte in [0,360], derive first/last valid value, slope and range when supported, threshold flags, availability, distinct measurement times, row count, time to last result, inter-test interval summaries, total distinct analytes, stable interface/system strata and revision-known indicators. The state block contains demographics, admission context and measured physiologic/value summaries. Availability is explicit, so missing analytes are not conflated with normal values. The process block adds timing, counts, recency, interval, interface and revision-documentation features; no hospital ID or site summary enters the primary model.

B0 is a pooled elastic-net discrete-time cause-specific logistic model with hourly rows from 360 through 1,800, two heads for recorded death and alive discharge, and state plus simple availability. B1 is the matched transparent process-aware model with exactly the same rows, outcome definitions, folds, state and availability inputs, adding only patient-level process features. Development-fold medians, scaling, splines and categorical mappings are fit in development hospitals only.

The primary estimand is paired outer-fold macro-hospital integrated Brier-score difference for recorded-death cumulative incidence. Secondary estimands are macro-hospital calibration-in-the-large/slope, patient-weighted Brier difference, risk-enrichment at top 1%, 5% and 10% alert burden (or a predeclared supported subset), and decision-curve net benefit over a predeclared threshold range. Net benefit is reported as a mathematical risk-ranking/workload summary with an explicit false-positive-cost range; it is not clinical utility. Report event counts and uncertainty for every burden/threshold cell and do not average away hospitals with no events without a prespecified macro-hospital rule.

Use five grouped outer folds, each holding out approximately 20% of hospitals once, with all stays of a `uniquepid` together. Within each outer training set, split hospitals approximately 75/25 for fitting and tuning. Stratify hospital folds by eligible and event counts where possible. Estimate uncertainty by resampling hospitals and, separately, patients within hospitals; never use patient-random splitting as the primary transport estimate.

## Substantive learned alternative and falsification

The substantive alternative is a one-layer GRU or temporal convolution over six one-hour bins covering [0,360], using the same analyte values, availability, process channels and measured state inputs as B1. It predicts recorded death, alive discharge and next-bin observation; the observation head characterizes temporal measurement information and is not a clinical endpoint. This can reveal abrupt co-movement, persistence and order effects lost by summary features. It is deferred if common analyte support, event counts, convergence, calibration or hospital-level uncertainty is inadequate. Its selection is scientific, not a complexity or GPU preference.

Primary falsifications are: (1) permute process vectors within hospital and baseline-state/risk strata while preserving marginal process distributions; (2) compare with a site-only model using hospital descriptors to detect a workflow fingerprint; (3) repeat on a common analyte panel and stable unit/interface subset; (4) test a negative-control process feature defined after the predictor window; and (5) repeat with explicit revision-known versus revision-unknown handling. A process gain that survives the first falsification is not interpreted as causal; it indicates residual state information, imperfect exchangeability or a faulty permutation design and triggers inconclusive/adverse interpretation.

## Support gates and interpretation

Before model comparison, require an auditable codebook, at least 20 event-bearing hospitals, adequate events and non-events per outer fold, adequate support in the common analyte panel, no severe positivity failure in process strata, and successful calibration diagnostics. The parent feasibility audit satisfies neither the final codebook nor all fold-level gates automatically.

Supportive evidence requires: (a) the predeclared macro-hospital Brier/calibration improvement has uncertainty compatible with a nonzero gain; (b) improvement is present in most held-out hospitals and at fixed alert burden; (c) process ablation attributes the difference to patient-level process channels rather than site-only features; and (d) state-matched process permutation removes the gain. This establishes a transportable retrospective signal only.

Adverse evidence includes a null/worse B1, transport collapse, unstable calibration, site-only equivalence, or a process increment that survives permutation. Inconclusive evidence includes sparse support, fold failure, wide uncertainty, severe missingness/positivity problems, or contradictory metrics. None establishes that more testing, less testing, or alerts improve mortality. Clinical adjudication of test indication, true bedside testing, symptoms, treatment limitations, staffing/bed capacity, and prospective alert response is required for a clinical intervention claim. External validation in a new health system and prospective impact evaluation are also required.
