# Creatinine–cystatin C discordance as a prognostic marker of limited kidney reserve in UK Biobank

Parent: `[prior hypothesis]`  
Seed: `[starting question]` (“Discordant kidney-function estimates and pre-illness reserve”)  
Dataset: UK Biobank snapshot `[source checksum]`  
Status: prospective, observational, untested hypothesis; no fitted result is claimed.

## Substantive question and advance

The clinical question is whether a participant whose creatinine-based filtration estimate looks preserved but whose cystatin-C-based estimate is lower has clinically important kidney vulnerability that routine creatinine surveillance misses, and whether low grip strength identifies a particularly vulnerable subgroup. This could inform whether a confirmatory cystatin-C measurement and closer kidney monitoring are justified when creatinine appears reassuring.

The parent seed said “severe acute kidney injury” and treated poor grip as reserve. Those are too strong for this snapshot. The repaired estimand is the association between a prespecified baseline discordance/reserve phenotype and subsequent first *coded* AKI (ICD-10 N17 proxy), with coded CKD and death as secondary outcomes. “Reserve” is an operational label for the joint biomarker–grip phenotype, not a directly observed physiologic construct. The study cannot establish AKI stage, structural injury, measured GFR, causality, or benefit from cystatin-C-guided care.

The critical-illness reference Haines et al. (CJASN 2023; local readable text at `references/expert-seeds/papers/kidney-function/article.readable.txt`) supports only the narrower premise that creatinine and cystatin-C estimates can diverge with muscle loss in a small ICU cohort that also had iohexol clearance and ultrasound. It does not validate this community-dwelling UKB estimand.

## Hypothesis

Among UKB participants with baseline creatinine-based eGFR >=60 mL/min/1.73 m² and no recorded prior AKI or CKD, those with cystatin-C-based eGFR <60 (the primary discordant group) will have a higher 5-year cumulative incidence of first coded AKI than those with both estimates >=60. The excess association will be larger among participants with lower baseline grip strength.

The primary contrast is:

- discordant: eGFRcr >=60 and eGFRcys <60;
- concordant preserved reference: eGFRcr >=60 and eGFRcys >=60.

The opposite-discordance group (eGFRcr <60, eGFRcys >=60) and concordant-low group (both <60) are retained as prespecified descriptive/heterogeneity contrasts, not silently discarded. A continuous analysis of eGFRcr - eGFRcys and a continuous interaction with sex-standardized grip are prespecified secondary checks; the categorical contrast remains the sole primary estimand, regardless of event counts.

## Exact derived exposures

Before fitting, audit the official UKB Showcase metadata for fields 30700, 30720, 46, 47, 53, 21001, and 21022, including units, allowable ranges, missing codes, and whether 30700/30720 are serum measurements taken at the baseline visit. The local catalog confirms the columns and values but says that complete per-field labels and units are not locally present.

Assuming the audit confirms the observed UKB conventions (creatinine in µmol/L and cystatin C in mg/L), convert creatinine to mg/dL as `Scr = 30700-0.0 / 88.4`; retain cystatin C as `Scys = 30720-0.0`. If the official units differ, apply the documented conversion and record it; do not silently fit with guessed units.

Calculate non-race-adjusted 2021 CKD-EPI estimates in mL/min/1.73 m²:

- eGFRcr = 142 × min(Scr/κ,1)^α × max(Scr/κ,1)^−1.200 × 0.9938^Age × 1.012^Female;
- eGFRcys = 133 × min(Scys/0.8,1)^−0.499 × max(Scys/0.8,1)^−1.328 × 0.996^Age × 0.932^Female.
- eGFRcombined = 135 × min(Scr/κ,1)^−0.544 × max(Scr/κ,1)^−1.200 × min(Scys/0.8,1)^−0.323 × max(Scys/0.8,1)^−0.778 × 0.9961^Age × 0.963^Female.

Here κ=0.7 and α=−0.241 for females, κ=0.9 and α=−0.302 for males; `Female` is 1 for `31-0.0=0`, otherwise 0. Do not use race coefficients. As a sensitivity analysis, calculate the 2021 combined creatinine–cystatin-C equation, but do not replace the primary contrast with it.

Define `discordance = eGFRcr - eGFRcys`. Define grip as the mean of valid `46-0.0` and `47-0.0`, or the single valid side. Standardize grip within sex using the eligible cohort’s prespecified mean and SD; lower values indicate the operationally lower-reserve direction. BMI is `21001-0.0`, used as an adjustment/sensitivity variable, never as a muscle-mass measure. Report raw-field missingness, field-validity exclusions, and counts in every discordance/grip stratum before analysis.

## Outcomes

Primary outcome: first post-risk-clock occurrence of a paired `41270-k.j=N17` code with its same-slot valid `41280-k.j` date. This is a first-occurrence diagnosis proxy for AKI.

Secondary outcomes:

- first `41270-k.j=N18` or `N19` with paired `41280-k.j` date (coded CKD);
- all-cause death using the earliest valid `40000-*` date, with the outcome field’s official semantics verified before execution.

Use exact three-character matching for N17/N18/N19; do not infer disease from narrative text or from neighboring codes. If official field semantics, code/date slot alignment, or date format fail the audit, stop and report a prerequisite failure rather than substituting an unverified outcome.

The outcome fields are repeated wide arrays, not event-level rows. The executable parser must iterate every `k.j` code/date pair in `health_outcomes`, preserve the pair’s slot, discard blank/field-specific missing values only under the audited dictionary, and retain the earliest date after the relevant boundary. Death is a competing event for AKI and CKD, not ordinary non-informative censoring in the primary competing-risk analysis.

## Analysis and baselines

Estimate adjusted 5-year cumulative incidence and risk difference for the primary discordant-versus-concordant-preserved contrast, with death as a competing risk. Fit:

1. a cause-specific Cox model for AKI with age at `t0`, sex, BMI, and the prespecified discordance-group indicator;
2. a Fine–Gray subdistribution model as a sensitivity/estimand comparison;
3. an interaction model adding continuous sex-standardized grip and discordance-group × grip;
4. the same models for coded CKD and death as secondary outcomes.

Use restricted cubic splines for age if the audit shows sufficient support; otherwise use age categories set before looking at outcomes. Use robust standard errors and report hazard ratios, 95% confidence intervals, numbers at risk, event counts, and 5-year cumulative-incidence contrasts. No causal wording is permitted.

Decision-relevant nested baselines are:

- A: age, sex, BMI, and eGFRcr (creatinine-only);
- B: A plus eGFRcys;
- C: B plus grip;
- D: B plus the prespecified discordance × grip interaction.

The main scientific comparison is the adjusted absolute risk contrast, not a small AUC gain. For model comparison, report calibration at 5 years, Brier score, and likelihood-ratio comparison with bootstrap uncertainty; decision-curve analysis is exploratory and cannot establish clinical utility. Compare the primary biomarker-complete cohort with the full eligible population’s availability/missingness profile and, if supported by observed covariates, use inverse-probability-of-availability weighting as a sensitivity analysis.

Missingness and uncertainty are part of the result. The primary analysis is complete-case for the required exposure fields after field-specific validity rules. A prespecified multiple-imputation sensitivity analysis may impute exposure covariates using age, sex, BMI and other verified baseline structured variables, with outcome and censoring information handled without leakage; report the estimand change. Bootstrap participants for 95% intervals of cumulative-incidence contrasts and model-performance differences. Do not interpret a wide interval as evidence of no association.

Prespecified robustness checks are: no washout and 365-day washout; 2-year delayed outcome onset; continuous discordance; combined eGFR equation; sex-stratified models; age-stratified models; BMI adjustment omitted/retained; availability weighting; and adjustment for an inflammatory biomarker only if field 30710’s label, unit, and baseline timing are verified. A result that exists only after an unplanned coding, cutoff, subgroup, or missingness choice is not confirmatory.

## Falsification and interpretation

The primary directional claim is supported only if the discordant-versus-concordant-preserved AKI contrast is above 1.25 for the adjusted hazard ratio (or at least a 1 percentage-point higher 5-year cumulative incidence) and its 95% interval excludes the null in the prespecified direction, with materially similar direction under the no-washout and competing-risk analyses. The low-grip gradient is supportive only if its prespecified interaction ratio is above 1.25 with a 95% interval excluding 1 and the joint low-grip/discordant group has higher absolute risk than the reference.

- Supportive: the primary contrast meets the directional threshold, survives the lag/competing-risk checks, and adding cystatin C changes absolute risk meaningfully relative to the creatinine-only baseline. This supports prognostic value of the phenotype in this selected UKB population, not a treatment recommendation or proof of low physiologic reserve.
- Adverse/falsifying: the primary contrast is directionally protective with a 95% interval excluding the null, or the discordance × grip interaction is directionally opposite with a 95% interval excluding the null. This would argue against the proposed risk mechanism in this operational definition and would not be rescued by a better-performing prediction metric.
- Inconclusive: the interval spans clinically meaningful harm and benefit, event counts are inadequate, or findings depend on one timing/coding/missingness choice. A null point estimate with a wide interval is inconclusive, not falsification.
- Mixed: AKI supports the hypothesis but CKD or mortality does not. Report this as outcome-specific evidence; do not generalize a coded AKI association to persistent kidney damage.
- If code/date auditing fails, the result is a computational feasibility failure, not evidence for or against the hypothesis.

Computationally checkable claims include field presence, transformations, cohort counts, missingness, code/date matching, event counts, risk estimates, confidence intervals, competing-risk handling, baseline comparisons, and whether conclusions match the reported outputs. Clinical adjudication is required for KDIGO AKI stage, admission setting, dialysis, structural kidney injury, true measured GFR, medication implications, and whether a cystatin-C test improves care. Those require linked laboratory trajectories, urine output, imaging, adjudicated events, treatment data, measured GFR, or an interventional/externally validated study.

## Evidence limitations and demonstration dispositions

- Natural-history demonstration: the local readable article text was inspected, including its UKB first-occurrence diagnosis construction and cautions about missingness/selection. Disposition: methodological context only; do not reproduce its generative model or treat predictive accuracy as clinical truth. This proposal uses a transparent kidney-specific estimand and explicitly reports missingness and calibration.
- Cancer demonstration: only `references/research-ambition/cancer/supplement.readable.txt` was inspected. It describes a UKB external-validation case-control cohort, but the main article and full STAR Methods remain unavailable (the README records 403/401 retrieval failures). Disposition: non-use for design or claims; no cancer staging, treatment response, raw images, narrative records, or unavailable main-paper method is imported.
- Bayesian demonstration: the full local readable article text was inspected for its longitudinal EHR/genetic framework and UKB follow-up description. Disposition: conceptual context only; no latent signature or genetic inference is used because this question is answerable with auditable prespecified biomarkers and outcomes, and the configured genomics file is not a sequence/variant bundle. Its reported results are not evidence that the present kidney hypothesis is true.

Additional limitations: UKB healthy-volunteer/selection bias and biomarker availability selection; uncertain specimen-to-assessment timing; absent complete local field units/coding dictionaries; non-GFR determinants of cystatin C and non-reserve determinants of grip that are incompletely measured; no repeated creatinine trajectory, urine output, albuminuria, KDIGO stage, dialysis, measured GFR, medication exposure, narrative adjudication, raw imaging, raw waveform, or sequence variants; first-occurrence diagnosis fields may miss events and cannot establish severity; incomplete/heterogeneous outcome ascertainment; and residual confounding. These limitations restrict interpretation to associations in the frozen UKB snapshot.
