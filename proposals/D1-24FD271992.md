# Provisional eICU hypothesis: monitoring reduction during persistent instability

Status: provisional, untested design draft. This is not a result and does not establish causality.

## Proposed relationship

Among adults with a continuous ICU stay who remain physiologically unstable at a prespecified index, a reduction in laboratory-testing intensity during the immediately preceding 12 hours is associated with higher risk of a time-local deterioration in the following 12 hours, beyond contemporaneously measured severity and recent support. The association is expected to attenuate when end-of-life documentation, discharge/transfer context, and recent support de-escalation are handled explicitly. A residual association in held-out hospitals would be a monitoring-process signal, not proof that changing laboratory orders causes deterioration.

## Evidence-supported versus unresolved claims

The visible eICU catalog and dataset README support that the configured dataset contains longitudinal, timestamped laboratory, vital, respiratory, infusion, patient, and hospital records, with `patientunitstayid` as the within-dataset join key and `hospitalid` for hospital-level stratification. The expert seed [starting question] supplies the opening that testing frequency/missingness may encode hospital practice and may transport poorly. It does not establish that reduced testing causes deterioration.

The unresolved claim is whether a time-local monitoring reduction is independently associated with subsequent deterioration after handling severity, support, discharge/transfer, and end-of-life documentation. The strongest rival is that apparent “monitoring gaps” are consequences of de-escalation, impending discharge, death, or sparse end-of-life documentation rather than a process signal.

## Population and temporal design

Use adult ICU stays with a continuous stay and valid 18-hour intervals. Define an index at a prespecified time after ICU admission and require the stay to continue through the outcome window. The exposure is a prespecified reduction in the number of qualifying laboratory observations in the preceding 12 hours relative to the preceding baseline window, with a minimum observation requirement to avoid treating complete absence of data as a meaningful test. The outcome is a prespecified time-local deterioration in the following 12 hours, combining clinically defined respiratory/infusion deterioration with competing death, discharge, or transfer. Do not use post-index measurements as exposure features.

The exact operational thresholds for “unstable,” “reduction,” and “deterioration” must be frozen after inspecting the complete table schemas, units, time fields, and source-row feasibility. No row counts or endpoint comparability have been verified in this checkpoint.

## Baseline and substantive alternative

The simple baseline is a prespecified logistic or discrete-time hazard model for the next-12-hour deterioration, using the monitoring-gap indicator plus contemporaneous severity, recent support, time since admission, and competing-event indicators. It estimates an adjusted association and is interpretable as a monitoring-process signal.

The substantive alternative is a longitudinal model (for example, a discrete-time hazard model with time-varying severity and support, or a mechanistic state-transition representation) that treats monitoring intensity as a time-varying process and explicitly separates current severity, recent trajectory, and documentation/transition context. This can reveal whether the apparent gap is a stable monitoring process or merely a marker of recovery, de-escalation, or discharge selection. It should be evaluated on the same held-out-hospital split, with uncertainty intervals and calibration, not judged by a small predictive gain alone.

The actual deliverable is a newly fitted comparison of these two models on the frozen eICU cohort, with exposure/outcome definitions, event counts, uncertainty, hospital-held-out performance, and a prespecified falsification/negative-control analysis. No full analysis has been executed here.

## Falsification and interpretation

Supportive evidence would be a reproducible association across hospitals after adjustment, with the association weakening under the longitudinal/documentation-aware alternative and negative controls not showing the same pattern. Adverse evidence would be attenuation to null after handling competing events and end-of-life context, or a similar association for a pre-specified irrelevant monitoring variable, indicating severity or documentation selection. Inconclusive evidence would be sparse qualifying intervals, wide uncertainty, or non-transportable hospital effects; this would justify measurement/process work rather than a causal monitoring claim.

A positive result would not establish that increasing laboratory testing improves outcomes. Clinical adjudication of deterioration, treatment intent, and end-of-life care is unavailable or incomplete in the automated data. External validation, expert review, and ideally prospective or quasi-experimental data would be required for stronger causal or treatment claims.
