# Episode 3 Lead child: a frozen competing-risk experiment for repeated instability at ICU discharge

## Parent, change, and scientific claim

Parent: `[prior hypothesis]`, assessed valid by the Lead. This child keeps its clinically consequential prognostic question and repairs the remaining executable ambiguities: the primary index is selected before any feature or outcome construction; all time intervals are half-open or explicitly tie-resolved; same-time ICU-return/death observations are retained as an indeterminate audit category rather than silently assigned; feature ties, oxygen normalization, coverage, and analytic inclusion are deterministic; and `transfers` is no longer asked to supply an undefined “strict ward” taxonomy.

The unresolved question is:

> At the moment an adult ICU stay ends while the hospital admission continues, does repeatedly abnormal, observed physiology across the final 24 hours identify near-term ICU return or death before ICU return beyond a realistic last-observation discharge snapshot?

This is clinically important because ICU-to-ward transfer determines where monitoring and rescue capacity are available. A calibrated signal of residual instability could support targeted post-transfer surveillance or a prospective discharge-readiness study. It would not, by itself, justify delaying discharge or changing oxygen treatment.

The strongest evidence-supported claim before fitting is limited to provenance and availability: the frozen MIMIC-IV core 3.1 archive contains the linked admissions, patients, ICU stays, ICU chart events, item dictionary, and hospital transfers specified below; the local dictionary confirms the named item IDs and units. The residual-instability association, incremental predictive value, and clinical usefulness remain untested. The expert seed `[starting question]` is an untested question, not evidence.

Primary falsifiable hypothesis:

> In the primary analytic cohort, a larger prespecified proportion of covered 4-hour blocks containing at least one abnormal valid vital-sign observation during the 24 hours before ICU outtime is associated with higher 7-day cause-specific risk of first later ICU return and of death before ICU return, and adding that repeated-burden representation to a last-value model improves locked-test competing-risk Brier score and calibration.

The estimand is prognostic association and incremental prediction at ICU outtime. It is not a causal effect of discharge, oxygen, monitoring, or treatment.

## Cohort and index construction

1. Parse timestamps as datetimes. Join `icustays` to `admissions` on `(subject_id, hadm_id)), and `patients` on `subject_id). Record unmatched keys and impossible chronology.
2. Retain an ICU stay only if `anchor_age >= 18`, `intime` and `outtime` are non-null, `admissions.dischtime` is non-null, `outtime < dischtime`, and `admissions.deathtime` is null or strictly later than `outtime`. The MIMIC-coded `anchor_age=91` is retained and reported as >89, not converted to an exact age.
3. For each `(subject_id, hadm_id)`, sort eligible stays by `outtime), then numeric `stay_id`. Select the first eligible stay as the primary index before reading any chart exposure or calculating outcomes. This prevents multiple opportunities from one admission and ensures a later ICU stay is an outcome. A secondary all-eligible-stay analysis uses all eligible stays, clusters by `subject_id), and is not the primary estimand.
4. Set `t0 = index outtime). The exposure interval is exactly `[t0 - 24 hours, t0)). The six blocks are `[t0-(j+1)*4h, t0-j*4h)` for `j=0,...,5); a row at `charttime=t0) is excluded. Retain only chart rows whose `stay_id) is the index stay and whose `(subject_id, hadm_id)) match it.
5. A primary analytic record requires at least 4 of 6 blocks with at least one valid vital-domain observation. All eligible indexes, including those failing this coverage rule, remain in the manifest and are compared by care unit, anchor-year group, outcome, and chart density. No threshold is changed after seeing events.

## Exact chart items and deterministic feature construction

The selected item IDs, verified from `icu/d_items`, are:

- HR `220045) bpm; arterial MAP `220052) mmHg; non-invasive MAP `220181) mmHg.
- RR `220210) insp/min; SpO2 `220277) %.
- Temperature Fahrenheit `223761) °F; temperature Celsius `223762) °C.
- O2 Flow `223834), additional-cannula flow `227287), and BiPap O2 Flow `227582), all L/min.
- Inspired O2 Fraction `223835), dictionary unit None.
- O2 Delivery Device(s) `226732), text only.
- Code Status `223758), text only.

For each numeric row use `valuenum), retain `value, valueuom, warning` for an audit, and count rather than silently repair invalid rows. Validity domains are HR 20–250, RR 4–80, SpO2 50–100, MAP 20–200, temperature 30–43 °C, nonnegative flow, and FiO2 either 0.21–1.00 or >1–100. FiO2 values >1 and <=100 are divided by 100; values after normalization outside 0.21–1 are invalid. Fahrenheit is converted by `(F-32)/1.8`. No dictionary normal range is substituted for these fixed rules.

A valid vital is abnormal when HR <50 or >100, RR <8 or >24, SpO2 <92%, MAP <65, or temperature <36 or >=38 °C. For each block, arterial and non-invasive MAP are retained separately; the combined MAP is the minimum valid MAP in that block. The block is “vital-abnormal” if any valid vital domain is abnormal. The primary repeated burden is:

`abnormal_block_count / covered_vital_block_count`.

The denominator is the number of six blocks containing at least one valid HR, MAP, RR, SpO2, or temperature observation. The primary analytic gate ensures the denominator is at least four. Also save abnormal-block count, covered-block count, total valid vital observations, per-domain valid/invalid counts, and the fraction of valid vital observations abnormal.

For a domain with multiple observations at the same timestamp, the last-value feature is the median of valid values at the latest `charttime) (then temperature is in °C and FiO2 normalized). This avoids relying on a nonexistent row ID. Across distinct timestamps, the latest timestamp wins. Save separate arterial and non-invasive MAP last values and a combined-MAP last value. Oxygen features are maximum valid flow across the three flow items and maximum normalized FiO2; item-specific maxima and observed indicators are retained. The text device field and code-status field contribute only observed/not-observed indicators and audited value frequencies, never a validated device or treatment-limitation classification.

The secondary joint description is repeated burden >=0.50 plus `max_flow >=4) L/min or `max_fio2 >=0.40). It is fixed before fitting, descriptive, and not a discovered treatment threshold. The proposal does not interpret charted flow or FiO2 as delivered oxygen, work of breathing, or a respiratory support mode.

## Outcomes and competing-event estimands

For every primary index, define `tau = min(t0 + 7 days, admissions.dischtime)`. Event times at `tau) are included; follow-up otherwise ends at `tau). A later ICU stay is a candidate return only if it has the same `(subject_id, hadm_id)), a different `stay_id), and `intime >= t0). Stays with `intime < t0) are not returns; overlap and same-admission key anomalies are counted. The first candidate is selected by `intime), then numeric `stay_id).

- Cause 1, ICU return: candidate `intime <= tau) and strictly earlier than a qualifying death time.
- Cause 2, death before ICU return: `deathtime > t0), `deathtime <= tau), and strictly earlier than the first candidate ICU return.
- If return and death have exactly equal timestamps, label the record `tie/indeterminate), exclude it from the primary competing-event fit at that time, and report it. A sensitivity assigns all ties to return and another assigns all ties to death.
- If neither cause occurs by `tau), censor at `tau). Death after ICU return is not cause 2; it is a secondary descriptive outcome.
- The primary composite is cause 1 or cause 2. Secondary horizons are 48 hours and 12 hours, with the same rules, and follow-up to hospital dischtime is descriptive only.

This yields three estimands: 7-day cumulative incidence of ICU return; 7-day cumulative incidence of death before return; and their composite cumulative incidence. Report Aalen–Johansen estimates, with the competing cause treated as an event rather than ordinary censoring. Cause-specific hazard ratios are associations per one training-cohort SD increase in continuous burden; no causal language is used.

`hosp/transfers` is joined only on `(subject_id, hadm_id)) to audit transfer chronology and the relationship between ICU-stay rows and hospital movement. No unverified care-unit taxonomy is used to manufacture a “strict ward return” endpoint. A sensitivity instead requires a gap of at least two hours between index `outtime) and return `intime); all same-admission return definitions and overlap counts are reported.

## Baselines, analysis, and uncertainty

The same primary analytic records, index, outcome rules, and locked partitions are used in all nested comparisons.

- B0: gender, anchor_age, anchor_year_group as a descriptive stratum only (not calendar time), admission_type, admission_location, insurance, first_careunit, last_careunit, ICU LOS, hours from `admittime) to `t0), and count of earlier ICU stays in the same admission whose `outtime <= t0). Include explicit missingness indicators.
- B_last: B0 plus the last valid HR, combined/arterial/non-invasive MAP, RR, SpO2, temperature, flow, and FiO2 values; each domain’s observed indicator; and arterial/non-invasive source indicators.
- B_repeat: B_last plus abnormal-block count, repeated burden, covered-block count, valid vital observation count, per-domain measurement density, maximum item-specific flow/FiO2, and oxygen/code-status/device observation indicators.

Do not use `deathtime), `dischtime`, `dod`, `hospital_expire_flag`, `discharge_location`, post-`t0) chart rows, future ICU rows, discharge notes, radiology text, or post-index transfer information as predictors. The exposure and all predictors are computed before outcomes.

Assign subjects, not rows or stays, to development/validation/test using SHA-256 of `subject_id) with fixed partitions 60%/20%/20%; all admissions for a subject remain together. Do not use anchor-year chronology as a validation split. Use development data only for preprocessing, one-hot levels, standardization, ridge penalty selection, and any missingness model. Use no outcome-driven cutpoints or automated feature search.

For each B model, fit two cause-specific Cox models (ICU return and death-before-return) with the prespecified feature set and ridge penalty selected by inner development cross-validation. Derive competing-risk cumulative incidence from the two fitted cause-specific hazards. The association report includes linear burden hazard ratios and 95% intervals; fixed burden strata are descriptive. The primary incremental endpoint is the paired locked-test difference in 7-day cause-specific Brier score and calibration slope/intercept for B_repeat versus B_last, with B0 as a context baseline. AUROC/AUPRC and decision-curve net benefit at predeclared absolute-risk thresholds are secondary and cannot rescue failure of calibration.

Use 1,000 subject-level bootstrap resamples of the locked test set for 95% percentile intervals for risk metrics and model differences, with a fixed seed. Also report cause-specific model coefficient intervals from a subject-clustered bootstrap or robust sandwich estimator. If a partition has fewer than the feasibility requirements below, do not alter the model or endpoint; mark that estimate infeasible.

Before model fitting, write a manifest containing total admissions, patients, ICU stays, eligible stays, primary indexes, age and chronology exclusions, per-item valid/invalid rows, vital block coverage, oxygen/code-status observation rates, return/death/tie/censor counts, overlap counts, and all counts by anchor-year group and last care unit. The existing audit job is a prerequisite artifact, not a result.

## Feasibility, missingness, and falsification gates

The primary experiment may fit the composite model only if the primary analytic cohort has at least 1,000 indexes and at least 100 composite events. Cause-specific 7-day inference requires at least 50 ICU returns and 50 deaths-before-return; if either count fails, that cause is reported by Aalen–Johansen only with an explicit sparse-event/inconclusive label, and no unsupported cause-specific conclusion is made. At least 60% of all eligible indexes must have >=4/6 valid vital blocks for the coverage design to be considered feasible. These are execution gates, not asserted counts.

Missingness is part of the clinical signal, not silently imputed away. Primary B models use prespecified missingness indicators and development-only median/mode imputation. Report density and coverage by outcome, care unit, burden group, and split. A sensitivity uses stabilized inverse-probability-of-coverage weights estimated from B0 and care unit in development, truncated at the 1st/99th percentiles; it is a sensitivity to ascertainment, not a causal correction. If association is confined to dense charting or disappears after density adjustment, interpret it as documentation/acuity evidence.

Required falsification and integrity checks:

1. Assert zero included feature rows with `charttime >= t0), zero feature rows from another stay, zero use of `storetime) for eligibility, one index per primary admission, no prohibited fields in the feature matrices, and valid chronology for every event.
2. Repeat the full pipeline after shifting the exposure window 24 hours earlier to `[t0-48h,t0-24h)), retaining the same index and outcomes. A similar or stronger signal implies general illness/charting rather than a discharge-proximal residual-instability signal.
3. Use the earlier window’s measurement density as a negative-control feature. A strong association without final-day residual features indicates ascertainment or overall severity.
4. Permute outcomes within subject-grouped locked partitions while preserving feature coverage. Held-out performance must return to chance within bootstrap uncertainty; failure invalidates computation.
5. Repeat with arterial and non-invasive MAP separately, with the two-hour return-gap sensitivity, with tie assignments, and at 48 hours/12 hours. Do not cherry-pick a favorable version.
6. Compare primary and all-eligible-stay results with subject/hospital-admission clustering; large divergence is selection dependence, not confirmation.
7. Audit `223758) code-status availability and raw values but do not label goals of care automatically. If the signal is concentrated in a small, clinically unreviewed code-status subgroup, report it as a limitation.

Supportive results require a monotonic or positive prespecified burden association for both competing causes or a clearly reported cause-specific pattern, agreement of Aalen–Johansen direction with the fitted risks, locked-test improvement in B_repeat calibration/Brier over B_last with uncertainty excluding trivial/no improvement, and no material collapse in the density, earlier-window, MAP-source, return-gap, or tie sensitivities. A small AUC gain without calibration or absolute-risk separation is not supportive of clinical usefulness.

Adverse results are a narrow null or negative association, no improvement or worsening of locked-test calibration/Brier, or reversal after competing-event, density, earlier-window, source, or return-gap checks. This would argue that repeated charted instability adds no reliable information beyond a last snapshot or reflects ascertainment/general severity.

Inconclusive results are failed cohort/event/coverage gates, wide or unstable intervals, sparse cause-specific events, an outcome permutation failure, contradictory tie or source sensitivities, or inability to distinguish chart density from physiology. A failed permutation check invalidates the computation; it is not evidence against the clinical hypothesis.

## What the experiment cannot establish

Computationally checkable claims are source/member availability, exact cohort and feature counts, timestamp ordering, item validity rules, competing-event labels, model estimates, uncertainty, calibration, density effects, and falsification results within this snapshot. MIMIC cannot establish:

- that ICU discharge caused a return or death, that delaying discharge would help, or that a monitoring/oxygen intervention would change outcomes;
- true delivered FiO2, work of breathing, bedside instability, functional/cognitive readiness, staffing, or clinician discharge intent;
- adjudicated readmission reasons, goals-of-care decisions, or a validated treatment-limitation phenotype from code-status text;
- causal treatment effects, external validity, transportability, prospective utility, or a safe clinical threshold.

Those claims require clinician adjudication, richer prospective physiology and treatment context, external validation, and a prospective intervention or target-trial design.
