# Pre-illness biomarker heterogeneity in cardiovascular risk after infection

## Scientific question and hypothesis

A severe infection hospitalization may identify people at elevated subsequent cardiovascular risk, but it is unresolved whether that excess risk is concentrated among people with a pre-assessment biomarker profile suggestive of endothelial/coagulation or systemic inflammatory vulnerability. The falsifiable hypothesis is:

> Among UK Biobank participants free of the prespecified cardiovascular endpoint at the biomarker assessment, a code-defined infection hospitalization during follow-up is associated with a larger subsequent hazard of incident cardiovascular disease among participants with higher pre-assessment endothelial/coagulation/inflammatory biomarker scores, after adjustment for baseline cardiovascular risk.

This is an effect-modification hypothesis, not a claim that the measured proteins mediate post-infection disease. The strongest available evidence supports only that infection and cardiovascular outcomes can be temporally ordered in linked registry fields and that baseline biomarkers can be measured before those outcomes; it does not establish infection severity, biological mediation, or clinical utility.

The substantive advance is to test whether pre-illness biological heterogeneity identifies who has the largest *post-infection* cardiovascular risk increment, rather than estimating only an average infection association or building an unanchored prediction model.

## Population and time

Use participants with nonmissing `eid`, age/sex, biomarker values and follow-up outcome fields. Index is the biomarker assessment represented by the eligible baseline instance (prefer the first valid biomarker instance; retain a sensitivity analysis using the second available instance). Exclude anyone with the primary cardiovascular endpoint before index, anyone whose infection hospitalization precedes index, and anyone with missing/invalid dates or impossible ordering. Do not treat UKB instance numbers as elapsed time.

Follow-up begins at index and ends at the first primary cardiovascular event, death, loss to available follow-up, or the prespecified administrative end date encoded by the available outcome snapshot. The exposure is the first qualifying infection-related hospitalization after index and is handled as a time-varying exposure to avoid immortal-time bias. A secondary landmark analysis starts at that hospitalization and compares cardiovascular risk after infection against matched/noninfected risk sets, with the risk-set construction documented.

Because exact field labels and coding dictionaries are not fully present locally, the executable implementation must freeze a code list from the approved UKB Showcase metadata before fitting. If that code list cannot be verified, the result is a data-audit failure, not a positive or negative clinical result.

## Estimand and analysis

Primary estimand: the ratio of cause-specific hazards for incident primary cardiovascular disease comparing a qualifying post-index infection hospitalization with no qualifying hospitalization, and its interaction with a one-standard-deviation increase in the baseline biomarker score. Report the interaction coefficient, marginal 5-year risk differences under observed covariate distributions, 95% confidence intervals, and calibration/positivity diagnostics. Death is a competing event; report cause-specific and Fine–Gray sensitivity analyses, while making clear they answer different questions.

Fit a transparent baseline Cox model with restricted cubic splines for continuous score, infection as a time-varying exposure, prespecified age/sex/BMI/smoking/diabetes/hypertension/kidney function and assessment center/ancestry covariates where available. Use elastic-net Cox only as a high-dimensional baseline if the number of analytes requires it; selection occurs within training folds. Prespecify a 60/20/20 participant-level train/tuning/test split by eid, with all preprocessing and biomarker-score fitting inside training data. No outcome-derived feature may enter the biomarker score.

Substantive alternative: a bounded longitudinal/heterogeneity model that learns a low-dimensional biomarker representation and infection-by-representation interaction (for example, sparse group-lasso or a small autoencoder followed by penalized Cox), trained only on the same baseline biomarker/covariate inputs and evaluated on the same held-out test participants. Its scientific purpose is to detect nonlinear correlated biomarker patterns and heterogeneous post-infection risk that a single prespecified score can lose, not to claim a deployable model. Compare interaction estimates, held-out calibration, Brier score/time-dependent AUC, and bootstrap uncertainty. If the assay dictionary or event support is too sparse, defer the learned alternative and retain the transparent score analysis; record the dependency rather than silently substituting a different question.

Approximate budget: CPU preprocessing/penalized models and bootstrap on the selected fields, under 10 minutes per fit and under 2 hours total; the alternative is deliberately small and CPU-feasible. GPU is unnecessary and earns no scientific credit.

## Falsification and interpretation

Falsification criteria are set before seeing outcomes:

1. No reproducible infection-by-biomarker interaction in the held-out test set, with the prespecified confidence interval excluding the minimally important interaction threshold, falsifies the primary hypothesis.
2. A similar interaction for a negative-control hospitalization category or for a cardiovascular outcome that should not plausibly be affected by the proposed pathway weakens biological specificity.
3. If the interaction disappears after correcting for baseline cardiovascular disease/risk and ascertainment, it supports confounding or detection bias rather than the proposed vulnerability.
4. If pre-index biomarker score predicts cardiovascular disease equally before and after infection, it does not support infection-specific effect modification.
5. If the learned model improves prediction trivially but gives no stable, interpretable heterogeneity estimate beyond the baseline, it is not a substantive scientific advance.

Supportive results would be a prespecified interaction replicated across the primary and sensitivity definitions, with acceptable missingness/positivity, calibration, and no comparable negative-control signal. They would support prognostic heterogeneity and motivate external validation or clinical adjudication—not protein mediation, treatment targeting, or a change in surveillance policy.

Adverse results would be a null or reversed interaction, strong code-definition sensitivity, or evidence that biomarker score is merely a general cardiovascular-risk marker. These are informative negative findings and would argue against using pre-illness biomarkers to select post-infection follow-up.

Inconclusive results include too few qualifying infections/events, unstable analyte definitions, wide intervals, missing dictionary support, or discordant primary/negative-control analyses. These should lead to no clinical conclusion.

Clinical adjudication of infection severity, infection phenotype, cardiovascular subtype, treatment, frailty, and competing noncardiovascular causes is unavailable in this snapshot. Residual confounding, selection into UKB, measurement error, and lack of an external cohort remain. Causal mediation and clinical utility require adjudicated data, treatment information, an independent validation cohort, and ideally a prospective intervention/decision-curve study.
