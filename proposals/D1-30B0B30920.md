# Laboratory observation process as a transportable ICU risk signal

Status: substantive episode-15 repair of `[prior hypothesis]`, with the complete feasibility-backed formulation from `[prior hypothesis]` retained as evidence. This is an observational prognostic and measurement-process study. It is not a causal test of ordering laboratory tests, a hospital quality ranking, or evidence that process features improve patient outcomes.

## Scientific deliverable

The future solver must newly freeze a leakage-safe adult first-stay cohort, an outcome-blind analyte/unit/revision codebook, six-hour ICU-relative feature windows, and grouped whole-hospital folds. It must fit matched models with and without patient-level laboratory observation-process features and produce held-out-hospital cumulative-incidence predictions for recorded ICU death and competing alive ICU discharge.

Completion requires:

1. a cohort-flow table and data dictionary with row counts, exclusions, analyte mappings and revision audit;
2. state, availability and process feature matrices built without post-landmark information;
3. five grouped outer-fold fits and out-of-fold predictions for both competing events;
4. macro-hospital and patient-weighted integrated Brier scores, calibration-in-the-large and slope/error, fixed-alert-burden summaries, uncertainty and channel ablations;
5. a prespecified within-hospital, state-risk-stratified process permutation and common-panel sensitivity;
6. a compact temporal learner only if event/support, convergence and calibration gates pass; and
7. an interpretation file linking every conclusion to computed output rows.

The primary scientific output is the estimated incremental, transportable predictive information in observation history and its failure modes—not the best AUROC.

## Strongest supported claim and unresolved hypothesis

The available evidence supports feasibility, not the hypothesis. The prior outcome-blind eICU audit attached to the parent direction reported 135,680 eligible adult first stays, 2,470 recorded ICU-death events by 1,800 minutes, and 208 hospitals (175 event-bearing; 195 with at least 20 eligible episodes). These counts support a bounded whole-hospital experiment. They do not establish that testing history predicts death, represents clinician intent, transports across institutions, or improves care. My independent read-only patient scan confirmed 200,859 patient rows, 208 hospitals, and the same order of magnitude for the corrected first-stay screen; its slightly different eligibility count reflects conservative parsing and is not treated as a fitted result.

The eICU-02 expert seed asks whether testing-frequency/missingness models lose transport performance. Existing evidence and the read demonstrations support the narrower premise that dated EHR observation processes can be informative and that workflow shift can damage transport. The unresolved claim is whether patient-level observation timing and intensity retain reproducible information beyond measured patient state and simple availability when the hospital itself is held out.

Falsifiable hypothesis: among adult first ICU stays, laboratory observation timing and intensity during a fixed six-hour ICU window improve zero-shot prediction of 24-hour recorded ICU death beyond measured patient state and analyte availability in held-out hospitals. The improvement should attenuate when process vectors are conditionally permuted within hospital and baseline-risk strata. A gain that survives that permutation, or is obtained only from hospital/site features, is evidence of leakage or a workflow/site fingerprint rather than patient-level transportable information.

A supportive result would inform whether deployment should monitor observation-process shift and retain carefully audited process features. It would not justify ordering or withholding tests.

## Variables, baselines and estimands

For each retained analyte, derive first and last value, slope and range only when supported, threshold flags, an availability indicator, distinct measurement times, result-row count, time from landmark to last result, time from first to last, inter-test interval summaries, number of distinct analytes, and stable system/interface and revision-documentation indicators. Fit medians, scaling and categorical mappings within development hospitals only.

The state block contains demographics, admission context and laboratory/physiologic values known by 360 minutes. The availability block contains only prespecified any-value indicators. The process block adds timing, count, interval, interface and revision-documentation features.

B0 is a pooled elastic-net discrete-time cause-specific logistic model with hourly rows from 360 through 1,800 and separate recorded-death and alive-discharge heads. It includes state plus availability. B1 uses the identical rows, outcomes, folds, regularization procedure and state/availability features, adding only patient-level process features. The primary estimand is the paired outer-test difference in macro-hospital integrated competing-risk Brier score; patient-weighted difference and calibration error are secondary. A fixed top-5% alert-burden analysis is descriptive, not clinical utility.

## Split, uncertainty and substantive alternative

Use five grouped outer folds, each holding out approximately 20% of hospitals; keep all stays for a `uniquepid` together. Within each outer training partition, use approximately 75%/25% hospital development/validation splits for regularization, codebook choices and early stopping, stratified by eligible and event counts when possible. No patient-random split may replace the primary test; it is only an optimism diagnostic.

The substantive alternative is a one-layer GRU or temporal convolution over six one-hour bins, with the same values, availability, counts, elapsed-time, interface and revision channels as B1. Its heads predict recorded death, alive discharge and next-bin observation; the observation head characterizes temporal information and is not a clinical endpoint. This alternative can reveal abrupt changes, co-movement and ordering that B1's summary slopes/counts lose. It is deferred if common feature support, event support, convergence or held-out calibration fails. CPU is appropriate for B0/B1; a single allocated A100 using `cuda:0` may be used for the compact temporal fit only if needed. The future envelope is at most 16 CPUs, 262,144 MiB, 8 GPUs and 28,800 seconds; this is planning capacity, not a measured runtime. The bounded patient scan took about 2.3 seconds on CPU; full lab parsing and all model runtimes remain unmeasured.

## Falsification and interpretation

Before fitting, require at least 100 total primary events, at least 10 event-bearing hospitals per evaluation fold, at least 20 eligible episodes per retained hospital, positive value/availability overlap across folds, and no revision feature requiring post-landmark information. These are stability gates, not clinical thresholds.

Support requires a prespecified relative Brier improvement (10% target) in at least two held-out transport folds, uncertainty excluding no improvement, preserved direction under strict unit/revision codebooks, no hospital attributes in the model, process-channel ablation localizing the gain, improved fixed-burden calibration, and no dominance by one hospital or lab family. Conditional process permutation shuffles process features within hospital and state-risk strata while preserving state and outcome; the expected loss of gain is a falsification of a patient-level process signal. A common-analyte panel and a patient-random split are sensitivity analyses.

Adverse evidence is no transport gain, a reversal after strict codebook/revision handling, calibration worsening, collapse under hospital-held-out evaluation, or a gain that is reproduced by the conditional permutation/placebo. This refutes the measurable process advantage in this snapshot; it does not show testing is clinically irrelevant.

Inconclusive evidence includes insufficient support/events, non-positivity, unstable calibration or uncertainty, fold-specific reversals, target leakage, inability to separate alive discharge from death, or temporal nonconvergence. Report diagnostics only and do not select a model by AUROC.

Harbor can check computation, bindings, leakage, folds, model pairing, calibration, uncertainty, ablations and whether conclusions follow from output files. It cannot establish clinician intent, whether a test was indicated, physiologic correctness, unrecorded death, clinical benefit, or causal effects. Those require chart review/adjudication, external hospitals and prospective impact evaluation.
