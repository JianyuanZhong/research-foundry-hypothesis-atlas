# Cross-table audit of frozen care-plan labels against literal RT Vent On/Off: decisive events are too sparse and setting differences predate D

## Substantive child and clinical importance

This is a substantive child of assessed-valid `[prior hypothesis]` and `[prior hypothesis]`. It preserves the exact frozen 540 person-landmarks, 443 stays/persons, five landmarks, first-label rule, decision time D, exact care-plan labels, and inherited exclusions. It also preserves both completed negative findings: durable liberation cannot be validly ascertained, and the earlier 12-hour action proxies were adverse/unstable and largely driven by peak-pressure charting. From the RT parent it preserves literal `Start`, `Off`, and `Suspended`; exact-record deduplication followed by deterministic earliest-anchored 30-minute clustering; separation of Off from Suspended; fixed ICU boundaries; fixed 6-, 24-, and 48-hour horizons; and the sequence, site-concentration, and repeated-observation cautions.

The unresolved hypothesis was whether these exact care-plan labels are followed by reproducibly different literal RT transition documentation and structured respiratory-setting trajectories. If so, this would support only cross-table documentation concordance and might justify a later adjudication study. If literal transitions are sparse, occur before rather than after D, or setting differences are present in sham/pre-D windows and unstable across hospitals, the labels cannot be validated through these respiratoryCharting fields. This is clinically important because it prevents a seemingly direct cross-table link from being mistaken for evidence of actual SBT delivery, extubation, ventilation discontinuation, or liberation.

## Locked event and trajectory definitions

For trimmed/lowercased `respchartvaluelabel = RT Vent On/Off`, only literal `Start`, `Off`, and `Suspended` are decisive chart values. Exact tuples including row identity are deduplicated, then records within a stay are sorted and clustered from the earliest record through anchor +30 minutes. Mixed Start/off-direction clusters are conflicts. A mixed Off+Suspended cluster is ambiguous. Off and Suspended are never pooled. A post-D event must occur strictly after D; same-offset records are separately audited and cannot be called subsequent. Prior state is the latest clean cluster strictly before D. The first post-D state is the earliest clean Start, Off, or Suspended before/on the ICU boundary; separate first-Off, first-Suspended, and first-Start times are retained.

Fixed complete-observation horizons are `[D,D+360]`, `[D,D+1440]`, and `[D,D+2880]`. Matched pre-D windows are `(D-h,D]` with the event at D excluded by the strict event rule, and the shifted sham is `(D-720,D-360]`. `Continued` is exact-record deduplicated but remains a nontransition workflow control and never enters the state machine.

The inherited setting marker is rebuilt without carry-forward as at least two distinct plausible families in each fixed bin: set respiratory rate, set tidal volume, machine minute volume, machine tidal volume, and machine pressure, using the inherited labels and plausibility ranges. Bins are `[D-360,D)`, `[D,D+360)`, `[D+360,D+1440)`, and `[D+1440,D+2880)`, plus the shifted `[D-720,D-360)` control. Generic respiratoryCharting presence is measured separately to detect documentation loss.

## Actual coverage and prespecified stop rule

The 443 target stays contributed 359,227 respiratoryCharting rows. They contained only 41 decisive RT records, all exact-unique and all separate 30-minute clusters: 22 Start, 12 Off, and 7 Suspended, with no target-stay conflict or mixed Off+Suspended cluster. There were also 675 exact-unique Continued rows.

Only 18/443 stays and 18/540 person-landmarks had any clean RT cluster before or after D. Prior state was observable in 18/540 rows: 17 Start and one Suspended. Only eight rows had any later clean state before the ICU boundary: seven first Off and one first Suspended; no first subsequent Start was observed. There were no same-offset clean events. Five evaluation and two no-trial rows had first Off; the one first Suspended row had the evaluation label. These eight sparse records do not establish a label difference.

The frozen escalation gate required, for a state-specific outcome, at least 20 post-D events, at least five events under each label, at least three event hospitals, and at least three hospitals with at least five complete rows per label. No literal event outcome met it at any horizon. Consequently, state-specific regression, blocked permutation, or model-based rescue was stopped. This is an executed ascertainment result rather than a power calculation.

## Fixed-horizon literal event results and timing falsification

At six hours, 539 rows had complete ICU observation (309 evaluation, 230 no-trial), and there were zero subsequent clean Start, Off, or Suspended clusters under either label. Wilson upper bounds for any event were 1.23% for evaluation and 1.64% for no-trial, but zero chart events cannot be interpreted as no bedside action.

At 24 hours, 512 rows were completely observed (293 evaluation, 219 no-trial). Only three evaluation rows and no no-trial rows had a decisive event: two Off and one Suspended, across only two hospitals. At 48 hours, 446 rows were completely observed (252 evaluation, 194 no-trial); there were again two Off and one Suspended event, all under evaluation, across hospitals 66, 71, and 73. The eligible event rows were not identical across horizons: the 24-hour set included one hospital-73 Off row whose ICU stay ended before 48 hours, while the 48-hour set included a hospital-71 Off first charted between 24 and 48 hours. Thus there were four unique event rows across the two complete-horizon analyses, but only three at either horizon. No subsequent Start occurred at any horizon.

The pre-D and shifted controls undermine temporal specificity rather than support it. In the six hours before D there were three Start records, all among evaluation rows, versus no post-D event. In the 24 hours before D there were six events among evaluation rows—five Start, one Off, and one Suspended with overlap possible at row level—versus three post-D events. In the observable 48-hour pre-window, any-event rates were 5/240 = 2.08% for evaluation and 4/171 = 2.34% for no-trial, while complete post-D any-event rates were 3/252 = 1.19% and 0/194. The shifted 12-to-6-hour sham already contained two evaluation events and none under no-trial. Thus the few post-D Off/Suspended records are not accompanied by a broader post-D transition signal and cannot overcome the extremely sparse coverage.

The 675 Continued records were more common than decisive states but behaved as a workflow control: at six hours, Continued appeared post-D in 11/309 evaluation and 6/230 no-trial rows, versus 10/309 and 6/231 pre-D. At 24 hours, corresponding post-D counts were 12/293 and 5/219 versus 11/309 and 6/231 pre-D. This modest, pre-existing documentation activity does not validate a state transition.

Event concentration precluded hospital transportability assessment. The three 24-hour decisive events came from only hospitals 73 (two) and 66 (one); 48-hour eligible event rows were one each at hospitals 66, 71, and 73. No event occurred in the principal care-plan common-support hospitals 248, 252, and 420. There were 76 repeated-landmark stays contributing 173 rows, but none of the sparse 24-/48-hour event rows came from those repeated rows. That fact avoids event double-counting here but does not remove repeated-landmark dependence from setting analyses.

## Structured setting trajectory and calibration

Structured setting coverage was high, unlike literal transition coverage. Setting-marker positivity was already lower under evaluation than no-trial well before D:

- shifted 12-to-6 hours: 83.50% versus 93.07%, difference -9.58 points;
- pre-D six hours: 86.08% versus 95.67%, difference -9.59 points;
- post-D six hours: 80.58% versus 93.04%, difference -12.46 points;
- 6-to-24 hours: 79.86% versus 90.87%, difference -11.00 points;
- 24-to-48 hours: 74.60% versus 85.57%, difference -10.96 points.

The relevant within-row post-six-hour-minus-pre-six-hour contrast was only -5.50 points under evaluation versus -2.61 under no-trial, a difference-in-differences of -2.89 points with stay-clustered percentile interval -7.36 to +1.77 points. In common-support hospitals 248, 252, and 420, equal-hospital difference was -0.52 points; site differences were +0.21, -0.85, and -0.92 points, and every leave-one-hospital-out estimate remained near zero (-0.88 to -0.32 points). A hospital × landmark × prior-state blocked label-permutation calibration used 356 rows and yielded observed -1.46 points with two-sided randomization p=0.646. The permutation is a falsification calibration only, not an exchangeability or causal claim.

Generic respiratoryCharting presence fell more under evaluation in the post-six-hour window: the post-minus-pre difference contrast was -7.76 points with stay-clustered interval -12.14 to -3.45. Yet this contrast disappeared after hospital/landmark/prior-state blocking (356 rows; observed +0.33 points; p=1.0), and in common-support hospitals the equal-hospital contrast was only -1.78 points. This points to site/workflow composition rather than a reproducible label-triggered setting trajectory.

Longer-window unadjusted setting-level differences also did not transport cleanly. At 6-to-24 hours, the all-row difference was -11.00 points, but equal-hospital common-support difference was -2.67 points and leave-one-site-out estimates ranged from +0.41 to -5.49 points, including reversal. At 24-to-48 hours, the all-row difference was -10.96 points and equal-hospital difference -5.82 points; LOSO remained negative but ranged from -1.91 to -11.09 points, and blocked permutation p was 0.266. These are descriptive documentation patterns, not evidence of different clinical actions.

Prior literal RT history could not be meaningfully conditioned on: 522/540 rows had no prior clean state, 17 had prior Start, and one had prior Suspended. The setting-change result among the 17 prior-Start rows was too sparse and label-imbalanced for interpretation.

## Falsification decision and strongest supported claim

A supportive cross-table result required adequate literal event coverage; a new post-D signal exceeding pre-D and shifted windows; reproducibility in hospital common support and leave-one-site-out analyses; and setting changes not explained by generic documentation workflow. None was achieved.

The strongest claim supported is negative but consequential: in this exact frozen cohort, literal `RT Vent On/Off` records cannot validate the two care-plan labels because decisive coverage is near-zero (41 clusters anywhere in the target stays, only eight first post-D states, and only three completely observed events within 24/48 hours), while the apparent structured-setting difference is largely present before D and collapses toward zero in common-support, within-row, blocked-permutation, and LOSO analyses. This is adverse evidence for cross-table documentation concordance and an inconclusive clinical-state result. It is not evidence that the labels are equivalent, that no SBT or extubation occurred, or that either care plan had no effect.

The prior parent conclusions remain unchanged: durable liberation is not ascertainable, the 12-hour care-plan action proxies are adverse/unstable and peak-pressure dominated, and Off/Suspended chart values have site- and sequence-dependent documentation semantics in their broader RT-defined cohort. The current sparse overlap explains why those broader RT semantics do not transport into this frozen care-plan cohort.

Stronger claims require independently timestamped ventilator connection/device logs, invasive versus noninvasive modality, airway insertion/removal and extubation/reintubation times, actual SBT delivery and result, RT/nursing workflow provenance, post-ICU respiratory support, and blinded clinical adjudication sampled across hospitals and discordant records. No computable field here establishes actual SBT delivery, extubation, ventilation discontinuation, liberation, quality, benefit, or causality.
