# Scientific Proposal: Glycemic-Vasopressor Synergy and Organ Injury in Septic ICU Patients

## Unresolved Question

**Current state of knowledge:**
- Hyperglycemia and vasopressor use are independently associated with organ injury in sepsis
- Most studies examine these factors separately, ignoring potential interactions
- The "J-shaped" curve hypothesis (both hypo- and hyperglycemia harmful) is partially supported but not mechanistically understood
- Organ recovery trajectories are typically measured by binary AKI diagnosis, not dynamic progression

**The gap:**
We lack evidence on how *temporal patterns of glycemic excursions* interact with *vasopressor intensity and duration* to predict *organ-specific injury trajectories* in septic patients.

## Hypothesis

**Primary hypothesis:**
In septic ICU patients, the **multiplicative interaction** between cumulative hyperglycemic burden and vasopressor exposure predicts acute kidney injury (AKI) onset and recovery trajectory more strongly than additive effects.

Specifically:
1. **Cumulative hyperglycemic burden** (area under glucose curve above 180 mg/dL during first 48 hours) independently predicts AKI onset
2. **Vasopressor dose-duration product** (total dose × duration) independently predicts AKI onset
3. **Interaction effect**: Patients in the highest quartile of both glycemic burden AND vasopressor exposure have disproportionately worse AKI outcomes than expected from simple risk factor addition
4. **Recovery trajectory**: Time to creatinine nadir and rate of renal recovery differ by glycemic pattern

**Secondary hypothesis:**
The glycemia-vasopressor interaction is non-linear, with a threshold effect: synergistic risk emerges only when both factors exceed specific thresholds.

## Clinical Importance

1. **Treatment implications:** Current glycemic targets (140-180 mg/dL) may be insufficient for patients on vasopressors. Risk-stratified glycemic management could reduce organ injury.

2. **Prognostication:** Temporal glycemia patterns may improve risk stratification beyond traditional severity scores (APACHE II, SOFA).

3. **Resource allocation:** Understanding synergistic risk could inform ICU bed management and family communication.

## Substantive Advance Over Existing Knowledge

**Limitations of prior work:**
- Most studies use cross-sectional or simple longitudinal analyses
- Few examine the *interaction* between glycemic and hemodynamic management
- Organ recovery is typically measured by binary AKI diagnosis, not dynamic progression
- Hospital-level confounding (treatment protocols, sepsis bundles) is rarely addressed

**What this study adds:**
1. **Dynamic risk metric:** Cumulative hyperglycemic burden (AUC above threshold) rather than peak/average glucose
2. **Interaction effects:** Tests multiplicative vs additive models for glycemia-vasopressor synergy
3. **Organ-specific resolution:** Tracks renal recovery trajectory, not just binary AKI
4. **Temporal alignment:** Matches glycemic and hemodynamic events within tight time windows
5. **Non-linear modeling:** Detects threshold effects beyond linear models

## Falsifiable Predictions

1. **Prediction 1:** Patients in the highest quartile of cumulative hyperglycemic burden will have 1.5-2.0x higher odds of developing AKI within 48 hours, independent of baseline severity.

2. **Prediction 2:** The glycemia-vasopressor interaction is non-additive: patients in the highest glycemic burden AND highest vasopressor dose quartile will have disproportionately worse outcomes than expected from simple risk factor addition (interaction p < 0.01).

3. **Prediction 3:** Organ recovery trajectories (time to creatinine nadir, rate of decline) differ by glycemic pattern, with "sustained hyperglycemia" showing worse recovery than "intermittent excursions" at equivalent average levels.

4. **Prediction 4:** A threshold effect exists: synergistic risk emerges only when both glycemic burden AND vasopressor dose exceed specific thresholds.

## Data Requirements and Bindings

**Primary datasets:**
- `lab.csv.gz` - Glucose measurements (labname="GLU", labresult)
- `intakeOutput.csv.gz` - Patient identifiers and temporal alignment
- `infusionDrug.csv.gz` - Vasopressor dosing (drugname, drugrate, infusionoffset)
- `apachePatientResult.csv.gz` - Baseline severity (apachescore, predictedicumortality)
- `hospital.csv.gz` - Hospital-level confounders (teachingstatus, region)

**Key columns:**
- `patientunitstayid` - Primary join key (patient-stay identifier)
- `labresult` - Glucose values (numeric)
- `infusionoffset` - Temporal alignment for vasopressor dosing
- `drugname` - Vasopressor identification (norepinephrine, epinephrine, etc.)
- `drugrate` - Vasopressor dose (units/hour)
- `labresultrevisedoffset` - Temporal precision for glucose

**Temporal boundaries:**
- Study period: ICU stay from admission to discharge or death
- Exposure window: 0-72 hours post-admission (early sepsis period)
- Outcome window: 0-96 hours for AKI onset; 72-168 hours for recovery trajectory

**Inclusion criteria:**
- Age ≥18 years
- Admission diagnosis of sepsis/septic shock (ICD-10 codes in diagnosis.csv.gz)
- At least 3 glucose measurements post-admission
- At least 1 vasopressor administration
- ICU stay duration ≥24 hours

**Exclusion criteria:**
- Pre-existing diabetes with documented treatment
- Extracorporeal support (ECMO, CRRT)
- Multiple organ failure at admission (SOFA ≥13)

## Baseline Model

**Simple logistic regression:**
```
logit(P(AKI)) = β₀ + β₁(glucose_mean) + β₂(vasopressor_dose) + β₃(severity_score) + β₄(hospital)
```

**What baseline reveals:**
- Main effects of glycemia, vasopressors, and severity
- Confounding by indication
- Hospital-level variation

## Mechanistic/Learned Alternative

**Interaction model with temporal features:**
```
logit(P(AKI)) = β₀ + β₁(glycemic_burden) + β₂(vasopressor_dose) + β₃(glycemic_burden × vasopressor_dose) + β₄(temporal_features)
```

**Temporal features:**
- Number of hyperglycemic episodes (>180 mg/dL)
- Duration of sustained hyperglycemia (>4 hours)
- Time to first hyperglycemic episode
- Vasopressor dose-response curve slope

**Random forest or gradient boosting:**
- Predict AKI onset and recovery trajectory
- Feature importance analysis to identify key temporal patterns
- Non-linear interactions automatically detected

**What the alternative reveals:**
- Non-linear thresholds in glycemic risk
- Complex interactions beyond simple multiplication
- Individual patient risk profiles based on temporal patterns

## Falsification Criteria

**Study will be falsified if:**
1. No significant interaction between glycemic burden and vasopressor dose (p > 0.05 after multiple testing correction)
2. Cumulative hyperglycemic burden shows no independent association with AKI (OR < 1.1, 95% CI includes 1)
3. All observed effects are fully explained by baseline severity scores (APACHE II, SOFA)
4. Hospital-level variation explains all between-center differences in treatment effects

**Study will be supported if:**
1. Significant glycemia-vasopressor interaction (p < 0.01)
2. Independent association between cumulative hyperglycemia and AKI (OR > 1.2, 95% CI excludes 1)
3. Non-linear patterns in risk (e.g., threshold effects at specific glucose levels)
4. Effects persist after hospital-level adjustment

**Study may be inconclusive if:**
1. Insufficient power due to limited sample size
2. Heterogeneous treatment protocols across hospitals
3. Missing data prevents proper temporal alignment
4. Confounding by unmeasured factors (e.g., specific sepsis etiology)

## Limitations and Unresolved Questions

**Data limitations:**
- Glucose measurement frequency varies by hospital (may miss brief excursions)
- Vasopressor dosing may have rounding errors
- Lack of continuous glucose monitoring data
- Potential misclassification of sepsis diagnosis

**Analytical limitations:**
- Cannot establish causality (observational design)
- Reverse causation (AKI may affect glucose metabolism)
- Unmeasured confounding (e.g., infection severity not captured)

**Clinical translation limitations:**
- Risk metric may not be actionable without treatment guidance
- Need prospective validation in intervention trials
- Thresholds may vary by patient population

**Key questions requiring further research:**
1. What is the optimal glycemic target for patients on vasopressors?
2. Can tight glycemic control reduce organ injury in high-risk patients?
3. How should glycemic management be individualized based on hemodynamic status?
