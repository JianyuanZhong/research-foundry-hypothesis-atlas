# Encounter-initiated HCC surveillance selection after resection

Status: estimand-repair child of [prior hypothesis]. The outcome-independent contact audit was executed on aggregate data; no hypothesis model was fitted. The biological-recurrence route remains frozen.

## Scientific opening and claim boundary

The strongest supported clinical claim is that incomplete AFP response measured at a 4–6-week visit was strongly prognostic for recurrence and survival in a selected cohort of 549 resected patients with preoperative AFP above 400 ng/mL [K1]. It does not show that persistent AFP is residual tumor, that measurement availability is ignorable, or that testing improves outcomes.

EHR observation is generated by healthcare. At two hospitals, presence and timing of common laboratory orders often predicted survival independently of recorded values [K2]. A review distinguishes patient presence from longitudinal observation and warns that prediction and explanation need different interpretations [K3]. These works support informative observation as a concern; they do not explain why an HCC patient receives targeted surveillance.

The unresolved falsifiable claim is: after a recorded encounter begins, does pre-landmark marker history alter the choice of an HCC-targeted mark beyond measured severity and generic testing? The leading explanation is marker-directed surveillance selection. The main rival is nonspecific intensity: marker history tags sicker or more connected patients who receive every kind of test. A timing rival is database misalignment between encounter entry and later events in the same care episode. They predict different target-versus-control associations inside encounter-initiated windows. Resolving this determines whether retrospective biomarker cohorts transport beyond highly monitored patients. This is ascertainment research, not recurrence or treatment-effect research.

## Estimand repair and hypothesis

The parent defined generic opportunity G using encounters plus non-marker labs, nonhepatic examinations and non-antitumor medications, while reusing generic labs and nonhepatic imaging as control outcomes. Conditioning on outcomes used to create G makes specificity partly deterministic and can omit target-only records.

The repair defines opportunity only from the encounters table. Primary A(i,t)=1 when week t intersects entry through entry+14 days for any encounter; entry is admission time, else visit time, else discharge time. The fixed window never uses labs, examinations, procedures, medications, target/control marks, or eventual discharge, so an outcome cannot create or lengthen it. Overlaps are unioned. The 14-day choice was frozen after one support audit comparing point, 7-, 14- and 30-day anchors, before estimating marker associations.

The unit is patient-week, days 61–365 (44 bins; final bin days 362–365). Target marks are L-M (AFP, PIVKA-II or AFP-L3), S (hepatic/upper-abdominal CT/MR), and secondary T (recorded HCC-directed procedure/systemic medication). Controls are N-L (exact-label sodium, creatinine or hemoglobin) and N-I (nonhepatic CT/MR). L-I (ALT, AST, bilirubin, albumin, INR, platelets) is a severity diagnostic and never defines A.

Primary estimand: within A=1 weeks, the standardized marker-history by mark-type interaction from separate recurrent models for L-M, S, N-L and N-I. Full-cohort history is marker observed versus absent during days -90:+60. Among marker-observed patients, use measurement count plus AFP direction/relative change within exact label/specimen and calendar-era strata; PIVKA-II values are secondary because vitamin K, warfarin and antibiotics interfere. Continuous effects are train-standardized and shown as 75th-versus-25th percentile contrasts. Never impute a value for marker-absent patients.

Report per-opportunity-week risks, risk differences/ratios, patient-level cumulative risk over observed A weeks, and log(RR target)-log(RR control). Controls never define eligibility, A or weights. Keep the full weekly grid to model encounter arrival and count every out-of-anchor mark; do not recode such events as no outcome. Secondary estimands assess encounter-window arrival, T as unclassified treatment occurrence, 7/30-day anchors, an all-week A-by-history model, and pre-first-T follow-up. No local event means absence in this snapshot, not no care or nonrecurrence.

Across 685,300 patient-weeks, a canonical encounter-date anchor produced 4,717 weeks but would exclude 1,410/4,027 (35.0%) target and 1,694/3,951 (42.9%) control weeks; reject it as primary.

Thus it has genuine negative opportunities and non-deterministic choices but is sparse (2.06% of all weeks).

Full admission-to-discharge spells cover 99.6% of target and 99.8% of control weeks, but 38/4,796 intersecting rows last over 30 days, 3 over 90, maximum 170.9. Spell anchoring is sensitivity only because duration can be downstream of illness/care. Counts establish computability, not the hypothesis.

## Matched methods

Split patients by int(SHA256(患者主索引)[:8],16) mod 100: 0–59 train, 60–69 validation, 70–79 locked test, 80–99 reserve. Freeze cohort, anchor, dictionaries, transformations, gates and thresholds before test. Both methods use identical grids, predictors, marks and splits.

Transparent baseline: first model encounter entry/A on all weeks with recurrent-count GEE and pooled logistic regression. Within A=1, fit separate elastic-net pooled-logistic models with patient-clustered GEE inference for L-M, S, N-L and N-I (T/L-I secondary). Inputs include marker history, age, sex, year, service, index stay, pathology/severity, prior treatment, injury history, exact label/specimen, era, PIVKA medication flags, prior encounter entries and prior marks through t-minus only. Estimate standardized contrasts by g-computation and patient bootstrap. This asks conditional choice directly but loses within-window order, co-occurrence and persistent policy states.

Learned/mechanistic alternative: an encounter-triggered two-state semi-Markov observation-policy model on the same windows and marks. A latent state evolves at encounter entry from prior state, elapsed time, severity and prior history. Multi-label emissions model L-M, S, T, N-L and N-I with exact ordering/co-occurrence. Call a state target-enriched only if held-out L-M/S odds relative to N-L/N-I are higher with stable sign; otherwise leave states unlabeled. Compare one state, two states without marker-history transition effects, and the full model. It can reveal persistence/bundling lost by regressions but cannot identify clinician intent or biology.

Require simulation recovery under observed sparsity, no state/label instability over three seeds, 90–98% interval coverage, and held-out gain confined to target/co-occurrence likelihood, not controls. Otherwise use the baseline.

Solver estimate: baseline 8–16 CPU, at most 64 GiB, 1–2 h; alternative one allocated A100, 8 CPU, at most 64 GiB, 2–4 h, with CPU HMM fallback. Estimates are unverified; aggregate extraction measured 74.9 s on 8 CPU/64 GiB. A transformer is deferred because prediction gain would not separate opportunity from selection.

## Gates and falsification

Require at least 150 events per primary mark overall and 20 in validation/test; at least 500 anchor-only and 100 target-only/control-only train weeks; at least 50 marker-absent validation/test patients (previously 56/73); overlap ESS at least 100; no separation after label/era stratification. Split-specific 14-day gates remain for solver verification.

Review at least 100 medication-positive and 100 oncology-like dictionary-negative snippets with PPV/NPV at least 0.85 before HCC-directed T interpretation. Blindly review encounter timestamp semantics and 100 target/control timing records. If entry is backfilled or cannot define forward time, the estimand is inconclusive.

Supportive evidence requires a prespecified history-by-mark interaction favoring L-M and/or S over both controls, Holm-adjusted 95% interval excluding no specificity and specificity ratio of risk ratios at least 1.5, stable in validation/test. Controls must lie within equivalence RR 0.8–1.25 or target specificity is not claimed. The alternative must improve held-out target/co-occurrence score without improving control score and yield a stable target-enriched state.

Adverse evidence is precise equivalence: target interactions within 0.8–1.25, controls at least as associated, and one-state/baseline fit as well. This favors ordinary severity/contact.

Inconclusive: intervals span adverse and supportive regions; sparse split marks; overlap/timestamp/review failure; conclusions reverse across 7/14/30 days; marks concentrate outside A; assay-era incompatibility; or unstable latent states. An imprecise null is not adverse. Similar N-L/N-I association, or a target state equally enriched for controls, falsifies specificity. Train-only marker permutation within observation/era strata must erase marker-specific target association while preserving encounter structure.

## Deliverable and limits

The solver must newly produce cohort/anchor flow; overlap tables by split; all out-of-anchor marks; review results; encounter-arrival model; conditional baseline fits; one/two-state alternatives; standardized contrasts and clustered uncertainty; held-out calibration/log scores; simulation/seed diagnostics; and a threshold-linked supportive/adverse/inconclusive conclusion.

A verifier can check hashes, columns, joins, anchor independence, leakage, splits, support, fits, uncertainty and interpretation rules. It cannot establish recurrence, complete/outside care, assay equivalence, treatment indication, clinician motivation, causal testing effects or biological latent-state meaning. Those require clinical review, linked registry/claims, or prospective scheduled surveillance.

Alternatives retained: reject parent composite G; reject point anchor as primary; retain full spell as sensitivity; select fixed 14-day entry anchor; retain all-week model as sensitivity; select semi-Markov alternative only after recovery; accept baseline-only completion if it fails; keep biological recurrence frozen.
