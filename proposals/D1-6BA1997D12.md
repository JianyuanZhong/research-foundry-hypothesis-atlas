# In-stay attributable assay content versus capture intensity for observation-qualified HCC return

## Episode, scientific deliverable and substantive advance

This is episode 41’s substantive child of `[prior hypothesis]`. The parent’s central question is clinically useful and appropriately narrow: among adults with a first locally recorded HCC-coded inpatient encounter, does strict prior laboratory content add information about a subsequent local recorded admission beyond the amount and recency of prior local record capture, and is that information about return or only about later local observability? Its three-state outcome and nested follow-up windows are the strongest current way to expose the ambiguity in a binary local-return endpoint.

A remaining temporal-validity problem is material. The parent requires a source row to link to a non-anchor encounter whose discharge precedes the index admission and requires the source timestamp to be before the index admission. That does not prove that the row’s native timestamp occurred during the linked prior stay: a retrospectively entered order, a result posted after discharge, or an export timestamp displaced from the encounter could satisfy both conditions. This child makes the primary history policy more conservative and auditable:

> A prior source row is primary-history eligible only when its source-specific native availability time is inside the linked prior encounter’s native admission–discharge interval and inside the 730-day pre-index window; the linked encounter must be completed and its discharge strictly before the index admission.

The parent’s policy remains a prespecified timestamp-only sensitivity. This is not a cosmetic restriction: it changes the estimand from “all pre-index rows attributable to a completed prior encounter” to “information recorded during a completed prior stay,” and tests whether the putative assay-content increment survives removal of retrospective/peri-encounter timing ambiguity.

The future solver must newly produce:

1. a frozen adult first-observed HCC-coded anchor cohort and a complete encounter/source-row temporal ledger;
2. two locked information-policy versions: primary in-stay attributable history (I) and the parent-compatible completed-prior policy (C), both with Q capture/process features and E assay-content features;
3. the parent’s binary 30-day local recorded-return label and exhaustive observation-qualified states (Z_L\in\{R,N^+_L,N^-_L\}) for (L=60,90,180);
4. matched transparent regularized models and a matched interval multistate model for Q and E under the same patient partition, labels and evaluation patients;
5. paired proper-score increments, calibration, patient-clustered uncertainty, temporal-policy and content/process falsifications, and a claim-to-output/evidence-limit table.

Completion is these newly fitted models, locked predictions, uncertainty and audit artifacts—not a favorable result and not readiness.

## Unresolved question and falsifiable hypothesis

The available evidence supports only that the HCC release has native encounter admission/discharge times, source-specific timestamps for laboratories, procedures, orders, medications and examinations, and enough local encounter history to define a reproducible *local recorded* return and later local re-observation. The existing feasibility audit reported nondegenerate 30-day returns and later local encounters, but no model result and no clinical adjudication.

The unresolved claim is:

> Among adults with a first locally recorded adult HCC-keyed inpatient encounter and a discharge eligible for follow-up, does laboratory assay content observed during completed prior stays improve calibrated prediction of the 30-day local recorded-return state beyond the same in-stay capture intensity, admission/discharge context and no-assay process information? If so, is the increment larger for a 30-day return (R) than for a later local re-observation (N^+_L)?

Primary estimands are the E-versus-Q differences in held-out log loss and Brier score for (i) (R) versus no (R), and (ii) the three-state (Z_{90}). The primary history policy is I. The C policy is a temporal-policy sensitivity, not a post hoc alternative. State-specific contrasts and (L=60,90,180) nested windows test whether the conclusion is observation-policy dependent.

Supportive evidence requires a calibrated I-E versus I-Q improvement for (R), a nontrivial and directionally stable (Z_{90}) result, attenuation after assay-content or complete-history permutation, no Q-only reproduction, and agreement across at least two administratively supported L values. A larger I-E increment for (R) than (N^+_L) supports only the bounded statement that in-stay assay content contains more information about the local recorded-return distribution than about later local re-observation. It does not identify biology, an avoidable readmission, treatment indication or causal benefit.

Adverse evidence is a null or harmful I increment, calibration deterioration, complete reproduction by Q, persistence after payload permutation, an increment confined to (N^+_L), or reversal under the in-stay policy. If C is positive but I is null, the evidence is observation/timestamp-policy sensitive and the in-stay assay-content claim is not supported. If I is positive only for (N^+_L), the result is informative about future local observation, not return risk. Inconclusive evidence includes sparse state/interval support, wide patient-clustered intervals, dominant invalid or out-of-stay timestamps, unresolved ledger discrepancies, material boundary sensitivity, or L-window sign changes.

## Primary I policy and C sensitivity

For every source row linked on (`患者主索引`, `就诊号`) to a non-anchor encounter for the same patient, require the linked encounter’s native admission and discharge to be parseable, nonnegative and discharge < (t_0). Require the source-native time to be parseable, in [(t_0-730) days, (t_0)), and in the half-open interval [linked admission, linked discharge]. A row at linked discharge is included; a row at linked admission is included; a row at (t_0) is excluded. If linked stays overlap or source timestamps cannot be assigned unambiguously, retain the row-level exclusion reason and do not silently resolve it.

The source-native times are: laboratories `检验时间`; procedures `开始时间`; orders `开立时间` (never later `开始时间`); medications `开始时间`; examinations `开始时间`. The primary policy does not treat narrative completion, source order status or post-discharge event times as availability. C sensitivity uses the parent-compatible rule: linked encounter completed before (t_0), source-native time in the 730-day window and before (t_0), without requiring containment inside linked admission/discharge. A timestamp-only policy that ignores encounter linkage is an exploratory audit only.

For I and C separately, Q contains no assay identity or result and no clinical text: completed prior encounter count/duration, active days, time since last completed encounter, source-specific row counts and distinct linked-encounter counts for laboratories, procedures, orders, medications and examinations, observed source-type count, missing/invalid/out-of-stay time counts, left-truncation and no-history indicators. Do not include names/doses/routes/frequencies/statuses of drugs, procedure names, order names or examination text in Q.

E adds to the corresponding Q block only laboratory content from eligible rows: `检验`, `定性结果`, valid assay-specific `定量结果`, `标本类型`, recency, span, count, missingness and within-assay change/slope only with at least two distinct valid `检验时间` values. Numeric values are never pooled across assays because metadata confirms no separate laboratory unit column. Assay-specific scaling/ranking must be fit only in the fit bucket and recorded. No direct identifiers (`姓名`, `身份证号`, `手机号`, `医保/就诊卡号`, `住院号`) enter any model.

The row-level ledger must distinguish: missing/invalid source time; timestamp outside the 730-day window; anchor-linked row; linked encounter with missing dates; source time outside linked stay; source time after (t_0); duplicate source keys; unmatched encounter keys; and row inflation. Report I-versus-C differences by source, encounter and patient. This is a key scientific output: it quantifies the temporal-policy change rather than hiding it in preprocessing.

## Baseline and substantive alternative

All model versions use identical eligible people, labels, feature manifests, preprocessing, patient partition and calibration protocol. The transparent baseline is fit-only standardized regularized logistic regression for binary (R), and matched regularized multinomial logistic regression for (Z_L), producing (B_{I,Q},B_{I,E},B_{C,Q},B_{C,E}). Use only fit/selection patients for imputation, standardization, feature selection, penalty and calibration selection. The primary comparison is (B_{I,E}) versus (B_{I,Q}), with C as policy sensitivity; context-only A/A_d models are references.

The substantive alternative is a matched discrete-time multistate observation model (H), fit separately with Q and E under I and C. From (t_d), create intervals [0,7), [7,14), [14,30); for 30-day nonreturns, create a later-local-observation transition risk interval [30,L). Fit complementary-log-log cause-specific hazards with interval offsets, an absorbing first-return state, and a post-day-30 local-observation transition. Use only interval intercepts, predeclared E-by-interval terms and a small ridge/weakly informative penalty selected before locked labels. Convert hazards to coherent probabilities for R, (N^+_L), (N^-_L). The model is scientifically useful because it can attribute an E increment to the return hazard versus the later-observation hazard while respecting timing and competing observed transitions; the multinomial baseline cannot reveal this transition decomposition. H versus B is a method comparison on the same scientific target, not a contest for a small predictive gain.

An irregular latent sequence model is explicitly deferred. It could model missingness and irregular assay trajectories, but it would add representation assumptions before the temporal availability question is resolved and is not necessary to answer the primary estimand. Revisit it only if I-policy support is adequate, H completes within the solver envelope, and a prespecified pilot shows residual within-assay temporal structure not captured by E summaries. A document/pathology or multimodal branch is deferred because `clinical_documents` and `pathology` lack native time and validated semantic extraction, and local images are absent; this is a dependency decision, not a blanket neural-method prohibition.

## Split, uncertainty and evaluation

Use the frozen patient partition SHA-256(normalized `患者主索引`) modulo 100: 0–59 fit, 60–69 preprocessing/hyperparameter/fit-only calibration selection, 70–79 locked evaluation, 80–99 inaccessible. Keep every encounter, source row, outcome interval and later observation for a patient in one bucket. Freeze cohort ledger, I/C eligibility rules, feature manifest, assay scaling, model dimensions, penalty and calibration before reading locked outcomes or predictions. The same locked patients are used for all I/C and Q/E comparisons.

Primary evaluation is paired patient-clustered locked log loss and Brier, lower better: E minus Q for binary R and vector (Z_{90}). Report calibration intercept/slope and reliability by state, interval-specific cumulative probabilities, and the predeclared state contrast (Delta_R-Delta_{N^+_L}). Report H versus B only for scientific interpretability and coherence, not as a clinical claim. Use >=1,000 patient-clustered paired bootstrap resamples of locked predictions, retaining all intervals for each patient, for 95% intervals. Do not use AUROC/AUPRC as decision criteria.

Support-stratify by Q capture intensity, assay support/no-history, calendar era and department as internal heterogeneity checks. Declare minimum locked support per state and interval before evaluation. If a state or interval is sparse for calibration or bootstrap inference, report it as inconclusive rather than merging states post hoc. Standardize O_L-stratum score contrasts to the observed O_L composition and verify algebraically that the weighted stratum result equals the all-state result within a declared tolerance.

## Falsification and validity gates

Mandatory tests are:

1. Shuffle complete I histories across patients within each split while preserving Q, labels and source counts; I-E versus I-Q should attenuate.
2. Permute laboratory payload within assay and partition while preserving assay identity, timestamps and counts; content increments should attenuate.
3. Permute assay identity while preserving values, times and counts; assay-specific signal should attenuate.
4. Shuffle eligible source times within patient and source while preserving payload/counts, then rerun containment checks; timing-sensitive increments should attenuate.
5. Compare H_Q with H_E. If H_Q reproduces the E result for R and (Z_L), the assay-content interpretation is rejected despite predictive performance.
6. Compare I and C under locked, identical patients where both policies are defined. A material sign or calibration reversal is policy-sensitive and adverse to an unqualified assay-content claim.
7. Permute labels within calendar era and department; persistent performance indicates leakage or a label artifact.
8. Verify no row at/after (t_0), anchor-linked primary row, current-anchor row, post-discharge feature, future-window feature, outcome-derived (O_L) feature or cross-bucket patient data enters any model.
9. Publish duplicate-key, multiplicity, unmatched-key and row-inflation audits for every two-column join, and reconcile the prior feasibility ledgers (42,681/4,913 versus 42,688/4,916) before confirmatory interpretation.
10. Verify coherent H probability sums, hazard bounds, binary-return probability agreement, and the O_L algebraic decomposition.
11. Repeat exact half-open boundaries, equal-time ties, overlapping stays, missing-time exclusions and earliest-anchor choice. Material changes are inconclusive.
12. Repeat L=60,90,180 only after publishing administrative eligibility. A sign change or one-window-only result is observation-process-sensitive.

The strict in-stay policy is supportive only if it retains adequate support and produces the prespecified calibrated increment with no leakage, Q-only reproduction or unresolved ledger discrepancy. A positive C result with null I is not evidence for the primary claim; it motivates a timestamp/release audit. A positive result confined to (N^+_L) means prediction of later local observability. A negative finding can be scientifically important: it would show that apparent pre-index assay signal is not robust to defensible availability timing or is largely a capture-process artifact.

## Evidence boundaries, demonstrations and compute

This proposal is a bounded adaptation, not reproduction. The natural-history demonstration’s full article/XML and supplements and the Bayesian longitudinal article/supplement are available and were used for method context; the cancer demonstration’s main article and full STAR Methods remain unavailable, and only its supplement was inspected. Those demonstrations do not establish this HCC hypothesis. The available method guidance supports dated-history and longitudinal alternatives but does not require a GPU or a neural model.

Measured discovery evidence is source-guide/schema/header inspection and the incumbent bounded encounter/timing audit; no model, GPU fit, locked evaluation or clinical conclusion was run in this episode. Planning estimate, not measured: up to 16 CPUs, 262,144 MiB RAM and 28,800 seconds in the separate future solver envelope. The I/C ledger and transparent fits are expected to be CPU-first; feature construction/fits are estimated at 1–4 CPU hours and bootstrap/permutation work at 1–4 hours, unverified. H is expected to remain CPU-first after interval aggregation. A GPU is not required. If a future irregular sequence pilot is approved, it must request an allocated A100 and use `cuda:0`; ordinary-shell CUDA absence is not evidence of unavailable hardware. No proposer or solver weight training is authorized.

The HCC release cannot establish active disease, cirrhosis, stage, tumor burden, liver reserve, admission cause, indication, planned versus unplanned status, treatment receipt/completion, death, outside-hospital care, complete follow-up, benefit, safety, utility or external validity. Clinical documents/pathology cannot safely fill those gaps because native time and validated extraction are unavailable. Stronger treatment or clinical-policy conclusions require expert adjudication, source release/availability logs, dated imaging/pathology, stage and intent labels, death/outside-care linkage, prospective external validation and a causal or utility study.
