## 1. Unresolved question and clinical importance

The decision to transfer a patient out of the ICU is one of the highest-stakes, least-standardised
choices in critical care; unplanned readmission is associated with higher mortality and is used as a
quality signal. Preprescribed static scores (SOFA-type severity, last labs) are the usual inputs, but
clinicians actually judge *dynamic* readiness - whether oxygen demand is still climbing, whether vitals
are still swinging. Whether that dynamic "residual instability" carries information BEYOND a static
snapshot, or is an artifact of overall severity / how often the patient was measured, is unresolved and
consequential: it determines whether discharge-readiness tools should model trajectories or just levels.
[K1] shows pre-discharge data suffice to predict 48h readmission (opaque ensemble; not MIMIC-IV);
[K2] shows the whole literature is heterogeneous and high-risk-of-bias; [K3] shows, on MIMIC-IV,
apparent readmission predictors can be non-actionable frailty/administrative confounding.

## 2. Leading claim vs strongest rival (discriminating observation)

- Leading claim (H1): in patients discharged alive from the ICU, a prespecified DYNAMIC instability
  footprint in the 24h before ICU discharge (O2-requirement escalation and vital-sign variability)
  adds prognostic information about 48h unplanned ICU readmission / 7-day post-discharge death beyond
  a static last-day severity model.
- Strongest rival (H0): the footprint is fully explained by (a) overall severity/comorbidity and
  (b) observation intensity (sicker patients get measured more, and frequent sampling inflates
  variance estimates) - i.e., the signal is confounding/sampling, not a distinct physiologic state [K3].
- Observation on which they differ: the held-out INCREMENTAL discrimination (ΔAUROC/ΔAUPRC) of the
  dynamic model over the static model, and whether that increment survives a measurement-frequency
  control and a permuted-label negative control.
- What outcomes would/would not establish: a positive increment establishes PREDICTIVE utility only -
  not that delaying discharge or lightening support improves outcomes, which needs prospective/target-trial
  work. A null increment would not prove instability is clinically meaningless; it bounds what
  pre-discharge routine vitals encode.

## 5. Method exploration (baseline vs substantive alternative on the SAME question)

- B0 (baseline, interpretable): penalised logistic regression on static predictors (age, SOFA-surrogate,
  nearest lactate, ICU LOS, admission_type, Charlson). Reports held-out AUROC/AUPRC + calibration.
- B1 (summary-instability): B0 + window summaries (mean/SD/range of HR/SBP/RR/SpO2; FiO2 slope;
  O2-flow escalation; abnormal-threshold excursion counts).
- A1 (learned temporal alternative): a sequence model (small GRU) over the 24h multivariate vitals/O2
  series on a regular 1h grid, which can encode TEMPORAL PATTERNS (intermittent desaturation, drift)
  that mean/SD summaries lose. Value it could reveal: whether instability is a pattern (H1) rather than
  a level; if A1 ~ B1, temporal structure adds nothing.
- Rival/negative controls: a measurement-frequency-only model; a permuted-label model (must be null).
- Compute: all three are small; CPU is adequate; a single A100 is optional and NOT required. Estimated
  < 1h, within solver envelope (16 CPU / 8 GPU / 8h). Discovery did not fit models.

## 6. Analysis and falsification criteria (prespecified)

- Primary: ΔAUROC and ΔAUPRC of A1 and B1 vs B0 on the LOCKED held-out split; 1000-sample bootstrap
  95% CIs; calibration (slope, Brier). Decision-curve net benefit as a secondary descriptive.
- Supportive (H1): ΔAUROC(A1 vs B0) >= 0.02 with 95% CI excluding 0 on held-out, AND the instability
  increment remains >0 after adding measurement frequency, AND the permuted-label control is null.
- Adverse (H0 wins): ΔAUROC(A1 vs B0) <= 0.01, or CI includes 0, or the increment is fully attenuated
  by measurement frequency (CI including 0) - i.e., the footprint is confounding/sampling.
- Inconclusive: wide CI (low readmission prevalence / power), miscalibration, or A1 and B1 disagree
  such that temporal vs summary structure cannot be separated.
- Honest non-result possible: 48h readmission prevalence is low (review range 1.3-13.7% [K2]), so a
  precise null or an inconclusive result is a legitimate outcome.
