# Forward validation of contemporaneous P beyond remote B in repeat-TACE HCC

## Research question and clinical advance

In adults with HCC who have a qualifying repeat TACE event and complete pre-event/baseline, contemporaneous pre-repeat, and post-event assay triplets, does the contemporaneous pre-repeat assay P improve prediction of the future same-assay value Y beyond remote baseline B and the identical compact decision-time core? The clinically consequential unresolved claim is transport/prognostic: whether a measurement available immediately before a repeat-TACE decision contains reproducible future-era information that is not already represented by remote baseline status and decision-time context. This is not a treatment-effect, safety, response, toxicity, or clinical-utility claim.

The parent evidence supports a frozen repeat-TACE complete-triplet cohort, exact B/P/event/Y clocks, and the nested conceptual comparison. It does not establish future-era transport or incremental value. This successor adds outcome-blind chronological validation and sparse-support diagnostics while retaining the frozen reconstruction and denominators.

## Estimand and models

The primary estimand is pooled within assay, not a treatment-arm contrast. For each assay separately and each forward test window, compare paired out-of-sample prediction error for:

- M0: remote B plus the locked compact core: age, sex indicators, systemic/comparator indicator, TACE2-to-repeat timing (`days`), and P lead time (`P_lead_hours`).
- M1: exactly M0 plus contemporaneous pre-repeat P.

P is excluded from M0 and added only to M1. Arm-specific errors, systemic/comparator fractions, and systemic test-to-training ratios are descriptive transport/selection diagnostics; they do not support an arm-balanced treatment-effect claim. Albumin and bilirubin are not pooled because their units differ.

Use ridge regression with alpha grid `[0.01, 0.1, 1, 10, 100]`. For every origin, training rows are strictly before the origin and test rows are in the following 365-day interval. Age median imputation, missingness indicators, sex encoding, variance filtering, centering/scaling, and alpha selection are learned from training rows only. The same folds and preprocessing rule are used for M0 and M1 within a split. Alpha is selected by inner leave-one-out error using training outcomes only. Compare paired RMSE and MAE; use patient-frequency paired bootstrap stratified by arm for uncertainty. Origins are fixed from calendar dates before outcome-dependent metrics: 2021-01-01, 2022-01-01, 2023-01-01, and 2024-01-01.

A linkage-placebo/permutation diagnostic is specified, but this execution used `N_PLACEBO=0`; therefore no placebo-based conclusion is made. A future rerun may add positive placebo repetitions without changing the population, clocks, estimand, or model.

## Computed forward results

The corrected execution used the nested design above and produced distinct M0/M1 predictions. This is important because an earlier preliminary runner accidentally included P in both models and its zero differences are invalid and discarded.

Albumin pooled RMSE gains (M0 minus M1), with paired bootstrap 95% intervals, were:

- 2021 origin, test n=45 (14 systemic/31 comparator): 0.706; CI 0.385 to 1.018; standardized gain 0.134.
- 2022 origin, test n=28 (10/18): 0.820; CI 0.334 to 1.366; standardized gain 0.178.
- 2023 origin, test n=29 (5/24): 0.532; CI −0.143 to 1.448; standardized gain 0.099.
- 2024 origin, test n=14 (1/13): 1.125; CI 0.142 to 2.081; standardized gain 0.217.

Bilirubin pooled RMSE gains were:

- 2021 origin, test n=45 (14/31): 2.756; CI 1.496 to 4.224; standardized gain 0.250.
- 2022 origin, test n=28 (10/18): 3.598; CI 0.867 to 5.694; standardized gain 0.334.
- 2023 origin, test n=29 (5/24): 0.720; CI −0.938 to 3.345; standardized gain 0.058.
- 2024 origin, test n=16 (2/14): 9.392; CI −0.129 to 16.973; standardized gain 0.474.

Both assays were favorable in all four pooled point estimates, but the 2023 intervals include zero for both assays. The apparently large 2024 gains are not strong transport evidence because systemic support is extremely sparse: albumin has 1 systemic test row and bilirubin 2; the systemic test-to-training ratios are 0.027 and 0.054, respectively. The 2021 and 2022 splits provide the clearest supportive forward evidence; 2023 is inconclusive; 2024 is sparse-support inconclusive despite favorable pooled point estimates. Arm-specific results are not used to upgrade this interpretation. For example, albumin 2023 has adverse systemic RMSE change (−0.331; n=5) while comparator improves (0.966; n=24), illustrating why composition and arm-specific results remain diagnostic only.

## Interpretation and falsification

Supportive evidence would require positive paired gains for both assays across multiple prespecified forward origins, with uncertainty compatible with improvement and adequate arm support, plus no material deterioration in either assay. The observed 2021–2022 pattern is supportive of a reproducible incremental prognostic signal, but not definitive because later windows are sparse and 2023 is inconclusive.

Adverse evidence would be repeated negative pooled gains, deterioration in both RMSE and MAE, or a consistent failure of M1 in later eras. Inconclusive evidence is expected when intervals span zero or an arm-support gate fails; it must not be converted into either confirmation or refutation. A future positive linkage-placebo run would be a falsification warning that apparent incremental value may arise from linkage or model artifacts rather than patient-level P information.

The experiment can computationally verify source reconstruction, frozen counts, clocks, temporal train/test separation, model nesting, training-only preprocessing/tuning, paired predictions, bootstrap intervals, and composition diagnostics. It cannot establish that the prediction improvement is clinically important, causal, actionable, safe, or beneficial; cannot recover unrecorded toxicity, response, recovery, or treatment exposure; and cannot rule out selection/collider bias induced by conditioning on complete triplets and repeat-TACE participation. Clinical adjudication, external validation with richer outcomes, and a prospective or target-trial-compatible study would be required for those stronger conclusions.
