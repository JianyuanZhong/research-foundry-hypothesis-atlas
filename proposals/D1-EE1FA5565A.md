# Episode 86 successor: can local intercept recalibration make a transported repeat-lactate threshold trustworthy?

## Unresolved question, clinical importance, and substantive advance

A hospital considering a silent seven-day mortality-review queue faces two different decisions. A transported score may order a fixed number of patients acceptably while its displayed probabilities are miscalibrated in the receiving practice. If the latter occurs, should the receiving hospital abandon the score, or can it repair the absolute-risk display with a small, auditable local validation step?

The selected Episode 85 design tests whether the unmodified R_full score transports both in rank and at a prespecified 0.10 predicted seven-day in-hospital-death threshold across low- and high-repeat-lactate practices. That is an important baseline, but an adverse threshold result only says that local validation/recalibration is needed. The unresolved, clinically actionable claim is whether *intercept-only* local recalibration—leaving all feature effects and patient ordering untouched—restores threshold validity across the opposite practice stratum. This is a substantive successor, not a relabeling: it tests a concrete deployment remedy and its required validation design.

The strongest evidence currently available is narrower. The inherited evidence record reports that Baysan et al. (2022; DOI 10.1097/CCE.0000000000000750; PMCID PMC9444407) found only modest incremental discrimination from 24-hour lactate in a measured sepsis cohort (recalibrated APACHE IV C-statistic 0.62 to 0.64). The inherited record also reports that Sisk et al. (2020; DOI 10.1093/jamia/ocaa242; PMCID PMC7810439) found frequent missing observation/process representation in prediction studies. Those findings support examining repeat-lactate value and observation process, not cross-practice threshold validity or a recalibration remedy. I have not treated the unavailable cancer main article or its full STAR Methods as inspected; the research-ambition README says only its supplement and metadata are available.

### Primary falsifiable hypothesis

For each directed transfer between the frozen low- and high-repeat-practice hospital strata, leave-one-target-hospital-out intercept recalibration of the source-trained R_full score will reduce the worst absolute observed-minus-predicted D7 error across the prespecified repeat-availability × threshold-side cells by at least 0.02 and will bring that worst error to at most 0.05:

[
q_{local}(d)=max_{s,z}|mean(D7-p_{local})|,
quad
q_{source}(d)=max_{s,z}|mean(D7-p_{source})|,
]

where (s=F_available), (z=1[pgeq0.10]), and the primary paired improvement is (I=q_{source}-q_{local}). The primary summaries are the pooled out-of-fold target-person q values for each direction and the worst-direction
(I_* = min_d I(d)) and (q_*=max_d q_{local}(d)). Support requires lower95((I_*)) >= 0.02 and upper95((q_*)) <= 0.05, with at least 30 people in every one of the eight local cells in the original target data and at least 1,900 finite joint bootstrap replicates. This is a threshold-calibration hypothesis, not a claim that a real review action improves survival.

A secondary, deterministic invariance hypothesis is that intercept recalibration does not change within-target-hospital R_full ordering or any equal-capacity queue membership: because (p_{local}=expit(eta_{full}+delta_h)) with one constant (delta_h) per hospital, rankings and the inherited Delta7/DeltaInf queue functionals must agree exactly (up to explicitly reported numeric ties). This makes the remedy auditable: it can repair a probability display but cannot manufacture queue value.

## Falsification and interpretation

First classify not-computable for source/hash/header/schema/key or inherited cohort/outcome-count mismatch, changed clocks/features/folds/capacities, target-label leakage into held-out predictions, nonfinite fits/roots, wrong queue cardinality, altered cell definitions, insufficient finite bootstrap, or missing output artifacts. A calibration failure is not a queue failure.

The local-recalibration hypothesis is supportive only if all eight original local cells pass n>=30, >=1,900 joint replicates pass, lower95(I_*) >=0.02, upper95(q_*) <=0.05, and q_local deletion does not depend on one hospital (support is inconclusive if >30% of leave-one-hospital-out deletions violate 0.05). It is adverse if the computation is valid and lower95(q_*) >0.05 (the minimal remedy fails the threshold screen), or upper95(I_*) <0.02 (it does not achieve the prespecified improvement). Otherwise it is inconclusive. If q_source already meets 0.05, report that no recalibration was needed; the new remedy is still supportive only if its improvement gate is met, not because it confirms the parent.

Supportive local recalibration means only that this cross-fitted, historical target-label procedure produced the specified EICU threshold-calibration pattern. It does not establish ordering, assay/specimen validity, true norepinephrine administration or indication, lactate ordering/release/viewing, clinical review, staffing/capacity, preventability, fairness, safety, causal treatment effect, external/temporal transport, or patient benefit. Adverse or inconclusive results do not refute general lactate prognosis and do not prove recalibration is useless elsewhere. A rank-supportive/threshold-adverse pattern means a rank-only queue may remain usable as a model screen while absolute-risk thresholding requires more local data or a different model. A threshold-supportive but rank-adverse pattern cannot justify deployment of a queue. Stronger claims require adjudicated medication/laboratory and workflow data, stakeholder-defined utility/capacity, independent temporal/external validation with prespecified recalibration, subgroup review, and ultimately an impact study or randomized evaluation.
