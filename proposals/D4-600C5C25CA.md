# Provisional hypothesis (episode 1)

## Proposed relationship
Among adults with two UK Biobank assessment-center visits, a similar rise in HbA1c between visits is associated with greater subsequent short-term pancreatic cancer risk when accompanied by weight loss than when accompanied by weight gain. The proposed advance is a temporally explicit comparison of metabolic deterioration with an observable body-composition trajectory, rather than treating diabetes or obesity as a single risk state.

## Grounding and current evidence
This is a provisional adaptation of expert seed UKB 40, whose imported proposal states that comparable HbA1c rise plus weight loss may mark occult pancreatic cancer and identifies active weight loss, glucose-lowering treatment, and other wasting diseases as rivals. The seed explicitly requires verification of repeat-measurement overlap, dates, missingness, and event support; no such verification has yet been executed in this episode. The seed's supplied links are background pointers, not inspected evidence establishing the hypothesis.

## Uncertainties and missing evidence
The key unresolved issue is whether weight loss adds prognostic information beyond glycemic change, age, sex, BMI, assessment interval, smoking, and glucose-lowering treatment, and whether the association is specific to incident cancer after the second visit. Existing evidence does not establish onset of diabetes from measurements years apart, nor distinguish treatment-induced weight change, preclinical cancer, and general wasting. Cancer ascertainment is limited to available registry/outcome fields and may miss non-hospitalized disease. Full literature inspection and three-key-reference delivery remain incomplete.

## Falsifiable test and data binding
Use the UKB/ukb672073 completed subset and its documented Parquet derivative. Restrict to participants with two assessment-center dates and paired HbA1c and weight measurements, no pancreatic cancer before the second visit, and follow-up beginning at the second visit. Compare incident pancreatic cancer in prespecified near-term and later windows, stratifying by direction and magnitude of weight change at similar HbA1c change; adjust for baseline covariates and treatment where available. The primary falsification is a prespecified interaction/contrast estimate with uncertainty: no excess risk for weight loss at comparable HbA1c rise, or loss of association after treatment/interval handling, would weaken the claim. A positive result would support a prognostic association only, not causality or a cancer mechanism.

Required source fields are UKB field IDs 30750 (HbA1c), 30740 (glucose), 21002 (weight), 21001 (BMI), 20003 (treatment/medication code), 40005 (date of cancer diagnosis), 40006 (cancer type ICD10), 41270 (diagnoses ICD10), 41280 (date of first in-patient diagnosis), and 53 (assessment-center date). The exact table names, columns, archive members, and join keys must be verified against the catalog before execution; the provisional design does not assume an unverified cross-dataset join.

## Method comparison
A simple baseline is a prespecified Cox or discrete-time model with HbA1c change, weight-change direction, interval, and clinical covariates, including an interaction term. A substantive alternative is a joint trajectory model (or structured two-stage model) that fits repeated HbA1c and weight trajectories and tests whether the slope/curvature combination predicts incident cancer beyond the endpoint contrast. The alternative could reveal whether the signal depends on timing and persistence of the metabolic/body-composition pattern rather than a single between-visit difference; it would be deferred if repeated measurements are too sparse or event support is inadequate. Both use the same population, split, outcome, and uncertainty evaluation. CPU is adequate for the baseline; the trajectory alternative is CPU-feasible for a bounded cohort unless profiling shows otherwise.
