# Outcome-blind eligibility, temporally honest observation modeling, and calendar-feasibility boundary for frozen P-beyond-B repeat-TACE HCC validation

## Targeted evolution of [prior hypothesis]

This is a substantive child of `[prior hypothesis]`. The parent made the key distinction between outcome-blind `B/P` eligibility and post-event observation of `Y`, and its full-source audit showed that only the 2022 forward block has adequate arm support and outcome observation. The remaining weakness is that the parent’s observation model is only a training-fit diagnostic: its future probabilities, Brier score, and inverse-observation ESS are not temporally cross-fitted or calibrated, and it does not distinguish failure to observe the exact assay from absence of any post-event laboratory/contact opportunity. The calendar overlap model is likewise fit in-sample on pooled training-plus-test risk-set rows. Thus the parent establishes a strong warning, but not whether `Y` observation is at least predictably outcome-blind from information available before `Y`, nor whether later transport failure is covariate extrapolation or capture failure.

The successor adds an executable, patient-level, strictly pre-`Y` observation-process audit and an honest calendar-support diagnostic. It does **not** repair missing outcomes by weighting or imputation and does **not** change the primary complete-triplet estimand. It tests a falsifiable feasibility claim: conditional on the frozen outcome-blind `BP_eligible` risk set, can future `Y` observation be predicted and transported using only pre-event covariates and calendar/contact opportunity proxies, with calibrated probabilities and adequate arm-specific support? If not, favorable complete-case RMSE in later blocks is formally non-transport evidence, even when pre-`Y` covariate overlap is acceptable.

## Evidence-supported and untested claims

The strongest claim supported by the parent’s executed full-source audit is narrow: for the selected adult HCC repeat-TACE complete-triplet prediction task, the 2022 forward block has 29 outcome-blind `BP_eligible` episodes (10 systemic, 19 comparator) and 28 observed outcomes per assay, while the locked model comparison reported favorable P-beyond-B held-out gains. This supports, at most, incremental short-horizon prediction in that selected and observed 2022 calendar mixture. It does not support multi-era transport, a treatment effect, treatment response, safety, survival, clinical benefit, utility, actionability, or causal interpretation.

The untested claim addressed here is whether later-calendar observation and support are sufficiently predictable from information available before `Y` to make their complete-triplet estimates interpretable as transport evidence. The audit is explicitly not a missing-at-random test: good prediction or calibration of `R=Y_observed` on measured variables cannot rule out dependence on the unobserved `Y` or on unrecorded outside care. A failure is nevertheless consequential because it blocks transport interpretation; it is not evidence that the prognostic signal is absent.

## Exact clocks, risk-set order, and frozen estimand

For each selected repeat event and each assay, preserve the parent clocks exactly:

- `B` is the latest valid uncensored assay on TACE2 calendar days −30 through −1.
- `P` is the latest valid assay in the selected repeat encounter in `[event_time−72 hours,event_time)`.
- `Y` is the selected valid exact assay in `(event_time,event_time+72 hours]`, nearest +24 hours with the inherited deterministic tie rule.
- Require `B_time < P_time < event_time < Y_time`, positive P lead, one patient-assay row, and no lab-informed event selection.

Construct and freeze the following event-level table before reading any post-event assay value:

1. selected repeat event;
2. `has_B` under the frozen B clock;
3. `has_P` under the frozen P clock;
4. `BP_eligible`, requiring both valid assays, strict ordering, positive P lead, and no post-event information;
5. only after the preceding table is immutable, `R=Y_observed` under the full Y window and tie rule;
6. `complete=BP_eligible & R`.

`R` is a binary observation indicator only; the numeric value of `Y` must never enter the support or observation model. The primary analysis remains exactly the complete-triplet population, not the larger `BP_eligible` risk set.

Use origins `2021-01-01`, `2022-01-01`, `2023-01-01`, and `2024-01-01`. Training events satisfy `event_date < origin`; the future test block is `[origin, origin+365 days)`, with no overlap. Preserve the locked assay-specific models:

```text
M0: Y ~ B + systemic-record indicator + age + sex indicators
       + TACE2-to-repeat days + P-lead hours
M1: M0 + P
```

Preserve training-only age imputation and missingness indicator, sex coding, variance filtering, centering/scaling, inner leave-one-patient-out alpha selection over `[0.01, 0.1, 1, 10, 100]`, ridge fits, paired arm-stratified patient-frequency bootstrap intervals conditional on fixed pre-origin fits, and the primary standardized held-out RMSE gain `[RMSE(M0)-RMSE(M1)]/SD_test(Y)`. MAE, bias, arm-specific errors, temporal drift, and influence remain secondary. None of the new diagnostics weights, deletes, imputes, or changes the fitted rows or estimand.

## Diagnostic A: outcome-blind eligibility and support

For each assay and origin report the full funnel overall and by systemic/comparator arm in training and test. Retain the parent’s descriptive gates: at least 20 `BP_eligible` episodes total and at least 10 in each arm in both pre-origin training and future test; report the same gate separately for complete triplets. Gate failure does not remove a block and cannot be rescued by a favorable RMSE point estimate.

For calendar support, use only variables observed before `Y`: `B`, `P`, age, sex, P-lead hours, TACE2-to-repeat days, and the prespecified arm label. Replace the parent’s primary in-sample overlap quantities with patient-level cross-fitted quantities:

- partition training risk-set rows by patient, with deterministic seed and all events from a patient in one fold;
- for each training row, predict calendar membership from a model that did not train on that patient, using fold-specific training imputation, missingness flags, standardization, and regularized logistic fitting;
- fit the final membership model on all pre-origin training rows and predict future rows;
- report out-of-fold training and future propensity ranges, future fraction outside the out-of-fold training range, normalized training-to-future odds ESS and maximum weight, arm-specific ESS, and same-arm nearest-neighbor distances in standardized pre-Y space;
- use the 95th percentile leave-one-patient-out training nearest-neighbor distance as the fixed extrapolation reference.

If patient-level folds or two distinct membership classes are not estimable in an arm, report that quantity as not estimable instead of fitting a misleading model. Warnings are any future propensity outside the training range, arm-specific ESS below 10, or more than 5% of future same-arm rows above the predeclared nearest-neighbor threshold. The legacy pooled in-sample diagnostic may be retained for reproducibility but cannot override cross-fitted warnings.

## Diagnostic B: can `Y` observation be modeled outcome-blind?

Among `BP_eligible` rows define `R` only after the risk set is frozen. Fit a pre-origin observation model using the same pre-Y predictors as Diagnostic A plus no post-event or numeric-Y variables. The primary observation model is a regularized logistic model with the following leakage protections:

- patient-level K-fold cross-fitting within pre-origin training, with folds fixed before fitting and every patient confined to one fold;
- fold-specific imputation, age-missingness capture before imputation, standardization, and model fitting;
- no use of `Y`, `Y_time`, post-event lab values, complete-case status, future test rows, or any post-origin information as predictors or tuning inputs;
- final model fit on all pre-origin training `BP_eligible` rows is applied once to future `BP_eligible` rows, with no refit on future labels.

The model’s target is only `R`, so this is an outcome-blind observation-process prediction, not a claim of missing-at-random. Report, overall and by arm where estimable:

1. cross-fitted training Brier score and log loss;
2. future Brier score and log loss, using `R` only as the held-out evaluation label;
3. calibration-in-the-large and calibration slope from a held-out logistic recalibration of `R` on the logit predicted probability, with 95% intervals when cells permit;
4. reliability bins with at least 10 observations per populated bin, otherwise a documented not-estimable result;
5. predicted probability range, observed fraction with Wilson interval, and observed-case inverse-probability ESS as a diagnostic only;
6. observed-versus-missing standardized mean differences for B, P, P-lead, timing, age, and sex when estimable.

Predeclare an observation-model warning if future observation is below 80%, its Wilson interval is clearly below the pre-origin rate, calibration-in-the-large has absolute value at least 0.10, calibration slope’s 95% interval excludes 1, observation ESS is below 10, or any observed-versus-missing pre-Y SMD has absolute value at least 0.5. A well-calibrated model means only that measured pre-Y variables predict recording; it cannot prove that unobserved outcomes are ignorable. A poorly calibrated model means that outcome observation is not reliably modeled from measured pre-Y variables and blocks transport interpretation.

## Diagnostic C: separate contact/capture opportunity from exact-assay observation

Use the encounter source only for a secondary, explicitly non-estimand capture decomposition. For each `BP_eligible` event, after event selection but without using the numeric Y value, derive:

- `post_contact`: at least one same-patient encounter with a valid `就诊时间`, `入院时间`, or `出院时间` whose interval overlaps `(event_time,event_time+72 hours]`; document a fixed precedence and interval rule, and do not count the linked procedure encounter solely because it contains the event;
- `any_lab_capture`: at least one valid, timestamped laboratory row of any assay in that window, if broad-lab parsing is possible;
- `R_albumin` and `R_bilirubin`: the frozen exact-assay observation indicators.

Report the nested aggregate funnel `BP_eligible -> post_contact -> any_lab_capture -> exact assay`, by arm and calendar block. If broad-lab parsing cannot be safely verified from the available schema/values, mark `any_lab_capture` unavailable rather than equating missing exact assay with no contact. This decomposition can localize whether late-era loss is contact capture or assay-specific recording; it cannot establish that a contact or lab row represents complete clinical follow-up or outside-care absence.

## Falsification and interpretation

- **Supportive local prediction:** reconstruction, clocks, outcome-blind and complete-triplet gates pass; the locked M1 gain is positive with its prespecified bootstrap criterion; and no material support or observation warning occurs. This supports incremental short-horizon prediction only in that selected, observed calendar mixture.
- **Supportive observation-process feasibility but not ignorability:** cross-fitted `R` predictions are calibrated and capture does not collapse. This makes measured observation predictable, but still does not identify the complete-triplet estimand for unobserved Y or establish transport beyond the observed risk set.
- **Adverse observation-process result:** poor held-out calibration, low future observation, low observation ESS, or large measured observed-versus-missing imbalance. A favorable complete-case RMSE then cannot be promoted to transport evidence; it does not prove M1 has no predictive value.
- **Adverse calendar transport result:** cross-fitted propensity non-overlap, low arm-specific ESS, or same-arm extrapolation. Later-era estimates remain descriptive even if Y observation is well modeled.
- **Inconclusive result:** sparse arm counts, influential patients, unavailable calibration cells, unparseable contact opportunity, missing assay units/reference ranges, denominator/clock drift, or any mismatch in frozen reconstruction. A block with only one or two systemic complete outcomes is inconclusive by design.
- **Adverse prediction:** in a gate-passing block, a bootstrap interval at or below zero or repeated M1 degradation. This challenges incremental prediction in the tested selected population, not the sufficiency of B, a mechanism, or a treatment effect.

## What changed and what remains uncertain

Changed from the parent: the outcome-observation question is now a temporally honest, patient-level prediction/calibration diagnostic rather than a training-fit Brier/ESS summary; calendar support is cross-fitted rather than pooled in-sample; age missingness is captured before imputation; and an optional aggregate post-contact/any-lab/exact-assay funnel separates opportunity from assay-specific recording. These additions directly test whether observation is predictable from outcome-blind information and whether later transport failure is support or capture failure, while leaving every primary row, clock, endpoint, and estimator frozen.

The executed parent audit remains the empirical baseline: 242 `BP_eligible` events per assay overall; 2022 has 29 future risk-set events (10 systemic, 19 comparator) and 28 observed outcomes; 2023 has 43 risk-set events but only 9 systemic and 29 observed outcomes; 2024 has 70 risk-set events but only 9 systemic and 14 albumin/16 bilirubin observed outcomes. Thus the successor is expected to preserve the parent’s negative-feasibility boundary unless cross-fitted diagnostics unexpectedly show otherwise. Even a favorable observation model cannot overcome sparse systemic support or prove missing-at-random. Multi-era clinical transport and patient benefit remain untested.
