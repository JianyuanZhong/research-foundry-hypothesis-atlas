# UKB-12: CAD Polygenic Risk Expression in Different Lipoprotein and Inflammatory Backgrounds — Residual CAD Risk in High-PRS, Low-ApoB Individuals by CRP Level

## 1. Unresolved Question and Clinical Importance

Polygenic risk scores (PRS) for coronary artery disease (CAD) identify individuals whose genetic susceptibility is far above population average; post hoc analyses of completed trials indicate that high-PRS individuals derive the greatest benefit from LDL-cholesterol-lowering strategies [K1]. In parallel, multivariable Mendelian randomization (MR) shows that among circulating lipoprotein traits, apolipoprotein B (ApoB) — the number of atherogenic particles — is the trait that retains a robust causal relationship with coronary heart disease (CHD) after conditioning on LDL-C and triglycerides [K3]. And the CANTOS randomized trial demonstrated that lowering inflammation (IL-1β blockade) without changing lipid levels reduces recurrent cardiovascular events [K2].

Taken together, these three bodies of evidence imply that CAD risk decomposes into at least two partially separable components: a lipid-driven (ApoB-driven) component and an inflammatory component. **The unresolved question is how these components express themselves inside genetically stratified subgroups.** Specifically: among individuals with *high* CAD PRS but *low* ApoB (i.e., whose genetic risk is not currently expressed through the lipid pathway), does elevated C-reactive protein (CRP) — a marker of systemic inflammation — independently predict incident CAD?

This is clinically important because:
1. **Risk reclassification beyond lipid levels.** Current preventive cardiology decisions (statin intensity, imaging, screening) are driven primarily by LDL-C and, increasingly, by PRS. A high-PRS / low-ApoB patient is a "genetically at risk but lipid-normal" phenotype. If CRP stratifies risk within this group, it identifies a subgroup for whom anti-inflammatory or non-lipid strategies (lifestyle, imaging surveillance, potentially anti-inflammatory therapy) may be warranted — a decision CANTOS-style evidence supports in principle [K2] but for which no prospective risk-stratification rule exists.
2. **Decomposition of genetic risk.** PRS captures both lipid-mediated and non-lipid (e.g., inflammatory, metabolic) genetic pathways. Testing whether inflammation explains residual risk in the low-ApoB, high-PRS stratum is a direct, prospective test of the non-lipid fraction of genetic risk — something PRS alone cannot distinguish [K1].
3. **A negative result is equally informative.** If CRP does not stratify risk in this stratum after full adjustment, it argues that the residual risk of high-PRS/low-ApoB individuals is carried by pathways not captured by a single circulating inflammatory marker (or by measurement/selection artifacts), redirecting effort toward other biomarkers or toward the lipid pathway itself.

## 2. Hypothesis (Specific and Falsifiable)

Among UK Biobank participants with no prior CAD at baseline, **within the stratum of top-20% CAD PRS and ApoB below the population median, individuals with CRP ≥ 3 mg/L have a higher hazard of incident CAD (inpatient ICD10 I20–I25) than individuals with CRP < 3 mg/L.**

Primary estimate: Cox proportional-hazards hazard ratio (HR) for the CRP contrast within the high-PRS/low-ApoB stratum, adjusted for age, sex, genetic principal components (first 10), and self-reported ancestry.

**Falsification criterion:** if the fully adjusted HR for CRP ≥ 3 vs < 3 mg/L in the target stratum falls in 0.90–1.10, the hypothesis is considered falsified (no clinically meaningful inflammatory residual risk in this stratum); HR < 0.90 or > 1.10 with a 95% CI excluding 1.0 supports the hypothesis.

## 3. Relationship to Closest Prior Work (K1–K3)

- **[K1] Klarin & Natarajan 2021 (Nat Rev Cardiol)** — full text inspected. Establishes that CAD PRS predict incident CAD across cohorts and that high-PRS individuals derive the greatest relative and absolute benefit from LDL-C lowering (post hoc trial analyses). *What this work does not establish:* whether, after conditioning on measured ApoB (the particle number that MR identifies as the dominant causal lipid trait [K3]), residual risk in high-PRS individuals is carried by inflammation. The review frames PRS as a tool for prioritizing subgroups but does not test PRS × ApoB × CRP joint stratification prospectively.
- **[K2] Ridker et al. 2017, CANTOS (NEJM)** — abstract/metadata inspected (NEJM full text not accessible). Establishes that IL-1β blockade lowers recurrent cardiovascular events in post-MI patients with hs-CRP ≥ 2 mg/L, independent of lipid change. *What this work does not establish:* that baseline CRP stratifies *incident* (first) CAD risk in a primary-prevention population, or that the effect generalizes to genetically high-risk, lipid-normal individuals. CANTOS is a secondary-prevention RCT in enriched (post-MI, hs-CRP ≥ 2) patients; it motivates the inflammatory component but cannot supply the prospective, primary-prevention, PRS-stratified estimate this study targets.
- **[K3] Richardson et al. 2020 (PLoS Med)** — full text inspected. Establishes via multivariable MR that ApoB, not LDL-C, retains the robust causal relationship with CHD after conditioning on co-occurring lipid traits. *What this work does not establish:* the prospective, observational expression of the ApoB–inflammation decomposition inside genetically defined subgroups; MR estimates population-average causal effects and cannot characterize heterogeneity by PRS stratum.

**The specific advance over [K1]–[K3]:** a prospective, PRS-stratified test of whether CRP carries residual incident-CAD risk in the high-PRS/low-ApoB stratum — a joint (genetic × lipid × inflammatory) stratification that none of the three works performs.

## 4. Study Design

### 4.1 Population and Temporal Boundaries
- **Source:** UK Biobank completed subset (ukb672073 split), three Parquet tables sharing the `eid` identifier namespace:
  - Genomics: `[internal dataset path]` — 502,370 rows; columns `eid`, `26227-0.0` (standard CAD PRS), `22009-0.1`…`22009-0.40` (40 genetic principal components, **1-indexed**), `22006-0.0` (self-reported ancestry).
  - Hospital records: `[internal dataset path]` — 502,370 rows; columns `41270-0.0`…`41270-0.258` (259 ICD10 diagnosis slots) and `41280-0.0`…`41280-0.258` (259 corresponding first-inpatient-diagnosis dates).
  - Main clinical: `[internal dataset path]` — columns `30640-0.0` (ApoB, 467,086 non-null), `30710-0.0` (CRP, 468,446 non-null), `53-0.0` (assessment date).
- **Join key:** `eid` (same namespace across all three tables; no crosswalk needed).
- **Baseline definition:** assessment date from field 53. Exposure measurements (ApoB, CRP) and PRS are baseline values.
- **Exclusions:**
  - Prevalent CAD at baseline: any inpatient ICD10 I20–I25 with date ≤ baseline date (using the first inpatient diagnosis dates 41280; if a code slot has a date, that slot defines the event; slots without dates are ignored for the prevalent check but flagged).
  - Missing CAD PRS, missing ApoB, or missing CRP.
  - Out-of-range CRP (e.g., < 0 or > 200 mg/L) flagged and excluded from the primary analysis (sensitivity: include).
- **Expected analytic cohort:** on the order of 400K+ participants with all three exposures; ~60K incident CAD events observed in the full genotyped population (episode-11 feasibility: 60,725 CAD events).

### 4.2 Variables
- **Exposure strata (prespecified, computed in the analytic cohort):**
  - High PRS: CAD PRS (`26227-0.0`) ≥ 80th percentile of the analytic cohort.
  - Low ApoB: ApoB (`30640-0.0`) < median of the analytic cohort.
  - Inflammatory contrast: CRP (`30710-0.0`) ≥ 3 mg/L vs < 3 mg/L (the 3 mg/L threshold is the conventional "elevated" cutpoint used in JUPITER/CANTOS-adjacent literature; sensitivity at 2 and 5 mg/L).
- **Outcome:** incident inpatient CAD, ICD10 I20–I25 (unstable angina, MI, chronic post-MI changes, other/acute ischemic heart disease), first diagnosis after baseline date, from `41270`/`41280`.
- **Covariates:** age (from field 53 date and date of birth if available; otherwise age at assessment), sex, first 10 genetic PCs (`22009-0.1`…`22009-0.10`), self-reported ancestry (`22006-0.0`).
- **Follow-up:** from baseline date to first CAD event, end of HRO data availability, or administrative censoring; competing death handled in sensitivity analysis only (primary is cause-specific hazard for CAD).

### 4.3 Primary Analysis
- **Model:** Cox proportional-hazards model, restricted to the high-PRS/low-ApoB stratum:
  `hazard(CAD event) ~ CRP_high (≥3 vs <3) + age + sex + PC1..PC10 + ancestry`
- **Primary estimand:** HR for CRP ≥ 3 vs < 3 mg/L in the target stratum, 95% CI.
- **Proportional-hazards check:** Schoenfeld residuals; if violated, time-stratified Cox (5 strata) as sensitivity.
- **Secondary contrasts (pre-specified):**
  1. Same CRP contrast in the high-PRS/**high**-ApoB stratum (to test whether the inflammatory signal is specific to the low-ApoB background).
  2. Same CRP contrast in the low-PRS/low-ApoB stratum (negative control for the PRS stratification).
  3. CRP as continuous (per mg/L, and per SD) in the target stratum.
  4. ApoB × CRP and PRS × CRP interaction terms in the full cohort (effect-modification tests).

### 4.4 Method Comparison (Primary vs Sensitivity)
- **Primary:** Cox PH (interpretable HR, standard for time-to-event with prespecified contrasts).
- **Sensitivity:** XGBoost survival model (or gradient-boosted hazard) trained on the full cohort with features {CAD PRS, ApoB, CRP, age, sex, PC1..PC10, ancestry}, evaluated by discrimination (C-index) and calibration in the target stratum, with SHAP-style feature attribution to check whether CRP's contribution persists when PRS and ApoB are in the model. *Purpose:* to test whether the Cox contrast is an artifact of the linear hazard specification or of the prespecified cutpoint; XGBoost captures nonlinearities and interactions the Cox model cannot. A materially different conclusion (e.g., CRP important only above a nonlinear threshold) would be reported as such.
- **Deferral:** no competing-risk Fine-Gray model in the primary (CAD events are the dominant event in this population; death is a sensitivity analysis). No MR extension (would require instrument selection outside this dataset's scope).

### 4.5 Alternative Explanations and Their Tests
1. **Treatment/selection artifact (leading rival):** low ApoB in high-PRS individuals may reflect statin use (i.e., the "low ApoB" stratum is enriched for treated, adherent individuals), and CRP differences may reflect treatment intensity or residual confounding. *Test:* the configured parquets do not include a medication field in the verified bindings; this limitation is stated explicitly. Partial mitigation: (a) the contrast is *within* the low-ApoB stratum, so statin-induced ApoB lowering affects both CRP groups equally to first order; (b) sensitivity excluding the first 1–2 years of follow-up (to reduce reverse causation by subclinical disease driving both CRP and early events); (c) ancestry and PC adjustment to control population stratification. *If the HR persists after these, the treatment artifact is less likely but not excluded — clinical adjudication of medication history (not in the bound data) would be required for a definitive statement.*
2. **Reverse causation:** subclinical atherosclerosis raises CRP before the first inpatient event. *Test:* lagged follow-up (exclude events in first 2 years) as sensitivity; if the HR attenuates toward null with lag, reverse causation is a contributor.
3. **Measurement artifact:** single CRP measurement; acute infections at the blood draw inflate CRP. *Test:* CRP ≥ 10 mg/L (likely acute) excluded in sensitivity; the 3 mg/L threshold is below typical acute-infection levels but not immune to them.
4. **Population stratification:** PCs and ancestry adjust for broad structure; residual stratification is possible. *Test:* the 10-PC adjustment is standard; sensitivity with 20 PCs.

### 4.6 Interpretation of Possible Outcomes
- **Supportive (HR > 1.10, CI excluding 1.0 in target stratum; contrast absent or weaker in low-PRS/low-ApoB negative control):** evidence that inflammation carries residual incident-CAD risk in genetically high-risk, lipid-normal individuals. Would justify (a) considering CRP in risk communication for high-PRS/low-ApoB patients, (b) designing a trial of anti-inflammatory or intensive lifestyle intervention in this stratum. Would *not* by itself establish that anti-inflammatory therapy works in this stratum (that requires an RCT, as CANTOS did in secondary prevention [K2]).
- **Adverse/falsified (HR 0.90–1.10 after full adjustment):** no clinically meaningful inflammatory residual risk in this stratum. The non-lipid fraction of genetic risk is not captured by circulating CRP; effort should shift to other biomarkers (e.g., IL-6, fibrinogen, Lp(a)) or to the lipid pathway. This is a valuable, publishable negative result.
- **Inconclusive (HR outside 0.90–1.10 but CI includes 1.0, or event count in stratum too small):** underpowered for this contrast; report the estimate and CI, state the required sample size, and defer.

### 4.7 Computationally Checkable vs Clinical-Adjudication Claims
- **Computationally checkable (from the bound data):** cohort construction, exposure stratification, event ascertainment, Cox HRs and CIs, XGBoost discrimination/calibration, all sensitivity analyses above. A verifier can recompute every number from the three parquets.
- **Requires clinical adjudication / unavailable evidence:** (a) whether low ApoB reflects statin treatment (no medication field in bound data); (b) whether the CRP threshold and stratum definitions are clinically optimal (expert judgment); (c) whether a supportive HR would justify anti-inflammatory therapy in this stratum (requires an RCT, not this dataset); (d) cause-of-death misclassification (ICD10-based outcome). These are stated as limitations, not as claims the analysis can settle.

## 8. Limitations
- No medication field in the verified bindings → statin-treatment artifact cannot be directly tested (see §4.5).
- Single baseline CRP and ApoB measurements; no longitudinal exposure.
- 41280 records first inpatient diagnosis only; outpatient CAD may be undercounted.
- ICD10-based outcome subject to coding misclassification.
- K2 inspection is abstract-only (NEJM full text not accessible); claims from K2 are limited to the abstract's reported endpoints.
- UK Biobank is a self-selected, predominantly European-ancestry cohort; generalizability to other populations is limited.
