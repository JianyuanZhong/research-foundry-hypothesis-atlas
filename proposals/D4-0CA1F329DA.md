# Provisional hypothesis: body-fat distribution separates metabolic from functional/fall risk trajectories

## Status and scientific deliverable

Provisional, not selected and not an observed result. The deliverable is a newly fitted, held-out comparison of a simple total-adiposity baseline with a prespecified body-composition model testing whether visceral fat and posterior-thigh muscle fat infiltration (MFI) identify distinct subsequent metabolic and functional/fall trajectories. Completion requires cohort and overlap counts, a temporally ordered event table, fitted estimates with uncertainty, calibration/discrimination or trajectory summaries, and a conclusion classified as supportive, adverse, or inconclusive. No association or clinical conclusion has been computed.

## Unresolved question and clinical importance

At similar total body fat, does higher visceral fat predict subsequent metabolic disease more specifically, while higher posterior-thigh MFI predicts subsequent functional decline or falls more specifically? Resolving this matters because a positive result could justify targeted body-composition assessment rather than treating adiposity as one generic risk factor; a negative result would argue against separating these phenotypes for clinical risk stratification.

The leading claim is that fat distribution contains clinically distinct information beyond total adiposity. The strongest rival is that apparent divergence reflects current severity, age/sex, measurement timing, selection into MRI, or observation practices. This is an observational test, not a mechanism or treatment-effect study.

## Evidence boundary

The closest inspected evidence supports that myosteatosis/MFI is associated with metabolic and functional outcomes [K1], that IMAT/MFI spans metabolic, cardiovascular and oncologic settings but is heterogeneous across imaging and disease contexts [K2], and that UKB abdominal MRI phenotypes including visceral fat improve internal prediction of incident T2D [K3]. These works do not establish the proposed divergence, causality, or clinical utility. The UKB-17 seed supplies the data opening, not a result. No association has been computed here.

## Population, time, outcomes, and analysis

Population: participants with valid second-assessment body-composition measurements, including total adiposity, visceral fat, and posterior-thigh MFI, with identifiable `53-2.0` time zero. Exclude non-identifiable temporal ordering and prevalent metabolic disease using diagnosis arrays available before or at baseline; exclude baseline functional limitation/falls where feasible. Preserve source-specific identifiers.

Temporal boundaries: index at second assessment. Use a prespecified one-year lag for metabolic outcomes, with no-lag and longer-lag sensitivities. Functional outcomes use later repeated measurements at instance 3 and must occur after baseline. If event dates or ascertainment are unavailable, defer that endpoint rather than infer it.

Outcomes: incident metabolic disease from verified diagnosis fields after baseline exclusion; incident falls; and change in functional measures where repeated measurements exist. The primary contrast is whether visceral fat and MFI have distinct outcome-specific associations after adjustment for total adiposity, age, sex, ethnicity, and measurement timing.

Baseline: adjusted total-adiposity model. Alternative: prespecified composition model adding visceral fat and MFI, with outcome-specific contrasts. Compare held-out calibration/discrimination and, more importantly, direction and uncertainty of visceral-versus-MFI contrasts. A learned sequence/trajectory model is deferred unless repeated functional support and missingness diagnostics justify it; it could reveal nonlinear time-varying decline, but complexity alone is not a scientific objective.

## Falsification and interpretation

Supportive: visceral fat and MFI show prespecified, outcome-specific associations of sufficient magnitude with uncertainty excluding the null, after total-adiposity adjustment, with stable direction in held-out or sensitivity analyses. This supports distinct descriptive risk phenotypes, not causality.

Adverse: the composition model adds no stable outcome-specific information, effects reverse, or associations are explained by baseline severity/timing/selection diagnostics. This weakens the divergence claim.

Inconclusive: insufficient overlap, sparse events, missing dates, wide uncertainty, or unstable calibration. This motivates a larger or differently ascertained study, not a positive interpretation.

A positive result would not establish that changing visceral fat or MFI improves outcomes. Causal, mechanistic, and treatment claims require stronger longitudinal design, clinical adjudication, and additional covariates not guaranteed by these files.
