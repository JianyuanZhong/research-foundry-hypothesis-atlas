## Opening, supported claim, unresolved hypothesis, and clinical advance

The strongest inspected evidence supports only three bounded claims. Large eGFRcys-minus-eGFRcr differences are common in UK Biobank and have kidney and non-GFR predictors [K1]. In SPRINT, that difference was associated with frailty and broad adverse outcomes, and a combined equation may discard prognostic information [K2]. Administrative AKI coding can be insensitive and subgroup-dependent relative to creatinine-defined AKI [K3]. None establishes biological AKI selectivity at fixed combined eGFR, the meaning of UKB first appearances as encounters or onset, or a clinical action.

The unresolved hypothesis is: among supported P− and P0 profiles at the same combined eGFR, P− has a larger ten-year contrast for first recorded N17 than for first recorded N18 after measured baseline characteristics and pre-index first-appearance history are represented. Define `theta17=log RR10(N17-first; P−/P0)`, `theta18=log RR10(N18-first; P−/P0)`, and the frozen renal-selectivity estimand `D=theta17−theta18`, standardized over the same eligible cohort.

The leading bounded explanation is recorded acute-renal selectivity. The strongest rival is stable generalized morbidity/recording propensity: persistently greater opportunity for discovery or denser same-day coding may increase both later N17 and N18 recording. They differ observably if a calibrated pre-index process predicts broad recording yet the N17-over-N18 contrast persists; attenuation under that process favors the rival. Neither comparison identifies healthcare contact, testing, admission severity, coding intent, morbidity, or biological onset.

The advance is a same-question comparison: a transparent joint competing-risk baseline versus a sparse-history-aware hurdle/shared-frailty process, selected by held-out calibration and recovery rather than complexity. It can reveal whether the timing and conditional density of pre-index first appearances carry stable information that scalar counts discard.

## Chosen substantive alternative: support-aware hurdle with calibrated one-factor escalation

The sparse-history evidence supports one primary recording-process alternative, not two person factors: an explicit any-history hurdle followed by one posterior-integrated recurrent-date frailty. Conditional marks may load on that same factor only if separately recoverable. The nested variants answer the same question on the same population, clock, outcomes, covariates, split, standardization, and bootstrap:

- **M0, transparent renal baseline:** the scalar pre-index history block above, with no latent process.
- **M1, support-aware fixed-effects process:** model `H_i=1` for any valid nonrenal discovery date in `[index−10y,index)` using age, sex, assessment year/centre, clinical covariates, and available-window terms. Conditional on H=1, fit yearly piecewise-exponential recurrent-date intensity and zero-truncated negative-binomial bundle marks. M1 is the calibration floor and never labels H=0 as healthy.
- **M2, planned substantive alternative:** add one intensity frailty `b_i~N(0,sigma_b^2)`. For each participant integrate `p(b_i|pre-index support/history)` by adaptive quadrature or at least 50 deterministic posterior draws. A no-history participant retains the covariate-conditioned population distribution plus the explicit hurdle indicator; never substitute zero or an empirical-Bayes point.
- **M3, conditional-mark extension:** allow the conditional bundle model to load on the same `b_i` through `lambda_mark*b_i`. This remains one factor, not a frequency factor plus a density factor.

Fit process parameters marker-blind in training. M2 is selected if it passes held-out calibration and recovery; M3 replaces it only if the shared mark loading is stable and materially improves held-out mark fit. M1 is retained when frailty recovery fails, but then the process is only a falsifier/calibration model and the renal result is inconclusive for robustness to stable latent recording propensity. Never revive a second person factor.

Before any profile effect is opened, require:

- hurdle calibration within 5 percentage points overall and within sex × age-band and assessment-period cells with at least 100 participants;
- yearly date-count observed/expected 0.8–1.25 in at least 8/10 years with no systematic early/late sign reversal;
- conditional bundle mean error <=20% and 90th-quantile error <=20% or one group;
- converged quadrature and no >0.01 integrated-Brier degradation versus M0 for sufficiently supported pooled N17, N18, or death;
- at least 90% convergence in 200 parametric recovery datasets preserving the observed no/one/two/three-plus history pattern, median relative bias <=20% for nonzero `sigma_b` and `lambda_mark`, and empirical 95% coverage 85–98%;
- stability to deleting either five-year half. M3 additionally requires the `lambda_mark` 95% interval to exclude both zero and effects smaller than a 10% bundle-mean change per frailty SD; an interval also spanning a >=25% change is too unstable, so select M2.

After freezing M1/M2/M3 on validation, evaluate once on test and generate five-fold cross-fitted posterior nodes using only pre-index history and fold-external population parameters. Estimate standardized P−/P0 contrasts in hurdle probability, annual date intensity, and conditional bundle mean as prespecified falsifiers, then fit the renal model. Bootstrap process fitting, cross-fitting, corrected matching, renal fitting, and standardization together. No post-S event may update the process.

M2 preserves recurrent timing while propagating uncertainty for thin histories; M3 can additionally test whether conditional density shares that stable tendency. M0 loses this timing/mark covariance. All variants still conflate morbidity, contact, testing, severity, and coding behavior. Conditioning may block morbidity pathways, so attenuation is a robustness contrast, never a causal direct effect.

Deferred alternatives: two correlated person factors require denser repeated histories plus successful recovery; plug-in empirical-Bayes scores understate uncertainty; code-composition NMF answers a different salience/comorbidity question; a transformer is unsupported by sparse first-appearance summaries; latent true-GFR modeling requires measured GFR or repeated-marker error data. Revisit only with the named data or a failed calibration target the method can specifically repair.
