# Provisional repaired eICU monitoring-gap hypothesis

## Proposed relationship

Among adults in a continuous ICU stay who are physiologically unstable at a prespecified hourly index, a reduction in laboratory-testing intensity during the immediately preceding 12 hours is associated with a higher risk of a time-local deterioration in the following 12 hours, beyond contemporaneous measured severity and recent support. The primary interpretation is a monitoring-process signal, not a causal claim that reducing laboratory orders causes deterioration.

The endpoint is deliberately time-local: the first qualifying escalation in respiratory support or vasopressor/infusion support during (t, t+12]. It is not a broad mortality or discharge outcome. Death, discharge, or transfer before the endpoint is a competing event, not a non-event. This preserves the distinction between a monitoring-process explanation and the rival explanations of end-of-life care, discharge/transfer, and deliberate support de-escalation.

## Population and temporal design

Use adult patient stays with a valid unitdischargeoffset greater than 18 hours and a continuous ICU interval. The primary index times are hourly from 6 through unitdischargeoffset minus 12 hours. Require at least one valid vital observation and one laboratory observation in the preceding 24 hours. Label missingness separately from zero testing. Do not use post-index laboratory values as predictors.

At index t, define the current testing interval as (t-12,t] and the prior interval as (t-24,t-12], using distinct laboratory result timestamps and distinct analyte names. The primary exposure is a clinically interpretable reduction: current distinct-analyte count is zero after a positive prior count, or falls by at least 50% when the prior interval has at least two episodes. Continuous change and episode counts are secondary. Revised results are not counted as new tests unless their result time is distinct.

## Time-local deterioration endpoint

The primary endpoint is the first event in (t,t+12] meeting one of the following prespecified rules:

1. Respiratory escalation: a new or escalated airway/ventilation state from respiratoryCare, using respcarestatusoffset, airwaytype, ventstartoffset, ventendoffset, and highironoxlimit; or a new/escalated respiratory support state from respiratoryCharting, using respchartoffset, respchartentryoffset, respcharttypecat, respchartvaluelabel, and respchartvalue.
2. Hemodynamic escalation: a new or escalated vasopressor/infusion from infusionDrug, using infusionoffset, drugname, drugrate, infusionrate, and patientweight.

The event time is the earliest qualifying timestamp in the outcome window. If multiple qualifying records share the same timestamp, the first is retained after a deterministic tie-break by table and row order. The endpoint is not defined by a post-index laboratory value, mortality field, or discharge status.

Death before the endpoint is a competing event. Discharge or transfer before the endpoint is also a competing event, with a separate sensitivity analysis excluding stays that leave before the window. apachePatientResult mortality fields actualicumortality and actualhospitalmortality are secondary descriptive outcomes, not substitutes for the time-local endpoint.

The endpoint is a proxy for clinically consequential deterioration, not adjudicated organ failure. Its validity depends on the availability and semantics of the respiratory and infusion records; a source audit must verify that the relevant fields contain usable timestamps and state/rate values, and must report missingness and value distributions before any association is interpreted.

## Rival explanations and falsification

Adjust only for measurements available at or before t: vitalPeriodic observations (observationoffset, temperature, SaO2, heart rate, respiration, systemic pressures), respiratory state from respiratoryCharting, recent infusion/support, age, sex, unit type, and hospital. Do not adjust using future values or post-index labs.

End-of-life is a rival explanation. Use carePlanEOL fields cpleolsaveoffset, cpleoldiscussionoffset, and activeupondischarge to define prespecified EOL-context strata and a sensitivity analysis excluding documented EOL plans before t. Missing EOL documentation is not equivalent to absence of EOL care. Also model discharge/transfer status/location and recent support de-escalation.

The primary analysis is a discrete-time competing-risk model with the same index times, hospital-held-out validation, clustered uncertainty, calibration, discrimination, and absolute risk differences. A longitudinal recurrent-event/discrete-time hazard alternative may be fitted on the same question to reveal whether the signal is transient, persistent, or preceded by a trajectory; it is not justified by predictive gain alone.

Falsification checks include within-hospital permutation of testing intervals while preserving stay length and outcome, a post-index testing exposure that should not predict the prior deterioration, and a documentation-timestamp control from non-laboratory charting where available. A supportive result requires a reproducible held-out-hospital association after state and EOL/discharge handling, with negative controls null. Adverse results include null, instability, or attenuation after explicit rival adjustment. Inconclusive results include wide intervals, sparse events, or inability to distinguish interface interruption from clinical intent. None establishes causality or a recommendation to increase testing.

## Method alternatives and selection

The simple baseline is a prespecified discrete-time competing-risk logistic model with contemporaneous severity, recent support, EOL context, hospital effects, and clustered uncertainty. It is interpretable and directly tests whether testing reduction adds information beyond current state.

The substantive alternative is a longitudinal recurrent-event/discrete-time hazard model using the same inputs and target, with time-varying testing intensity and physiologic trajectories. It can reveal whether a monitoring reduction is a transient blip, a persistent process, or preceded by a trajectory that the baseline collapses into a single index-time exposure. It should be compared on held-out hospitals with calibration and uncertainty, not selected for a small predictive gain.

Both methods require the same source audit and endpoint semantics. CPU is appropriate for the baseline; the alternative may use CPU or a bounded GPU allocation depending on the measured size of the longitudinal fit. No GPU is required for this provisional design.
