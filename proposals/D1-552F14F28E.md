# Provisional HCC trajectory-versus-care-process hypothesis (episode 8)

## Proposed relationship

Among adults with an exact documented hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) diagnosis and an inpatient encounter, an ordered 7-day within-assay laboratory trajectory of liver, coagulation, renal, and tumor-marker measurements may add held-out information about a subsequent inpatient procedure-based escalation proxy beyond an admission snapshot, treatment/procedure indicators, and documentation intensity.

The primary unresolved claim is whether any incremental temporal signal persists when routine HCC treatment procedures and care-process intensity are represented. The procedure endpoint is a computable proxy for escalation of care; it is not a validated measure of death, ICU admission, clinical harm, or causal treatment effect.

## What is supported now

The inherited HCC source audit reports 105,044 encounter rows, 101,457 valid admission times, 104,554 exact HCC patient/admission keys, 338,040 procedure rows, 321,207 timed HCC procedure rows, 15,774 untimed HCC procedure rows, and 20,052 HCC procedure rows after day 7 and before discharge (6,779 keys). These are availability and ascertainment counts, not evidence of a trajectory effect or clinical outcome.

## Falsifiable test

Define an index cohort with an exact HCC diagnosis and a valid admission time, and restrict the feature window to admission through day 7. Define the primary outcome as a prespecified non-routine procedure occurring after day 7 and before discharge. The solver must freeze a deterministic routine-procedure dictionary and report its event support before fitting.

Compare:
1. a snapshot baseline using admission demographics, department, admission laboratory values, treatment/procedure indicators, and documentation intensity;
2. a time-aware alternative using ordered within-assay values, slopes/persistence, missingness timing, and cross-marker change over the same window.

Use patient-level temporal or encounter-level held-out splits without leakage, prespecified calibration/uncertainty intervals, and a timing-shifted negative control. The trajectory model is supportive only if its incremental held-out performance is nontrivial and stable for the non-routine endpoint, attenuates for routine treatment procedures, and is absent in the negative control. A null, unstable, or routine-only signal is adverse to the evolving-state interpretation. Low event support, missing units, or non-identifiable assay mappings make the result inconclusive.

## Missing evidence and limits

The procedure source lacks a validated ontology, indication, severity, or clinical outcome field. The exact routine/non-routine dictionary, laboratory units, assay mapping, and endpoint support are not yet frozen. Clinical adjudication is required to decide whether a procedure proxy represents meaningful deterioration. No model result, causal effect, or clinical recommendation is claimed in this draft.

## Method comparison and actual deliverable

The simple baseline estimates whether static severity and care intensity suffice. The substantive temporal alternative can reveal persistence, slope, and cross-marker ordering that the snapshot discards. The solver must newly fit both models on the same cohort and report held-out discrimination/calibration, uncertainty, ablations, and negative-control results. A CPU tabular implementation is a reasonable first stage; GPU use is not scientifically required. A mechanistic model is deferred because the available procedure endpoint cannot validate a biological mechanism without adjudication.

The three demonstration dispositions remain: the multimodal cancer demonstration cannot be reproduced because local images and original cohort/methods are unavailable, but a lab-only adaptation is the current branch; the natural-history/proteomic-aging demonstration is not pursued because its required indicators and independent assay units are unavailable; the Bayesian demonstration is not required for this question and is deferred unless uncertainty analysis reveals a substantive need.
