Title: Closing the inpatient-to-outpatient loop for incidental pulmonary nodules: does actionable discharge handoff improve timely CT surveillance in MIMIC-IV?

Clinical question and falsifiable hypothesis

Among adults whose inpatient CT report identifies a new or indeterminate incidental pulmonary nodule and gives an unconditional, explicit recommendation for repeat chest CT in 3, 6, or 12 months, a closed-loop discharge handoff that names the nodule, names chest CT, and carries forward an actionable interval will be associated with more guideline-timed CT surveillance documented at the same health system than a discharge summary that omits any actionable nodule follow-up plan.

The primary hypothesis is quantitative and falsifiable: the adjusted risk of a MIMIC-observed chest CT in the prespecified timely window will be higher under actionable discharge documentation; the null is a standardized risk difference of 0. An adverse result is a risk difference below 0. A secondary mechanistic hypothesis is that a dedicated RECOMMENDATION(S) section in the index radiology report is associated with successful propagation into the discharge summary, compared with an equally explicit recommendation embedded only in narrative impression/body text.

Why this matters and what is unresolved

Failure to transfer an incidental nodule plan across hospital discharge can delay surveillance of a potentially malignant lesion, while indiscriminate surveillance causes radiation, cost, anxiety, and cascades. The intervention implied by this study is modest and deployable: require a structured, interval-specific nodule task in the discharge plan, potentially auto-populated from radiology, rather than merely issuing a radiology recommendation.

Existing evidence supports the existence of the care gap, not the causal claim proposed here. Kwan et al. studied 65 patients with explicit follow-up recommendations after inpatient/ED CTPA; 53.8% lacked repeat imaging in the recommended time frame, and discharge-summary mention was associated with timely imaging (J Hosp Med 2019; DOI 10.12788/jhm.3128; PMID 30794133; PMCID PMC6625441). Bates et al. reported that only 34 of 208 inpatient abdominal-CT patients warranting surveillance had a follow-up recommendation documented in the discharge summary (J Hosp Med 2017; DOI 10.12788/jhm.2757; PMID 28574537). More recent pre/post work reports that a supported identification/outreach workflow can improve nodule-specific follow-up, but did not establish improved cancer stage (Respir Med 2026; DOI 10.1016/j.rmed.2026.109045; PMID 42501874). These findings establish plausibility and importance. They do not establish whether interval-specific closed-loop discharge handoff, across inpatient CT types and longitudinal episodes at MIMICed health system, is independently associated with timely surveillance, whether report structure changes propagation, or whether any association survives restriction to patients demonstrably still observed in-system.

Strongest claim currently supported: inpatient incidental pulmonary-nodule recommendations are often not propagated or completed, and documentation is associated with follow-up in prior cohorts.

Unresolved claim tested here: an actionable closed-loop handoff strategy is associated with greater same-system timely CT ascertainment after balancing measured clinical and care-transition factors. Even a supportive result remains observational and cannot establish that forcing documentation causes imaging, prevents delayed lung-cancer diagnosis, or improves survival.

Population and temporal design

Unit: the first qualifying nodule-recommendation admission per subject, avoiding correlated repeated inclusion. Adults age >=18.

Index eligibility, determined without future information:
1. CT performed during the admission/linked ED window, not a scheduled low-dose lung-screening examination.
2. Report asserts a pulmonary/lung nodule or indeterminate focal nodular pulmonary opacity and gives an unconditional recommendation/need for chest CT in 3, 6, or 12 months. Exclude conditional/optional text such as “if high risk,” reports saying no follow-up is needed, and recommendations for a non-pulmonary nodule. A rule-based high-sensitivity screen is followed by blinded dual manual adjudication of report impression/recommendation, with disagreements resolved by a third reviewer. The manual form captures nodule size, solid/subsolid status, multiplicity, location, interval, new/changed/stable, index examination, and recommendation location.
3. Incidental means the CT indication is not pulmonary-nodule surveillance or known/suspected lung cancer. Exclude explicit lung cancer/metastatic evaluation, active palliative/hospice discharge, death before exposure, no usable discharge summary, and recommendations already satisfied before the discharge summary is stored.
4. “New/indeterminate” requires no prior report establishing guideline-complete stability. Prior radiology text up to index `storetime` may be used; future text may not. The primary analysis permits subjects with no available two-year MIMIC imaging history but marks prior-history missing. A high-specificity sensitivity analysis requires >=2 years between the first available MIMIC radiology report and index.
5. No minimum future observation is imposed for the exposure/propagation endpoint. For surveillance completion, the primary complete-observation cohort requires evidence that the patient remained observable at this health system through the end of the timely window: any later MIMIC admission or radiology report after that date, or death recorded before it. Patients lacking such evidence are right-censored/unknown and are not called nonadherent. An inverse-probability-of-observation sensitivity analysis uses all eligible subjects.

Exposure strategies

Primary exposure is actionable discharge handoff, defined by context-aware text adjudication in the final index discharge summary available by `dischtime + 7 days`:
- Closed-loop: an affirmed patient-specific pulmonary nodule plus an action naming follow-up chest CT and a time interval/date compatible with the radiology recommendation. Generic copied guideline boilerplate without a patient-specific plan does not count.
- Partial handoff (secondary): nodule and follow-up CT but no interval, or nodule mention alone.
- Omission comparator: no patient-specific actionable nodule follow-up plan.

Negated, historical, family-history, hypothetical, and copied radiology text are excluded. The detector is developed on a stratified annotation sample and then frozen. Primary binary contrast is closed-loop versus omission; partial is reported separately and included only in a predeclared ordinal trend analysis.

Secondary upstream exposure: explicit recommendation appears under a dedicated `RECOMMENDATION(S)` heading versus embedded in impression/body, among otherwise unconditional interval-specific recommendations. This tests propagation to the discharge summary, not clinical outcome causality.

Outcomes

Primary outcome: same-system timely surveillance, a chest CT/CTA report with `charttime` from 42 days before through 42 days after the recommended due date (index `charttime` + 3, 6, or 12 calendar months), and after exposure time. It is an observed MIMIC imaging endpoint, not proof of all care received.

Key secondary outcomes:
- Any same-system chest CT after exposure and by due date +42 days, to include clinically justified early evaluation.
- Days from recommended due date to first subsequent chest CT (negative = early), with death as a competing event and loss of system observation explicit.
- Nodule-focused follow-up CT, requiring indication/comparison/impression reference to nodule surveillance, adjudicated without viewing discharge exposure.
- Radiographic escalation on the first follow-up report (new/growing/suspicious nodule, PET-CT/biopsy recommendation) versus stable/resolved; descriptive only and manually adjudicated.
- Propagation endpoint: actionable discharge documentation, for the recommendation-heading analysis.
- Safety/cascade endpoint: multiple chest CTs before the recommended lower window and invasive lung evaluation before due date among reports initially adjudicated as surveillance rather than urgent work-up. This is exploratory because procedure coding lacks full clinical intent.

Estimand, analysis, and baselines

Primary estimand: adjusted standardized risk difference and risk ratio for observed timely CT under closed-loop versus omitted discharge handoff in the complete-observation cohort. This is an associational target-trial analogue, not a causal effect estimate unless conditional exchangeability, positivity, consistency, and observation assumptions are judged plausible.

Baseline models:
1. Unadjusted risks with Wilson intervals and Fisher/exact inference if sparse.
2. Main propensity-overlap-weighted logistic outcome model, with bootstrap confidence intervals clustered by subject (one row/subject). Exposure propensity uses only information known by exposure time: age category, sex, race/language/insurance (care-access proxies, not biology), admission route/type, length of stay up to discharge, ICU stay, number of transfers, discharge destination, final service, recommendation interval, index exam type, nodule size/type/multiplicity/newness, prior same-system admissions and chest CT count, COPD/emphysema, smoking-code availability, malignancy history excluding active lung cancer, and severity/comorbidity. Continuous terms use prespecified splines where sample size permits. No outcome-derived feature enters the propensity model.
3. Doubly robust weighted regression standardization if events-per-parameter and overlap diagnostics permit; otherwise a prespecified parsimonious model and exact stratification by recommendation interval, CT type, and medical versus surgical service.
4. Time-to-imaging cumulative incidence with death competing; loss of MIMIC observability is not equated with no imaging.
5. Secondary mediation-compatible decomposition reports (a) recommendation heading -> discharge documentation and (b) discharge documentation -> observed CT. It will not label discharge documentation a causal mediator because unmeasured confounding is likely.

Balance/positivity: report weighted standardized mean differences, effective sample size, propensity distributions, and trimming sensitivity at 0.05/0.95. If either arm has <30 adjudicated subjects, <15 outcome events, or severe non-overlap after trimming, only stratified descriptive estimates are allowed and the causal-style model is declared inconclusive.

Missingness and measurement

- Missing prior smoking history is “unknown,” never “nonsmoker”; conditional guideline recommendations are excluded precisely because MIMIC cannot reliably reconstruct pack-years.
- Missing nodule size/type or ambiguous recommendation makes the report ineligible unless manual adjudication can resolve it.
- Race, language, and insurance missingness receive explicit categories; estimates are also run without these variables to avoid interpreting administrative labels as clinical biology.
- Missing discharge summary means ineligible, not omission.
- No later MIMIC event means outcome unknown, not no follow-up. Complete-observation restriction and inverse-probability-of-observation sensitivity bracket this selection problem.
- Death before due-window end is a competing event; in-hospital death before exposure is excluded.
- Storetime is used to prevent temporal leakage; a sensitivity analysis requires the discharge summary be stored by discharge +24 hours.
- NLP performance is reported against manual adjudication with sensitivity, specificity, PPV, and uncertainty separately for report eligibility, discharge exposure, and outcome focus. All primary estimates are repeated in the manually adjudicated subset.

Negative controls and robustness

1. Temporal negative-control outcome: chest CT occurring before the index recommendation/exposure. Closed-loop discharge documentation cannot cause prior imaging; an association after adjustment indicates residual utilization/confounding or extraction error.
2. Specificity control: same-system non-chest imaging in the timely window. A similar association with all imaging would suggest that the exposure is merely a marker of health-system engagement.
3. Exposure-label control: discharge mention of an unrelated stable benign finding with no recommended action should not predict nodule CT after adjustment for utilization.
4. Restrict to CTPA, then separately to abdomen/pelvis CT with lung-base nodules; require exact 3/6/12-month recommendation; exclude any cancer; exclude CT obtained during a later acute admission; restrict to patients with a post-window local encounter; vary timely tolerance from +/-42 to +/-90 days.
5. Compare dedicated recommendation heading with embedded text within matched strata of interval, nodule characteristics, exam type, final service, and report explicitness. Because cross-subject calendar time is invalid under deidentification, no calendar-year adjustment, secular-trend claim, or interrupted-time-series interpretation is allowed.

Feasibility actually measured

Read-only scan of all 2,321,355 radiology rows found 1,138,200 reports linked to an admission within the stated ED/inpatient window. A broad nodule/follow-up lexical screen found 55,479 reports in 33,125 subjects, demonstrating abundant raw text but also substantial false-positive scope.

A stricter computable screen requiring inpatient-linked CT, pulmonary nodule language, a strong/unconditional 3/6/12-month chest-CT recommendation, and excluding lung screening found 723 reports and 692 first-report subjects. Crude text screening flagged 66 as known-cancer contexts. Of 692, 507 discharge summaries mentioned the nodule and 466 contained a local actionable follow-up pattern; these are unvalidated proxy counts, not final exposure prevalence. There were 278 subjects with evidence of radiology observation through due +90 days; after the crude known-cancer exclusion, 246 remained (170 proxy documented, 76 proxy omitted), with 76 observed follow-up chest CTs. The conservative >=2-year prior-radiology plus future-observation subset had 125 crude eligible subjects (86 documented, 39 omitted) and 38 observed chest CTs. These counts establish feasibility for careful adjudication but warn that the high-specificity sensitivity analysis may be underpowered.

Sampling provenance: scripts scanned every radiology row, every discharge row for candidate admissions, and the complete admissions archive member; no row sample was used for counts. Thirty candidate snippets and source rows were inspected directly to discover false positives such as conditional “optional if high risk,” adrenal CT recommendations near pulmonary-nodule text, known malignancy, and lung-screening reports; rules were tightened before freezing the feasibility result. Final cohort construction still requires blinded clinical annotation and cannot use these crude labels as truth.

Falsification and interpretation

Supportive result: closed-loop handoff has a positive adjusted risk difference whose confidence interval excludes 0, balance/positivity and annotation thresholds pass, and the effect is specific to nodule-focused chest CT rather than prior imaging or generic utilization. Interpretation: actionable discharge documentation is a promising, testable communication target associated with better same-system surveillance. It justifies prospective implementation or randomized/stepped-wedge evaluation; it does not prove prevention of cancer delay or mortality benefit.

Adverse result: adjusted risk difference is <=0 with adequate precision/overlap, or closed-loop documentation is associated with excess premature CT/cascade without better guideline-timed surveillance. Interpretation: documentation alone may not close the loop and could increase low-value imaging; active tracking/outreach or better risk adjudication should be tested instead.

Inconclusive result: confidence interval spans clinically meaningful benefit and harm (predeclare +/-10 percentage points), fewer than 30 per arm or 15 events, poor overlap, annotation PPV <0.90 for primary exposure/outcome, materially discordant complete-observation and observation-weighted results, or a positive temporal/generic-utilization negative control. Interpretation: MIMIC cannot resolve the strategy effect; do not convert missing external care into failure.

Secondary report-structure result: dedicated headings that improve propagation but not CT suggest a handoff bottleneck downstream; no propagation benefit argues against template-only intervention; higher CT with no propagation would imply other channels and invalidate the proposed mechanism.

Essential adjudication and unavailable evidence

Required expert review: distinguish true pulmonary nodules from infection/atelectasis/granuloma and nonpulmonary nodules; incidental from cancer staging/surveillance; unconditional from optional risk-contingent recommendations; prior completed stability; clinically appropriate interval; patient-specific discharge action versus copied boilerplate; and whether follow-up CT was nodule-focused, urgent for new symptoms, stable/resolved, or escalated. At least two blinded clinicians/radiology-trained reviewers are required for the primary annotated subset.

Unavailable: imaging pixels (MIMIC-CXR images are not present), pathology linkage sufficient to establish malignancy truth, reliable pack-years and complete outpatient risk factors, ordering/referral records, patient notification, scheduled appointments, primary-care/pulmonary visits, outside-hospital imaging, residence/access data, clinician rationale, patient preference/refusal, and complete post-discharge mortality/cancer stage. Therefore an automatic verifier can check joins, time ordering, extraction logic, cohort counts, models, confidence intervals, controls, and whether conclusions match outputs. It cannot verify true guideline eligibility, clinical appropriateness, malignancy, causal benefit, or patient-important outcomes without adjudication/additional data.

Computable stopping rule

The proposal is falsified as a deployable documentation-only strategy if the primary adjusted risk difference is nonpositive with its upper 95% confidence bound below +5 percentage points, or if any apparent benefit disappears under the post-window-observability restriction while negative controls remain associated. A result is not called supportive unless all prespecified extraction accuracy, overlap, event-count, and negative-control gates pass.
