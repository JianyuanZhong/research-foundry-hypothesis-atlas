# Hospital-held-out patient-state versus documentation-process signal in eICU

Status: complete proposal child of [prior hypothesis]. No fitted study result is claimed. This is a design and data-binding audit, not an executed study.

## Scientific opening and unresolved claim

The inspected evidence supports three narrower claims, not the central mechanism. Rajkomar et al. [K1] show that broad sequential EHR representations can predict mortality across two medical centers. Gao et al. [K2] report that missing-indicator information can be predictive and caution that changing EHR missingness can harm transportability; the inspected evidence is abstract-only. Hadler et al. [K3] show that an external mortality model can retain discrimination while local EHR patterns and data availability improve local calibration/performance. None isolates a laboratory-result process channel from patient state in ICU mortality, and none identifies a causal effect of ordering, recording, or missingness.

The unresolved question is: among adult first eligible eICU ICU stays observed beyond six hours, does adding early laboratory-result presence, frequency, and timing to laboratory values create a hospital-held-out transport penalty because it encodes hospital/documentation process, or does it add patient-aligned information about latent acuity/care intensity that remains useful in another hospital?

Leading explanation: the process vector P contains hospital-specific measurement/documentation practice. Its apparent internal value will be less transportable than value-only features, including absolute risk calibration and a prespecified flagging policy.

Strong rivals are: (1) P is mainly a proxy for patient state or care intensity not captured by selected values, so aligned P retains held-out value and within-hospital permutation removes its patient alignment; (2) case mix, hospital severity, or the six-hour landmark creates the gap independently of documentation, so value-only models show a similar gap or the gap remains after P is permuted conditional on coarse value risk; and (3) timing/label artifacts drive the association, so a time-reversal placebo changes timing-dependent features.

The strongest available evidence supports predictive process sensitivity as plausible, not a documentation mechanism. The falsifiable claim is:

> The process-aware model has greater internal-to-hospital-held-out deterioration than the value-only model, and a process-permuted model that preserves hospital-specific P distributions and coarse observed-value state reproduces a material part of that deterioration.

This is a descriptive transportability claim, not a causal claim about testing or documentation.

## Clinical importance and decision boundary

A hospital-held-out process liability matters before silent deployment: a score can retain ranking but misstate absolute risk or trigger too many reviews when the receiving hospital records tests differently. A supportive result would justify site-specific measurement review, external calibration, and considering a value-only deployment specification. An adverse result would justify retaining process information only with prospective measurement validation; it would not prove biological stability.

The modeled action is a hypothetical flag for senior review/goals-of-care discussion and monitoring review. Thresholds 0.05, 0.10, and 0.20 are policy-scenario thresholds, not validated clinical decision thresholds. Net benefit is NB(t)=TP/N−FP/N×t/(1−t), with no assumed treatment benefit, harm, or resource valuation. These outputs describe alert/false-positive tradeoffs only; they cannot establish clinical utility, recommend treatment, or select a bedside threshold. Clinical adjudication and an impact study are required for those claims.

## Substantive design repair

The inherited proposal evaluated an internal 20% reference sample after fitting on the entire outer training hospitals. That makes its internal metric optimistic and can manufacture the internal-to-external deterioration used to support a documentation interpretation.

Repair: in each outer hospital fold, make a deterministic patient-grouped 80/20 split within outer training hospitals before feature selection or fitting. Fit each model only on the 80% development portion. Score the untouched 20% internal reference and held-out hospitals with that same fit, without recalibration. Feature selection, imputation medians, category levels, scaling, model fitting, and permutation strata are learned from the 80% development portion only. If the grouped split cannot be formed, stop rather than return an optimistic result.

A second bounded diagnostic sharpens the remaining rival. In addition to unconditioned within-hospital P permutation, permute P within hospital and five coarse bins of the fitted value-only V-GBM risk score. Bin cutpoints come from development predictions; cells under 10 are merged with an adjacent bin by a fixed rule. For held-out hospitals use development cutpoints and independently permute P within hospital-by-bin cells, without labels. This preserves hospital P marginals and coarse observed-value risk while breaking exact patient alignment. It does not remove unmeasured severity or prove workflow causality, but is more discriminating than unconditional permutation.

## Features, folds, and leakage controls

Retain hospitals with at least 100 eligible primary-cohort stays, using no outcome or laboratory feature; require at least five hospitals. Sort hospital IDs by SHA-256 of UTF-8 eicu-transport-v2 + NUL + hospitalid and assign contiguous hospitals to five folds by greedy eligible-stay balancing. All stays from a hospital remain in one outer fold.

Within each outer training set, select a deterministic patient-grouped 20% reference by uniquepid hash, with hospital representation and outcome strata only to stabilize prevalence; fit on the remaining 80%. Reference labels are not used in fitting, selection, threshold choice, or calibration. Record any deterministic small-cell merge and stop if the split cannot be formed.

Select up to 30 analyte keys by distinct development-stay coverage, requiring >=1% development-stay coverage. If fewer qualify retain all and report the number. Repeat selection separately in each outer fold using only development rows. V features are age, gender, ethnicity, admissionweight and latest finite value per selected analyte in [0,360]. Impute missing values with development-fold medians and learn unknown category levels in development. ## Models, estimands, uncertainty, and outputs

V-Logit is fixed elastic-net logistic regression on V. V-GBM is a fixed histogram gradient-boosting classifier on V. VP-GBM is the identical learner, seed, preprocessing, class-weight policy and fixed hyperparameters on V+P. The primary process comparison is VP-GBM versus V-GBM. T-Logit is the prespecified temporal alternative.

Fit each model on the 80% development portion and predict both reference and held-out hospitals. No held-out metric selects a model or threshold. Define D_AUC(m)=AUROC_internal−AUROC_external; D_Brier(m)=Brier_external−Brier_internal; and D_slope(m)=absolute calibration-slope error_external−absolute calibration-slope error_internal. The main contrast is D_AUC(VP-GBM)−D_AUC(V-GBM); Brier, slope and policy-scenario net-benefit contrasts are secondary. Report direct held-out VP-GBM−V-GBM metrics and per-hospital paired values. Compute AUROC, AUPRC, Brier, calibration intercept/slope, reliability without site recalibration, and net benefit at 0.05/0.10/0.20. Use 2,000 hospital-cluster bootstrap replicates over paired hospital-level prediction contributions and report effective hospital counts; patient bootstrap alone is insufficient.

Unconditioned P permutation permutes complete P vectors within hospital in each development set, fits the same VP-GBM, and independently permutes P within hospital for reference/external prediction using recorded seeds/manifests. State-matched P permutation uses development-derived V-GBM risk-score quintiles; permute within hospital-by-bin cells, merge cells under 10 only by a fixed adjacent-bin rule, and independently apply the same rule without labels to reference/external hospitals. Time reversal replaces each event offset t with 360−t before first/last and T features, preserving analytes, values, revisions and counts; refit/predict without retuning.

The solver must output a frozen cohort/fold/development-reference manifest; V/P/V+P/T features; predictions for V-Logit, V-GBM, VP-GBM, T-Logit, both permutation controls and time reversal; seeds/manifests; prohibited-field/future-window audit; per-hospital metrics; bootstrap intervals; calibration and policy-scenario net-benefit tables; and an interpretation report linking every conclusion to exact computed outputs. Completion is these outputs with uncertainty, not a favorable result.

## Interpretation and unavailable evidence

Supportive of a documentation/workflow-consistent liability requires: positive primary process deterioration with a 95% cluster interval excluding zero in at least one primary metric; worse held-out calibration or net benefit for aligned VP-GBM at at least two policy thresholds; no equally large value-only deterioration; and state-matched permuted-P retaining a material development process signal or transport penalty while exact patient alignment is broken, without the pattern being solely time-reversal. This means only that observed process signals are transportability liabilities consistent with documentation dependence.

Adverse: aligned VP-GBM retains held-out incremental value and state-matched permutation collapses to V-GBM; this favors patient-aligned information or another explanation, not proof of biological stability. Inconclusive: intervals span zero, too few hospitals/events, unstable repeat exclusion or analyte selection, equal value-only deterioration, discordant controls, or incompatible discrimination/calibration/threshold patterns. A wide interval is imprecision, not refutation. Conclusions must cite prediction, metrics and bootstrap files; readiness is not evidence.

Unavailable evidence includes orders/cancellations, specimen collection time, interface outages, clinician rationale, unmeasured bedside acuity, care pathways, end-of-life decisions, death timestamp and complete narrative notes. Hospital-held-out folds do not randomize practice or eliminate case mix, severity, selection or measurement bias. Clinical adjudication, external order/interface data, prospective silent deployment and impact evaluation are needed for workflow, causal or clinical-benefit claims.

## Methods, compute, and alternatives

The selected study is CPU tabular. Readiness is one fold first, then plan 16 CPUs and 128–256 GiB within the 28,800-second/16-CPU solver envelope. Streaming the 516 MB compressed lab source, five outer fits, controls and manifests is estimated at 2–8 hours; the full estimate is unverified. Bootstrap replicates do not refit. GPU allocation is available but unnecessary: fixed tabular summaries do not require it and GPU capacity cannot supply absent event semantics.

V-Logit is the simple baseline and loses nonlinear interactions and chronology. VP-GBM is the substantive learned alternative with identical target/split/uncertainty; its information gain is value-by-process interaction, not a leaderboard gain. T-Logit uses explicit early/late bins with identical target/split/uncertainty and can reveal timing information lost by latest-value summaries at low inspectable cost. A neural event-sequence model is deferred because missing order/specimen/interface/rationale semantics make extra capacity uninformative for the key ambiguity; revisit only if T-Logit and time reversal show reproducible chronology and one-fold readiness shows adequate events per hospital.
