# Landmark observed-risk heterogeneity after red-cell transfusion under record-defined physiologic stress

## Status, question and required scientific deliverable

This is a substantive child of the assessed-repairable expert seed `[prior hypothesis]` (`[starting question]`). It preserves the seed's consequential clinical question—whether the relationship between red-cell transfusion and subsequent deterioration differs at similar hemoglobin when a patient has oxygen supply/demand stress—but does not rename EHR proxies as ischemia and does not estimate a causal treatment effect.

No cohort size, prevalence, fitted effect, model performance, or clinical conclusion is claimed. The future Harbor solver must newly produce:

1. an auditable flow from adult low-hemoglobin ICU observations through a six-hour exposure landmark, including deaths/exits before the landmark and every exclusion;
2. row-level provenance for hemoglobin, PRBC exposure, stress components, covariates and outcome events, with clinical-time and recording-time leakage audits;
3. a prespecified record-defined stress phenotype and its component/coverage table;
4. standardized observed-risk contrasts after recorded PRBC versus no recorded PRBC within high and low observed-stress strata, their heterogeneity contrast and subject-level uncertainty;
5. separate death, nonfatal ICU escalation and ICU-exit cumulative-incidence results;
6. matched transparent, process-only and longitudinal models on the same population, inputs, split, target and evaluation;
7. support/overlap/calibration/falsification reports and a conclusion ledger that maps every statement to a computed output or marks it as requiring clinical adjudication.

Completion is either a gate-passing estimate or a complete prespecified not-estimable report. It is not confirmation of the hypothesis.

## Evidence, unresolved claim and clinical advance

The strongest evidence currently supported is data-availability evidence. Direct inspection of the configured MIMIC-IV 3.1 archive and local catalog verifies a blood hematology hemoglobin item, four ICU input-event PRBC items, a separate massive-transfusion procedure, longitudinal lactate/MAP/heart-rate/respiratory-rate/ventilation/vasoactive fields, clinical and store timestamps, and timed ICU/death/support outcomes. The imported seed is an untested expert question; MIMIC documentation does not establish effect heterogeneity. The research-ambition examples justify comparing an interpretable baseline with a bounded chronological model, but they do not prescribe this topic or prove that a learned model is useful here.

The unresolved, falsifiable claim is:

> Among adult ICU episodes reaching a six-hour post-hemoglobin landmark with hemoglobin 6.5–8.0 g/dL and no recorded PRBC in the preceding 12 hours, the adjusted observed association between recorded PRBC initiation during the six-hour exposure window and 24-hour death or new ICU escalation differs by a prespecified high versus low record-defined oxygen supply/demand-stress phenotype.

The primary claim is two-sided because the seed supports no defensible direction. A robust nonzero interaction could justify prospective validation of these proxies as transfusion-trial stratification variables. A precise null would argue against using this MIMIC phenotype to enrich such a trial. Neither would prescribe transfusion.

The advance over a hemoglobin-only association is a leakage-safe, component-auditable test of whether the same recorded hemoglobin and treatment label have different subsequent risk meaning across a frozen physiologic context, while separating physiologic values from observation/care-process intensity.

## Population, index and non-overlapping temporal boundaries

Use one deterministic episode per `subject_id` from MIMIC-IV 3.1. Start with `icu/icustays`; order stays by `intime, stay_id`. Within each stay, identify hemoglobin rows from `hosp/labevents` item 51222 with numeric `valuenum` in g/dL, `charttime` within the ICU interval, `charttime >= intime+12h`, and `storetime <= charttime`. Candidate decision time `t0` is the first such row with `6.5 <= valuenum <= 8.0`; ties use `charttime, storetime, labevent_id`. Keep the earliest qualifying episode across stays per subject. Item 50811 blood-gas hemoglobin is a sensitivity, not mixed into the primary source.

Require age at admission at least 18 using `anchor_age + year(admittime)-anchor_year`; cap/display ages above 89 as MIMIC's 91 representation and never use raw cross-subject calendar dates as biologic time. Require no administration-compatible PRBC start during `[t0-12h,t0)`, no PRBC already active at `t0`, and no massive-transfusion procedure 229620 during `[t0-12h,t0]`. The retained population is accurately named “no observed recent PRBC or massive-transfusion proxy,” not “nonbleeding.” Report a sensitivity retaining massive-transfusion-proxy episodes and a sensitivity excluding all OR/PACU PRBC exposure.

Define exposure landmark `tau=t0+6h`. Primary analysis is restricted to episodes alive and still in the indexed ICU at `tau`; death, ICU exit and missing ascertainment during `[t0,tau)` are separate pre-landmark flow states, never silently coded unexposed or as post-exposure outcomes. The outcome clock begins strictly after `tau`; therefore exposure ascertainment `[t0,tau)` and outcome `(tau,tau+24h]` do not overlap. This is a conditional landmark observed-risk study, not a baseline target-trial effect and not generalizable to patients who fail before `tau`.

All predictors use clinical time no later than `t0` and, where a recording timestamp exists, `storetime <= t0`. PRBC exposure is the sole permitted post-`t0` model input. No value, missingness flag, count, care-unit field or diagnosis learned after `t0` is a predictor.

## Exposure and modifier

### Recorded PRBC exposure A

Set `A=1` when `icu/inputevents` has item 220996 (Packed Red Cells), 225168 (Packed Red Blood Cells), 226368 (OR Packed RBC Intake), or 227070 (PACU Packed RBC Intake) with `starttime in [t0,tau)`, and either positive `amount` or positive `totalamount`, in a row whose amount unit audits to mL. Otherwise set `A=0`. Audit `endtime, storetime, amount, amountuom, totalamount, totalamountuom, statusdescription, ordercategoryname, ordercomponenttypedescription, orderid, linkorderid`; report conflicting units, zero/missing amounts, duplicated orders and status values. Primary exposure does not use a post-`tau` event or a completed-unit assumption. Sensitivities use only item 225168, exclude OR/PACU items, require at least 250 mL, and censor/relabel ambiguous status rows. Plasma, platelets, cryoprecipitate and autotransfusion are not PRBC.

### Record-defined observed stress S

Use `[t0-12h,t0]`, with both clinical time and `storetime <=t0`. Freeze valid units/ranges before outcome access and retain each binary component plus its value/time provenance.

Supply components are:

- lactate at least 2 mmol/L: labevents 50813, 52442 or 53154;
- MAP below 65 mmHg: chartevents 220052 arterial or 220181 non-invasive;
- active vasoactive support at `t0`: inputevents 221289/229617 epinephrine, 221653 dobutamine, 221749/229630/229631/229632 phenylephrine, 221906 norepinephrine, 221986 milrinone or 222315 vasopressin, with `starttime <=t0<endtime` and positive recorded amount/rate.

Demand/support components are:

- heart rate at least 110/min: chartevents 220045;
- respiratory rate at least 24/min: chartevents 220210;
- invasive or non-invasive ventilation active at `t0`: procedureevents 225792 or 225794 with `starttime <=t0<endtime`.

`S=high` requires at least one supply and one demand/support component, or at least two independent supply components. `S=low` requires no positive component plus at least one valid observed supply-domain value and one valid observed demand-domain value in the window. All other episodes, including inadequate domain coverage, are `S=indeterminate`. Do not merge or retune these groups after outcomes. The primary estimand compares high with low; indeterminate is retained in flow and a secondary three-level analysis.

Troponin, oxygen flow/FiO2 and pO2/SaO2 are covariates/sensitivities only because assay/reference and support-context harmonization are not yet adequate for the modifier. This phenotype is not myocardial ischemia, tissue hypoxia, low cardiac output, or measured oxygen delivery/consumption.

## Covariates, outcomes and estimand

Use only pre-`t0` information. For hemoglobin, lactate, MAP, HR, RR, SpO2/SaO2, pO2, FiO2/O2 flow, PEEP, creatinine, platelets, INR, pH and bicarbonate, construct prespecified latest/minimum/maximum/mean/count and time-since-last features where meaningful, along with missingness. Laboratory IDs are hemoglobin 51222; lactate 50813/52442/53154; pO2 50821; oxygen saturation 50817; creatinine 50912/52546; platelets 51265/53189; INR 51237/51675; pH 50820; bicarbonate 50882. ICU chart IDs include SaO2 220227, SpO2 220277, O2 flow 223834, FiO2 223835 and PEEP 220339. Include active vasoactive/ventilation state, preceding 12-hour total recorded input, age, sex, admission type, time from hospital/ICU admission, first care unit, the `hosp/transfers` care-unit interval containing `t0`, prior admission/ICU counts and `anchor_year_group`. Include counts/distinct times for labs, vitals, supports and procedures as observation-intensity variables. Do not use terminal `last_careunit`, discharge fields, `hospital_expire_flag`, `deathtime`, `dod`, post-`t0` diagnosis/procedure rows, notes or post-`t0` values as predictors.

From `tau` through `tau+24h`, assign the first mutually exclusive state:

- D: in-hospital death with `admissions.deathtime` in the window;
- E: new nonfatal ICU escalation before D—new invasive ventilation (procedure 224385 or 225792), vasoactive start from the listed input items when no vasoactive is active at `tau`, or new renal replacement therapy (procedure 225441, 225802, 225803, 225805, 225809 or 225955) when none is active at `tau`;
- X: indexed ICU exit with `icustays.outtime` before the horizon and before D/E;
- N: no D/E/X through `tau+24h`;
- U: irreconcilable/missing timestamps or ascertainment.

Tie order is D, then E, then X; same-time component ties are reported. Primary Y is D or E. Estimate D- and E-specific cumulative incidence with X competing, never interpret X as recovery.

For stress stratum `s`, fit the prespecified outcome model, standardize predictions to the measured pre-`t0` covariate distribution of the overlap-eligible test episodes in that stratum, and define:

`Delta_s = Risk_model(Y | A label=1, S=s) - Risk_model(Y | A label=0, S=s)`

`H = Delta_high - Delta_low`.

This is a model-standardized observed-risk label contrast. It is not an average treatment effect, and setting the model input label does not make it counterfactual. Report crude risks, standardized `Delta_s`, `H`, D- and E-specific versions, X incidence and 95% intervals.

## Matched transparent and longitudinal analyses

Freeze a deterministic `subject_id` 70/15/15 train/validation/test split before fitting or looking at test outcomes.

- B0: penalized six-hour discrete-time cause-specific hazards for D, E and X using A, S, spline hemoglobin and the full prespecified pre-`t0` adjustment/missingness/observation block, but no A-by-S term.
- Bhet: B0 plus the locked A-by-S interaction; this is the transparent primary scientific test.
- Bmask: identical hazards using demographics, care context, observation counts/masks and A/S labels, with physiologic values masked; this tests whether care/measurement process alone reproduces the result.
- J1: a masked chronological GRU over twelve one-hour pre-`t0` bins of exactly the same physiologic/support streams, masks and time-since-observation channels, with the same static context and A label, predicting the same D/E/X hazards.

J1 tests whether persistence, ordering, short excursions, irregular sampling and nonlinear coupling reveal information lost by Bhet's summaries. It cannot repair unmeasured indication, bleeding or ischemia. Select tuning and early stopping on train/validation only, use five fixed seeds, and lock one test evaluation. Compare held-out Brier score and calibration slope/intercept for D/E/X first; AUROC/AUPRC and fixed 10% event capture are secondary. A small discrimination gain without calibrated improvement or stable `H` is not substantive evidence. Bhet remains primary because it directly audits the prespecified interaction. The detailed comparison, compute assumptions and deferred alternatives are in the supporting method note.

## Support, uncertainty and falsification

Before fitting, require valid member/header/dictionary/unit audits; zero post-`t0` predictor rows; no unhandled reversed intervals or duplicate episode keys; and a complete flow for high/low/indeterminate stress, A, D/E/X/N/U and pre-landmark states.

The primary estimand is not estimable unless each A-by-high/low cell has at least 50 subjects and 20 Y events, there are at least 10 D and 10 E events across each stress stratum, empirical treatment propensity has common support in [0.05,0.95], weighted effective sample size is at least 100 in each stress stratum, and weighted standardized mean differences are no greater than 0.25 for prespecified continuous/current-state covariates. Report untrimmed and trimmed populations; do not alter thresholds, merge strata or add repeated episodes to rescue support.

Use 1,000 subject-level bootstrap resamples of the locked test set for standardized risks, H, calibration and model comparisons, preserving the fitted model; add 200 train/validation refit bootstraps if the future envelope permits and report the actual count. Use simultaneous intervals for primary H and its D/E decomposition. A clinically material heterogeneity region is `|H| >=0.05` absolute 24-hour D/E risk; this threshold is an interpretive region, not a known minimal clinical difference.

Prespecified falsifications/sensitivities are:

1. permute A within hemoglobin (0.25 g/dL), stress, care-unit and anchor-year-group strata; H should center near zero;
2. permute high/low stress within hemoglobin, A, care-unit and anchor-year-group strata; H should center near zero;
3. compare Bmask with Bhet; similar H/performance is evidence for a care-process explanation;
4. estimate the association of future A with the same escalation composite in `(t0-12h,t0]`; a strong “pre-outcome” association quantifies residual indication/severity confounding and forbids treatment-effect language;
5. repeat with blood-gas hemoglobin 50811, hemoglobin 7.0–8.0, item 225168 only, no OR/PACU PRBC, and stress-component leave-one-out analyses;
6. use non-PRBC blood products as a process-control exposure—FFP 220970/226367/227072, platelets 225170/226369/227071 and cryoprecipitate 225171/226371—without assuming biological nullness;
7. audit stability by t0 care unit, hospital day, anchor-year group and observation-intensity quintile;
8. scan source rows and model matrices for forbidden post-`t0` values, terminal/discharge/death fields and post-exposure physiology.

## Supportive, adverse and inconclusive results

Supportive evidence requires all source/time/support/calibration gates, a simultaneous 95% interval for H wholly outside [-0.05,0.05], compatible sign and magnitude in Bhet and J1, disappearance under label permutations, and no comparable Bmask interaction. It supports only observed-risk heterogeneity in these record-defined strata and the value of prospective/adjudicated trial-stratifier validation.

Adverse evidence includes a precise H inside [-0.05,0.05], opposing Bhet/J1 directions, Bmask equivalence, strong pre-outcome or process-control associations, or concentration in one component/care context. This challenges the EHR phenotype as a transfusion-stratification proxy; it does not show transfusion has homogeneous causal effects or that any threshold is safe.

Inconclusive evidence includes failed coverage/event/overlap/ESS gates, broad intervals spanning material benefit-like and harm-like directions, high U, incompatible units/status semantics or severe landmark selection. The correct output is “not estimable with this snapshot,” not a relaxed study.

## Evidence limits, compute and stronger claims

MIMIC lacks clinician intent, transfusion indication, blood availability/refusal, reliable completed-unit status, continuous bleeding adjudication, ECG/symptom adjudication, echocardiographic/cardiac-output context, direct oxygen delivery/consumption, randomized assignment and complete post-ICU escalation. Diagnosis rows generally lack onset times. MIMIC-CXR images and raw waveforms are not in this configured snapshot. Clinical review is essential to determine active bleeding, ischemia, appropriateness and preventability.

The transparent extraction/fits are CPU-first. Future planning envelope is up to 16 CPUs, 262,144 MiB, eight A100-SXM4-80GB GPUs and 28,800 seconds; current discovery budget is 7,200 seconds. These are capacity limits, not runtime measurements. J1 uses twelve bins, 32/64 hidden units, dropout, early stopping and five seeds; begin CPU-first and allocate one A100 with `cuda:0` only if repeated fits are materially limiting. No proposer/solver training is authorized, and GPU use is neither required nor evidence.

A supportive result cannot establish causal benefit/harm, a transfusion threshold, true ischemic effect modification, treatment appropriateness or transport outside this snapshot. Those require adjudicated bleeding/ischemia and intent, prospective temporally external validation, clinician review, and ultimately a randomized or otherwise causally identified study. A causal g-formula/TMLE redesign would change the estimand and must return to the Lead; it is not a compiler repair. A mechanistic oxygen-delivery model should be revisited only when cardiac output, oxygen content/consumption and bleeding data exist.
