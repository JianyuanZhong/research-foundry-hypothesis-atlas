# Grip-triggered cystatin C testing: replicated policy yield among people without previously documented kidney disease

## Decision and unresolved question

A primary-care or outpatient service may be able to order cystatin C for only a fixed fraction of adults whose creatinine-based eGFR is 60–89 mL/min/1.73 m². The clinically consequential question is not whether cystatin C is prognostic in general; it is whether adding a routinely obtainable grip measure changes who receives the confirmatory assay, and whether the extra assays identify previously undocumented low-filtration phenotypes that are subsequently associated with a recorded kidney hospitalization.

The inherited experiment made an important move to equal-budget, held-out policy yield. Its remaining weaknesses are material: it treated a prior N17 code as the only known kidney history, made the sparse product (reclassification × later AKI) the decisive gate, used one internal split while leaving the 20% remainder unused, and described the cystatin-observed cohort as if it were the operational assay population. This child repairs those points while preserving the kidney selective-testing question.

## Evidence versus hypothesis

Directly inspected Yim et al. (PMCID PMC9747759) supports the biological premise that creatinine depends on muscle mass and that cystatin C may reveal creatinine–cystatin discordance. It was a 138-person Korean study, used cystatin-based eGFR as its reference rather than measured GFR, and did not establish a UK allocation rule, clinical outcome benefit, or external validation. The available evidence therefore supports a plausible enrichment signal, not that grip-triggered testing improves patient outcomes.

The falsifiable claim is narrower and computable:

> Among UK Biobank adults with race-free eGFRcr 60–89, no previously recorded inpatient N17/N18 kidney diagnosis, and a valid baseline grip measure, a model adding grip to age, sex, BMI and eGFRcr will, at the same 20% cystatin-assay budget, identify more previously undocumented low-filtration reclassifications per 1,000 assays than the clinical-baseline model, with the gain replicated in a second untouched participant partition. The incremental reclassifications will not be clinically empty: their subsequent 10-year incident inpatient kidney-event yield (N17 or N18, with death competing) will also be higher per assay, if the event-count rule permits that inference.

“Low-filtration reclassification,” not CKD or measured GFR, is the target. “Incident inpatient kidney event,” not adjudicated AKI or CKD progression, is the outcome. Testing or treatment benefit is not hypothesized by this observational experiment.

## Frozen policy experiment

Use all eligible participants with an independent hash, keeping every participant’s modalities together:

h = int(SHA256(utf8("ukb-grip-cys-v3|" + canonical_decimal_eid)).hexdigest(), 16) % 100.

Use h<60 for development, 60≤h<80 for the locked policy test, and h≥80 for a second locked replication. Canonical eid, UTF-8, namespace, hexadecimal-to-integer conversion and modulo 100 must be recorded. No participant may overlap partitions. The second partition is an internal replication, not an external cohort.

Fit only on development L and never use K in model fitting:

- B: age, sex, BMI and eGFRcr.
- G: B plus maximum valid baseline left/right grip and sex×grip.

Fit penalized logistic models with restricted cubic splines for continuous variables, knots at development 5th/35th/65th/95th percentiles, and ridge penalty selected by five-fold participant-level cross-validation within development. Freeze complete-case rules, knots, coefficients, penalty and preprocessing before opening either locked partition. No cystatin measurement, target, N17/N18 outcome or death field may enter a predictor.

Within each locked partition and its label-complete set, each policy selects exactly floor(0.20×N) people with highest predicted H probability. Break ties with SHA256(utf8("ukb-grip-cys-tie-v3|" + canonical_decimal_eid)), interpreted identically. Also evaluate random 20% (analytic prevalence plus repeated deterministic draws), oldest-first 20%, lowest-eGFRcr-first 20%, and universal testing as references. Report G∩B, equal-size G-only and B-only swap sets. The model’s deployment score can be produced for O; yield inference is conditional on L unless the missing-label sensitivity passes.

For policy S, with nS=floor(0.20×N):

YH(S)=1000×Σ[S×H]/nS and YHK(S)=1000×Σ[S×H×K]/nS.

The primary policy estimand is the paired incremental laboratory yield ΔH=YH(G)−YH(B) and its ratio YH(G)/YH(B). The prespecified laboratory decision threshold is ΔH≥2 additional H cases per 1,000 assays and ratio ≥1.25, with a two-sided 95% paired interval excluding 0 for ΔH and 1 for the ratio in both locked partitions. The 2/1,000 threshold prevents a trivially significant but clinically negligible gain; it is fixed before model fitting.

The key clinical-relevance estimand is ΔHK=YHK(G)−YHK(B), the number of newly reclassified people per 1,000 assays who later have K. It is a validation of clinical enrichment, not evidence that testing prevents K. Require ΔHK≥1 per 1,000 and its paired 95% interval above 0 in the pooled locked partitions only if the event sufficiency rule below passes; show each partition separately. Also report policy-specific 10-year Aalen–Johansen cumulative incidence of K with death competing in G-only, B-only, overlap and all selected participants, plus the target-positive risk among selected participants. Cause-specific Cox and Fine–Gray models are explanatory, not substitutes for yield estimands.

Intervals use 2,000 paired participant bootstrap resamples within each locked partition and a prespecified pooled bootstrap stratified by partition; models are not refit. Decompose ΔH and ΔHK into G-only gains minus B-only losses. Report target prevalence, sensitivity, PPV, calibration slope/intercept and Brier score. A calibration slope outside 0.8–1.2 is a deployment warning and prevents a “supportive” label even if a yield gate passes.

## Event sufficiency and falsification

Before interpreting ΔHK, require at least 100 H×K events in the union selected by G or B across both locked partitions and at least 20 in each unique swap set. If either fails, report the computed point estimate and uncertainty but classify the clinical-relevance gate inconclusive; do not lower the threshold or merge endpoints after seeing the count. For partition-level statements, fewer than 30 H×K events in a partition prohibits a partition-specific positive claim. The laboratory gate remains interpretable if its labels are adequate.

Prespecified falsification checks are:

- Permute grip within development strata defined by sex, age decade, BMI quintile and eGFRcr quintile, refit the full development pipeline, and repeat the locked scoring under fixed seeds. Incremental target yield should center on zero; systematic positive yield indicates leakage, residual ID/time structure or a pipeline failure.
- Re-run with maximum, mean and minimum available hand grip; a conclusion that reverses only under one hand definition is unstable.
- Require code/date position matching, no baseline leakage, exact equal assay counts, and no overlap across partitions.
- A G-only swap set with no higher H yield than B-only, or a positive laboratory result driven solely by one partition, is adverse to the grip policy.
- One- and two-year lags, the alternative discordance target, and 10%/30% budgets are robustness analyses. They cannot rescue a failed locked 20% primary gate.

Supportive results require, in both locked partitions, the laboratory threshold, calibration warning absent, and no failed falsification; after event sufficiency, pooled ΔHK must also meet its threshold. This supports a prospective selective-testing evaluation. Laboratory support with an inconclusive or adverse clinical-enrichment gate means grip improves biochemical allocation but has not shown that the extra cases are clinically consequential. Adverse results are ΔH≤0, ratio≤1, failure of the absolute threshold, adverse swap-set decomposition, or reproducible calibration/leakage failure. Inconclusive results include wide intervals, label missingness above 1% without successful bounds, sparse H×K events, denominator zero, partition disagreement, unresolved code/date integrity, or sensitivity dependence.
