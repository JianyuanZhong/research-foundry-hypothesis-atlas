# Does a recorded creatinine rise reach a hospital-dependent renal-support escalation process?

## Scientific deliverable and substantive advance

The future solver must newly construct one auditable eICU six-hour landmark cohort and a 30-minute post-rise event ledger, then estimate whether hospital variation in a recorded creatinine rise extends to a more clinically consequential, directly observable renal-support documentation process.

The primary deliverable is the supported-development-hospital 90th-minus-10th percentile spread in the standardized 48-hour cumulative incidence of a first **strict, newly documented renal-support record** after a first qualifying recorded creatinine rise, restricted to stays with no strict renal-support record from ICU minute 0 through that rise. Call this (H^{support}_{90-10}). The solver must report:

- the primary support CIF and its hospital spread;
- support documented before a six-hour confirmation is possible, support after a distinct confirmation, and same-minute support/confirmation ties;
- the parent composite (confirmation OR support) and confirmation-only spreads as secondary endpoints;
- repeat-creatinine opportunity, confirmation conditional on a repeat, support conditional on confirmation, and recorded unit death/alive exit/unknown-status censoring;
- additive-baseline and ordered-multi-state estimates, uncertainty, support counts, calibration and hospital-held-out transport; and
- strict versus source-specific renal-support sensitivities and all prespecified boundary, clock, unit, MAP and split sensitivities.

This is a study of hospital heterogeneity in a recorded renal-care process. It is not a dialysis-initiation, treatment-receipt, biological-AKI, hospital-quality-ranking, or causal pressor/treatment study.

The substantive advance over the parent is endpoint specificity. The parent asks whether a recorded rise is confirmed later; its composite successor asks whether confirmation OR support occurs. The present successor makes newly documented high-specificity renal support the primary outcome, so a common laboratory-confirmation signal cannot by itself produce a “supportive” renal-escalation finding. If the support spread is robust, this would show that hospital variation in a documented creatinine signal reaches a renal-care documentation state that matters more for downstream clinical workflow. If confirmation varies but support does not, the scientifically important result is that the parent signal is a surveillance/measurement portability problem without evidence that it reaches documented renal-support escalation.

## Evidence-supported claim and unresolved hypothesis

The inspected local evidence supports availability and timing semantics, not the proposed conclusion. The frozen eICU 2.0 snapshot has ICU-relative observations, ICU-keyed laboratory, treatment and intake/output rows, patient unit disposition, hospital identifiers, and admission severity descriptors. A bounded read-only source scan recorded 40,060 renal/dialysis-related treatment rows across 8,284 stays and 58,473 dialysis/CRRT/hemofiltration-labelled intake/output rows across 6,159 stays. These are whole-source feasibility counts, not index-cohort event rates, prevalence, onset, treatment receipt or association estimates.

Public clinical guidance is consistent with treating this as an observable process rather than an AKI-treatment label: KDIGO advises that renal replacement decisions use broader clinical context and laboratory trends rather than an isolated creatinine threshold. The official eICU documentation defines offsets relative to the ICU/unit admission and distinguishes unit discharge status from hospital discharge status. Neither source supplies missing adjudication for this experiment.

The strongest claim currently supported is therefore:

> This snapshot can measure a first recorded creatinine rise, later creatinine confirmation, and high-specificity renal-support documentation with ICU-relative timing, while unit exit and incomplete status must be represented explicitly.

The unresolved claim is:

> Among adult ICU-unit stays with a first recorded creatinine rise and no earlier strict renal-support record, hospitals differ materially in the 48-hour probability of a newly documented renal-support record after standardizing measured severity, early MAP history, rise characteristics, prior testing opportunity and observation/exit processes.

Primary hypothesis, frozen before outcome inspection: (H^{support}_{90-10} geq 5) percentage points, its two-sided 95% hospital-cluster interval excludes zero, and a nontrivial portion of the spread remains after repeat-testing and competing-exit processes are represented in the ordered model. The five-point margin is a prespecified scale for site comparability, not a clinical treatment threshold.

A null, reversed, support-sparse, source-definition-dependent, or non-transportable result falsifies or renders inconclusive this hypothesis. No result can establish that a patient had biological AKI, that renal replacement was initiated, that treatment was received, that timing was appropriate, or that one hospital provides better care.

## Primary renal-support endpoint

The primary strict support record is the first eligible post-rise record in either of the following sources, after excluding all strict support records from minute 0 through (r):

1. `treatment.treatmentstring) case-insensitively identifies a high-specificity renal-support modality, including renal dialysis, hemodialysis, CVVHD, CVVH, CAVHD, peritoneal dialysis, SLED or hemofiltration; or
2. `intakeOutput.cellpath` or `celllabel` exactly identifies a high-specificity source label such as `Dialysis (ml)|In`, `Dialysis (ml)|Out`, `CRRT Actual Pt Fluid Removed`, `CRRT Out`, `CRRT - UF removed`, `CRRT- UF removed` or `Hemofiltration`, with valid numeric `cellvaluenumeric` or nonzero `dialysistotal`.

The solver must output the matched strings/labels and counts before applying the endpoint, so that the modality map is auditable. Exclude catheter insertion, dialysis-access surgery, line flushes, generic renal terms without a modality, and the treatment category `ultrafiltration (fluid removal only)` from the strict primary endpoint. Include ultrafiltration only in a broad fluid-removal sensitivity. Deduplicate exact `treatmentid` and `intakeoutputid` rows.

Use the earliest source offset after (r) and before (E). Raw source offsets determine ordering; if confirmation and support share a source minute, retain both indicators and classify the tie. In the 30-minute ledger, do not silently order events by bin: use raw offsets for the tie/pathway audit.

The primary estimand is deliberately called “newly documented support,” not new renal failure or renal replacement initiation. The no-prior-support gate is primary because a continuing chronic or pre-existing support record is not a new response to the recorded rise. A secondary retained analysis includes prior-support stays, reporting them separately as continuation/previously observed support.

Required source-specific sensitivities are:

- treatment-only strict modality records;
- intake/output-only named labels;
- broad union of strict treatment and named intake/output records;
- strict endpoint with a conservative requirement for nonblank/valid source value fields;
- inclusion of fluid-removal-only records; and
- revised-offset timing.

If treatment-only and flowsheet-only definitions disagree materially, or if the primary strict endpoint does not meet support thresholds, the support hypothesis is inconclusive rather than rescued by the broad definition.

## Matched transparent baseline

Fit a regularized additive cause-specific pooled-logistic model on identical 30-minute intervals, cohort, split, landmark covariates and uncertainty procedure for support, confirmation and composite outcomes. Include transition-specific elapsed-time linear/spline terms and common covariate slopes, with transition-specific hospital random intercepts and a hospital-by-transition time slope only when support permits.

Nested fits are:

- B0: case-mix and time effects, no hospital heterogeneity;
- B1: B0 plus transition-specific hospital random intercepts; primary transparent fit;
- B2: B1 plus hospital-context fixed effects;
- B3: B1 plus repeat-testing opportunity and urine-observability covariates;
- B4: B1 without hospital-by-time slopes, a stability/null-heterogeneity reference.

Post-rise laboratory values are outcomes, never predictors. Derive all hospital CIFs, (H^{support}_{90-10}), parent confirmation spread, composite spread, observed-versus-predicted curves and process contrasts from the same fitted framework. B0/B4 are stability and falsification references, not alternate scientific questions.

## Substantive mechanistic alternative

Fit an ordered piecewise-exponential multi-state model using the identical cohort, covariates, hospital split, standardization distribution, 30-minute interval ledger and 500 hospital-cluster bootstrap procedure.

The states/transitions are:

- first rise with no repeat and no support;
- repeat-test opportunity without confirmation;
- confirmed rise without support;
- strict support, absorbing for the primary first-support endpoint;
- recorded unit death;
- alive unit exit; and
- unknown-status censoring.

Permit support from the no-repeat and repeat states, confirmation only after a distinct qualifying test and the structural six-hour delay, and support after confirmation from the confirmed state. Keep direct support-before-confirmation, confirmation-before-support, and same-minute ties identifiable. Use transition-specific hospital random effects with shrinkage and hospital context only where estimable.

This alternative reveals whether hospital heterogeneity is carried by testing opportunity, confirmation conditional on testing, direct renal-support documentation, post-confirmation support, or competing exit. The additive baseline loses that clinical order by treating endpoint causes in parallel. Agreement on the primary support spread across models is stronger evidence of a robust documented-process finding. A spread only in repeat opportunity supports surveillance variation; a spread only after confirmation suggests a downstream documentation pathway but still not treatment initiation; a null support spread with a large confirmation spread argues against using the parent endpoint as a renal-care escalation marker.

A learned sequence model is deferred, not excluded. Revisit only if both prespecified models show reproducible shape-specific held-out miscalibration for the same support/pathway estimand and a learned model can preserve component decomposition, hospital-held-out evaluation and uncertainty. A urine-output/KDIGO branch is deferred because `intakeOutput` lacks a uniformly valid collection duration and reliable time-varying weight denominator. A causal pressor branch is deferred because `infusionDrug` lacks validated administration, dose, indication, intent and complete exposure. These are evidence-based deferrals, not blanket exclusions of neural networks, GPUs or structured data.

## Falsification and interpretation

Supportive evidence requires all of:

- primary strict support (H^{support}_{90-10}) at least 5 percentage points;
- a two-sided hospital-cluster interval excluding zero;
- at least 10 supported hospitals and the prespecified per-hospital risk/event support;
- qualitatively concordant direction and pathway decomposition in additive and multi-state models;
- a nontrivial spread after repeat testing and competing exits are represented;
- stability across treatment-only, intake/output-only and strict union definitions; and
- acceptable held-out calibration and timing overlap.

Even then, the conclusion is only that hospitals differ in a newly documented renal-support process after a recorded rise. It does not validate a renal intervention or identify biological AKI.

Adverse evidence includes a null/reversed support spread, an interval compatible with zero, disappearance after the no-prior-support gate or observation adjustment, support only in broad fluid-removal labels, unstable treatment-versus-flowsheet results, or a large confirmation spread with no support spread. That last pattern is a positive measurement-process finding and an adverse result for the claim that the parent signal reaches renal-support escalation.

A process falsification is a short-gap repeat-measurement control: estimate hospital spread in a repeat creatinine within 0-6 hours of the first rise. It should detect testing-practice variation; failure to reproduce it indicates inadequate process measurement or model instability. It is not a clinical endpoint. Additional falsifications are: shuffle support-record times within hospital and risk strata, compare pre-rise support-label rates, use the non-invasive MAP channel, vary the confirmation clock, and enforce strict versus permissive unit-discharge ties. A purported clinical support result that is reproduced by time-shuffled support records is falsified as a temporal renal-escalation process.

Inconclusive evidence includes fewer than 10 supported hospitals, fewer than 10 support events per supported hospital, sparse composite/confirmation transitions, extreme weights, poor overlap, bootstrap instability, failed convergence, strong dependence on NULL-status handling, treatment/flowsheet disagreement, or held-out calibration failure. Inconclusive is neither evidence for nor against a renal intervention.

## Evidence limits and future clinical decision relevance

Computationally checkable claims include source hashes and headers; exact joins; unit verification; raw-offset deduplication; no-prior-support exclusion; strict ICU boundary; confirmation delay; source modality matching; state transitions; deterministic split; standardized CIFs; support gates; model convergence; bootstrap intervals; calibration; and reproducibility of all sensitivities.

Clinical adjudication or another study is required to determine whether a rise is biological AKI; whether a treatment or flowsheet row means actual renal-replacement initiation; whether support was chronic before the ICU stay; whether a support record reflects fluid removal, dialysis or documentation artifact; whether urine output is oliguria; and whether hospital differences reflect treatment choice, patient selection, workflow or coding. Expert review is required before changing repeat-creatinine surveillance, dialysis referral or treatment policy. No hospital ranking, causal pressor effect, MAP target, renal benefit/harm or biological renal null may be reported.

If supportive, this study justifies a follow-up chart/order validation study and cautions that recorded creatinine-based endpoints may require site-calibrated renal-support ascertainment. If adverse, it supports the narrower interpretation that hospital variation is confined to observation/confirmation and does not establish downstream support heterogeneity. If inconclusive, the next requirement is additional validated evidence or a larger/linked dataset, not post hoc endpoint broadening.
