# Provisional hypothesis: discordant HbA1c rise and weight trajectory predict incident diabetes

## Status

This is a provisional, evidence-bounded repair draft. It is not a result, not a selection, and not evidence that the hypothesis is true. The parent question is clinically consequential: whether the same worsening glycemic state has different near-term diabetes risk when body weight is falling.

## Proposed relationship

Among UK Biobank participants with two valid assessment-center visits, no diabetes or cancer before the second visit, a comparable rise in HbA1c is associated with higher subsequent incident-diabetes risk when accompanied by weight loss than when accompanied by weight gain or stability. The primary contrast is an interaction between HbA1c change and weight trajectory, not a claim that weight loss causes diabetes.

## What is observed versus unresolved

The prior board context supports only that this is a proposed restart after a zero-event pancreatic-cancer direction failed feasibility; it does not establish the proposed association. The following are unresolved and must be verified before selection:

- exact catalog source paths, table names, columns, join keys, time fields, and archive members;
- whether two HbA1c and weight measurements are available with adequate overlap and measurement dates;
- incident-diabetes ascertainment, prevalent-diabetes/cancer exclusions, follow-up origin, and event counts;
- whether weight loss is measured as change, trajectory, or a clinically adjudicated variable;
- whether missingness and selection into repeat assessment bias the contrast;
- whether the pattern is specific to discordant glycemia/weight trajectories or merely reflects baseline severity, treatment, or observation intensity.

## Falsifiable test

Compare incident-diabetes risk after the second visit across prespecified discordant trajectories: HbA1c rise plus weight loss versus HbA1c rise plus weight gain/stability, with similar HbA1c rise and comparable baseline covariates. Estimate the interaction or standardized risk contrast with uncertainty, using a prespecified follow-up window and exclusions.

Supportive result: a reproducible, uncertainty-qualified excess risk for the weight-loss trajectory after adjustment and sensitivity analyses, with adequate event support and no material change after excluding prevalent disease, early events, or extreme measurements.

Adverse result: no clinically meaningful contrast, a reversed contrast, or a contrast that disappears under reasonable ascertainment/missingness checks. This would weaken the proposed interpretation and favor a single-state or baseline-severity explanation.

Inconclusive result: too few events, poor repeat-measurement overlap, unstable estimates, or inability to distinguish weight-loss trajectory from selection/observation artifacts. This would justify deferral or a narrower measurement/feasibility study rather than a clinical claim.

## Method comparison

Baseline: a prespecified multivariable survival or logistic model using HbA1c change, weight change, and baseline covariates, with the interaction as the primary contrast.

Substantive alternative: a trajectory model (for example, joint or latent trajectory modeling of repeated HbA1c and weight) fitted to the same participants and outcome, with the same temporal split and uncertainty evaluation. It could distinguish a transient change from a persistent trajectory and reveal whether the association depends on the shape of change, information lost by a single change score. The alternative is deferred unless repeat-measurement feasibility and event support are verified; it is not selected for complexity.

## Required deliverable

The future solver must newly estimate the prespecified risk contrast and uncertainty, report the fitted baseline and trajectory analyses if feasible, and link conclusions to computed outputs. It must not claim causality, occult cancer, or clinical actionability from this association alone.
