# MerTK inhibition selectively abolishes the protective FLS program while sparing the pathogenic FLS program in rheumatoid arthritis

## Seed
D2-T2 (Protective versus pathogenic states)

## Domain
Therapeutic Target Prioritization

## Parent
[prior hypothesis] (MerTK necessity for protective FLS program in E-MTAB-8873)

## Question
Does MerTK inhibition selectively abolish the protective FLS program (induced by MerTK/CD206pos macrophages) while sparing the pathogenic FLS program (induced by MerTK/CD206neg macrophages) in E-MTAB-8873, and is the protective-program effect replicated in E-MTAB-8316?

## Background
In rheumatoid arthritis (RA), synovial tissue macrophages (STMs) play a central role in shaping the synovial microenvironment. FACS-sorted MerTK/CD206pos STMs from RA patients in remission induce a protective (repair) program in synovial fibroblasts (FLS), characterized by increased expression of collagens (COL1A1, COL1A2) and TGFβ response genes (TGFBI). In contrast, MerTK/CD206neg STMs from patients with active RA induce a pathogenic (inflammatory) FLS program. [E-MTAB-8873 repository.json]

The episode 1 candidate ([prior hypothesis]) established the question of whether MerTK signaling is *necessary* for the protective FLS program. This candidate extends that to a *selectivity* question: does MerTK inhibition differentially affect the two programs?

The therapeutic implication is direct: if MerTK is a shared node required for both programs, then MerTK inhibitors (e.g., UNC1062) would be a blunt tool, disrupting both tissue repair and inflammation. If MerTK is selectively required for the protective program, then MerTK inhibitors would preferentially disrupt tissue repair without affecting the inflammatory program — a different risk profile that changes the therapeutic calculus for MerTK inhibitor use in RA.

[K1] Kuo et al. 2019 (GSE95588) showed that HBEGF+ inflammatory macrophages in RA promote fibroblast invasiveness in an EGFR-dependent manner, and that most RA medications targeted these macrophages. This established the principle that macrophage-fibroblast crosstalk is a key driver of RA pathology and that medications can redirect macrophage states. The selectivity question extends this: do different macrophage states use different signaling nodes to drive different FLS programs?

[K2] Mosallanejad et al. 2026 reviewed the role of macrophages in tissue repair, emphasizing efferocytosis and metabolic rewiring as key drivers of inflammation resolution. MerTK is a key efferocytosis receptor, and its signaling is thought to be part of the resolution program that macrophages use to induce repair in surrounding cells. The review does not address whether MerTK signaling is shared between protective and pathogenic programs or selective for one.

[K3] Wei & Xu 2026 proposed a "failed-resolution switch" in difficult-to-treat RA: imprinted pathological fibroblasts and a macrophage compartment stripped of its pro-resolving, efferocytic subsets work against resolution. Their reciprocal, feed-forward signaling assembles a self-sustaining stromal-myeloid circuit. This framework motivates testing whether MerTK is a key mediator of the protective effect and whether it is selectively required for the protective arm.

## Hypothesis
MerTK signaling is required for the protective FLS program (COL1A1, COL1A2, TGFBI up in Mpos vs FLS_only) but NOT required for the pathogenic FLS program (inflammatory genes up in Mneg vs FLS_only). If true, MerTK is a selective mediator of the protective arm, and MerTK inhibitors would preferentially disrupt tissue repair without affecting the inflammatory program.

## Rival hypotheses
1. **Shared-node hypothesis**: MerTK signaling is required for BOTH the protective and pathogenic FLS programs. In this case, MerTK inhibition would reduce both programs, making MerTK inhibitors a blunt tool. This is the most parsimonious alternative if MerTK is a general macrophage activation receptor.
2. **No-effect hypothesis**: MerTK signaling is not required for either program. The protective and pathogenic FLS programs are driven by other macrophage-derived factors (e.g., TGFβ, IL-10, SPMs) that are not affected by MerTK inhibition. In this case, MerTK inhibition would not affect either program.
3. **Pathogenic-only hypothesis**: MerTK signaling is required for the pathogenic program but not the protective program. This would be the opposite selectivity from the main hypothesis and would suggest that MerTK inhibitors could selectively reduce inflammation without affecting repair.

## Analysis plan

### Step 1: Define the protective FLS program
- Compare Mpos (none) vs FLS_only across all 5 FLS donors
- For each gene, compute the mean log2 fold change (Mpos vs FLS_only) across donors
- Define the protective program as genes with mean log2FC > 0.5 in Mpos vs FLS_only
- Expected protective genes: COL1A1, COL1A2, TGFBI, TGFB1, TGFBR1, TGFBR2, FN1, SPP1, TIMP1, TIMP2, THBS1, HMMR, APOE, ARG1
- Use all 8 Mpos samples and 5 FLS_only samples; donor-aware analysis (FLS donor as random effect or blocking factor)

### Step 2: Define the pathogenic FLS program
- Compare Mneg (none) vs FLS_only across all 5 FLS donors
- For each gene, compute the mean log2 fold change (Mneg vs FLS_only) across donors
- Define the pathogenic program as genes with mean log2FC > 0.5 in Mneg vs FLS_only
- Expected pathogenic genes: IL6, TNF, IL1B, CXCL8, CXCL1, CXCL2, CXCL9, CXCL10, CXCL11, CCL2, CCL3, CCL4, CCL5, MMP1, MMP3, MMP9, MMP12, MMP13, MMP14, ICAM1, CD80, CD86, CD40, IL1RN, TNFAIP3, TNFRSF11B, TNFRSF13C, TNFRSF17, TNFRSF25, TNFRSF4, TNFRSF8, TNFRSF9, TNFSF10, TNFSF11, TNFSF13, TNFSF13B, TNFSF14, TNFSF8
- Use all 6 Mneg samples and 5 FLS_only samples; donor-aware analysis

### Step 3: Test MerTK inhibitor effect on the protective program (primary)
- For each of the 4 paired Mpos Inhibitor vs Mpos none contrasts, compute the log2 fold change for each gene in the protective program
- Donor-aware paired analysis:
  - Primary: paired t-test (or Wilcoxon signed-rank test) on the log2FC values across the 4 pairs
  - Secondary: linear model with FLS donor as a fixed effect
- Primary outcome: does the protective FLS program decrease with MerTK inhibition?
  - Metric: mean log2FC of protective program genes in (Mpos_Inhibitor - Mpos_none)
  - Expected: negative (reduction) if MerTK is required for the protective program
  - Significance: p < 0.05 (two-sided)
- Gene-level analysis: which specific protective genes are most affected?
  - Focus on COL1A1, COL1A2, TGFBI as pre-specified markers
  - Also report all protective program genes with |log2FC| > 0.5 in the inhibitor contrast

### Step 4: Test MerTK inhibitor effect on the pathogenic program (selectivity)
- For the 1 paired Mneg Inhibitor vs Mneg none contrast, compute the log2 fold change for each gene in the pathogenic program
- **Critical limitation**: Only 1 pair. This is a descriptive comparison, not a statistical test.
- Report:
  - Mean log2FC of pathogenic program genes in (Mneg_Inhibitor - Mneg_none)
  - Gene-level changes for all pathogenic program genes
  - Focus on IL6, TNF, CXCL8, MMP1, MMP3, MMP9 as pre-specified markers
- **Selectivity test**: Compare the magnitude of the protective program effect (Step 3) with the pathogenic program effect (Step 4)
  - If protective program effect is large and pathogenic program effect is small/absent → selective
  - If both are large → shared node
  - If neither is large → no effect
  - Statistical test: not possible with n=1 for pathogenic; use effect size comparison with qualitative interpretation

### Step 5: Direct FLS control
- Compare R1_SA256A_FLS_Inhibitor vs R1_SA256A_FLS_only
- This controls for any direct effect of the MerTK inhibitor on FLS without macrophage co-culture
- If the inhibitor has a direct effect on FLS, this would confound the Mpos/Mneg Inhibitor contrasts
- Report: genes with |log2FC| > 0.5 in FLS_Inhibitor vs FLS_only

### Step 6: Validation in E-MTAB-8316
- For each of the 4 FLS donors, compare LPS vs LPS+UNC106238
- Check whether the protective program genes (from Step 1) are affected by UNC106238
- Check whether the pathogenic program genes (from Step 2) are affected by UNC106238
- **Limitations**:
  - All samples are LPS-stimulated (no unstimulated control)
  - M-CSF-differentiated macrophages, not FACS-sorted STMs
  - Bulk RNA-seq, not scRNA-seq
  - The macrophage phenotype may differ from E-MTAB-8873
- Report:
  - Mean log2FC of protective program genes in (LPS+UNC106238 - LPS) across 4 donors
  - Mean log2FC of pathogenic program genes in (LPS+UNC106238 - LPS) across 4 donors
  - Donor-aware paired analysis (4 pairs)
  - Gene-level changes for pre-specified markers

### Step 7: Selectivity summary
- Combine Steps 3-6 to assess selectivity:
  - **Selective (hypothesis supported)**: Protective program significantly reduced by MerTK inhibition (Step 3), pathogenic program not significantly reduced (Step 4), and protective program effect replicated in E-MTAB-8316 (Step 6)
  - **Shared node (rival 1)**: Both protective and pathogenic programs significantly reduced by MerTK inhibition
  - **No effect (rival 2)**: Neither program significantly reduced by MerTK inhibition
  - **Pathogenic-only (rival 3)**: Pathogenic program reduced but protective program not
  - **Inconclusive**: Inconsistent results across donors or datasets, or insufficient sample size for pathogenic program

## Falsification criteria
- **Supportive**: MerTK inhibition significantly decreases the protective FLS program (COL1A1, COL1A2, TGFBI) in E-MTAB-8873 (p < 0.05, 4 paired contrasts), the pathogenic FLS program is NOT significantly decreased in the single Mneg Inhibitor contrast (descriptive), and the protective program effect is replicated in E-MTAB-8316 (p < 0.05, 4 paired contrasts). This would indicate that MerTK is a selective mediator of the protective arm.
- **Adverse**: MerTK inhibition does NOT decrease the protective FLS program in E-MTAB-8873, OR both protective and pathogenic programs are decreased (shared node), OR the effect is not replicated in E-MTAB-8316. This would indicate that MerTK is not a selective mediator of the protective arm.
- **Inconclusive**: The effect is inconsistent across FLS donors, the single Mneg Inhibitor contrast shows a large effect on the pathogenic program (suggesting shared node but n=1), or the E-MTAB-8316 validation is inconsistent. The small sample size for the pathogenic program (n=1) means that a null result in Step 4 cannot distinguish "no effect" from "underpowered."

## Clinical importance
- RA patients in remission have a protective macrophage population (MerTK/CD206pos) that induces repair in FLS. Understanding whether MerTK is selectively required for this protective effect has direct implications for the use of MerTK inhibitors in RA.
- If MerTK is selective for the protective program: MerTK inhibitors would preferentially disrupt tissue repair, potentially worsening joint damage in patients with active disease. This would argue against MerTK inhibitor use in RA.
- If MerTK is a shared node: MerTK inhibitors would disrupt both repair and inflammation, making them a blunt tool with unclear net benefit.
- If MerTK is not required for either program: MerTK inhibitors would not affect the macrophage-fibroblast crosstalk in RA, and other targets should be pursued.
- This question directly informs the therapeutic decision of whether to use MerTK inhibitors in RA and in which patient population (active vs remission).

## Limitations
1. **Mneg Inhibitor contrast has only 1 sample**: The selectivity test for the pathogenic program is descriptive, not statistical. A null result cannot distinguish "no effect" from "underpowered." This is the most critical limitation.
2. **445-gene targeted panel**: The BD Rhapsody panel limits which genes can be tested. Unmeasured genes may be important mediators of the protective or pathogenic programs.
3. **Different systems in E-MTAB-8316**: M-CSF-differentiated macrophages (not FACS-sorted STMs), bulk RNA-seq (not scRNA-seq), all LPS-stimulated (no unstimulated control). The macrophage phenotype may differ from E-MTAB-8873.
4. **Off-target effects**: The MerTK inhibitor (UNC1062/UNC106238) may have off-target effects on other kinases. The FLS_Inhibitor control (Step 5) partially addresses this for direct FLS effects, but off-target effects on macrophages cannot be ruled out.
5. **Small sample size**: 4 paired Mpos Inhibitor contrasts, 1 paired Mneg Inhibitor contrast, 5 FLS_only samples. Statistical power is limited.
6. **Donor-specific effects**: The FLS response may be donor-specific. The 4 Mpos Inhibitor contrasts span 3 FLS donors (SA256A, SA257A, SA266A), but the Mneg Inhibitor contrast is only from SA256A.
7. **No original publication found**: The E-MTAB-8873/8316/8322 datasets were not found via Europe PMC search. The repository.json descriptions are used as the primary source for the experimental design. The identity of UNC106238 (presumed same as UNC1062) is not confirmed in the repository metadata.

## Method alternatives considered
- **Alternative 1: Pseudobulk analysis with DESeq2/edgeR**: Could be used for the E-MTAB-8316 validation (bulk RNA-seq, 12 samples). This is a standard approach and would provide gene-level p-values. However, the primary analysis (E-MTAB-8873) is a targeted panel with small n, so a simpler paired t-test on log2FC is more appropriate.
- **Alternative 2: Mixed-effects model with FLS donor as random effect**: Could be used for the Mpos Inhibitor analysis to account for donor correlation. However, with only 4 pairs and 3 donors, the random effect is not well-estimated. A paired t-test is more robust for this sample size.
- **Alternative 3: Network-based analysis**: Could be used to identify the signaling pathways connecting MerTK to the protective/pathogenic programs. However, the 445-gene panel is too small for meaningful network analysis, and the sample size is insufficient for co-expression analysis.
- **Selected approach**: Donor-aware paired analysis (paired t-test) for the Mpos Inhibitor contrast, descriptive comparison for the Mneg Inhibitor contrast, and paired t-test for the E-MTAB-8316 validation. This is the most appropriate approach given the sample sizes and data structure.
