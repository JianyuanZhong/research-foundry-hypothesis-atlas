# Recovery trajectory phenotypes before live ICU discharge

## Deliverable and advance

The future Harbor solver must newly construct and evaluate an auditable recovery-trajectory phenotype, not report a fitted result. It must produce (1) a cohort/coverage/unknown-label flow; (2) twelve one-hour bins of physiology, support and observation process with row-level provenance; (3) a mutually exclusive 48-hour competing-risk outcome; (4) held-out B0/Btraj/Bmask/Bseq predictions and uncertainty; (5) ascertainment/coverage and falsification analyses; and (6) a conclusion-to-output audit.

The unresolved hypothesis is: among first eligible live ICU discharges, does the direction and persistence of observed abnormality during the final 12 hours distinguish ICU-readmission-first from timed-death-first risk beyond terminal physiology, ICU/admission context, and observation intensity? The substantive advance over parent [prior hypothesis] is an interpretable recovery estimand: persistent, worsening, recovering, and stable-low trajectories, rather than an undifferentiated “more history” increment. The clinically meaningful result is a cause-specific pattern, not a small predictive gain. No fitted result is claimed.

The strongest pre-experiment evidence is only that MIMIC-IV contains within-person timestamped ICU observations, interval inputs, urine output, labs, ICU boundaries, and admission death/discharge fields. It does not establish true organ recovery, incremental prognostic value, preventability, discharge appropriateness, or benefit from surveillance or treatment.

## Competing-risk outcome

Within (t0,min(t0+48h,dischtime)], assign the first resolvable state:

- R: a later icustays row with same subject_id,hadm_id, different stay_id, intime>t0;
- D: admissions.deathtime>t0 and <=t0+48h, with no earlier R; tied R/D assigns D first;
- S: dischtime<t0+48h, no earlier R/D, hospital_expire_flag=0, and deathtime null or after dischtime. This is terminal because same-admission readmission is not ascertainable after discharge;
- N: no R, D or S by 48 hours while still under observation;
- U: hospital_expire_flag=1 with missing deathtime or any contradiction preventing death-time ascertainment.

U stays in the flow and unknown-label analyses; it is never N or S. A later ICU stay after dischtime is outside this same-admission estimand. Report cause-specific CIFs for R,D,S,N at 12/24/48 hours and secondary R-or-D fixed-horizon results with S censoring counts. The primary prediction target is the four-state distribution using hourly cause-specific hazards or equivalent competing-risk estimation.

## Matched alternatives and estimands

Common context is anchor_age, gender, anchor_year_group, first_careunit, last_careunit, ICU duration at t0, admission_type, admission_location, earlier ICU-stay count and pre-window observation/process summaries. Exclude discharge_location, notes, dod and post-t0 fields.

B0 is a penalized multinomial/discrete-time cause-specific hazard model with common context, terminal-bin values/masks and terminal process fields. Btraj adds the fixed phenotype, first/second-half burden and change, support change, fluctuation, coverage and testing/output summaries. Its target is R/D/S/N; primary comparison is paired held-out Brier and CIF calibration Btraj-B0 plus phenotype-specific standardized R and D contrasts. This is the primary scientific test.

Bmask adds the same history masks, measurement counts, testing indicators, Foley charting and support presence/minutes but no physiologic values. It tests whether Btraj adds physiology beyond charting. Bseq is a small masked chronological GRU (hidden 32 or 64, validation-only early stopping/dropout, five fixed seeds) receiving the same twelve-step numeric/mask/process vectors and context, with one competing-risk head. Bseq may reveal reversals and coupled order lost by Btraj; it is secondary and not selected for complexity. All models use identical population, source whitelist, endpoint, subject split and train/validation-only preprocessing.

Freeze an approximately stratified 70/15/15 subject split. Fit scaling, regularization, calibration, weights, thresholds and any phenotype audit using training/validation only. Evaluate multiclass Brier, CIF calibration at 12/24/48h, cause-specific risk contrasts and secondary one-vs-rest metrics. Use 1,000 subject-level bootstrap replicates within the solver cap, otherwise report completed count and execution-limited intervals. Preserve predictions, split, seeds, model states and bootstrap indices.

## Falsification and result interpretation

Prespecify within-subject bin-order shuffles, within-subject value permutation preserving masks, Bmask process-only comparison, terminal-only and order-blind alternatives, training-stratum label permutation, fixed-horizon endpoint sensitivity, and coverage thresholds 4/6/8 valid bins. Estimate stabilized inverse-coverage and inverse-ascertainment weights from pre-t0 context/coverage/process only; report overlap, clipping and ESS. Unknown-death complete-case, adverse/non-adverse U bounds, and all-eligible weighted results must be shown separately. Report exploratory subgroup analyses by care unit, age, admission type and coverage with multiplicity labels.

Support requires adequate event/phenotype support, no severe positivity/ESS failure, a prespecified Btraj-versus-B0 Brier/calibration improvement with uncertainty excluding zero, reproducible phenotype-specific R/D pattern across weighting/coverage sensitivities, and no comparable Bmask or order-shuffle explanation. Adverse evidence includes null/reversed phenotype pattern, loss after ascertainment correction, strong endpoint dependence, or Bmask parity; it weakens the physiologic interpretation but can reveal observation bias. Inconclusive means sparse states, heavy U, poor overlap, unstable intervals or nonconvergence; it supports neither hypothesis nor negation.

A verifier can check construction, leakage, metrics, uncertainty and whether conclusions follow from outputs. It cannot establish true recovery, preventability, discharge appropriateness, utility, causality or external validity. MIMIC lacks randomized policy, clinician intent, treatment limitation, ward monitoring, complete source-control/adjudication, measured GFR/cystatin C, raw waveforms and images. Notes are available but not validated automatic labels; clinical adjudication and external/prospective validation remain necessary.
