# JAK-STAT Dependence of Macrophage-Induced FLS Inflammatory Response in RA Synovial Tissue

## Scientific Opening

In rheumatoid arthritis (RA), synovial tissue exhibits a self-reinforcing inflammatory loop between macrophages and fibroblast-like synoviocytes (FLS). The crosstalk is well documented: FLS secrete IL-34, CCL2, and CXCL12 that recruit and polarize macrophages toward the M1 phenotype, while M1 macrophage-derived TNF-α, IL-6, and IL-1β further activate FLS. However, the **specific signaling pathways** that mediate the macrophage-induced component of FLS activation remain incompletely characterized.

JAK-STAT signaling is one of the major signaling pathways that mediate cellular interactions in the RA synovial microenvironment, contributing to inflammatory persistence and progressive remodeling [K1]. JAK-STAT signaling is involved in the pathogenesis of rheumatoid arthritis [K2]. JAK-STAT inhibitors are effective in rheumatoid arthritis [K3].

Three non-equivalent explanations are compatible with the current evidence:

1. **JAK-STAT dependent model**: Macrophage-induced FLS inflammatory gene expression is selectively dependent on JAK-STAT signaling. JAK-STAT inhibition reduces the macrophage-induced component without affecting baseline FLS expression.
2. **Broad kinase inhibition model**: JAK-STAT inhibition affects multiple signaling pathways, not just JAK-STAT. The observed effect is not specific to JAK-STAT.
3. **Composition-driven model**: Changes in cell composition (e.g., macrophage infiltration) drive the apparent effect rather than a genuine signaling interaction.

## Hypothesis

**Hypothesis**: Macrophage-induced FLS inflammatory gene expression is selectively dependent on JAK-STAT signaling, as demonstrated by selective attenuation of the macrophage-induced component under JAK-STAT inhibition.

## Clinical Importance

Resolving the specific signaling pathway that mediates macrophage-induced FLS activation is clinically consequential: it determines whether JAK-STAT inhibitors can selectively disrupt the inflammatory feedback loop without affecting baseline FLS function. This could inform targeted anti-inflammatory strategies in RA.

## Falsifiable Test

Donor-aware mixed model on inhibitor effect: if JAK-STAT inhibition selectively reduces the macrophage-induced component of FLS inflammatory gene expression (IL6, TNF, IL1B) without affecting baseline FLS expression, the hypothesis is supported. If inhibition has no effect or affects both baseline and macrophage-induced components equally, the hypothesis is falsified.
