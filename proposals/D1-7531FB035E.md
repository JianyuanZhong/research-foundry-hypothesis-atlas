TITLE
Validation-first gate for a decision-time study of documented ventilator-liberation readiness in eICU

SUBSTANTIVE CHANGE FROM PARENT
The parent proposed a multicenter clone/censor/weight emulation of a grace-period exposure, while explicitly deferring strict-screen size, within-hospital overlap, and marker concordance. I ran a bounded full-source feasibility computation before changing the estimand. The resulting cohort is large enough for a narrowly restricted exploratory association but does not satisfy the parent's own multicenter falsification requirements: among 540 eligible person-landmarks retained after the first qualifying ICU stay per uniquepid, only 3 hospitals have at least 10 landmarks in each strategy and only 2 have at least 20 in each. Preliminary liberation-marker concordance is poor: at least two marker types within 24 hours occur in 18/309 evaluation landmarks (5.83%) and 4/231 no-trial landmarks (1.73%); the union is dominated by a care-plan transition, while RT Vent Off is present in only 3 and 2 landmarks, respectively. Therefore the primary objective is changed to a validation-first gate. The consequential readiness question remains as a prespecified secondary, exploratory decision-time association within the small overlap set, and no causal or multicenter practice claim is permitted unless the gate passes.

CLINICAL QUESTION AND HYPOTHESIS
Can routinely recorded eICU care-plan states distinguish a clinically interpretable ventilator-liberation decision and a subsequent durable liberation proxy among objectively screen-stable adults, sufficiently consistently across hospitals to justify comparative inference?

Validation hypothesis: among adult ICU person-landmarks with pre-landmark invasive-ventilation evidence and a strict oxygenation, ventilator-support, respiratory-rate, oxygen-saturation, and MAP screen, the exact care-plan states "Ventilated - with daily extubation evaluation" and "Ventilated - with no daily extubation trial" will show reproducible temporal ordering and clinically coherent agreement with independent liberation markers (RT Vent On/Off = Off and exact endotracheal-tube removal treatment), without exposure-definition or documentation-density differences that preclude comparison.

Consequential secondary hypothesis, tested only if the gate passes: a first documented daily-evaluation decision is associated with higher 72-hour durable documented liberation than a first documented no-trial decision. This is an adjusted association for a documented care-plan decision, not the effect of an actual SBT or a causal practice recommendation.

WHAT IS ALREADY SUPPORTED AND WHAT REMAINS UNTESTED
The parent and prior full scans support that both exact care-plan labels occur and that respiratory and treatment tables contain potentially useful liberation components. They do not support that either label proves an SBT, that a care-plan transition proves extubation, or that the two strategies have adequate within-hospital positivity after strict readiness screening.

The new computation supports the following feasibility claims only: 200,859 patient rows and 323,079 deduplicated nonnegative Ventilation-group care-plan rows were scanned; 3,743 adult, ICU-observed-beyond-decision candidate person-landmarks were constructed; 540 remained after strict screen, invasive evidence, exclusion, and first-stay filtering; exposure counts were 309 evaluation and 231 no-trial. It does not establish a clinical liberation event or treatment benefit.

VALIDATION-FIRST PRIMARY DESIGN
The validation cohort is the 540 final person-landmarks above. The validation unit remains (patientunitstayid, landmark), with patient and hospital identifiers retained for clustering and documentation-density summaries. The primary validation report must include, by strategy, landmark, and hospital:

A. Exposure integrity
- first decision offset, decision delay, exact label, same-window competing labels, and repeated label transitions through 72 hours;
- counts of labels that are only care-plan states versus labels accompanied by explicit SBT Method, RSBI, weaning-protocol, Sedation Wean, or ETT Sedation Vacation records in respiratoryCharting;
- chart-entry delay where respchartentryoffset is present.

B. Liberation-marker component behavior
For D = decision_offset, search D < offset <= D+4,320 minutes for:
- carePlanGeneral "Spontaneous - adequate";
- respiratoryCharting RT Vent On/Off = Off;
- exact ETT-removal treatment string.
Record each component separately, first timestamp, component pattern, number of observed types, and whether at least two first timestamps are within 1,440 minutes. Missing components are unknown, not negative agreement.

The completed feasibility result already shows 72-hour first-marker patterns. Evaluation: care-plan only 115, ETT-removal only 4, care-plan plus removal 16, all three 3, none 171; union-positive 138/309 (44.66%) and two-type concordance 18/309 (5.83%). No-trial: care-plan only 77, ETT-removal only 1, RT-Off only 1, care-plan plus removal 3, care-plan plus RT-Off 1, none 148; union-positive 83/231 (35.93%) and two-type concordance 4/231 (1.73%). These figures are feasibility signals, not final outcome rates.

C. Documentation-density and hospital feasibility
For every hospital, report the denominator and availability of each component by strategy. The comparative gate passes only if:
1. both strategies are present in at least 3 hospitals with >=10 landmarks per strategy;
2. no component required for the primary durable endpoint is absent in >90% of either strategy within the restricted hospitals;
3. care-plan-only and independent-marker-only algorithms are estimable in both strategies;
4. component availability differences do not exceed a prespecified absolute 20 percentage points without a documented adjustment/sensitivity plan;
5. the temporal placebo below is not materially positive;
6. final outcome and recurrent-invasive-marker counts are sufficient for uncertainty estimation. Because only three hospitals currently meet the minimal overlap criterion and independent RT-Off concordance is sparse, the gate is expected to fail unless the completed durable-event audit materially improves independent marker availability.

D. Outcome adjudication proxy audit
This computational gate does not adjudicate true extubation. It compares three algorithms:
1. care-plan-only: Spontaneous - adequate after a qualifying ventilated state;
2. independent-only: RT Vent Off or exact ETT-removal treatment;
3. union with durability: earliest component followed by no high-specificity invasive marker for 48 hours.
For each, report first event, 48-hour durability, recurrent invasive marker, live ICU discharge, death, and unknown/truncated status. Death before durable liberation is a competing outcome. Live ICU discharge counts as durable only when a liberation component precedes discharge and unitdischargelocation is not another ICU; otherwise it is unknown/truncated.

SECONDARY DECISION-TIME ASSOCIATION, ONLY AFTER A PASSED GATE
If and only if the validation gate passes, use D rather than L as time zero. Assign the earliest label in [L,L+360] and retain only one observed strategy, avoiding the parent's clone/adherence-weighting implementation. This is a substantive simplification motivated by the observed decision delay and by the need to avoid treating a delayed label as known at L. Adjust using covariates measured strictly before L, plus decision delay and landmark. Retain multiple eligible landmarks with patient-clustered inference; first landmark per stay is a sensitivity analysis.

The primary exploratory estimand is a standardized 72-hour risk difference for durable documented liberation under the observed decision strategy, averaged over the restricted overlap population. It is not a causal effect of an SBT. Use overlap weighting or doubly robust standardization with hospital fixed effects, but only in hospitals satisfying the gate; bootstrap at hospital level and cluster repeated landmarks by uniquepid. Report effective sample size, weight distribution, standardized mean differences, and leave-one-hospital-out estimates. Do not fit a comparative model if positivity or event counts fail. The parent clone/censor/weight analysis is retired from the primary executable design; it may be a sensitivity only if the gate passes and an independent compiler review confirms correct risk-set construction.

Covariates are pre-L admission and trajectory summaries available in the parent: age, admission diagnosis/type, ICU type/source, APACHE variables, ventilation duration, FiO2, PEEP, SpO2, RR, MAP, labs, sedative/opioid and vasopressor charting, prior care-plan states, measurement counts, and time since last measure. Missing physiology is not normality. Vasopressor non-escalation remains sensitivity-only because infusionDrug is point charting without validated intervals.

OUTCOMES AND RECURRENCE
Primary candidate outcome for the gated secondary analysis is 72-hour durable documented liberation: a qualifying liberation component after D followed by 48 hours without a high-specificity recurrent invasive marker. Recurrent markers are new RT Vent On/Continued, ETT insertion treatment, invasive ventilation care-plan state, or invasive airway plus ventilator settings after at least 6 hours without invasive markers. Sensitivities use 1-, 12-, and 24-hour gaps. Secondary outcomes are first-marker time, recurrent invasive marker within 48 hours and 7 days, ventilator-free days through day 7 with death assigned zero, ICU death, and ICU discharge. The explicit SBT/weaning fields are process-validation endpoints, never proof of an SBT and never patient-benefit endpoints.

NEGATIVE CONTROLS AND FALSIFICATION
1. Temporal placebo: liberation components in the 24 hours before L. A strong same-direction association with the post-L decision indicates residual trajectory/documentation bias.
2. Documentation placebo: compare outcome availability and component-density differences by strategy and hospital; a strategy effect present only for care-plan labels but absent for independent markers is not supportive.
3. Exposure validation: compare SBT Method/RSBI/weaning documentation after each exact label, while acknowledging sparse fields.
4. Outcome-definition sensitivity: care-plan-only, independent-only, and union/durability algorithms must be directionally coherent; material reversal is inconclusive.
5. Hospital sensitivity: restricted overlap set, >=20 threshold, fixed/random hospital effects, and leave-one-hospital-out analyses.
6. First landmark/first stay, EOL exclusion, tracheostomy exclusion if operationalized, vasopressor/sedation sensitivity, and chart-entry-time sensitivity.

Interpretation rules:
- Supportive validation requires adequate independent-marker availability, coherent component timing, no material strategy-specific documentation-density artifact, sufficient recurrent-event ascertainment, and a null temporal placebo. Only then can a gated exploratory association be reported as a robust association supporting prospective evaluation.
- Adverse validation means substantial strategy-specific missingness, marker discordance, or a placebo signal. The study then reports that this operationalization cannot distinguish readiness decisions from documentation practice; it does not claim that daily evaluation is harmful.
- Inconclusive validation means sparse independent markers, failed overlap, fewer than three minimal-overlap hospitals, or uncertainty spanning clinically important benefit and harm. In the current feasibility state, the expected interpretation is inconclusive unless the final durable-event audit passes.
- A precise adverse association, if the gate passes, argues against assuming benefit but still cannot establish causal harm.

UNAVAILABLE EVIDENCE AND REQUIRED ADJUDICATION
The dataset cannot establish whether an SBT occurred, clinician reasons for no trial, true bedside extubation eligibility, airway protection, secretion burden, planned procedures, complete sedation contraindications, true extubation/reintubation, post-ICU reintubation, or causal clinical benefit. Structured care-plan labels are not proof of SBT performance. Any claim about bedside readiness, protocol efficacy, or mandated practice requires expert chart adjudication, richer ventilator data, or a prospective study. An automatic verifier can check offsets, labels, joins, attrition, marker components, overlap, model outputs, and consistency of conclusions with computed results; it cannot validate those clinical claims.
