# Working proposal: post-discharge laboratory trajectories after acute coronary syndrome

## Question
Among adults with an acute coronary syndrome (ACS) diagnosis during an index hospitalization, does the direction and rate of change in early post-discharge biomarkers predict 30-day all-cause readmission beyond what is captured by discharge laboratory values alone?

## Clinical importance
ACS patients are at high risk of readmission, and post-discharge monitoring decisions often rely on a single discharge laboratory snapshot. If early post-discharge trajectories (e.g., troponin, creatinine, hemoglobin, platelet count) carry incremental information, this could inform follow-up intensity and readmission prevention.

## Evidence base
- [K1] Liu et al. 2026: In-hospital inflammatory-nutritional-renal trajectory phenotypes are associated with graded 90-day risks after ADHF discharge and provide limited additional prognostic information beyond baseline clinical and biomarker data.
- [K2] Marcus et al. 2026: In patients hospitalized with ADHF, a decline in platelet count from normal at admission to thrombocytopenia at discharge was associated with excess 30-day readmission and mortality.
- [K3] Galván-Ruiz et al. 2026: Persistent albuminuria after acute heart failure identifies a high-risk phenotype, whereas early reduction was associated with more favourable unadjusted outcomes.

## Design
1. Define index encounter: first hospitalization with an ACS diagnosis (诊断名称 matching ACS terms).
2. Define post-discharge window: labs within 7 days after 出院时间.
3. Define outcome: any new hospitalization within 30 days after 出院时间.
4. Baseline model: discharge labs + demographics + discharge medications.
5. Trajectory model: add early post-discharge lab slopes/rates of change.
6. Compare held-out performance (AUC, NLL) with prespecified improvement threshold.

## Falsification
- Supportive: trajectory model improves AUC/NLL beyond threshold.
- Adverse: no improvement or worse performance.
- Inconclusive: insufficient post-discharge lab coverage or unstable estimates.

## Alternatives
- Post-discharge lab ordering intensity may reflect clinician concern rather than true trajectory.
- Medication changes may confound the association.
- Sensitivity analyses: restrict to patients with comparable lab coverage, adjust for medication exposure.

## Method comparison
- Simple baseline: logistic regression on discharge labs + demographics.
- Learned alternative: gradient-boosted model or longitudinal sequence model on early post-discharge labs.
- The alternative could capture nonlinear trajectory patterns that a linear slope misses.
- Selection: start with the simple baseline; use the learned alternative if the baseline is inadequate.

## Unresolved
- Exact ACS diagnosis terms in 诊断名称 need verification.
- Post-discharge lab coverage and timing need a bounded probe.
- Whether the trajectory estimand is identifiable with available data.
