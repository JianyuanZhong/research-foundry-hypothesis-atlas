# HCC episode 19: assay-specific laboratory trajectories after the first strict repeat TACE

## Proposal status and substantive change

This is a substantive child repair of `[prior hypothesis]`. It preserves that candidate's frozen cohort, treatment-pathway ontology, event clocks, selectors, and noncausal interpretation. The repair makes two changes required for a falsifiable and identifiable analysis:

1. It empirically tests the proposed relaxation from common albumin/bilirubin timestamps to assay-specific timestamps, using the complete HCC source files and direction-blinded selectors. The relaxation does not materially rescue support: the primary assay-specific triplets are 40 systemic / 118 comparator for albumin and 41 / 119 for total bilirubin, versus 40 / 118 joint common-timestamp triplets. The proposal therefore records a negative feasibility result rather than claiming that assay-specific timing solves sparse interaction support.
2. It replaces the parent's purportedly equivalent anti-coupling interaction model with a genuinely nonredundant baseline-plus-trajectory model and an explicit trajectory-by-arm contrast. Arm modification is exploratory and must be suppressed when stability gates fail; the primary estimand is the within-repeat-event assay trajectory association, not a causal treatment effect.

The clinical advance sought is a testable distinction between a laboratory trajectory already underway before repeat TACE and the subsequent acute laboratory level, while checking whether that trajectory differs by the pre-specified systemic-pathway arm. This can inform whether a pathway-associated vulnerability marker is reproducible, but this retrospective EHR design cannot establish treatment benefit, biological mechanism, procedural success, or causation.

## Evidence-supported claim, unresolved claim, and hypothesis

Evidence already supported by the inherited reconstruction is that a reproducible common TACE2 episode can be defined from the available procedure, encounter, diagnosis, and medication records, and that a first strict repeat TACE occurs on days 15–90 in 159/319 systemic-pathway patients and 526/1,491 comparator patients. The source also supports extraction of exact assay labels and event-proximal times, subject to missingness and the absence of laboratory units.

The unresolved claim is not that systemic therapy causes a laboratory change. The unresolved claim is whether, among patients with an independently selected repeat TACE and observed laboratory measurements, the pre-repeat albumin or bilirubin trajectory predicts the post-repeat level, and whether the trajectory slope differs between the frozen pathway arms after adjustment for baseline and measured selection variables.

Primary hypothesis, assay-specific and direction-agnostic:

> Conditional on the frozen repeat-event cohort and measured observation/selection covariates, a patient's pre-repeat trajectory in assay `a` is associated with the post-repeat level of the same assay, beyond the pre-TACE2 baseline; the direction is not assumed in advance.

Exploratory effect-modification hypothesis:

> The pre-repeat trajectory-to-post-repeat association differs by frozen pathway arm.

This is a vulnerability-marker association hypothesis. It is not an intention-to-treat, per-protocol, causal, or survival hypothesis.

## Laboratory selectors and estimands

For each assay `a` independently, define:

- `B_a`: latest valid numeric assay-specific result on TACE2 calendar days −30 through −1; TACE2 day 0 is excluded. Selection order is latest calendar/time, then deterministic source order for exact ties.
- `P_a`: latest valid assay-specific result in the selected repeat-event encounter, in `[event time −72 hours, event time)`. The event timestamp is excluded.
- `Y_a`: valid assay-specific result in the selected repeat-event encounter, in `(event time, event time +72 hours]`, chosen nearest nominal event +24 hours; an exact distance tie chooses the earlier timestamp. Event day 0 is excluded from a calendar-day +1–3 sensitivity selector.
- `D_a=P_a-B_a`: pre-repeat trajectory, defined only when both `B_a` and `P_a` exist.

The primary analysis is assay-specific; albumin and bilirubin need not have common timestamps. Joint common-timestamp albumin/bilirubin coherence is secondary corroboration only. The observed post times cluster around approximately +32–33 hours despite the +24-hour target; this is reported as a timing limitation and is not relabeled as +24 hours.

The primary vulnerability-marker estimand for assay `a` is the adjusted association of `D_a` with `Y_a` in the observed selected-repeat cohort, with baseline and arm retained as covariates. The direction is not prespecified. The exploratory arm-modification estimand is the slope difference in the following parameterization:

```text
Y_a = beta0 + betaB * B_a + betaD * D_a + betaA * A
      + betaDA * (D_a * A) + gamma' Z + epsilon
```

Here `A=1` denotes systemic and `A=0` comparator, and `Z` is the pre-specified vector of baseline/selection covariates. Since `(B_a,P_a)` maps one-to-one to `(B_a,D_a)` via `P_a=B_a+D_a`, `B_a` and `D_a` are not algebraically redundant. The comparator trajectory slope is `betaD`; the systemic trajectory slope is `betaD + betaDA`; the explicit trajectory-by-arm contrast is `betaDA`.

This replaces the parent's purported equivalent model using `pre_repeat`, `pre_TACE2`, arm, and `pre_TACE2 × arm`. That model has no interaction on the pre-repeat slope and does not transparently estimate the stated trajectory-by-arm slope difference when trajectory varies through pre-repeat at fixed baseline. The corrected model must not be described as equivalent to it.

Use robust standard errors or a prespecified small-sample method appropriate to the assay-specific sample size. Report estimates, confidence intervals, missingness, and influence diagnostics. No unit-based cross-assay standardization is allowed. If the event-level estimand is defined per patient, retain one selected event per patient; clustered alternatives are sensitivity analyses, not a new primary population.

## Selection and bias controls

The analysis must separate the laboratory trajectory association from selection into the repeat-event and observed-laboratory cohorts.

- Fit repeat-event selection models using only variables available before or at TACE2 and the frozen arm definition; do not use post-event labs. Use the inherited five-fold cross-fitted selection procedure and overlap weighting, with the exact folds fixed before outcome inspection. Compare the complete-case analysis with the weighted analysis.
- Fit assay-stage observation models separately for baseline, pre-repeat, and post-repeat availability, again using pre-event variables and event-independent information only. Report positivity/overlap and effective sample size (ESS) by arm and assay. Do not use post-repeat values to model their own observation.
- Prespecify minimum ESS and overlap gates for any adjusted interaction claim. If a gate fails, report the unweighted association descriptively and do not promote `betaDA` to a pathway-interaction conclusion.
- Report leave-one-out or influence diagnostics, robust residual checks, leverage/Cook-type measures, and results with the most influential observations omitted. A single patient or a few measurements cannot establish a clinical pathway effect.
- Address regression to the mean by retaining baseline in the model, reporting baseline distributions and measurement timing, and comparing the trajectory association with a baseline-adjusted formulation. Do not interpret a change from an extreme baseline as treatment response without a suitable untreated/repeated-measurement comparator.
- Repeat-event selection is post-TACE2 and may be affected by disease status, access, clinician choice, and survival/observation. It is therefore a selected-event association. The +14 observation requirement is institutional observation, not proof of survival.
- Medication orders do not establish exposure, adherence, dose, start completion, or continuation. Procedure rows do not establish treatment intent or technical success.

For each assay, first describe availability and distributions by arm without hypothesis-driven selector changes. Fit the primary model only on complete assay-specific triplets, then repeat using cross-fitted inverse-probability/overlap weighting for repeat-event and laboratory-stage selection. Covariate adjustment `Z` is limited to pre-specified pre-TACE2 demographics, encounter-history, disease/observation proxies, calendar era, and other variables available under the parent design; no post-event laboratory value or post-event medication is a confounder adjustment.

Report:

1. `betaD`, the adjusted trajectory association in the comparator reference arm;
2. `betaDA`, the exploratory trajectory-by-systemic-arm contrast;
3. the systemic slope `betaD + betaDA` with its confidence interval;
4. assay-specific results for albumin and bilirubin separately;
5. joint-direction coherence only as secondary corroboration, never as a prerequisite that changes the primary sample;

No estimator, window, or selector may be selected by the sign or magnitude of an observed outcome. The primary selector is fixed before fitting outcome models. The 24/48/72/168-hour pre windows, first/latest pre choice, post +24/+48 choice, first eligible post, and calendar-day +1–3 variant are locked sensitivity analyses.

## Falsification criteria and interpretation branches

Supportive results require a reproducible assay-specific `betaD` with uncertainty reported, similar direction/magnitude across unweighted and appropriately weighted analyses, no single-observation domination, acceptable overlap/ESS, and no materially stronger association in the negative-control pre-event interval. A supportive trajectory association does not prove causation or treatment benefit. A pathway-modification claim additionally requires a stable `betaDA` in both assays or a prespecified cross-assay corroboration rule, with all interaction gates passing; this is unlikely to be identifiable if effective systemic support remains near 40–41.

Adverse results include a null or opposite trajectory association, substantial attenuation after baseline/selection adjustment, strong influence dependence, poor overlap/ESS, or materially different estimates across locked timing selectors. These would weaken the vulnerability-marker hypothesis and indicate that the unadjusted association may reflect regression to the mean, observation, or event selection.

A null result with adequate precision and passed gates is evidence against a practically important trajectory association within this selected cohort. A null result with wide intervals, failed overlap, or sparse systemic support is inconclusive rather than evidence of no biology.

Selection-sensitive results are falsified as robust pathway evidence if the association disappears under cross-fitted observation/selection weighting or changes materially across plausible missingness models. Coupling/regression-to-mean sensitivity is adverse if baseline adjustment or a measurement-error-aware sensitivity analysis reverses or removes the association. Timing falsification occurs if the event-proximal result is no stronger than the locked negative-control interval or depends only on the calendar-day alternative. Negative-control falsification occurs if the pre-event control trajectory shows a comparable arm interaction, indicating residual temporal/selection confounding rather than an event-linked signal.

An interaction estimate is inconclusive, not positive, when confidence intervals are broad, ESS or overlap gates fail, influence is concentrated, or albumin and bilirubin disagree. Under those conditions report `betaDA` as unstable/descriptive only and explicitly prohibit promotion to a pathway-interaction conclusion. Do not widen windows, add assays, or change event definitions after seeing direction.

## Limits requiring further evidence

The available tables cannot adjudicate cirrhosis severity, tumor burden, laboratory units, transfusion/albumin infusion, infection, bleeding, renal or hepatic decompensation, procedural technical success, TACE intent, dose, adherence, treatment continuation, or out-of-hospital events. Diagnoses have no native time and lexical diagnosis matching is not expert adjudication. There are no images, raw waveforms, or validated clinical-note extraction sufficient to resolve these mechanisms. Expert review of records and procedure/imaging data would be required to determine whether a laboratory trajectory reflects hepatic reserve, acute illness, intervention complications, or treatment response. A prospective or emulated-target-trial study with verified administration, complete laboratory units and timing, repeated untreated comparators, clinical adjudication, and clinically meaningful outcomes would be required for causal or benefit claims.
