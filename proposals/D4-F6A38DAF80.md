# Normal-count macrocytosis and incident MDS/AML

## Hypothesis and decision

Among UK Biobank participants aged 50–69 with no recorded prior malignancy or macrocytosis-related diagnosis and normal baseline haemoglobin, leukocyte, neutrophil, and platelet counts, the joint phenotype MCV >100 fL plus RDW >=15% is associated with a clinically meaningful increase in 5-year cumulative incidence of MDS (ICD-10 D46*) or AML (C92.0) beyond demographics, alcohol, smoking, BMI, liver biomarkers, CRP, and the individual CBC components.

The clinical decision is whether this common, otherwise “isolated” CBC pattern merits prospective validation as a trigger for six-monthly CBC surveillance. This is a prognostic association hypothesis. It does not say macrocytosis causes neoplasia or justify marrow biopsy.

## Existing evidence and unresolved advance

**Strongest supported claim.** Younes et al. (PMID 23380746; full abstract inspected, full text unavailable) followed 43 clinically selected patients after liver disease, alcohol abuse, hypothyroidism, B12/folate deficiency, haemolysis, and culprit drugs had been excluded. Over median four years, 11.6% developed any primary marrow disorder, but only two developed MDS; mean time to a marrow-disorder diagnosis was 31.6 months. Rauw et al. (PMID 21704372; abstract inspected, full text unavailable) found age >=65, MCV, RDW, and LDH predicted MDS among 313 people already selected for marrow examination. These data support concern and follow-up in selected patients, not population-level absolute risk.

**Untested claim.** Whether RDW identifies a higher-risk subgroup within macrocytosis *before conventional cytopenias*, with sufficient absolute and incremental predictive value for a surveillance strategy.

**Advance.** The proposed population experiment estimates absolute 1-, 2-, and 5-year risk; uses exact pre-index measurements; models MCV and RDW jointly and continuously; separates events close to baseline from persistent years 2–5 risk; treats death as competing; checks specificity; and tests calibration/net benefit against simple baselines. The downloaded research-ambition demonstrations informed these temporal and baseline requirements only; they are not evidence for this hypothesis.

## Population and temporal boundaries

Include age 50–69 at (t0), with nonmissing index date, sex, MCV, RDW, haemoglobin, WBC, neutrophils, and platelets. Primary “de novo” analysis excludes any registry/inpatient malignancy C00–C97 or D45–D47 dated <=(t0); sensitivity exclusion is prior myeloid disease only. Also exclude pre-index E03*, D51–D53, D55–D59, and K70–K77 as recorded proxies for thyroid disease, nutritional/macrocytic anaemia, haemolysis, and liver disease. These codes cannot prove the causes absent.

Normal counts are haemoglobin >=13 g/dL male or >=12 female, WBC 4–11, neutrophils >=1.5, and platelets 150–450 x10^9/L. Category of interest is MCV >100/RDW >=15; reference is MCV 80–100/RDW <15. Primary information-preserving terms are restricted cubic splines for MCV and RDW plus their prespecified interaction.

Event windows are days 1–365, 366–730, and 731–1826. Follow-up ends at earliest event, death, loss, (t0+5) years, or applicable country-specific linkage cutoff.

**Essential missing datum:** local metadata explicitly lacks release/extraction timestamp and registry/hospital administrative coverage cutoffs. Before calibrated incidence is estimated, the compiler must obtain and freeze country-specific linkage censor dates from UKB provenance. Without them, event counting is computable but absolute-risk analysis is invalid and must be marked blocked.

## Computable analysis

1. Output a flow table with actual counts at every filter, missingness, baseline distributions, and events by source (registry only, inpatient only, both). Preserve questionnaire negative codes until field-specific recoding; there is no global negative-as-missing rule.
2. Estimate Aalen–Johansen 1-, 2-, and 5-year cumulative incidence with death competing for the four MCV/RDW categories.
3. Fit a cause-specific Cox association model and Fine–Gray absolute-risk sensitivity. Use spline age; sex, ethnicity, BMI, smoking, alcohol, log1p(CRP), ALT, AST, and GGT; haemoglobin, WBC, neutrophils, platelets; spline MCV, spline RDW, and interaction. If proportional hazards fail, report interval effects, not one HR.
4. Compare M0 (age/sex), M1 (M0 plus ethnicity, BMI, smoking, alcohol, CRP/liver markers), and M2 (M1 plus all CBC terms). Report added-term likelihood-ratio test, optimism-corrected time-dependent AUC and Brier score, calibration intercept/slope/plot, using 500 participant bootstraps. Add grouped assessment-centre validation as sensitivity.
5. Report decision curves for low-cost repeat-CBC surveillance at 5-year risk thresholds 0.25%, 0.5%, and 1%, compared with monitor-all/none. Do not interpret these as biopsy evidence.
6. Do not impute exposure/outcome. Multiply impute covariates only; report complete-case sensitivity and differential missingness.
7. Sensitivities: exclude year-1 events and estimate years 2–5; registry-only and inpatient-only; MDS and AML separately; age 60–69, sex, and broad ethnicity strata; stricter “likely unexplained” exclusions only after expert adjudication of `20002`/`20003`; exclude daily alcohol and abnormal ALT/AST/GGT; specificity outcomes melanoma C43* and all solid cancers. Comparable signals for unrelated cancers weaken marrow specificity.

## Prespecified falsification and interpretation

**Support for the association hypothesis** requires the joint category’s adjusted 5-year cause-specific HR >2 with 95% CI excluding 1, improved fit from MCV/RDW interaction, and adjusted 5-year absolute risk >=0.5%. Persistence after excluding year 1 supports—but does not prove—a pre-cytopenic marker rather than contemporaneous occult disease.

**Support for clinical surveillance** additionally requires calibrated predictions and positive bootstrap-corrected net benefit at one or more 0.25–1% thresholds. Association without incremental utility is scientifically supportive but clinically insufficient.

**Falsification as a useful marker:** upper 95% CI excludes HR 1.5, upper 95% CI for 5-year risk is <0.5%, or no positive net benefit at any threshold. HR <1 is adverse evidence. A signal confined to year 1 suggests diagnostic delay/occult disease, not durable risk. Similar melanoma/all-solid-cancer associations imply nonspecific illness or ascertainment.

**Inconclusive:** <100 primary events overall or <20 joint-category events; intervals span both HR 1 and 2; linkage censoring unresolved; or calibration too imprecise. “Not significant” alone is not falsification.

## Evidence limits and verifier scope

Computationally checkable: hashes/columns, `eid` uniqueness, suffix-preserving pairs, event chronology, filter counts, models/estimands, CIs, PH checks, bootstrap metrics, calibration, and whether conclusions obey supportive/adverse/inconclusive rules. The verifier must reject a causal statement, biopsy recommendation, or CHIP claim even if numerical criteria are met; it must also accept an appropriately qualified adverse or inconclusive conclusion.

Require clinical adjudication/additional data: validity and subtype of D46/C92.0, B12/folate/thyroid/haemolysis/medication causes, blood film, LDH, marrow morphology/cytogenetics, somatic variants/CHIP, treatment and symptoms, and surveillance harms/costs. UKB lacks these in this snapshot. External health-system validation and a prospective management-impact study are required before practice change.

## Feasibility risks

- Decisive: few events in the normal-count MCV>100/RDW>=15 subgroup; minimum-event rules prevent overinterpretation.
- Administrative linkage cutoffs are absent.
- Local coding dictionaries are mostly absent; medication/illness exclusions need frozen dictionaries plus haematologist/pharmacist review.
- UKB lacks B12, folate, LDH, blood film, marrow pathology, and raw variants; the phenotype cannot truly be called “unexplained.”
- Linked codes may date recognition, not biological onset, and miss outpatient workup.
- Healthy-volunteer selection and maximum baseline age 69 limit transportability to older/symptomatic patients.
