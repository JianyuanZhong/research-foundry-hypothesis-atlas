# Provisional HCC endpoint/measurement audit hypothesis

## Proposed relationship
Among adults with a first documented HCC diagnosis in the bound HCC dataset, early recorded liver-directed treatment may be associated with a lower 1-year mortality/contact endpoint than no early treatment. This is an observational association hypothesis, not a causal treatment-benefit claim. The central unresolved issue is whether mortality is actually observable in the HCC tables and whether treatment, stage, and follow-up are sufficiently measured to support that endpoint.

## Observed evidence and exact source locations
The parent proposal inspected the HCC catalog and representative rows. It reports that `diagnoses` (`[internal dataset path]`) contains 1,810,646 rows and HCC labels; `encounters` (`data_基本信息_2500296761891079109.csv`) contains 105,044 rows with `就诊时间`, `入院时间`, and `出院时间`; and `procedures` (`data_手术_8024330590283626027.csv`) contains 338,040 rows with `开始时间` and treatment labels. The parent explicitly states that the catalog has no explicit death table and that mortality must be validated in source rows before claiming 1-year mortality. No mortality effect estimate has been observed.

## Uncertainties and missing evidence
The exact number of incident HCC index patients, early-treatment coverage, stage coverage, follow-up beyond 30/90/365 days, and any death signal remain unquantified. Death may be absent, embedded in encounter/discharge fields, or only inferable from clinical text; these possibilities require a bounded audit. Treatment labels and stage labels require frozen mappings. Follow-up based on last contact is informative and is not equivalent to mortality. A validated external mortality registry, treatment intent, and adjudicated stage/performance status are not currently established.

## Falsifiable test
Audit all HCC source schemas and representative/complete rows for death-related fields or values; construct the first documented HCC index cohort; quantify treatment coverage in the first 30 days, follow-up coverage, and mortality/contact availability. If a validated death field exists, test the prespecified adjusted association with uncertainty and selection/observation diagnostics. If no validated death field exists, defer mortality as the primary endpoint and report only the computable contact/censoring endpoint with explicit limitations. A supportive result requires reproducible endpoint ascertainment and an adjusted association robust to stage/documentation/censoring checks; disappearance after adjustment supports selection or documentation explanations; sparse endpoint coverage or wide uncertainty is inconclusive.
