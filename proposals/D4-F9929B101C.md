TITLE
Cross-marker renal-threshold transition after baseline creatinine–cystatin-C discordance

SUBSTANTIVE CHILD ADVANCE
This child turns the parent's reciprocal observable-persistence question into a prespecified, clinically interpretable protocol-biomarker threshold experiment. It tests whether the marker already below a consequential eGFR boundary at baseline predicts crossing of the other marker below that boundary at the first repeat assessment, rather than merely predicting the same marker's repeat level. The primary event is therefore cross-marker transition, avoiding same-marker mathematical coupling. It remains strictly two-wave and noncausal.

EVIDENCE AND UNRESOLVED CLAIM
The parent supports positive measured-baseline attendance-weighted observable persistence for both directions. Only P1 (baseline cystatin-C conditional on creatinine predicting repeat creatinine conditional on creatinine) discriminates from both direct-LDL and vitamin-D matched controls; P2 discriminates from neither. The new untested claim is ordering of cross-marker reclassification at a fixed renal eGFR boundary. A positive result supports reproducible two-wave protocol-biomarker reclassification among observed repeat attenders under measured-baseline attendance weighting. It does not establish latent GFR, CKD diagnosis/progression or chronicity, measured-GFR decline, treatment indication/benefit, causality, MNAR correction, or transportability.

FALSIFICATION AND INTERPRETATION
Baseline-level falsification: fit the same model using above-threshold-marker baseline eGFR as the only exposure; if the directional difference vanishes after level matching, favor baseline level rather than ordering. Regression-to-mean falsification: compare matched opposite-direction transitions at the same q and common baseline ranges; a result present only before matching is adverse to ordering. Attendance falsification: compare unweighted, parent-weighted, and 0.005-floor-weighted analyses; material sign/interval changes are adverse to measured-selection robustness. Preserve the parent's adverse specificity result unchanged: P1 exceeds direct-LDL and vitamin-D controls, whereas P2 exceeds neither; no bidirectional renal-specificity claim is allowed.

Supportive results require supported q=60 cross-marker contrast, simultaneous interval excluding zero after baseline-level adjustment, concordant raw/weighted direction, and no material floor sensitivity. This supports predictive ordering of observed protocol eGFR classification only. Adverse results include null/reversed contrast, attenuation after matching, weight dependence, inadequate support, or effects restricted to same-marker persistence; these favor stable level, regression-to-mean, selection, or measurement explanations. Inconclusive results include sparse discordant cells, non-overlap, separation, or inability of two waves to distinguish biology from assay/attendance mechanisms.
