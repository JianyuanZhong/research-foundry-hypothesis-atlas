## Substantive successor change

The parent established a carefully gated UKB analysis of whether prior values improve pooled prediction in the frozen central 60% current-only risk band and whether that association is stable from assessments through 31 December 2012 to assessments in calendar year 2013. Its successor weakness is decision semantics: selecting 10% separately inside each of four Q strata does not define one fixed review budget. When Q cells differ in size, the combined policy uses a different number of slots than a 10% review list in A; when era sizes differ, the absolute workload changes again. That is a useful balanced-allocation experiment, but it is not by itself a coherent fixed-capacity clinical decision estimand.

This child retains the parent’s exact endpoint, cohort, temporal boundaries, source bindings, support gates, split, nested models, and falsification requirements. It changes the primary decision question to:

> Among participants in the parent’s frozen A band, does retaining the prior eight-field panel increase capture of subsequent recorded target events when one predeclared total review budget is held fixed across early and late eras, compared with current values plus verified repeat spacing?

The primary policy uses a fixed number of review slots K, defined without outcomes as floor(10% of the eligible early A population), and applies the same K to the late A population. The baseline and prior-panel policies each rank the same A participants by held-out T risk and select exactly K. This tests a real resource-constrained choice: whether historical measurements change which people occupy a limited review list. The parent’s 10%-within-Q policy is reproduced exactly as a secondary balanced-allocation estimand, while a deterministic equal-quota K/4 policy is reported as a separate allocation scenario.

The unresolved transport question is narrower than external generalizability: does the incremental fixed-slot capture observed in early UKB remain directionally and materially similar when an early-fitted policy is applied without refitting to the 2013 UKB assessment cohort? This is internal temporal transport only. It does not claim transport to other UKB participants, health systems, calendar periods, or clinical workflows. A positive result would support further prospective evaluation of a capacity-constrained review policy; a negative result would show that a pooled or balanced-stratum signal is not enough to justify retaining prior values under a stable slot budget. Neither result establishes clinical utility or a treatment benefit.

The parent’s pooled Brier and interaction estimands remain mandatory continuity outputs. The new primary deliverable is the uncertainty-quantified fixed-slot capture/yield contrast and its early-to-late stability, or a reproducible fail-closed report.

## Selection, split, and frozen risk strata

Create a complete participant flow from the population anchor through age/sex, T0/T1, interval, paired-panel completeness, prior-event exclusion, era, ascertainment, parent risk band, new risk strata, outcome, and policy eligibility. Report every count, including malformed outcome positions, target events, deaths, exclusions, the early and late A populations, the fixed slot count K, the four Q cells, and all policy-selected counts.

Fit a regularized selection model in early data using only pre-T1 T0 panel values, age, sex, and T0 calendar year. Apply it unchanged to late T0-complete participants. Use stabilized sensitivity weights w_i=P(S=1)/p-hat(S=1|X_i), truncating at the early 1st and 99th percentiles. ## Models and substantive alternatives on identical inputs

All models use the same eligible participants, endpoint, fixed split, frozen bands/strata, policy capacity, weights, and uncertainty procedure.

- M0: regularized multinomial current panel plus age at recruitment, sex, and T1 calendar year.
- M1a: M0 plus verified repeat spacing (L).
- M1b: M1a plus all eight literal prior-panel values. The parent’s primary prior-information contrast is M1b versus M1a.
- M1c: M1a plus eight current-minus-prior changes, only as a prespecified representation sensitivity after timing, units, and missingness certification. It cannot replace M1b versus M1a.
- M2: shallow gradient-boosted multinomial model with exactly M1b’s inputs.
- M2a: matched shallow gradient-boosted multinomial model with exactly M1a’s inputs.

M0/M1a/M1b/M1c use fold-local preprocessing and regularized multinomial probabilities. M2/M2a use a small prespecified depth, learning-rate, and regularization grid, fixed tree limit, and training-only early stopping or inner validation. No model uses outcome labels, event dates, late data, or post-T1 information in feature construction.

The transparent baseline answers the parent question and provides the counterfactual ranking policy: M1a’s current-plus-spacing T risk. The substantive learned alternative tests whether nonlinear current/prior discordance or spacing interactions alter a capacity-limited ranking in ways additive M1b averages away. Its scientific value is judged by the same fixed-capacity capture, yield, calibration, fold stability, and early-to-late transport outputs—not by a small predictive improvement alone. M2 cannot rescue a failed endpoint, timing, ascertainment, or transparent M1b-versus-M1a contrast.

## New decision-specific estimands and parent comparison

All policy estimands are evaluated only in the frozen A band. For model M and era e, let R_M,i be the held-out predicted probability of T, let B_M,i(K) select exactly K participants with the largest R_M,i among A, and let w_i be 1 for the primary analysis or the frozen selection-sensitivity weight. Define fixed-slot capture and yield as:

C_A(M,e) = sum_i [w_i * 1(Y_i=T) * B_M,i(K)] / sum_i [w_i * 1(Y_i=T)]

P_A(M,e) = sum_i [w_i * 1(Y_i=T) * B_M,i(K)] / sum_i [w_i * B_M,i(K)].

The primary prior-information contrast is Delta_C,e = C_A(M1b,e) - C_A(M1a,e), with the analogous yield contrast Delta_P,e. M1a is the transparent current-plus-spacing baseline; M1b adds the eight literal prior values. The primary hypothesis is Delta_C,late > 0.05, with a complete two-sided 95% interval above the prespecified five-percentage-point operational margin, and no material reversal from early to late. The margin is a research decision margin, not a validated clinical-benefit threshold. Report the actual K, selected counts, capture numerators and denominators, and yield in percentage points.

Define internal temporal transport of the incremental policy contrast as tau_C = Delta_C,late - Delta_C,early and tau_P analogously. Treat tau_C within [-0.05, 0.05] as a stability criterion only when all support gates, calibration, and overlap checks pass. It does not establish external transportability.

For the explicitly separate equal-quota policy, replace B_M,i(K) by B_M,i^quota, which selects K_h within each Q_h, and report its capture, yield, and M1b-minus-M1a contrasts. For the parent-comparability policy, report the exact 10%-within-Q capture and yield using its own nearest-integer quota. These two policy analyses must not be pooled or described as the primary fixed-slot estimand.

To locate any redistribution without changing the primary decision question, report selected fraction, target-event capture, yield, and missed-target rate by Q under the global K policy. These Q-specific quantities are descriptive heterogeneity outputs; a Q4-minus-Q1 contrast is supportive only if it is prespecified, simultaneous-interval controlled, and stable under the same fixed K. It cannot be interpreted as evidence for giving each Q its own 10% budget.

The parent continuity estimand remains mandatory:

d(g,e) = BS(M1b,g,e) - BS(M1a,g,e)
D(e) = d(A,e) - [d(L,e) + d(U,e)]/2
tau_A = D(late, no-refit) - D(early).

Report parent Brier/log-loss/calibration outputs alongside Delta_C, Delta_P, tau_C, and the allocation sensitivities. A favorable pooled Brier contrast with no fixed-slot signal does not support the new decision hypothesis. Conversely, a fixed-slot contrast does not erase a poorly calibrated pooled model or establish clinical utility.

## Interpretation boundaries

Supportive results require all semantic, code-domain, timing, ascertainment, join, and integrity gates; feasible and identical K across eras; adequate Q-cell support for the retained secondary analyses; calibrated and stable held-out predictions; positive fixed-slot Delta_C,late beyond the 0.05 operational margin; consistent direction under selection weighting; acceptable parent continuity outputs; no permutation or random-capacity failure; and a tau_C within the prespecified temporal-stability margin. This supports a conditional, capacity-normalized association between retaining prior values and capture of the specified recorded T endpoint in the supported UKB A-band population, with limited internal temporal stability. It suggests where a future triage study might focus.

Adverse results include no global fixed-slot improvement, improvement only under the parent’s separately normalized Q quotas, Delta_C at or below the operational margin, reversal under weighting, poor calibration, fold instability, early-to-late reversal, or failed permutation/random-capacity controls. These findings weaken the case for retaining prior values for this recorded-risk review task; they do not prove no value in another population, outcome, capacity, or clinical setting.

Inconclusive results include missing or failed code-domain/timing/ascertainment certification, malformed/unpaired positions, death-date disagreement, fewer than 100 T events, infeasible K, sparse Q cells, poor overlap/ESS, nonidentifiable cutpoints, wide intervals, conflicting endpoint or missingness sensitivities, or an unstable learned policy. “No qualifying record in the available source” under incomplete follow-up is inconclusive, not a negative clinical result.

Computationally checkable claims are source/schema/header integrity, full-array position handling, joins, flow counts, endpoint construction, event ordering, model fitting, held-out predictions, fixed-K and quota policy selection, capture/yield arithmetic, calibration, bootstrap intervals, overlap/ESS, and permutation behavior. Clinical adjudication or another study is required for true disease onset, outpatient events, severity, assay/specimen validity, complete ascertainment beyond supplied evidence, causal effects of repeat testing, validated clinical utility, clinical thresholds, external validation, absolute workload feasibility in practice, and prospective policy evaluation. No automatic verifier can establish those claims.

The temporal comparison is not an external validation study. The late cohort is within the same configured UKB discovery partition, and the reserved catalog partition is inaccessible. Selection weights address only a declared paired-panel/eligibility sensitivity model; they do not repair participation bias, outcome ascertainment differences, changing clinical practice, or missing external populations.

## Alternatives considered, deferred, and revisit conditions

A current-only/spacing baseline (M1a) was selected because its global top-K policy is the transparent counterfactual for a real fixed review budget and isolates the value of prior values. Additive prior values (M1b) were selected as the primary information comparison because they preserve literal measurements and isolate prior information from repeat spacing. The boosted M2/M2a pair was selected as a substantive learned alternative because the same inputs can reveal nonlinear current-prior discordance and risk-ranking interactions that an additive model may average away. All alternatives use the same A population, fixed K, endpoint, split, held-out evaluation, and uncertainty procedure; the target is held-out three-class T/D/N prediction plus fixed-slot capture/yield and temporal stability.

The parent’s 10%-within-Q policy is retained as a secondary reproducibility output because it answers a balanced allocation question, but it is not evidence about a fixed total workload. The equal-quota K_h policy is a second clinically interpretable allocation scenario; it is not selected as primary because equal Q allocation has no verified clinical resource rationale in this snapshot. The learned model is not selected merely for predictive gain: it must change fixed-slot event capture or reveal stable, interpretable redistribution under identical capacity, with calibration and null controls intact.

M1c raw change is retained only as a representation sensitivity: it may reveal deterioration-related information but is not the primary estimand because difference interpretation depends on certified units, specimen timing, and comparable assays. Physical/body-composition extensions are deferred despite available-looking assessment fields because local metadata does not certify labels, units, missing meanings, or clinical relevance. Genomic/latent-GP and disease-sequence models are deferred because the local genomics file is derived phenotype/metadata rather than a raw sequence/variant bundle, and the outcome history lacks a verified event-level date/code dictionary adequate for a mechanistic disease trajectory. A larger neural sequence model is not needed to answer this fixed-capacity question; it would be reconsidered only if verified dated multi-event histories and a distinct clinically meaningful estimand became available. These are evidence-bound deferrals, not blanket exclusions of structured data, neural methods, GPUs, or mechanistic models.
