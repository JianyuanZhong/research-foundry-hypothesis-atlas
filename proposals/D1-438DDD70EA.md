# Observation-adjusted partial identification of early post-ICU escalation

## Scientific deliverable

The solver must newly fit and audit a leakage-safe MIMIC-IV 3.1 first-ICU-discharge study. The primary deliverable is an observation-aware, partially identified estimate of whether a prespecified pre-discharge recovery pattern changes the interval for early post-discharge ICU-level escalation after accounting for the recorded discharge process. This is not a clinical policy recommendation.

Required outputs are `run_manifest.json`; `cohort_audit.parquet`; `hourly_features.parquet`; `topology_audit.parquet`; `partial_identification.parquet`; `process_adjustment.parquet`; `decision_sensitivity.parquet`; `predictions.parquet`; `calibration.parquet`; `uncertainty.parquet`; `falsification.parquet`; and `interpretation.md`. The manifest must record catalog [source checksum], MIMIC snapshot `[source checksum]`, source hashes, archive members, schema hashes, item-label checks, split seeds, bridge/tie rules, sensitivity grid, software/resource versions and output hashes. Every interpretation claim must map to output rows and be labelled supportive, adverse or inconclusive.

## Unresolved question and hypothesis

The strongest available claim is feasibility, not an association: a prior read-only audit found 52,591 eligible first ICU outtimes, 2,661 ICU returns, 2,060 returns with a documented non-ICU bridge, and 601 without a uniquely documented bridge. This supports an interval estimand, not a physiologic conclusion, causal effect, or discharge rule.

The unresolved question is whether apparent recovery immediately before ICU discharge identifies a clinically meaningful difference in early escalation risk after documentation intensity and discharge topology are separated from physiology.

Primary falsifiable hypothesis: among adults at a valid first ICU outtime, a prespecified recent-versus-durable recovery contrast—active/recently withdrawn versus source-concordant support-free evidence in respiratory and vasoactive domains—changes the 48-hour cumulative-incidence interval for latent ward-to-ICU escalation after process adjustment. Support requires a held-out, calibrated adjusted contrast whose confidence interval excludes zero across the prespecified bridge and unrecorded-support sensitivity grid. The directional expectation is higher escalation bounds with active or recently withdrawn support, but a null or reversed result is informative.

The lower endpoint is a first ICU return following a uniquely documented non-ICU transfer interval. The upper endpoint is any first ICU return after index outtime, including immediate or ambiguous topology. Death before return and alive hospital discharge before return are competing first events. Same-timestamp ties use a locked primary rule plus sensitivity rules. A hard confirmed/unconfirmed clinical interpretation is not permitted. This advances the parent by testing whether the signal survives process adjustment and by quantifying irreducible endpoint ambiguity.

## Analysis, baseline, alternative and evaluation

First calculate Aalen-Johansen competing-event CIFs and nonparametric lower/upper bounds, including widths and recent-minus-durable contrasts. Fit a transparent discrete-time cause-specific competing-hazard model with one-hour post-landmark bins, exposure states, pre-landmark observation counts and last-observation times, discharge destination/timing, demographics, stay duration and prespecified pre-outtime case-mix variables. Standardize to the primary cohort; bootstrap patients, not rows; report confidence intervals and overlap/selection diagnostics. This is prognostic adjustment, not causal control.

The substantive alternative is a constrained factorized observation-aware hidden semi-Markov model. One latent state represents respiratory/hemodynamic recovery duration and another observation reliability; emissions use exactly the same hourly values and masks as the baseline, with competing event hazards and explicit bridge-misclassification parameters. Fit only on training patients, calibrate on validation patients, and evaluate once on a locked patient-level test split. Report posterior states and sensitivity envelopes, but do not treat latent labels as ground truth or use the HSMM to narrow nonparametric bounds without an assumption audit.

Compare methods on held-out IPCW Brier score, cause-specific calibration, interval coverage/width under simulated bridge misclassification, and stability of standardized bounds across supported time strata. The scientific comparison is whether duration and observation structure explain a robust clinically relevant contrast; a small AUC gain is insufficient. The HSMM may reveal persistence and a separate measurement-reliability state that fixed summaries lose. If it fails calibration, state stability or falsification, defer it in favor of the baseline.

Prespecified falsifications: reverse temporal order and post-landmark leakage; shuffled/outcome-independent masks; source-channel ablations; 6/12/24-hour windows; bridge gaps q=0/2/6/12 hours; immediate-return exclusion; aggregate-return comparator; pseudo-landmarks with no first-hour outcome; negative-control alarm/chart domains; alternate tie and destination rules; and complete-case versus explicit uninformative-absence strata. A claim fails if it appears only with post-index data, one arbitrary bridge gap, or coding unobserved support as cessation.

A threshold/decision-sensitivity file may sweep alert thresholds and plausible cost ratios, but no clinical net benefit or policy recommendation is allowed because harms, utility weights, intervention assignment and discharge intent are unavailable.

## Interpretation and limits

Supportive evidence is a calibrated held-out adjusted contrast excluding zero for the lower endpoint and stable direction across the upper endpoint and sensitivity frontier without falsification failure. This supports prognostic/measurement evidence only. Adverse evidence is a null/reversed contrast, bounds overlapping clinically meaningful effects, or collapse after process/mask adjustment; it refutes robustness, not biology. Inconclusive evidence is poor overlap, sparse states, wide bounds, poor calibration, unstable latent fits, or unresolved death/discharge ascertainment.

The dataset cannot adjudicate planned versus unplanned returns, bedside readiness, treatment limitations, actual ward deterioration before documentation, clinician intent, outside care, or whether an alert changes treatment. External temporal/site validation, clinical review of transfer trajectories and a prospective/target-trial study are required before operational use.

## Compute and deferred alternatives

The feasibility counts above are measured audit evidence; no full model fit is claimed. Baseline extraction/fitting is expected to use 4-8 CPU cores and 16-32 GiB RAM for under two hours, unverified. HSMM and bootstrap/sensitivity grid are unverified; plan 8 CPU cores/32 GiB and up to four hours, with one allocated A100-80GB only if profiling shows repeated latent fitting is the bottleneck. Final solver limits in `inputs.json` control execution, and feature/checkpoint files must be preserved.

A full transformer/Delphi-style model is deferred because endpoint nonidentifiability, not sequence capacity, is the main uncertainty; revisit only if the HSMM fails to represent important duration structure and external validation is available. Causal treatment-policy/net-benefit analysis is deferred because intervention, harms and utilities are absent. eICU replication is desirable but its transfer semantics are not interchangeable. The three demonstrations were considered: structured generative longitudinal modeling is a possible later sensitivity; Oncoformer’s imaging modality is unavailable; the Bayesian latent-trajectory idea is adapted only as an EHR-only HSMM with changed assumptions.
