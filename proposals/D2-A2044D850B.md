# MerTK cell-context therapeutic-window hypothesis — episode 7 donor-aware revision

**Status: proposed and untested.** No Harbor analysis, model fit, or wet-lab intervention has been executed. The clinically important hypothesis remains that MERTK inhibition may reduce inflammatory macrophage-to-fibroblast-like synoviocyte (FLS) signaling in an inflammatory context while harming MerTK-associated resolution functions (including efferocytosis and FLS repair) in another context. A context-specific functional window could motivate cell-restricted preclinical development; it does not justify broad systemic inhibition or clinical use.

## Evidence, uncertainty, and decision

Published work associates MerTK-positive synovial macrophage states with rheumatoid arthritis remission and FLS repair [K1]; TAM-family homeostatic control of innate inflammation [K2] and local matrix/niche effects on synovial inflammation [K3] add biological plausibility. This evidence supports association and rationale, not causal benefit, selectivity, target engagement, or safety. The strongest rival is that reported inhibitor-linked changes arise from off-target/TAM-family activity, uncertain or implausibly high exposure, wash/carry-over, toxicity, donor/state imbalance, or cell-label/readout mismatch rather than MERTK inhibition.

The decisive future observation is concordance between verified pharmacologic MERTK engagement and orthogonal MERTK loss, with guide-resistant WT but not kinase-dead rescue restoring function, across donor-crossed cell contexts. Measure inflammatory benefit separately from macrophage efferocytosis/resolution, FLS matrix repair, and viability; include AXL/TYRO3, vehicle, toxicity, and wash/carry-over controls. Support would justify another translational test only. Drug-only effects or absent genetic/rescue concordance favor compound/exposure artifacts. Broad repair/efferocytosis harm despite anti-inflammatory effects weakens the target rationale. Failed engagement, inadequate donor precision, or unresolved dose/mapping is inconclusive. Expert review must set meaningful margins and validate assays; configured expression datasets cannot resolve these outcomes.

## Harbor-computable archive check (not a therapeutic-window test)

The computable question is narrowly whether the six historical E-MTAB-8316 configurations show a coherent *downstream FLS transcript pattern* after inhibitor exposure of LPS-pretreated macrophages. It is not an independent replication or target-validation study. RNA comes from RA FLS on transwell inserts after macrophage pretreatment (4 h LPS, final 2 h inhibitor), wash, and 48 h coculture; it is not macrophage RNA or a functional/time-course measurement.

These are six configurations but only four FLS donor clusters; SA049 and SA114 each recur. The six monocyte donors are nested and must not be joined to similarly named IDs in other accessions. Missing/ambiguous feature mapping fails that score; never substitute genes. Report all six configuration-level paired deltas visibly grouped by FLS donor, each feature and score, QC/normalization, mean/median, leave-one-configuration-out ranges, uncertainty, and secondary multiplicity-controlled gene contrasts. Also aggregate repeated configurations within FLS donor/condition and show four-cluster summaries plus leave-one-FLS-donor-out sensitivity. Because only four FLS clusters exist, treat inferential precision as weak; nominal six-configuration t/bootstrap intervals are conditional descriptive summaries, not population-level evidence. Do not claim six independent donor replications.

**Supportive archive result:** QC and mapping pass and the predeclared direction appears coherent across configurations and donor-cluster summaries. This supports only a description of this historical FLS-RNA pattern and data/QC feasibility; it does not advance or validate MERTK as a target. A five-of-six sign pattern is descriptive, not a significance rule, independent replication, or go/no-go threshold. **Adverse archive result:** a coherent reverse pattern weakens this historical transcript-pattern rationale only; it does not falsify MERTK function or the prospective therapeutic-window hypothesis. **Inconclusive:** ambiguous sample/feature mapping, dose uncertainty, failed QC, discordance between repeated-donor and configuration summaries, or wide/unstable uncertainty. Do not convert uncertainty to evidence of absence.

## Same-question methods and compute

Baseline is the prespecified fixed-score donor-aware descriptive analysis above. A substantive alternative is a partially pooled FLS random-slope model of the same score contrasts, with FLS donor as the grouping unit, weakly regularizing slope-SD prior, robust residual and prior-sensitivity/posterior-predictive checks. Its possible added information is shrinkage and cautious FLS-response heterogeneity, which the six unpooled configurations obscure. However four FLS clusters (two singleton) weakly identify slope variance; each monocyte donor contributes one configuration and its variance is confounded with residual. It cannot create replication or identify mechanism, so defer as inferential primary; if explored, label prior sensitivity only. Both methods use the same exact six configurations, outcomes, and no held-out independent cohort exists. No GPU is warranted for this small tabular analysis. CPU budget estimate: under 4 cores, 8 GiB, under 10 minutes (planning estimate, not measured); future prospective assay workload is unestimated and depends on donor/assay availability. Solver must newly compute and save mappings, normalized score tables, per-configuration and four-donor summaries, uncertainty/sensitivity, QC/exclusions, and machine-readable outcome classification. Verifier can check joins, calculations, donor grouping, reported uncertainty, and whether conclusions follow; it cannot establish target engagement, assay validity, or clinical adjudication.
