## Scientific opening, strongest supported claim, unresolved hypothesis, and advance

The strongest inspected evidence supports three bounded claims. Large eGFRcys-minus-eGFRcr differences are common in UK Biobank and have kidney and non-GFR predictors [K1]. In SPRINT, that difference was associated with frailty and broad adverse outcomes, and the authors cautioned that a combined equation can discard prognostic information [K2]. Administrative AKI coding can be insensitive and subgroup-dependent relative to serum-creatinine-defined AKI [K3]. These works do not establish that equal-combined-eGFR negative discordance selectively predicts biological AKI, that UKB first appearances are encounters or onset dates, or that latent recording scores identify contact.

The frozen unresolved hypothesis is: among supported P− and P0 profiles at the same combined eGFR, P− has a larger 10-year contrast for first recorded N17 than for first recorded N18 after measured baseline characteristics and pre-index first-appearance history are represented. Define
`theta17=log RR10(N17-first; P−/P0)`, `theta18=log RR10(N18-first; P−/P0)`, and `D=theta17−theta18`, standardized over the same eligible cohort.

The leading bounded explanation is recorded acute-renal selectivity. The strongest rival is stable generalized morbidity/recording propensity: a participant with persistently more discovery dates or denser same-day first appearances may later receive more N17 and N18 coding. They differ observably only if pre-index history predicts later recording well and the N17-versus-N18 contrast is robust to that measured process. Neither model can separate morbidity, healthcare contact, testing, admission severity, coding behavior, or biological onset because those variables are absent.

The substantive advance over [K1]–[K3] is not a new predictor. It is a fixed-combined-eGFR, joint competing-transition experiment that first decides whether N17-versus-N18 is precise enough to be informative, then compares a transparent multistate baseline against a pre-index-only, partially pooled shared-frailty model without pretending that two participant-specific latent dimensions are estimable from sparse histories.

## Substantive alternative: one-factor shared recording frailty with posterior integration

The parent’s correlated `u_i/v_i` model asks whether discovery frequency and conditional bundle density are separate stable person traits. Exact prior support showed median early and late discovery-date counts of zero and only 3,910/19,818 P− and 1,902/11,982 P0 participants with dates in both windows. Two individual random effects and their correlation therefore cannot be identified for most participants without prior-dominated scores. Cross-fitting prevents leakage but does not create within-person information. Do not report two person-level factors or interpret their correlation.

Instead fit one shared pre-index recording frailty `b_i ~ Normal(0,sigma_b^2)`, with partial pooling:

1. A yearly piecewise-exponential recurrent nonrenal discovery-date intensity on `[index−10 years,index)` has loading 1 on `b_i`, calendar/age/sex/clinical fixed effects, observed-history offset, and an explicit history-availability process.
2. Conditional bundle size on each discovery date follows a zero-truncated negative-binomial model with estimated loading `lambda_mark*b_i` and the same fixed-effect specificity.
3. Death is absorbing. The renal multistate model is otherwise identical to the baseline and estimates outcome-specific loadings on the frozen conditional distribution `p(b_i | pre-index history)`.
4. Integrate over that conditional distribution by adaptive quadrature or >=50 deterministic posterior draws; never insert an empirical-Bayes point score as known. A participant with no history retains the covariate-conditioned population distribution and a no-history indicator.
5. If `lambda_mark` is unstable or its 95% interval includes both a negligible loading and a loading that changes expected bundle size by >=25% per frailty SD, use the prespecified intensity-only frailty and retain transparent bundle summaries. Do not revive a second latent factor.

This alternative preserves all pre-index event times and marks, propagates sparse-history uncertainty, and can show whether one persistent measured recording tendency predicts both frequency and density beyond scalar summaries. It loses the parent model’s nominal separation of two latent traits, but that separation was unsupported. It still cannot identify contact, admission severity, coding intent, or morbidity as the cause of `b_i`.

Fit population process parameters marker-blind in training; choose between fixed-effects-only, intensity-only frailty, and shared frequency/mark frailty on validation; evaluate once on test. Before opening any profile effect, require: yearly observed/expected date counts 0.8–1.25 in >=8/10 years; zero-history calibration within 5 percentage points in both no-history/history-support strata; conditional bundle mean and 90th-quantile error <=20% or <=1 group; no integrated-Brier degradation >0.01 versus baseline for any sufficiently supported pooled N17/N18/death target; converged quadrature; and stable `sigma_b`/loading estimates under early/late window deletion. Process failure is diagnostic failure, not renal evidence.

After freezing the process choice, create five-fold cross-fitted posterior summaries and quadrature nodes using only each participant’s pre-index history and population parameters fit without that fold. Fit the alternative renal model on the full cohort and in matched data; bootstrap the entire process fit, cross-fitting, matching, renal fit, and standardization. Conditioning on baseline recording history changes the standardized contrast and may block morbidity pathways. Call it a robustness contrast, never a causal direct effect.
