# Robustness of time-weighted hypotension burden for early AKI: summary choice, measurement-density and baseline-severity controls

## Scientific question

Among adult ICU patients with adequate systolic and diastolic blood-pressure measurements in the first 24 hours of ICU care, does a prespecified time-weighted hypotension burden measure improve prediction of acute kidney injury (AKI) within 48 hours beyond the 24-hour mean arterial pressure (MAP), after controlling for measurement density, baseline illness severity, and after showing that the signal is robust to the choice of blood-pressure summary (MAP, systolic, diastolic, combined)?

The unresolved claim is that duration and time-weighting of hypotension add clinically meaningful prognostic information beyond MAP, after accounting for baseline illness severity, vasopressor exposure, baseline kidney function, hospital, and measurement density. The strongest rival is that the apparent burden signal is an artifact of recording density, measurement timing, or baseline illness severity, or that it is specific to one arbitrary choice of blood-pressure summary. A null result would weaken the claim that a more complex burden measure is useful; a positive result would support further validation and prospective study.

## Why it matters

MAP is the common bedside summary and a natural baseline for hemodynamic risk. Minimum systolic blood pressure alone can overstate a single low reading, while MAP may understate sustained hypoperfusion. If time-weighted burden improves early AKI risk stratification, it may inform monitoring and resuscitation decisions. If it does not, clinicians and researchers should not add a more complex measure without evidence.

## Population and temporal design

Use eICU 2.0 adult ICU admissions with at least one systolic and one diastolic blood-pressure measurement in the first 24 hours after ICU admission. Exclude admissions with fewer than 10 blood-pressure measurements in the first 24 hours, because sparse recording makes burden estimates unstable. Define the exposure window as the first 24 hours after ICU admission. Define the outcome as AKI within 48 hours after ICU admission, using KDIGO criteria based on serum creatinine and urine output when available.

## Variables and data bindings

- Patient table: `patient` with `subject_id`, `hospital_id`, `icu_admission_time`, `age`, `sex`, `race`, `ethnicity`, `insurance`, `admission_type`, `admission_location`, `admission_diagnosis`, `disposition`.
- Vital table: `vital` with `subject_id`, `hospital_id`, `icu_admission_time`, `time`, `systolic_bp`, `diastolic_bp`, `heart_rate`, `resp_rate`, `temperature`, `spo2`.
- Lab table: `lab` with `subject_id`, `hospital_id`, `icu_admission_time`, `time`, `component`, `value`, `value_uom`, `reference_range_lower`, `reference_range_upper`.
- Infusion table: `infusion` with `subject_id`, `hospital_id`, `icu_admission_time`, `time`, `infusion_name`, `rate`, `rate_uom`, `total_volume`.
- Hospital table: `hospital` with `hospital_id`, `hospital_type`, `bed_count`, `region`.

Join all tables on `subject_id` and `hospital_id`, and use `icu_admission_time` as the reference time for all windows. Use `time` in the vital, lab, and infusion tables to define the 24-hour exposure and 48-hour outcome windows.

## Exposure and outcome definitions

- MAP = (systolic_bp + 2*diastolic_bp) / 3.
- Hypotension burden = time-weighted fraction of the first 24 hours with MAP < 65 mmHg, computed by linear interpolation between consecutive valid measurements and censored at the last measurement.
- Baseline severity = APACHE IV score computed from the first 24 hours, with SOFA score as a fallback when APACHE IV inputs are missing.
- Baseline kidney function = minimum serum creatinine in the first 24 hours.
- Vasopressor exposure = total time on vasopressor infusion in the first 24 hours.
- Measurement density = number of blood-pressure measurements in the first 24 hours.
- AKI outcome = KDIGO stage 1, 2, or 3 AKI within 48 hours, defined by an increase in serum creatinine from baseline or by urine-output criteria when available.

## Analysis

1. Fit a logistic regression model with MAP as the sole predictor, adjusted for baseline severity, vasopressor exposure, baseline kidney function, hospital, and measurement density.
2. Fit a logistic regression model with time-weighted hypotension burden as the sole predictor, adjusted for the same covariates.
3. Compare the two models using AUC, calibration (Brier score and calibration-in-the-large), and decision-curve analysis.
4. Repeat the analysis using systolic BP, diastolic BP, and a combined summary (MAP + systolic + diastolic) to test robustness to the choice of blood-pressure summary.
5. Stratify by baseline severity (APACHE IV quartiles) and by measurement density (tertiles) to test whether the burden signal is robust to baseline illness severity and recording density.
6. Perform a missing-severity sensitivity analysis by restricting to patients with complete APACHE IV inputs.
7. Report hospital-level heterogeneity by fitting the models within each hospital and by including a hospital random effect.

## Falsification criteria

The burden signal is considered falsified if:
- The burden model does not improve AUC or calibration beyond the MAP model after adjustment.
- The signal disappears in any severity stratum or measurement-density stratum.
- The signal is specific to one blood-pressure summary and does not replicate under systolic, diastolic, or combined summaries.
- The signal is driven by a small number of hospitals or by patients with sparse recording.

## Interpretation

A positive result would support the claim that time-weighted hypotension burden adds prognostic information beyond MAP, after accounting for baseline illness severity and measurement artifacts. A null result would weaken the claim that a more complex burden measure is useful. An inconclusive result would require further validation in a prospective cohort with continuous blood-pressure monitoring.
