# Proposal: Boundary-validated observed first-stop prognosis and prospective enrichment after failed action positivity

## Why this successor is needed

The bedside question remains consequential: when an adult receiving low-dose concurrent norepinephrine (NE) and vasopressin (VP) is stable enough that one infusion is actually stopped, which patients will remain hemodynamically stable, and does the observed first drug stopped carry prognostic information beyond pre-stop state?

The selected parents establish two facts that must not be conflated. First, public evidence supports equipoise rather than a universal ordering rule. Earlier observational syntheses suggested less hypotension when NE was stopped first, while a 2025 weighted cohort was null. The current 2026 systematic review of 11 studies (2 randomized, 9 observational; 2,280 patients) found no overall hypotension difference (OR 0.43, 95% CI 0.18–1.03); observational studies favored NE-first (OR 0.26, 95% CI 0.13–0.53; I2 88%), whereas randomized trials favored VP-first (OR 4.04 for hypotension under NE-first, 95% CI 1.24–13.15; I2 65%) and no other outcome differed (`[source checksum]`, DOI `10.62675/2965-2774.20260197`). This discordance is stronger evidence against causal extrapolation from retrospective ordering than for either strategy.

Second, the completed full-table, genuinely pre-action audit found 6,040 eligible hourly landmarks but only 211 NE-first actions (3.49%) and 167 VP-first actions (2.76%) within the frozen 60-minute grace period, below the prespecified 5% positivity gate. Therefore MIMIC-IV cannot honestly support the assigned-strategy target-trial contrast without backward-indexing, post-treatment selection, or data-adaptive relaxation. That negative feasibility finding is final for that design.

The strongest claim presently supported is thus: documented discontinuation order is measurable, proximal post-stop instability is common and mostly observable, but a randomized-strategy effect is not identified by the frozen MIMIC landmark design. The unresolved, weaker and computable claim is prognostic: among patients at a documented first stop, can strictly pre-stop variables identify a reproducible low-risk group for 6-hour failure, and does the observed first-stop drug add transportable predictive information? This is not a treatment recommendation. Its clinical advance is to define and externally temporally validate an enrichment rule for a future randomized discontinuation study, while quantifying exactly where chart boundaries and endpoints fail.

## Frozen population, time zero, and outcomes

Use one episode per patient: the first documented qualifying NE/VP stop in the first ICU stay of each adult `subject_id`. Reconstruct positive administration intervals symmetrically from `icu/inputevents`; require at least six continuous hours of NE/VP overlap, valid supported doses, and the comparator active at the boundary. A true stop has no positive row beginning within 10 minutes. Bridge a gap of at most 5 minutes only with continuing `orderid` or `linkorderid` and no explicit stopped/paused/cancelled status. Exclude stops within 5 minutes of one another as simultaneous. Never require future absence or no restart. Time zero is the recorded end time of the first-stopped drug.

Eligibility uses only values at or before time zero: adult age; alive and in the ICU; NE 0–0.20 mcg/kg/min and VP 0–0.04 U/min at one second before stop and 30 minutes earlier; neither dose rising during that interval; no epinephrine, phenylephrine, or dopamine overlapping the preceding 2 hours; at least three valid MAP values in at least three of four 30-minute bins during the prior 2 hours, one in the last 30 minutes, median MAP at least 65 and no MAP below 55. Exclude an active mechanical-circulatory-support proxy in the preceding 24 hours using the frozen dictionary mapping, but report proxy-specific attrition because chart/procedure records do not adjudicate actual device activity. Do not require septic-shock coding, lactate testing, future ICU residence, future MAP, or survival.

The primary outcome is unfavorable status by 6 hours after stop: first of death; restart/re-initiation of the stopped study agent; new epinephrine, phenylephrine, or dopamine; NE dose at least 0.05 mcg/kg/min above its pre-stop dose or NE re-initiation; two MAP values below 60 in distinct 30-minute bins separated by 5–120 minutes; or ICU exit with an active pressor, prior rescue/failure, or an infusion-continuation signal. Alive exit off all pressors with no prior failure is success. A patient otherwise failure-free but without at least four represented post-stop 30-minute MAP bins and a MAP in hours 4–6 is endpoint-indeterminate. Report both (a) the monitoring-independent rescue/death/pressor-at-exit composite for everyone and (b) the full endpoint among determinate episodes. Components and an ordinal severity outcome are mandatory.

## Completed feasibility and the decision it supports

A no-sampling scan of the exact selected MIMIC-IV 3.1 source members produced 16,341 study-pressor stays, 10,460 adult first-ICU stays, 922 observed-stop candidates after structural/dose/competing-pressor criteria, 138 after the pre-stop MAP rule, and 137 after mechanical-support proxy exclusion. There were 84 NE-first and 53 VP-first episodes; 135/137 had adequate post-stop MAP and 136/137 had a determinate endpoint. Seventy-three had observed failure (59 restarts, 2 competing pressors, 19 NE escalations, 20 MAP failures; components overlap). Crude determinate failure was 49/83 after NE-first and 24/53 after VP-first, but this unadjusted contrast is selection-confounded and must not be described as a drug effect. The first-stop boundary had explicit stop/pause/cancel evidence in 83/84 NE-first and 51/53 VP-first episodes. These calculations are recorded in `observed_stop_endpoint_feasibility.py`, `observed_stop_endpoint_feasibility.json`, and `observed_stop_cohort.parquet` and are feasibility evidence, not clinical adjudication.

Because only 137 episodes remain, the primary study is a parsimonious prognosis/transportability experiment, not a flexible causal or high-dimensional machine-learning analysis. The intended bedside use is trial enrichment: identify a stable subgroup with sufficiently low predicted baseline failure that randomizing discontinuation order is ethically and operationally plausible, while retaining an outcome rate adequate for a trial.

## Prespecified prediction and transportability experiment

Predict 6-hour unfavorable status at the instant immediately before observed stop. The reference model is intercept-only. The clinical baseline model uses age (restricted spline with at most 3 knots), pre-stop NE dose, VP dose, overlap duration, median MAP, minimum MAP, MAP slope, MAP observation count, heart rate summary, mechanical ventilation, and missingness indicators for most recent pre-stop lactate and creatinine. A deliberately small enhanced model adds pre-stop lactate value/slope, creatinine, urine output, fluid balance, steroid exposure, ventilation/RRT status, care unit, hour/shift, and calendar group. No post-stop value, status, restart, boundary status, discharge code, future coverage, or observed first-stop drug enters these models.

Then add one binary term for observed NE-first versus VP-first and test its incremental prognostic value. This coefficient is an associational marker of clinician action and latent state, not a treatment effect; no counterfactual risk under switching order may be reported. An arm interaction may be explored only after validation and with shrinkage; it cannot define a treatment-benefit subgroup.

Use penalized logistic regression with penalty chosen within training data only. Avoid tree ensembles unless the effective event count and calibration remain adequate. Validate with grouped bootstrap optimism correction by `subject_id` and a frozen temporal transport test: train on earlier `anchor_year_group` groups and test on the latest group containing at least 30 episodes and 10 failures; if that gate cannot be met, use leave-one-anchor-year-group-out validation and label temporal transport untested. Also perform leave-one-care-unit-out validation. Report AUROC, AUPRC, Brier score, calibration intercept/slope, observed-to-expected ratio, calibration plots, and 95% bootstrap intervals. Compare the clinical baseline, enhanced, and action-added models by out-of-sample Brier score and calibration, not only discrimination.

A candidate prospective-enrichment threshold is selected only inside training folds to target predicted failure risk below 35%, then frozen in each validation fold. Report validation-fold low-risk count, observed failure risk and exact interval, sensitivity, specificity, and proportion retained. The enrichment rule is considered provisionally transportable only if pooled validation includes at least 40 low-risk episodes, upper 95% confidence bound for low-risk failure is below 50%, calibration slope is 0.7–1.3, calibration-in-the-large is within ±0.10 on the probability scale, and no care-unit or calendar holdout has observed failure above 50% unless its interval is too imprecise to distinguish that threshold. These are design-planning gates, not proof of safety.

The observed action term is clinically useful only if it improves validation Brier score by at least 0.01, does not worsen calibration, and has consistent direction across temporal and care-unit holdouts. Even then it is interpreted as evidence that the charted choice encodes otherwise unmeasured clinician information, not that one drug should be stopped. If the pre-stop model transports but the action term does not, the output is a strategy-neutral enrichment rule for prospective randomization. If neither model transports, MIMIC supports endpoint/boundary feasibility only and no bedside score should be advanced.

## Boundary validation, falsification, and prospective-study gate

Before interpreting prognostic results, a blinded critical-care pharmacist/clinician must review a stratified sample of at least 25 timelines per observed arm, oversampling non-explicit boundaries and restarts within 30 minutes. The review packet contains only relevant inputevents rows, order/link-order continuity, status/continuation fields, and MAP/rescue chronology; reviewers classify likely true stop, rate/bag transition, transfer continuation, indeterminate, and likely endpoint failure. Require boundary PPV at least 0.80 in each arm, arm PPV difference below 0.10, and endpoint PPV at least 0.80. Automated computation cannot satisfy this gate.

Required falsification and robustness checks are: 0/5/10/15-minute bridging; 5/10/15-minute restart window; simultaneous-stop windows of 2/5/10 minutes; arterial-only MAP; MAP thresholds 55/60/65; rescue-only versus full endpoint; pseudo-outcome of pre-stop instability predicted using the observed action label; and leave-one-unit/year-group analyses. Strong action-label prediction of pre-stop instability is expected evidence of confounding, not model success. Reversal of calibration or enrichment safety across reasonable boundaries, arm-linked MAP density, or failed adjudication makes the study boundary-feasibility-only.

A future prospective randomized/enriched study is warranted if: boundaries and endpoint pass adjudication; the pre-stop model meets transport/calibration gates; at least 40 validated low-risk patients exist with acceptable observed failure; and retrospective data retain clinical equipoise. It should randomize NE-first versus VP-first at a prospectively declared stable decision, record clinician intent and true pump actions, and use the same 6-hour outcome. MIMIC alone can estimate recruitment fractions and event rates but cannot establish efficacy, safety, exchangeability, or a treatment-selection rule. If the enrichment model fails, prospective randomization may still be warranted in an expert-defined population, but this EHR-derived score should not be used.

## Interpretation

Supportive: a calibrated pre-stop model transports across time and units, identifies the prespecified low-risk group, and remains robust to boundaries. This supports only using the phenotype for prospective trial planning. If observed order adds stable predictive value, it indicates residual clinician/action information, not causal benefit.

Adverse: no transportable low-risk group, worse outcomes in purported low-risk patients, boundary PPV failure, or action-label associations that reverse across strata. This falsifies the proposed enrichment rule and strengthens the case for an expert-defined prospective study without an EHR score.

Inconclusive: validation samples or failures below gates, wide intervals crossing 50%, differential monitoring, unstable calibration, or sensitivity reversals. The conclusion is endpoint/boundary feasibility only; neither an ordering recommendation nor a bedside risk score is justified.
