# CBC cancer-type contrast after generic burden and observation selection

Status: proposed substantive child of `[prior hypothesis]`. No cohort scan, model fit, or scientific result has been run in this branch.

## Scientific opening, hypothesis, and advance

The parent establishes a useful but unresolved question: a prespecified higher-platelet/lower-haemoglobin CBC phenotype may have a delayed CRC-versus-haematologic contrast. The strongest evidence currently supports only narrower claims. Nicholson et al. [K1] found blood-test associations with six-month cancer risk in a symptom-defined primary-care population, but also showed that clinical features can be associated with several cancer sites. Sala et al. [K2] reported retrospective CBC trajectories before CRC diagnosis in a CRC-only cohort. Hampton et al. [K3] shows why discrimination without calibration and external/temporal validation is insufficient. None establishes that a UKB contrast is site-specific, persists outside the near-diagnostic band, or survives alternative observation processes.

The strongest unresolved rival is that a delayed CRC-versus-haematologic contrast is caused by generic cancer burden, calendar-era coding/referral changes, registry versus inpatient ascertainment, or the fact that people with repeat observations are selected. The hypothesis is:

> Among UKB adults with a complete first CBC and no source-observed cancer at baseline, the prespecified CBC phenotype produces a positive, clinically material delayed CRC-versus-haematologic absolute-risk separation from T0+2 to T0+5 years that persists in both registry and inpatient ascertainment, remains larger for CRC than for other primary cancers, and is not confined to one calendar era or future-repeat-observation stratum.

This is a descriptive, temporally ordered association hypothesis. It is not a causal, mechanistic, diagnostic-accuracy, stage, treatment, referral-threshold, or deployment claim. The substantive advance is a decision-facing decomposition: it separates an absolute CRC-versus-haematologic contrast from a shared generic-cancer component and tests whether that contrast is stable across two imperfect sources and future-observation selection. A positive result would justify an indication-rich external validation; a negative result would redirect interpretation toward generic burden or capture; an imprecise result would identify missing evidence rather than support action.

### Three inspected works

[K1] Nicholson et al. bounds the question by showing that raised platelets, white-cell count and CRP can mark short-window cancer risk, while normal tests do not rule out cancer and clinical features are not uniquely site-specific. It does not establish a delayed UKB or cancer-type-specific contrast.

[K2] Sala et al. provides a plausible CRC prediagnostic CBC trajectory but is CRC-only, retrospective and not a comparison of cancer classes or ascertainment sources. This child adds those comparisons.

[K3] Hampton et al. motivates calibrated absolute risk, temporal validation and uncertainty because predictive discrimination alone is not enough. This child makes those outputs part of the estimand decision.

## Population and temporal boundaries

Create one common source-clean cohort so source comparisons do not change baseline eligibility.

1. Include one row per unique nonmissing `eid) with parseable T0 and complete instance-0 WBC, haemoglobin, RDW and platelets.
2. Exclude participants with a valid qualifying registry or inpatient event on or before T0, or death on or before T0. Report registry-only, inpatient-only and intersection exclusions separately.
3. Do not require T1/T2 for primary entry.
4. For the primary delayed risk set, additionally require alive and free of qualifying cancer in both sources through T0+2 years. This defines a conditional T0+2-to-T0+5 estimand; events before T0+2 are exclusions from that delayed risk set, never delayed events.
5. Follow each source separately to its earliest CRC, haematologic, other-primary, death, administrative censoring at 2022-12-31, or the window boundary. Source discordance is reported, not silently reconciled.

CRC is ICD-10 C18-C20; haematologic cancer is C81-C96; other primary malignant cancer is C00-C97 excluding C18-C20, C81-C96, and secondary C77-C79. Preserve invalid and unpaired observations in the audit.

Primary windows are (T0,T0+2 years] and (T0+2 years,T0+5 years], using exact dates and a stated day-boundary convention. The timing falsification partitions (T0,T0+90 days] and (T0+90 days,T0+2 years]. A future repeat stratum is R=1 when T1>T0, T1 is within 2-8 years, and all four instance-1 CBC fields are observed; otherwise R=0. R is never a baseline predictor.

## Decision-facing estimand and falsifications

For source s in {R=registry, I=inpatient}, class c in {CRC, HEME, OTHER, DEATH}, and window w, estimate standardized competing-risk cumulative incidence P_s(c,g,w) under two fixed development-defined profiles:

- g_CRC: platelets +1 development SD and haemoglobin -1 development SD;
- g_HEME: platelets -1 development SD and haemoglobin +1 development SD;
- WBC and RDW are at development means; observed age, sex, BMI, smoking, CRP and T0 year are retained for each person.

All standardization is over the same temporal holdout risk set. The primary decision-facing output is:

`E_s(w) = 1000 * ([P_s(CRC,g_CRC,w)-P_s(CRC,g_HEME,w)] - [P_s(HEME,g_CRC,w)-P_s(HEME,g_HEME,w)])`.

Report E_s as excess CRC-minus-haematologic cases per 1,000 comparable adults, its 95% interval, and all four component risks. The prespecified descriptive materiality boundary is 10 per 1,000 (1 percentage point); this is not a referral threshold. Also report the parent relative contrast D_s(w), and the source difference E_R-E_I.

To test generic cancer burden, report `Delta_s,c(w)=P_s(c,g_CRC,w)-P_s(c,g_HEME,w)` for all three cancer classes, `G_s=mean(Delta_s,CRC,Delta_s,HEME,Delta_s,OTHER)`, and `S_s=Delta_s,CRC-mean(Delta_s,HEME,Delta_s,OTHER)`. G is a shared ascertainment-associated component, not severity; S is a CRC-specific residual, not a causal effect. If other-cancer Delta is comparable to CRC Delta, a strong source-specific prediction does not support CRC specificity.

Prespecified checks:

1. Fit registry and inpatient analyses separately on the common source-clean population. Do not pool dates or choose a favorable source.
2. For same-class events captured in both sources, report n, median, IQR, 5th/95th percentile and fractions crossing 90 days, 2 years and 5 years for inpatient date minus registry date. Fewer than 50 paired events makes source-displacement interpretation inconclusive.
3. Report cross-source capture in both directions, stratified by development CBC score deciles and WBC/CRP severity strata. This tests severity-dependent capture, not which source is biologically correct.
4. Report 0-90 days, 90 days-2 years and 2-5 years. Early-only concentration supports proximity/ascertainment; persistence is compatible with, but does not prove, a delayed association.
5. Use three fixed T0-era bins defined by development-cohort tertiles of T0 year, then apply the same cut-points to holdout, restricted to participants with the relevant complete follow-up. Report era-specific E, CRC/HEME/OTHER Delta and interaction uncertainty. No era cut is chosen after viewing outcomes.
6. Report E separately for R=1 and R=0. Fit a development-only observation model `Pr(R=1 | T0 CBC, age, sex, BMI, smoking, CRP, T0 year)`; use stabilized inverse-probability weights as a sensitivity, report overlap, weight tails and effective sample size. Poor positivity is inconclusive, not a reason to trim toward a preferred result.
7. In development only, permute CRC and HEME labels while preserving dates, censoring and source; the type contrast should collapse toward zero. This is an implementation/specificity check, not a biological null.
8. Report pair integrity, duplicate IDs, impossible date order and missingness by source and era. A source can share coding errors with the other source, so concordance is compatibility with robustness, not proof of truth.

## Cox baseline versus learned competing-risk alternative

The transparent baseline is four source-specific cause-specific Cox models (CRC, HEME, OTHER and DEATH), using standardized WBC, haemoglobin, RDW and platelets plus age, sex, BMI, smoking, CRP and T0 year. Complete-case covariates are required after cohort screening; missingness denominators are reported and missing is not treated as normal. Convert predictions to model-standardized cumulative incidence with competing death and report proportional-hazard diagnostics and robust intervals.

The substantive same-input alternative is a CPU gradient-boosted discrete-time multi-task competing-risk model, fitted independently for registry and inpatient sources, with annual intervals and a separate first-90-day interval. It uses the identical people, source-clean exclusions, dates, four CBC fields, baseline covariates, event classes and fixed profiles. It can reveal nonlinear CBC ranges, interactions and time-varying source/class hazards that additive Cox may miss. It cannot reveal symptoms, test indication, pathology, true onset, mechanism or source correctness.

Use a locked temporal split: earliest 70% of T0 dates for development and latest 30% for holdout; each `eid` appears in one split. Development alone determines scaling, complete-case rules, tuning, observation weights and calibration. On the holdout report target-specific calibration intercept/slope, calibration plots, 2- and 5-year competing-risk Brier and log scores, all profile risks, E, Delta, G, S, D and source differences. Use 500 participant-bootstrap intervals for standardized holdout outputs; report Cox robust intervals separately. AUC/Uno C-index is secondary. Fewer than 50 held-out HEME events makes the learned type-specific comparison inconclusive; do not change the split or endpoint.

The learned alternative is retained because it tests a substantive information-loss question—nonlinearity, interactions and time-varying hazards—not because a small ranking gain is clinically valuable. A trajectory transformer/state-space model is deferred: only two broadly complete repeat CBC assessments are available, and latent trajectory assumptions cannot adjudicate indication or source timing. The future repeat flag is excluded from both primary models because it is leakage.

Approximate future solver resources: 8-16 CPU cores, 32-64 GiB RAM and 2-4 hours including selected-column parsing, two source-stratified fits, bridge tables and 500-bootstrap outputs; this is an unmeasured estimate. The inherited planning envelope is up to 16 CPUs, 262,144 MiB and 28,800 seconds. GPU is not required for this tabular workload; a GPU would be revisited only if a measured implementation demonstrated material feasibility or scientific benefit. This branch performs no fit.

## Interpretation and falsification criteria

Supportive, source-robust evidence requires all of the following when event and positivity thresholds are met: (a) E_R and E_I are positive with 95% intervals excluding zero and lower bounds above 10 per 1,000; (b) no source reversal and |E_R-E_I| <=10 per 1,000; (c) S is positive in both sources and other-cancer Delta is less than 75% of CRC Delta in each source when CRC Delta is positive; (d) the contrast is not early-only, confined to one era, or confined to R=1; (e) weighted and unweighted estimates agree within uncertainty with adequate overlap; (f) label permutation collapses; and (g) the learned model is calibrated and does not change the substantive decision classification solely through ranking.

Adverse evidence is a precise delayed reversal, source reversal, large source discrepancy, inpatient signal with large G and small S, comparable other-cancer Delta, early-only concentration, era reversal, R=1 confinement, large weighting shift, or a learned-only ranking gain without calibrated absolute-risk separation. This weakens a stable CRC-specific interpretation but does not refute a generic CBC-cancer association.

Inconclusive evidence includes fewer than 50 paired source events, fewer than 50 held-out HEME events, broad intervals spanning both zero and the 10-per-1,000 boundary, poor positivity, invalid pairing/date parsing, nonconvergence, or inadequate event support in an era/stratum. An imprecise null is not refutation. If Cox and learned models disagree, report the disagreement and calibration; do not select the favorable model.

Computationally checkable outputs are source bindings, participant flow, event counts, temporal bands, E and component risks, Delta/G/S/D, calibration/Brier/log scores, source-displacement and capture tables, era and repeat-selection diagnostics, permutation behavior, and whether the conclusion obeys these rules. Expert adjudication or another study is required for symptoms, CBC indication/order timing, biological onset, cancer-stage/treatment meaning, healthcare-use interpretation, causal mechanism, transportability, and clinical usefulness. A prospective indication-rich external cohort with adjudicated onset and a decision-impact study is required before deployment.
