# Hypotension Duration and Acute Kidney Injury in eICU: A Falsifiable Clinical Hypothesis

## Unresolved Question and Clinical Importance

**Hypothesis:** At the same minimum mean arterial pressure (MAP), patients with longer-lasting hypotension (greater time-weighted hypotension burden) experience more subsequent acute kidney injury (AKI) than patients with shorter-lasting hypotension, after adjustment for illness severity, recording density, and vasopressor exposure.

**Clinical importance:** Hypotension is a leading modifiable contributor to AKI in critically ill patients. Current clinical practice often focuses on the minimum MAP reached (depth) but underappreciates the duration of hypotension (burden). If duration adds independent prognostic information beyond minimum MAP, this would:
1. Justify earlier and more aggressive blood pressure support in patients with sustained (not just deep) hypotension.
2. Inform the design of hemodynamic monitoring and alerting systems in the ICU.
3. Provide a more complete risk stratification tool for AKI prevention.

The question is clinically consequential because the two explanations have different implications for care: if depth alone drives AKI risk, then the priority is avoiding the nadir; if duration matters independently, then the priority is minimizing the time spent below the threshold, even at moderate depths.

## Closest Relevant Prior Work

### [K1] Lee 2026 — Causal Effect of Intraoperative Hypotension Burden on Postoperative AKI
- **Citation:** Lee SB. "Causal Effect and Personalization of Intraoperative Hypotension Burden on Postoperative Acute Kidney Injury: A Doubly Robust Analysis of the VitalDB Cohort." J Pers Med. 2026;16(7):371. DOI: 10.3390/jpm16070371.
- **What it establishes:** Using augmented inverse-probability weighting (AIPW) in a cohort of 2,726 general-anesthesia cases from VitalDB, Lee demonstrated a dose-dependent association between time-integrated MAP<65 mmHg burden (≥30, ≥60, ≥120 mmHg·min) and KDIGO-defined AKI within 7 days. The risk difference at 60 mmHg·min was +3.00 percentage points (95% CI +0.84 to +5.26), with E-values of 2.08–3.44. The effect was concentrated in patients with elevated preoperative creatinine (conditional effect +9.86 pp).
- **What it does not establish:** The study was restricted to intraoperative hypotension in a surgical population. It did not test whether duration adds information beyond minimum MAP in a general ICU population. It did not account for the variable recording density of eICU. The causal interpretation depends on the assumption of no unmeasured confounding.
- **Relevance to this proposal:** This is the closest methodological precedent for using time-weighted hypotension burden as an exposure. Our proposal extends the question to the general ICU population (eICU) and explicitly tests the incremental value of duration beyond minimum MAP.

### [K2] Ensarioglu 2026 — Arterial Waveform Morphology and Postoperative AKI
- **Citation:** Ensarioglu M, Yildirim AK. "Intraoperative Arterial Waveform Morphology Trajectories and Prediction of Postoperative Acute Kidney Injury: A Latent-Class Analysis of the VitalDB Registry." J Clin Med. 2026;15(17):6871. DOI: 10.3390/jcm15176871.
- **What it establishes:** In 2,587 adult surgical cases with continuous invasive arterial waveforms, a base logistic model of log-transformed hypotension burden (time-weighted MAP<65 mmHg), age, sex, and baseline eGFR achieved an AUC of 0.725. Adding arterial waveform morphology features (dP/dtmax, tau, Eadyn) did not improve discrimination (optimism-corrected ΔAUC ≤0.007). The signal disappeared after adjustment for procedure type and comorbidity.
- **What it does not establish:** The study confirms that hypotension burden is a strong predictor of AKI, but it does not test whether duration adds information beyond minimum MAP. The population was surgical, not general ICU. The negative result for waveform morphology suggests that the bulk of the hemodynamic signal is captured by the burden measure itself.
- **Relevance to this proposal:** This work validates time-weighted hypotension burden as a meaningful predictor and provides a baseline AUC (0.725) for comparison. Our proposal tests a different question: whether the duration component of the burden adds information beyond the depth component (minimum MAP).

### [K3] Liu 2026 — Cumulative Intraoperative Hypotension and Postoperative ICU Admission
- **Citation:** Liu Z, Wang N, Chen M, Wang H. "Association between cumulative intraoperative hypotension and postoperative ICU admission after gastrointestinal lumen cancer surgery: a retrospective cohort study using the INSPIRE database." Front Med. 2026;13:1916589. DOI: 10.3389/fmed.2026.1916589.
- **What it establishes:** In 7,405 patients undergoing gastrointestinal cancer surgery, cumulative duration of MAP<65 mmHg was associated with higher odds of postoperative ICU admission. Adjusted odds ratios were 1.02 (95% CI 0.75–1.40) for >0 to <10 min, 1.29 (95% CI 1.00–1.66) for 10 to <30 min, and 1.78 (95% CI 1.36–2.33) for ≥30 min. Each 50-mmHg·min increase in the area below MAP 65 mmHg was associated with an OR of 1.07 (95% CI 1.04–1.10).
- **What it does not establish:** The outcome was ICU admission, not AKI. The population was surgical. The study did not test whether duration adds information beyond minimum MAP. The association does not establish causality.
- **Relevance to this proposal:** This work provides additional evidence that cumulative hypotension duration is associated with adverse outcomes. Our proposal focuses specifically on AKI and tests the incremental value of duration beyond minimum MAP in a general ICU population.

## Strongest Alternative Explanation

The primary rival explanation is **confounding by illness severity and recording density**:

1. **Severity confounding:** Sicker patients (higher Apache II scores, more organ injury at admission) are more likely to have both longer hypotension episodes and higher AKI risk. If severity is not adequately controlled, the association between hypotension duration and AKI may be spurious. We address this by including Apache II score, admission organ injury variables, and baseline creatinine in the adjustment set.

2. **Recording density:** eICU has variable vitals frequency. Patients with more frequent monitoring may have more recorded hypotension episodes simply because they are monitored more, not because they are sicker. We address this by including a recording density measure (number of BP measurements per hour during the observation window) and by restricting to patients with a minimum number of BP measurements.

3. **Vasopressor use:** Vasopressors are both a treatment for hypotension and a marker of severity. Patients on vasopressors may have their hypotension "masked" (MAP maintained above threshold) or their hypotension may be prolonged by the underlying condition. We address this by including vasopressor exposure (duration and dose) in the adjustment set and by performing a sensitivity analysis restricted to patients not on vasopressors.

4. **Reverse causation:** AKI can cause hemodynamic instability (fluid shifts, electrolyte abnormalities), which could prolong hypotension. We address this by defining the hypotension window to precede the AKI outcome window.

## Concrete Experiment Design

### Population
- **Source:** eICU 2.0 dataset.
- **Inclusion criteria:**
  - Adult patients (age ≥18) with at least one ICU admission.
  - At least 24 hours of ICU stay.
  - At least 10 blood pressure measurements in the first 24 hours of ICU stay.
  - Baseline creatinine available within 24 hours of ICU admission.
- **Exclusion criteria:**
  - Patients with AKI at admission (creatinine ≥2× baseline or urine output <0.5 mL/kg/h for ≥6 hours at admission).
  - Patients with missing or implausible blood pressure values.
  - Patients with fewer than 10 BP measurements in the observation window.

### Temporal Boundaries
- **Observation window:** First 24 hours of ICU stay (from ICU admission time to 24 hours later).
- **Hypotension window:** The observation window. Hypotension is defined as MAP <65 mmHg.
- **AKI outcome window:** 24–72 hours after ICU admission (to avoid reverse causation from AKI causing hypotension).
- **Baseline:** ICU admission time.

### Variables
- **Exposure:**
  - Minimum MAP during the observation window (depth).
  - Time-weighted hypotension burden: area under the MAP curve below 65 mmHg during the observation window (mmHg·min).
  - Duration of hypotension: total time (minutes) with MAP <65 mmHg during the observation window.
- **Outcome:**
  - KDIGO-defined AKI: creatinine ≥2× baseline within the outcome window, or urine output <0.5 mL/kg/h for ≥6 hours within the outcome window.
- **Covariates:**
  - Demographics: age, sex, race.
  - Severity: Apache II score, admission organ injury variables (respiratory, cardiovascular, gastrointestinal, neurological, hematologic, infectious).
  - Baseline kidney function: baseline creatinine, baseline eGFR.
  - Recording density: number of BP measurements per hour during the observation window.
  - Vasopressor exposure: duration (hours) and cumulative dose (μg/min·h) of vasopressors during the observation window.
  - Other: fluid balance, diuretic use, mechanical ventilation.

### Baselines
- **Baseline model 1 (minimum MAP only):** Logistic regression with minimum MAP as the sole hemodynamic predictor, plus covariates.
- **Baseline model 2 (minimum MAP + duration):** Logistic regression with minimum MAP and duration of hypotension as hemodynamic predictors, plus covariates.
- **Baseline model 3 (time-weighted burden):** Logistic regression with time-weighted hypotension burden as the sole hemodynamic predictor, plus covariates.

### Analysis
1. **Descriptive analysis:** Compare demographics, severity, and hemodynamic variables between patients with and without AKI.
2. **Primary analysis:** Compare the incremental value of duration beyond minimum MAP. Fit baseline model 1 (minimum MAP only) and baseline model 2 (minimum MAP + duration). Compare the models using:
   - Likelihood ratio test.
   - Change in AUC (with 95% CI).
   - Change in calibration (Hosmer-Lemeshow test).
   - Decision curve analysis (net benefit).
3. **Secondary analysis:** Compare the incremental value of time-weighted burden beyond minimum MAP. Fit baseline model 1 (minimum MAP only) and baseline model 3 (time-weighted burden). Compare using the same metrics.
4. **Sensitivity analyses:**
   - Restrict to patients not on vasopressors.
   - Restrict to patients with high recording density (≥4 BP measurements/hour).
   - Use a different hypotension threshold (MAP <60 mmHg).
   - Use a different AKI definition (creatinine-only KDIGO).
   - Adjust for additional covariates (fluid balance, diuretic use, mechanical ventilation).
5. **Falsification criteria:**
   - If the incremental value of duration beyond minimum MAP is not statistically significant (p > 0.05) and the 95% CI for the change in AUC includes 0, the hypothesis is not supported.
   - If the association is driven by a small number of patients with extreme hypotension, the hypothesis is not robust.
   - If the association disappears after adjustment for severity and recording density, the hypothesis is confounded.

### What Supportive, Adverse, and Inconclusive Results Would Mean
- **Supportive:** If duration adds significant incremental information beyond minimum MAP (p < 0.05, 95% CI for ΔAUC excludes 0), this would support the hypothesis that time-weighted hypotension burden is a more complete risk measure than minimum MAP alone. This would justify earlier and more aggressive blood pressure support in patients with sustained hypotension.
- **Adverse:** If duration does not add significant information beyond minimum MAP, this would suggest that depth (minimum MAP) is the primary driver of AKI risk, and that the duration component of the burden is not independently prognostic. This would support a clinical focus on avoiding the nadir rather than minimizing the time below the threshold.
- **Inconclusive:** If the 95% CI for the change in AUC is wide and includes both 0 and a clinically meaningful improvement, the result is inconclusive. This would require a larger study or a different population to resolve the question.

## Method Comparison: Simple Baseline vs. Learned Alternative

### Simple Baseline
- **Model:** Logistic regression with minimum MAP and duration of hypotension as predictors, plus covariates.
- **Inputs:** Minimum MAP, duration of hypotension, age, sex, race, Apache II score, admission organ injury variables, baseline creatinine, recording density, vasopressor exposure.
- **Target:** KDIGO-defined AKI within 24–72 hours of ICU admission.
- **Split:** 70% training, 30% testing (stratified by AKI status).
- **Evaluation:** AUC, calibration, decision curve analysis.
- **Resource:** CPU, <1 hour.

### Learned Alternative
- **Model:** Gradient-boosted trees (e.g., XGBoost) with the same inputs as the simple baseline, plus time-weighted hypotension burden as an additional feature.
- **Inputs:** Same as simple baseline, plus time-weighted hypotension burden.
- **Target:** Same as simple baseline.
- **Split:** Same as simple baseline.
- **Evaluation:** Same as simple baseline.
- **Resource:** CPU, <1 hour.

### What the Alternative Could Reveal
- The learned alternative could reveal non-linear relationships between hypotension burden and AKI risk that the simple baseline would miss.
- The learned alternative could reveal interactions between hypotension burden and other covariates (e.g., severity, recording density) that the simple baseline would miss.
- However, the learned alternative is less interpretable than the simple baseline. If the simple baseline is sufficient, the learned alternative is not needed.

### Selection or Deferral Reason
- The simple baseline is the primary analysis because it is more interpretable and directly tests the hypothesis.
- The learned alternative is a secondary analysis to check for non-linearities and interactions.
- If the simple baseline is sufficient, the learned alternative is deferred.

## Limitations
- The eICU dataset is a single-center dataset, so the results may not generalize to other populations.
- The hypotension threshold (MAP <65 mmHg) is arbitrary and may not be optimal for all patients.
- The AKI definition (KDIGO) is based on creatinine and urine output, which may be affected by other factors (e.g., fluid balance, diuretic use).
- The causal interpretation of the results depends on the assumption of no unmeasured confounding.
- The recording density in eICU is variable, which may affect the accuracy of the hypotension burden measure.
