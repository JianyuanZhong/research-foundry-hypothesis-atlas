# UKB two-panel metabolic change and transition to a second cardiometabolic domain

## Successor and scientific deliverable

This successor combines the two assessed UKB parents and preserves their verified limitation: the key repeated laboratory fields have only two usable panels. It narrows the clinical question to the transition from exactly one recorded cardiometabolic domain to a second, and binds the outcome arrays through the complete catalogued ranges. It does not infer a four-panel trajectory, a causal effect, or a chart-adjudicated phenotype.

The future solver must newly produce a private source/codebook audit and participant flow; dated two-panel features and landmark disease states; a simple current-level prognostic model and a transition-specific mechanistic alternative on the same primary estimand; transition associations with uncertainty; locked five-year predictions, calibration, Brier/discrimination, restricted mean event-free survival and decision-curve net benefit at a candidate 10% review threshold (5% and 20% sensitivities); and washout, selection, competing-death, negative-control and permutation falsification outputs. Readiness or a successful fit is not a solved result.

## Unresolved question, evidence boundary and hypothesis

The strongest supported claim is narrower than the proposed clinical use: current metabolic measures are prognostically associated with later coded diabetes, cardiovascular or kidney disease, and some participants have dated repeat laboratory measurements. Available evidence does not establish that within-person change adds information after the latest panel, whether it is particularly informative for illness deepening from one domain to two, or whether it is useful at a prespecified risk-review threshold.

Primary falsifiable hypothesis:

> Among UK Biobank participants aged 40–69 with exactly one of three coded cardiometabolic domains at a dated repeat-panel landmark, annualized worsening in HbA1c, triglycerides and/or HDL between the two available laboratory panels provides incremental prognostic information for the first transition to a second domain beyond the latest laboratory levels, existing-domain identity and prespecified covariates. The added signal improves calibrated five-year risk classification at a candidate 10% review threshold, is stronger for 1→2 than for the secondary 0→1 transition, and is not reproduced for acute appendicitis.

This is a prognostic/predictive hypothesis, not a causal claim. It does not claim that changing a biomarker causes disease, that monitoring or treatment based on the score improves outcomes, or that the coded domains are clinically adjudicated. A null is consequential: it would argue against adding a two-panel change alert to a risk workflow in this selected population.

The candidate 10% threshold is an analysis operating point, not a validated clinical cutoff. The study asks whether a model would move people across that operating point with calibrated benefit; it does not recommend a policy.

## Population and temporal boundaries

Eligibility for the repeat-panel cohort:

1. age 40–69 inclusive at recruitment from `population.21022-0.0`;
2. valid `population.31-0.0`;
3. finite, field-valid HbA1c, triglyceride and HDL at both `main` instances 0 and 1;
4. valid dates with (d_1>d_0) and a primary gap of 365–3650 days;
5. no date-order contradiction among available `assessment.53-0.0`–`assessment.53-3.0`;
6. no two-domain coded phenotype on or before d_1; and
7. alive through the risk origin and free of a new second-domain event during the primary washout.

The index is d_1, not an instance label. Primary risk origin is d_1+365 days. Record, but exclude, events in the washout. Follow-up ends at the first eligible transition, death, the maximum trusted outcome date in the frozen snapshot, or five years after risk origin. Report the number with five-year opportunity. Sensitivities use 0-, 2- and 5-year washouts and a 182–5475-day gap.

The primary estimand population is the subset with exactly one domain at risk origin. The event is the first date after origin at which either remaining domain occurs: 1→2. Zero-domain participants are retained for the secondary 0→1 analysis and joint M1 fit. Participants with two or more domains at origin are excluded. The flow must count participants lost before the landmark; their exclusion is not evidence of low risk.

## Matched baseline and substantive alternative

For the primary comparison both models predict the same one-domain participants, landmark, endpoint, competing-death rules, covariates, preprocessing and participant split.

M0, the simple prognostic baseline, is a cause-specific Cox model for 1→2. Inputs are latest `main.30750-1.0`, `30870-1.0`, `30760-1.0`; existing-domain identity; `population.21022-0.0`, `31-0.0`; latest assessment BMI `21001`, BP `4080/4079`, smoking `20116`, center `54`; panel gap; and missingness indicators. Use training-frozen restricted cubic splines, robust standard errors and no outcome-driven selection. A secondary version adds valid latest creatinine/cystatin-C.

M1 is a transition-specific multistate illness-deepening model with states 0 domains, one domain identified as diabetes/CVD/CKD, 2 domains and death. Permit 0→1, 1→2 and death; include direct 0→2 only if valid dates support it, otherwise use a prespecified interval-censoring sensitivity. M1 uses all M0 inputs plus the three dated changes, allows change coefficients to differ by 0→1 versus 1→2 and, if supported, by existing-domain identity. M1 may use eligible zero-domain participants to estimate the secondary 0→1 transition, but its primary 1→2 predictions and metrics are for exactly the same one-domain participants as M0.

M1 is substantive because it tests whether change is associated with illness deepening and can distinguish diabetes-first, CVD-first and CKD-first routes. A snapshot Cox model loses this state structure. M1 remains an association model, not a causal mechanism.

## Measurement selection, split and uncertainty

The primary inference is conditional on two usable panels. Among people with a valid panel-0 core panel, fit a panel-1 availability model using panel-0 values, age, sex, BMI, smoking and BP only. Report availability by age, sex and panel-0 risk strata. If positivity permits, repeat with stabilized inverse-probability-of-repeat weights truncated at the 1st/99th percentiles. This cannot recover unmeasured repeat-testing indication, medication changes, adherence, socioeconomic confounding or exact assay dictionaries.

Use participant-level catalog hash buckets 0–59 for fitting, 60–69 for all model/threshold decisions, 70–79 once for locked evaluation and 80–99 reserved. Record namespace and assignments privately without eids. Report transition coefficients, 95% intervals, five-year cause-specific/competing-death cumulative incidence, time-dependent/integrated Brier, discrimination, calibration intercept/slope/plots, RMST and decision curves. Use paired 200-resample participant bootstraps on locked test participants for M1−M0 metrics, preserving training preprocessing. At 5%, 10% and 20% predicted five-year thresholds report net benefit, sensitivity, specificity, PPV, number needed to review and threshold crossings. These are operating characteristics, not evidence of clinical utility.

## Falsification and interpretation

Supportive evidence requires:

- the joint change test supports worsening-direction incremental association for 1→2, with marker-specific estimates and no unexplained strong sign reversal;
- if event support permits, the prespecified 1→2 minus 0→1 change interaction has a 95% interval excluding zero; otherwise this secondary comparison is inconclusive and cannot support the hypothesis;
- on locked test data, M1 improves Brier score by at least 0.01 with a 95% interval wholly favoring improvement, and its net-benefit difference at 10% has a 95% interval above zero, without material calibration harm by sex, age or existing-domain identity;
- direction and performance persist under washout, gap, missingness, repeat-panel weighting and competing-death sensitivities; and
- acute appendicitis is null/materially weaker and within-state/sex/gap-bin permutations of change vectors do not reproduce the association or gain.

Adverse evidence is null/opposite change after latest levels and gap, failure or worsening of locked calibration/Brier/10% net benefit, disappearance under participant splitting or washout, a comparable negative-control signal, or a permutation signal. This falsifies the incremental transition-specific claim, not biological harmlessness.

Inconclusive evidence is source/date disagreement, unvalidated code/date pairing, too few complete panels or events, poor repeat-panel positivity, severe subgroup calibration failure, or intervals spanning meaningful benefit and no benefit. Do not replace the design with a single-panel analysis.

Computationally checkable claims include source hashes/headers, joins, filter counts, date/gap distributions, outcome extraction, split integrity, model fits, uncertainty, predictions, calibration, Brier, decision curves, permutations and sensitivities. Chart adjudication or another study is required for confirmed disease status, causal treatment effects, policy value, threshold transport, measurement indication, medication effects and external validity.

## Alternatives and limitations

M1 is selected because it directly addresses the transition-specific clinical uncertainty and is proportionate to two observations per marker. M0 is the essential comparator.

A compact recurrent/temporal-attention model was developed at matching specificity conceptually: same inputs, 1→2 target, participant split and metrics, with ordered two-panel vectors and exact gap. It could learn nonlinear change-by-domain interactions, but two observations offer little sequence structure and extra capacity could amplify repeat-testing selection. No neural fit or GPU benefit was measured. It is deferred until at least three dated panels or a bounded preregistered diagnostic shows stable transition value beyond M1 with locked calibration. This is a scientific adequacy decision, not a ban on neural networks or GPUs.

A four-instance slope/mixed-effects model is rejected because the exact main header lacks assay instances 2/3. Proteomic organ-age, raw imaging/fat distribution, CHIP variants, infection timing, sleep regularity, cancer stage/treatment and disease-specific PRS require unverified dependencies. Delphi is conceptual precedent only; no reproduction or Danish validation claim is made. ALADYNOULLI's genetic analysis is not reproduced because inputs are unavailable. Oncoformer’s main paper/full STAR Methods are unavailable locally and raw images are absent; no multimodal claim is made.
