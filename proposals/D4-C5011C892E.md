# Early menopause and cardiac structure in UK Biobank: a bounded exploratory hypothesis

## Scientific question and claim
Among women reporting natural menopause, is earlier age at menopause associated with greater cardiac structural change at the assessment, after accounting for age, body size, blood pressure, smoking, and HRT timing? The strongest existing evidence supports an observational association between early menopause and cardiovascular events [K2], but does not establish a causal hormonal mechanism; a recent MR review found no significant causal association for coronary heart disease or stroke [K1]. The unresolved claim here is narrower: whether the association is present in measured LV/LA structure and persists after measured cardiovascular load adjustment, extending the sex-specific UKB imaging context established for cardiovascular ageing [K3].

## Population and time
Use UKB `ukb672073` participants with sex=2, field 2724=1 (had natural menopause), and positive field 3581 (age at last menstrual period). Restrict to baseline assessment instance 0 for the primary cross-sectional association; do not use instance 3 as follow-up because the available MRI instance timing is not sufficient to establish a prospective change. Follow-up events are secondary and exploratory only. The assessment date is field 53-0.0; field 53-1.0 is available for a repeat-measurement sensitivity analysis but only 20,343 rows have it in the completed subset.

## Variables and estimand
Primary exposure: continuous age at natural menopause, standardized per 5 years; secondary binary early menopause <45 years versus >=45 years, with <40 years as a prespecified subgroup. Primary outcomes: LV myocardial mass and LA maximum volume at the same assessment, normalized by BMI or height if the solver can validate units; LA ejection fraction is secondary. Estimand: adjusted mean difference in outcome per 5-year earlier menopause among women with complete baseline measurements. This is descriptive/associational, not causal.

## Analysis and baseline
Primary baseline: ordinary least squares with restricted cubic splines for age at MRI, BMI, systolic and diastolic BP, smoking, and HRT start/end; cluster robust uncertainty is not appropriate for a single assessment. Report coefficient, 95% CI, missingness, and partial R². Use a complete-case primary analysis and a multiple-imputation sensitivity analysis. The substantive alternative is a penalized multivariate elastic-net model predicting the two primary structural outcomes jointly, with the same covariates and an interaction between menopause age and BMI/BP. It can reveal whether early menopause has a distinct joint structural pattern that the univariate baseline loses, but it cannot identify a mechanism. Compare coefficient stability and held-out standardized prediction error using participant-level 5-fold cross-validation; no GPU is needed. A mechanistic mediation claim is deferred because no reliable longitudinal exposure, repeated cardiac measurements, or adjudicated treatment indication is available.

## Falsification and interpretation
Supportive: the direction is consistent for LV mass and LA volume, the interaction is not driven by a single covariate, and the association remains in the repeat-measurement subset with uncertainty reported. Adverse: estimates are null or reverse after BP/HRT adjustment, or are confined to one MRI instance/one outcome; interpret as no evidence for a robust structural association. Inconclusive: wide CIs, separation, or missingness prevent resolution; do not call it negative. A positive result does not establish that menopause causes remodeling; residual confounding, selection into cardiac MRI, HRT, smoking, and common cardiovascular risk remain. Clinical adjudication and external replication would be required for causal or treatment claims.

## Observed feasibility and limitations
A read-only column-projection check found 165,321 rows with field 3581, 1083 rows with any cardiac MRI field, and 175 rows with both valid menopause age and cardiac MRI; the 175-row subset has menopause ages 31–58 years. This is too small for precise causal adjustment or reliable event analysis. The experiment must report this limitation and treat any effect as exploratory. Event counts and complete medication/indication data are unavailable or insufficient for the proposed clinical endpoint extension. The three demonstration methods were considered: Delphi-style sequence learning is deferred because only one cardiac assessment is usable; ALADYNOULLI-style latent longitudinal modeling is deferred because repeated imaging is sparse; Oncoformer-style multimodal learning is not applicable because no imaging payload is available. A simple baseline plus penalized multivariate alternative is the smallest scientifically useful comparison.

## Actual deliverable
Fit the baseline and alternative on the 175 eligible women, produce cohort flow, covariate balance/missingness, adjusted coefficients with uncertainty, cross-validation comparison, and a conclusion classified as supportive, adverse, or inconclusive. No full-study result is claimed in this proposal.
