## Unresolved question and clinical importance
ICU clinicians titrate blood pressure and choose MAP targets (e.g. 65 mmHg) largely on the idea
that *how low* matters. But two 2026 editorials show the field is genuinely split on whether
hypotension is a causal driver or a "cosmetic" severity marker [K1], and warn that hypotension
metrics/predictions have not translated into renal protection [K2]. A separable, decision-relevant
uncertainty remains: does the **duration** of hypotension (time below threshold) carry outcome
information beyond the **depth** (nadir) and the **mean** MAP? If yes, time-in-target monitoring
adds value; if duration adds nothing over depth, simpler nadir-based reasoning suffices and
duration-weighted "burden" indices are not justified as risk measures.

## Strongest claim already supported vs the claim tested
Supported: ICU hypotension exposure is associated with organ injury, and hypotension is common and
chart-dense in eICU [K3]. NOT established: that the *duration* component is separable from depth/mean,
or that it transports across hospitals. That is the unresolved claim this experiment tests.
This is an association/transport study, not a causal or mechanistic claim.

## Hypothesis (tested) vs leading rivals
- H1 (duration is separable): among adult ICU stays with hypotension in the first 24 h, greater
  time below 65 mmHg is associated with higher subsequent AKI and mortality **after adjusting for
  nadir MAP, mean MAP, severity (APACHE), age and monitoring density**, with a monotone
  burden-outcome gradient; the adjusted duration term is non-null.
- R1 (depth captures all): nadir/mean MAP absorb the association; adjusted duration term ≈ null.
- R2 (severity + monitoring-density confounding): lower MAP and denser sampling in sicker patients;
  attenuates the association.
- R3 (measurement/artifact): non-physiologic MAP values create spurious duration; blocked by a
  physiologic-range filter and a sensitivity using only plausible MAP.
- R4 (hospital-practice fingerprint): between-hospital variance dominates; LOHO transport fails.

## Baselines, learned alternative, discriminating test
- Baseline (simple): logistic regression — severity + nadir + mean + age + monitoring density.
- Learned alternative (substantive): gradient-boosted trees on the full 24 h MAP time-series
  features (time-below-threshold curves at 60/65/70, slope, nadir timing, sampling density). What it
  adds that the baseline loses: non-linear duration x onset-timing interactions (a long low episode
  early vs brief late dips) that a single linear duration term cannot express.
- Discriminator H1 vs R1: nested-model likelihood-ratio / incremental AUROC for the duration term
  over nadir+mean; quintile dose-response of burden. R2: adjust for severity + density, inspect
  attenuation. R4: leave-one-hospital-out transport (delta-AUROC, calibration, O:E, Brier).

## Falsification / interpretation
- Supportive (H1): adjusted duration term non-null with monotone quintile gradient AND incremental
  discrimination CI excluding 0 after R1/R2 adjustment.
- Adverse (R1/R2): duration term ≈ null after adjustment → depth/severity suffices.
- Inconclusive: wide CIs, non-monotone gradient, or transport failure with unstable estimates.
Each result would not establish mechanism (eICU lacks order-set/staffing data) and cannot justify a
treatment target.
