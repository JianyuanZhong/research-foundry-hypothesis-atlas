# Residual physiologic instability immediately before ICU discharge

Status: Lead-repaired Harbor proposal; planned experiment only. No cohort scan, model fit, or clinical result was run during discovery.

Parent: [prior hypothesis] (itself a repair of expert seed [prior hypothesis]). The prior selected AKI-recovery [prior hypothesis] is contextual only; this is a distinct discharge-safety question.

## Scientific opening and unresolved hypothesis

ICU readmission or death soon after ICU dismissal is clinically consequential, but a discharge-time risk score does not show that a patient was discharged too early or that delaying discharge would help. [K1] supports the importance of discharge-time respiratory/neurologic impairment while explicitly leaving the intervention question open. [K2] shows that learned discharge-risk models can lose performance outside their development setting. [K3] shows that MIMIC measurements are modular, sparse and partly generated by care processes.

The strongest available claim is therefore only that discharge-time abnormalities can be prognostic in some cohorts. The unresolved, falsifiable claim is:

> Among adults discharged alive from an ICU to a non-ICU destination after at least 24 hours, persistent oxygen-support or repeated abnormal vital-sign bins in the final 24 clinical hours are associated with higher first-event risk of ICU readmission or hospital death within 48 hours than an observed-stable state, after adjustment for measured context and explicit tests of destination, treatment-limitation documentation and observation intensity.

This is prognostic, not causal or mechanistic. A positive result would motivate prospective discharge/monitoring studies; it would not prove that discharge timing caused the event.

## Population, joins and temporal boundaries

Use the first valid ICU stay per hospital admission (earliest `icu/icustays.intime`) with `intime < outtime`, `los >= 1 day`, and `hosp/patients.anchor_age >= 18`. Join `icu/icustays` to `hosp/admissions` on `(subject_id, hadm_id)` and to `hosp/patients` on `subject_id`. Keep all admissions for one `subject_id` in one train/validation/test split.

Set the landmark `tL = icu/icustays.outtime`. Require the first `hosp/transfers` row with `eventtype='transfer'` and `intime >= tL` to have a non-ICU `careunit`; classify careunits containing ICU, CCU, CSRU, SICU or TSICU as ICU. ICU-to-ICU moves are excluded. Missing or unclassifiable immediate destinations are excluded from the primary cohort, counted in the flow table, and analyzed only in a labeled sensitivity using `hosp/admissions.discharge_location`. Destination is a context/selection variable, not the exposure.

Require `admissions.deathtime` to be null or strictly after `tL`. If `hospital_expire_flag=1` lacks a usable death time, timing is unresolved and the stay is excluded rather than reconstructed. Use patient-relative intervals because MIMIC timestamps are deidentified with within-person intervals preserved but cross-person calendar alignment invalid.

Primary features are limited to `[tL-24 hours, tL)`. For `icu/chartevents`, include only rows with `charttime < tL` and `storetime <= tL`; no post-landmark destination, discharge, death, later-stay or other outcome information is an input. Partition the window into four fixed six-hour bins. Measurements are summarized by clinical `charttime`; `storetime` is only an availability screen and a documentation-timing sensitivity.

## Verified residual-instability exposure

The primary item audit was inspected from `datasets/mimic/table-d1023acc404fd1d4.json` and the attached d_items extract. Use only these verified `icu/d_items` labels and IDs in `icu/chartevents`:

- 220045 Heart Rate (bpm)
- 220210 Respiratory Rate (insp/min)
- 220052 Arterial Blood Pressure mean and 220181 Non Invasive Blood Pressure mean (mmHg)
- 220277 O2 saturation pulseoxymetry (%)
- 223834 O2 Flow and 227287 O2 Flow (additional cannula) (L/min)

Do not use unverified FiO2 or oxygen-device item IDs in the primary or required sensitivity. If a future solver discovers an additional label, it must report it as exploratory and separately audited, not silently add it.

For each stream and bin, calculate median, count, valid-value mask and abnormality. Fixed, pre-outcome boundaries are HR <50 or >110 bpm; RR <8 or >25/min; MAP <65 mmHg; SpO2 <92%; and oxygen flow >=4 L/min. A bin is abnormal if any valid stream crosses a boundary; a respiratory-support bin is positive if either verified oxygen-flow item crosses its boundary.

Exposure states are: observed stable (at least 3 of 4 bins have all required streams, with no abnormal or support bin); persistent instability (at least 2 bins abnormal or support-positive); intermittent instability (exactly 1); and insufficient observation (fewer than 3 qualifying bins or no valid required stream). The primary contrast is persistent versus observed-stable. Keep intermittent and insufficient states visible; do not impute missingness to normal. Report component-specific burden and the number of qualifying bins.

## Outcomes and estimands

Follow from `tL` for 48 hours, bounded by same-admission `hosp/admissions.dischtime`. Define mutually exclusive first events:

- ICU readmission: a distinct subsequent `icu/icustays.stay_id` with the same `(subject_id, hadm_id)` and `tL <= intime < tL+48h`.
- Hospital death: `admissions.deathtime` in `[tL,tL+48h)), before any readmission.
- Alive hospital discharge before 48 hours: `dischtime < tL+48h`, with no earlier readmission or death.
- Event-free at 48 hours otherwise.

If death and readmission have identical recorded times, death is first by a fixed tie rule and the tie is reported. An expiration flag without death time is unresolved, not silently converted.

The primary estimand is the 48-hour cumulative incidence of composite readmission/death with alive discharge competing, and its standardized risk difference (persistent minus stable) over the eligible population. Secondary estimands are cause-specific cumulative-incidence contrasts for readmission and death. These are associations among ICU survivors discharged to non-ICU care, not treatment effects or principal-stratum effects.

## Baseline, alternative and method choice

The primary baseline is a transparent fixed-summary cause-specific competing-risk analysis using the four-bin summaries, exposure state, age, sex, admission type, ICU length of stay, first/last careunit, time from admission to ICU entry, qualifying-bin count and component burdens. Report crude risks before adjustment. Fit cause-specific first-event models or an equivalent multinomial first-event model, then standardize risks by exposure state. Use patient-cluster bootstrap confidence intervals with a fixed seed (target 1,000 resamples; if the solver envelope forces fewer, record the prespecified reduction).

The substantive alternative is a small masked temporal GRU-D-style model on the same four bins, with the same streams/context, value masks and time-since-last-observation, fit only on training patients, early-stopped on validation and evaluated once on a locked patient-level test set. It targets the same first-event states, not a different outcome. It can reveal ordering, persistence, co-movement and informative observation gaps that fixed medians/counts lose. The scientific comparison is whether those temporal features change persistent-versus-intermittent risk interpretation and held-out calibration, not whether a neural model wins a small AUROC. Report calibration slope/intercept, Brier, event-specific AUROC/AUPRC, calibration by exposure state/destination, patient-bootstrap or five fixed-seed uncertainty, and all seeds.

The baseline remains primary because its estimand is auditable. The temporal model is retained because it tests substantive information loss. A latent mechanistic model is deferred: the snapshot lacks adjudicated respiratory diagnoses, verified delivered-device settings, work-of-breathing assessments and reliable treatment intent. Future planning estimates, not measured discovery results, are 1–3 CPU hours for extraction/fitting, 2–4 additional CPU hours for bootstrap/artifact checks, and 2–6 hours on one allocated A100 for the small temporal model. The future envelope is 16 CPUs, 262144 MiB, up to 8 GPUs and 28800 seconds. No GPU or solver study was run here.

## Rival explanations, falsification and interpretation

Add the immediate non-ICU destination as a sensitivity. Add last eligible `chartevents` values for verified code-status IDs 223758 and 228687 only when text explicitly indicates DNR, DNI, comfort, hospice or no escalation; unknown remains unknown. This is a documentation proxy, not a goals-of-care adjudication. Compute vital/oxygen row counts, distinct bins and non-vital/non-oxygen chart counts in the same window. If the contrast attenuates with observation density or a process-only gradient is comparable, surveillance/recording is a stronger explanation.

Supportive evidence is a directionally consistent, clinically nontrivial persistent-versus-stable risk difference with uncertainty excluding a negligible range, persistence across destination/code-status strata, no comparable process-only gradient, and adequate calibration. Temporal improvement supports added ordered information only if held out and stable; it does not establish physiology or causality.

Adverse evidence is null/reversed or unstable contrast, risk concentrated in insufficient observation, attenuation after observation adjustment, disappearance within destination/code-status strata, or a temporal gain that fails held-out evaluation. This favors residual severity, discharge selection, treatment limitation or surveillance artifacts.

Inconclusive evidence is sparse events, wide intervals spanning meaningful harm, high missingness, unresolved timing, poor calibration, or inadequate strata. It warrants replication/measurement work rather than a favorable conclusion. True oxygen need, clinician intent, appropriateness of discharge, mechanism, causal benefit of delayed discharge, and transportability require expert adjudication, prospective monitoring/delay studies or an external hospital.
