# Trajectory information beyond current metabolic and renal levels

Parent: [prior hypothesis] (valid imported UKB seed, [starting question])

## Question and hypothesis

The unresolved clinical question is whether change between two available blood-biomarker assessments adds prospective information about a participant's first later cardiorenal/metabolic multimorbidity beyond the participant's current (second-assessment) levels.

The falsifiable hypothesis is: among participants with both biomarker instances and no recorded inpatient endpoint in the relevant domains before the index boundary, standardized within-person change in creatinine, cystatin C, glycaemia and lipids improves held-out prediction and calibration of a first subsequent two-domain event after adjustment for current values, age and sex. This is a prognostic-information hypothesis, not a causal or treatment hypothesis.

This would advance the seed and existing baseline evidence by testing whether repeat testing contains actionable trajectory information rather than merely showing that a single current level is associated with future disease. A supportive result could motivate a prospective study of trajectory-aware monitoring; a null result would support using current levels alone and avoiding costly/noisy repeat testing for this purpose.

## Evidence and limits

The local files establish that the UKB snapshot contains paired instance 0 and instance 1 columns for the relevant blood-assay field IDs. The local catalog also states that field-instance labels do not provide exact elapsed dates. Public Showcase context retrieved during discovery describes instance 0 as the recruitment assessment and instance 1 as the repeat assessment, generally 2006–2010 and 2012–2013, but this model-mediated lookup is untrusted context and is not a substitute for the solver's verified field dictionary.

The strongest claim currently supported by the available evidence is only that current biomarker levels can be evaluated as prospective risk stratifiers. No local result establishes that change adds information. The future solver must not report a scientific conclusion until it has computed the declared outputs.

The relevant local metadata is incomplete: approved semantic metadata is available for age field 21022 and sex field 31, while most UKB field labels/codings are not frozen locally. Therefore execution must first verify the analyte labels, units, assay direction and allowed missing-value codes from an approved field dictionary. If any mapping or unit is not verified, that field is excluded or the result is marked inconclusive; IDs must not be silently relabelled.

## Baseline and substantive alternative

Use one fixed participant-level five-fold split, derived from a documented hash of eid, for every model. All transformations, imputation, tuning and feature selection occur inside training folds. No outcome field may enter exposure preprocessing.

M0 is the simple clinical baseline: age, sex and the verified instance-1 current levels. Fit a cause-specific Cox model for time to the primary endpoint with death censoring, plus 5- and 10-year logistic models for directly comparable risks.

M1 is the simple longitudinal test: M0 plus one within-person change per verified biomarker (instance 1 minus instance 0), standardized using training-fold parameters. The primary inferential test is the joint change block; individual marker claims are secondary and multiplicity-controlled. Compare M1 against M0 using held-out time-dependent AUC, average precision, Brier score, calibration slope/intercept and decision-curve net benefit, each with participant-clustered bootstrap intervals.

M2 is a substantive learned alternative, not a score-chasing embellishment. Fit gradient-boosted risk models at 5 and 10 years using the same M0 inputs, verified changes, and only pre-specified current-by-change interactions. If the complete-case restriction is relaxed in a prespecified sensitivity analysis, add missingness indicators and fold-safe imputation. M2 can reveal clinically meaningful thresholds and discordance that the additive M1 loses, such as renal-marker deterioration at stable glycaemia or lipid deterioration despite a stable current value. Report grouped out-of-fold importance for current versus change features and pre-specified accumulated-local-effect/partial-dependence summaries. Treat an M2 improvement as scientifically useful only when a trajectory pattern replicates across folds, domains and sensitivity definitions.

The actual deliverable is newly fitted M0/M1/M2 models, fixed fold assignments, out-of-fold predictions, a population/outcome flow table, coefficient and feature summaries, calibration and discrimination metrics with uncertainty, and the falsification-control outputs. ## Falsification and interpretation

Supportive: the joint change block has a stable direction and materially improves held-out calibration or discrimination over M0 for the primary outcome, with compatible results for at least one domain-specific endpoint; M2 shows reproducible nonlinear or discordant trajectory patterns. This supports incremental prognostic information only. It does not establish causality, screening thresholds, benefit from repeat testing, or transportability.

Adverse: M1's change block is near-null or unstable, does not improve held-out calibration/decision-curve performance, or appears predictive only under leakage-prone/random rather than patient-level splits. A future-change permutation within age/sex strata removes the apparent increment, or the negative temporal control shows that the same change measure “predicts” pre-index disease after current levels. These outcomes falsify the added-information claim for this operational phenotype and favor current levels alone.

Inconclusive: post-filter endpoint counts are sparse, uncertainty is wide, biomarker semantics/units cannot be verified, complete-case selection materially changes the estimate, results vary across folds or domain definitions, or the administrative index cannot be defended. This means this snapshot cannot resolve the question; it is not evidence that trajectories have no clinical value.

Hard controls include: strict post-index outcome dates; no outcome-derived features; training-fold-only preprocessing; age/sex-stratified permutation of change vectors; and a negative temporal control using pre-index diagnosis. Repeat the primary comparison with inverse-probability weighting or a missingness-aware model only if the missingness model is itself prespecified and estimable.

The strongest claims require additional evidence: individual blood-draw dates, assay quality/units and batch metadata, primary-care and self-report histories, medication and treatment changes, adjudicated CKD/heart-failure definitions, serial clinical creatinine and urine-output data for AKI, and an external cohort. This study cannot establish a causal effect of repeat testing or a treatment recommendation.
