# Dynamic post-TACE liver-state signal versus observation process in HCC

## Hypothesis and clinical importance

The HCC snapshot contains many repeated treatment episodes and longitudinal laboratory measurements, but it does not contain adjudicated tumor response, treatment intent, outside-hospital outcomes, or validated Child-Pugh components. The strongest evidence available from inspected work is that baseline ALBI is prognostic [K1], pre-TACE routine variables can predict a clinically defined Child-Pugh increase in a retrospective preprint [K2], and a post-treatment change measure can be more informative than its baseline value in a TACE cohort [K3]. Those results do not establish that a dynamic laboratory signal in this institution reflects biological response or toxicity rather than who is measured more often and who is scheduled for another procedure.

Falsifiable hypothesis: among patients with a first locally recorded TACE episode, an assay-exact change in liver-state markers during days 7–42 after TACE predicts a second locally recorded TACE during days 43–180 after the index episode, beyond pretreatment severity and treatment history; the state contribution will remain after adjustment for observation intensity. The strongest rival is an observation/care-process explanation: patients with more visits, laboratory tests, imaging, orders or planned follow-up both exhibit a measurable trajectory and have more opportunity to receive repeat TACE, so the apparent state signal will disappear when process information or time order is controlled.

This is an association and temporal prediction estimand, not a treatment effect, mechanism, progression label, or recommendation to repeat TACE. Repeat TACE is a consequential operational endpoint because it reflects a future treatment decision, but it may be planned retreatment, residual disease, clinician preference, toxicity management, or access. Expert review would be required to classify intent and confirm clinical indication.

## Population, time and outcome

Use only the read-only HCC snapshot [source checksum]. Define an HCC-directed TACE procedure by a frozen rule applied to procedures.Surgery: case-normalized exact/substring matching to TACE, transcatheter hepatic arterial chemoembolization, transcatheter hepatic artery embolization, or arterial chemoembolization, including composite procedure names containing one of these terms; exclude rows containing only angiography, catheterization, bland embolization, or diagnostic imaging. The readiness check must print the matched names and counts before fitting. This is a local-record first TACE, not a claim of first-ever treatment.

Select the earliest eligible procedure per Patient Master Index with nonmissing Patient Master Index, Encounter Number, and Start Time, after excluding anyone with an earlier eligible TACE in the available local history. Join the index row to encounters on the documented composite key (Patient Master Index,Encounter Number). Index time is procedures.Start Time. Baseline is index time minus 365 days through index time. The response feature window is day 7 through day 42; no later value may enter predictors. The outcome window is day 43 through day 180. Primary outcome is any second eligible TACE procedure in that window, joined by Patient Master Index and its own Start Time.

Report two analysis populations: (a) a complete-observation 180-day cohort requiring a prespecified record-observation rule, such as a recorded encounter or test during both the late window and near the end of follow-up, and (b) the all-index cohort with a censoring/observation-availability sensitivity analysis. Absence of a second procedure is not proof of no progression, no death, or no outside care. A sensitivity endpoint may include a fixed local-locoregional procedure list, but it must not be called progression.

## Analysis and method comparison

Baseline B0 is a prespecified regularized logistic model for second TACE by day 180 using only index/baseline features: age/sex, baseline encounter and prior-procedure counts, fixed diagnosis counts, assay-exact pre-index lab summaries and missingness, index department and admission duration. B1 adds response-window observation-process summaries (number and timing of encounters, lab/test/order/medication counts and observed days) but not repeat-TACE rows. If event timing supports it, fit a complementary pooled-logistic discrete-time hazard model for days 43–180. Standardize and impute within patient-grouped training folds, retaining missingness indicators.

The substantive alternative is a compact time-aware GRU sequence model using the same index cohort and -365 to +42 input horizon. Tokens encode dated assay-exact lab values, diagnosis/procedure categories, encounters, examinations, medications and orders, time since index/previous event, and missingness. It has explicit state and process channels. Fit: state-only, process-only, combined state+process, and combined with response-window times permuted within patient while preserving counts. Use an untouched patient-level 20% test split; tune in the remaining 80% with grouped five-fold validation and three fixed seeds. This alternative can reveal event order, transient change, time gaps and state-process interactions that B0 compresses, while the process-only/permutation controls test whether that added information is merely opportunity-to-observe.

Primary evaluation is held-out log loss, Brier score, calibration intercept/slope, AUROC and AUPRC with patient bootstrap 95% intervals and paired bootstrap differences. Report hazard ratios/odds ratios for B0/B1, calibration plots, event and missingness counts, assay availability, and sensitivity by observation-complete cohort. Do not select a model because of a small AUC gain. The required scientific deliverable is newly fitted out-of-fold and held-out risks for B0/B1 and the sequence ablations, uncertainty intervals, a complete cohort/accounting table, and a negative-control result.

Supportive result: paired dynamic state features are available in a prespecified fraction; state-only and combined models improve held-out log loss/calibration beyond B0; the state increment persists after B1 and is absent from process-only and process-permuted controls, with stable direction across assay-complete and observation-complete analyses. This supports incremental recorded-state prognostic information, not biological mechanism, causality, recurrence, or treatment benefit.

Adverse result: B0 is comparable, or the state increment disappears after B1, or process-only/permuted marks reproduce it. This supports baseline severity or observation/care-process artifact and argues against using the trajectory as a treatment trigger.

Inconclusive result: too few paired assays/events, unstable exact-name mapping, unverified units, broad intervals, censoring, or coding instability. This does not refute the hypothesis; it identifies missing evidence needed for a study with laboratory units/reference ranges, expert treatment-intent labels, radiology/mRECIST labels, toxicity, transplant status, outside-hospital care and adjudicated death.

## Compute and alternatives

Discovery used bounded schema/row scans; no scientific model fit and no GPU result is claimed. The future solver envelope is 16 CPUs, 8 GPUs, 262144 MiB and 28800 seconds. Plan 8–16 CPUs and 64–128 GiB for chunked extraction/fold materialization from the 2.2-GB lab source (estimated 1–2.5 hours, unverified); CPU baseline fits under 1 hour after materialization; one allocated A100 with 32–64 GiB for the compact GRU and three seeds (estimated 2–4 hours, unverified). Checkpoint cohort construction and fold outputs. If the GRU exceeds the envelope, complete B0/B1 and negative controls and label the learned comparison incomplete rather than changing the estimand.

A multimodal image/text transformer was deferred because no image payload exists, documents lack temporal fields, and local text extraction is explicitly unvalidated beyond narrow cirrhosis axes. A mechanistic joint liver-tumor state-space model was deferred because assay units/reference ranges, adjudicated tumor burden, treatment intent and validated biological endpoints are unavailable. These are revisit conditions, not blanket method bans. A simple baseline remains primary because it directly tests severity versus process; the GRU is retained because timing and process separation are scientifically informative, not because it is complex or uses a GPU.

## Demonstration dispositions

The natural-history/Delphi demonstration supports the possibility that dated structured disease histories can add information through event order, but its UKB/Danish validation and population-scale context are unavailable; the GRU here is a bounded adaptation, not reproduction. The Bayesian/ALADYNOULLI demonstration shows a richer longitudinal latent model but depends on genetic/latent-trajectory assumptions not verified here, so it is deferred. The cancer/Oncoformer demonstration includes lab-only and image ablations, but the main article and full STAR Methods are unavailable and HCC images are absent; a lab-only transformer is not needed for this question and is deferred. None is used as clinical evidence.
