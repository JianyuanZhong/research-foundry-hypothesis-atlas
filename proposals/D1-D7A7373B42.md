# Discharge-proximal non-recovery after observed TACE: a density-gated successor for short-term return in HCC

## Candidate status and substantive change

This is a targeted successor to [prior hypothesis]. It preserves the parent’s primary early post-TACE prognostic estimand and its all-cause operational endpoint. The substantive change is a stricter, explicitly falsifiable discharge-time test: discharge non-recovery is evaluated only when the early and discharge-proximal windows contain repeated, temporally separated core measurements, and it must outperform a matched pre-discharge placebo window. This distinguishes a discharge-time recovery signal from a single late test, testing intensity, or a generic later-in-stay trajectory.

This remains an observational prognostic experiment. It does not estimate the causal effect of TACE, length of stay, discharge timing, laboratory testing, follow-up, or a clinical intervention.

## Hypotheses and evidence boundary

The preserved primary hypothesis is:

> Among adults with HCC who have a first observed TACE episode and are discharged, does the within-person change in core laboratory values from the 14 days before TACE to the first 72 hours after TACE add reproducible information about a subsequent inpatient admission within 30 days, beyond baseline reserve proxies, demographics, prior utilization, and recorded disease-history markers?

The sharpened nested hypothesis is:

> Among patients with at least 96 hours from observed TACE start to discharge, adequate repeated core measurements in both the early window and the last 24 hours before discharge, and complete baseline core measurements, do discharge-proximal residual changes (last-24-hour median minus early-window median) add held-out prognostic information beyond the baseline-plus-early model, and is that increment larger than the same analysis in a pre-discharge placebo window 24–48 hours before discharge?

The primary outcome is subsequent inpatient admission, not unplanned readmission. A secondary outcome is a prespecified diagnosis-coded liver-event phenotype. Both are operational phenotypes reconstructed from this record; neither is adjudicated clinical truth.

The strongest external evidence actually inspected is narrower. Sanai et al. (Scientific Reports 2026, DOI 10.1038/s41598-026-54466-4, PMCID PMC13457603, inherited frozen source [source checksum]) supports that baseline hepatic reserve and post-locoregional-treatment decompensation are clinically relevant in another multicenter cohort. Moore et al. (Mayo Clinic Proceedings: Innovations, Quality & Outcomes 2025, DOI 10.1016/j.mayocpiqo.2025.100661, PMCID PMC12541609, frozen source [source checksum]) reported that admission bilirubin and its day-7 change were associated with 90-day mortality in 355 patients with HCC and acute decompensation. That study is not a TACE cohort and its endpoint is mortality, so it does not establish this question.

What remains untested is whether time-ordered routine laboratory change after observed TACE adds prognostic information in this HCC snapshot, and more specifically whether persistence at the discharge boundary adds information beyond early change. Neither study establishes a safe discharge threshold, benefit from delaying discharge, an unplanned return, or a causal TACE complication.

2. Freeze the TACE normalization dictionary from the full procedures source before participant filtering. Normalize whitespace/case. Include observed labels containing TACE/tace or observed arterial-chemoembolization terms such as chemoembolization, hepatic arterial chemoembolization, hepatic artery chemoembolization, and injection chemoembolization, including observed composite arterial chemoembolization labels. Exclude isolated hepatic arteriography or isolated hepatic artery embolization unless the same row also has a chemotherapy/embolization term. 3. Define the index as the earliest parseable Start Time per participant after this normalization. “First” means first observed in this snapshot, not first lifetime TACE. The index must join to the encounter. Surgery Source is descriptive and a sensitivity factor, never an instrument.
4. Require an index or earlier encounter diagnosis name containing the exact observed term hepatocellular carcinoma. Obtain the diagnosis encounter date only through the encounters join using encounter time; preserve diagnosis type descriptively. This lexical eligibility rule is not cancer adjudication.
5. Require index start time <= 2025-12-02 23:59:59 for a complete 30-day calendar window. Require discharge time >= start time. The primary cohort requires complete core baseline and early laboratory blocks. The nested cohort additionally requires discharge time - start time >= 96 hours and the density gate below.
6. Define a subsequent-admission event as another encounter for the same patient master index with nonmissing admission time strictly after index discharge time and no later than 30 days after it; retain its encounter number for deduplication and exclude the index encounter. Do not label it unplanned, treatment-related, or caused by TACE. A 90-day admission outcome is secondary only for indexes no later than 2025-10-03 23:59:59. No later record is administrative censoring, not proof of no event.

## Laboratory exposure blocks

Use only exact test labels:

- core: total bilirubin, albumin, international normalized ratio;
- expanded secondary: alanine aminotransferase, aspartate aminotransferase, creatinine, platelet count.

Conservatively parse Quantitative Result as numeric. Retain Qualitative Result, Specimen Type, unparseable values, missing timestamps, and non-target labels in the availability audit. Include a value only after exact two-key encounter verification and only when Test Time is inside its window.

For each participant and assay calculate the median of valid values in:

- baseline: [TACE start time - 14 calendar days, TACE start time);
- early: [TACE start time, TACE start time + 72 hours);
- actual discharge: [discharge time - 24 hours, discharge time);
- placebo discharge: [discharge time - 48 hours, discharge time - 24 hours).

The preserved early trajectory is early median - baseline median for each core assay. The discharge residual is actual-discharge median - early median. The placebo residual is placebo median - early median. Use half-open intervals in implementation to prevent duplicate assignment. D1 is primary in the actual-discharge density-gated cohort. P1 is evaluated only in the paired subset that also meets the same two-per-assay density requirement in the placebo window; report this additional attrition rather than silently changing the D1 cohort.

The primary discharge density gate requires, for every core assay, at least two valid observations at two distinct inspection time values in the early window separated by at least 12 hours, and at least two valid observations at distinct inspection time values in the actual-discharge window separated by at least 8 hours. Baseline needs at least one valid measurement per core assay, as in the parent. Report the number lost at each assay/window gate, timestamp span, missingness, and selection by calendar period and care department. If the stricter gate leaves no adequate cohort, the discharge hypothesis is inconclusive; do not silently revert to a single-measurement result as confirmatory. A one-measurement version may be reported only as a clearly labeled exploratory sensitivity.

Use raw assay-specific differences only for descriptive tables. In each training fold, robust-standardize each assay feature with training-fold median/IQR. Do not calculate ALBI, Child–Pugh, MELD, a summed laboratory score, or a clinical cutoff: units are absent and cross-assay clinical scoring is unsupported. Expected directions (not imposed) are higher bilirubin, INR, ALT, AST, creatinine and lower albumin/platelets as adverse.

## Baselines and estimands

The preserved primary model is a patient-level baseline-only logistic model for the 30-day subsequent-admission phenotype containing age, sex, and index department from encounters; the three baseline core assay medians from labs; count of prior encounters in the 365 days before TACE start time, using visit time; and prior recorded indicators for cirrhosis, portal-vein thrombosis, ascites, diabetes, and prior liver surgery. Build diagnosis indicators by joining diagnoses to dated encounters and freezing observed lexical dictionaries for diagnosis name; build prior surgery from the frozen observed procedure dictionary in procedures. These are recorded-code/utilization markers, not adjudicated comorbidities.

The primary trajectory model adds the three core early-baseline assay changes. Its incremental estimand is the global likelihood-ratio comparison with baseline-only, supplemented by adjusted odds ratios per training-fold IQR, representative-pattern absolute risk differences, AUROC, AUPRC, Brier score, and calibration intercept/slope. Use patient-level bootstrap intervals and strictly out-of-fold metrics.

The nested discharge comparison is:

- D0: the same baseline block plus the three early assay medians (a predeclared baseline-plus-early parameterization);
- D1: D0 plus the three actual-discharge residuals;
- P1: D0 plus the three placebo-discharge residuals.

The primary nested estimand is D1’s incremental out-of-fold Brier/LR contribution over D0. P1 is a temporal-specificity falsification, not a second positive hypothesis. Do not adjust the primary models for length of stay, post-index medications, post-index orders, or later labs. A length-of-stay adjustment is sensitivity only because stay duration and monitoring can be downstream of the post-TACE course.

Use patient-level five-fold cross-validation in discovery buckets 0–79; all imputation, robust scaling, tuning, and feature selection occur inside training folds. Add a chronological derivation split (2010–2022) and test split (2023–2025-12-02), preserving participant separation. The reserved buckets are inaccessible and are not external validation. A bounded elastic-net logistic model is primary; a shallow gradient-boosted model can be a prespecified functional-form sensitivity with identical feature blocks. Model complexity and small discrimination gains are not clinical utility.

## Event adequacy, falsification, and interpretation

Before fitting inferential models, publish participant and event counts after every gate. Require at least 20 subsequent-admission events for an inferential early or nested model; if the gate fails, report descriptive distributions and uncertainty only. Require at least 20 liver-coded events before any liver-coded inferential model; otherwise it is descriptive/inconclusive regardless of p-value. Penalization cannot rescue an inadequate event count. The inherited full-file feasibility audit reported 404 core-complete discharge cases and 8 liver-coded signals before discovery filtering; these are not results and must be recomputed.

Call the preserved early hypothesis supportive only if all are met:

1. the prespecified trajectory block has global LR p < 0.05;
2. optimism-corrected out-of-fold Brier improvement over baseline has a 95% bootstrap interval excluding zero;
3. direction and calibration are not reversed in the chronological split;
4. gain is not confined to the high-capacity sensitivity, dense-testing subgroup, or in-sample estimates, and the participant-level timestamp shuffle/reversal attenuates the trajectory signal.

Call it adverse/falsified if there is no held-out incremental evidence, the Brier interval includes zero, the chronological result reverses, or the gain persists equally after timestamp randomization in a way consistent with misaligned testing rather than time order. No clinical-utility threshold is inferred from this statistical criterion. If AUROC improves but held-out calibration/Brier worsens, it is not supportive.

Call the sharpened discharge hypothesis supportive only when:

1. the repeated-measurement density gate and at least 20 all-cause events pass;
2. D1 improves held-out Brier over D0 with a 95% bootstrap interval excluding zero and has prespecified global LR evidence;
3. the direction/calibration conclusion is not reversed chronologically and is stable under minimum-gap and complete-case/missingness sensitivities;
4. D1’s increment is greater than, or clearly distinguishable from, P1’s placebo increment in a paired bootstrap comparison; and
5. the increment is not eliminated by a surveillance-aware sensitivity that adds early/discharge valid-value counts and distinct timestamp counts from labs to both D0 and D1. This sensitivity is not interpreted causally.

The discharge hypothesis is adverse if D1 has no held-out increment, P1 is as large as D1, the gain disappears after density handling, or the apparent signal remains under timestamp randomization. Early trajectory may still be retained as a narrower prognostic association if the discharge test is adverse.

Call either hypothesis inconclusive if the TACE dictionary cannot be reproduced, assay meaning/units cannot be validated, timestamps or two-key joins fail, the density/event gate fails, complete-case selection is strongly calendar/department dependent, or uncertainty cannot distinguish no incremental information from a meaningful predictive signal. Sparse liver-coded outcomes remain inconclusive even if all-cause admission is supportive.

Supportive computation would justify a prospective study with laboratory units/provenance, treatment intent and scheduling, tumor stage and burden, reliable outpatient capture, clinically adjudicated decompensation, mortality ascertainment, and a prespecified monitoring/discharge pathway. Adverse computation would argue against treating routine trajectory as a stand-alone escalation rule. Inconclusive computation identifies missing data and a need for expert review or another study. None of these outcomes establishes causal TACE harm/benefit, unplanned return, safe discharge, recurrence, response, mortality benefit, or clinical utility.
