# Dynamic within-stay care-plan transitions and persistence: an inconclusive transportability test

## Question and advance

The assigned parents established that the two exact Ventilation care-plan values are sparse, hospital-concentrated, and do not reproducibly improve hospital-held-out prediction of a later structured respiratory-setting reduction; exhaustive outcome-blind vocabulary expansion likewise did not rescue the field. The remaining clinically consequential question is narrower and temporally distinct: when a qualifying Ventilation care-plan value is newly received, persists from the preceding day, or changes from the latest prior value, does that within-stay transition state add reproducible and hospital-transportable information beyond prior respiratory state, prior label history, hospital, and generic documentation opportunity?

The testable alternative is that persistence, workflow concentration, sparse support, or measurement limitations make dynamic use non-informative or unstable. A positive result could justify prospective workflow and semantic adjudication of dynamic features. A negative result would discourage treating transitions as a generic multicenter phenotype. Neither result validates bedside ventilation, an extubation evaluation, SBT, readiness, quality, benefit, harm, or causality.

## Analysis

Each definition is restricted to complete outcome windows with observed `robust_reduction`. The process/history model contains inherited pre-landmark respiratory state, landmark, current generic documentation controls, prior generic documentation controls, and prior Ventilation history. Static models add current exact or broad identity; dynamic models add state-plus-current identity. Logistic regression uses one-hot categorical encoding with unknown levels ignored, median imputation with missing indicators, standardization, `C=1`, no class weighting, and `max_iter=2000`.

Validation is leave-one-hospital-out among hospitals with at least 20 rows and both outcome classes. Primary comparisons are micro and equal-hospital macro log loss for dynamic versus same-encoding static and process/history models. All repeated landmarks for a stay are clustered in resampling. The intended uncertainty procedure requests 100 nested within-hospital stay-cluster refit replicates with hospitals fixed.

## Executed results

The completed accelerated execution used 20 nested replicates rather than the intended 100; all 20 completed for every definition with zero failed fold attempts. Therefore the estimates below are feasibility evidence and do not satisfy the full 100-replicate precision gate.

Analysis rows were 3,296, 2,273, 1,555, and 1,018 for unbounded, 24-hour, 12-hour, and 6-hour definitions. Exact current receipts were 98, 82, 65, and 38 rows; broader receipts were 101, 85, 67, and 39. New plus transition rows for exact/broad encodings were respectively 74/78, 58/62, 43/46, and 27/29. Receipt concentration was high: largest-hospital shares were 0.704/0.713, 0.780/0.788, 0.800/0.806, and 0.684/0.692 for exact/broad encodings. No definition met the prespecified support requirement of at least 20 new/transition rows with new and transition represented in at least three hospitals, at least three hospitals having five receipts and two state classes, and largest-hospital share at most 0.60.

Point estimates were mixed rather than uniformly adverse. Exact dynamic minus static micro/macro log-loss deltas were -0.00090/-0.00033, -0.00140/-0.00050, +0.00010/+0.00042, and +0.00063/+0.00001 across the four definitions. Broad dynamic minus static deltas were -0.00066/+0.00038, -0.00136/+0.00015, -0.00006/+0.00040, and +0.00051/-0.00010. Exact dynamic minus process-history macro deltas were +0.00009, +0.00017, +0.00151, and +0.00362; broad values were +0.00089, +0.00097, +0.00155, and +0.00328. Thus dynamic encodings did not beat the process/history baseline in macro loss in any definition.

The 20-replicate 97.5th percentiles for dynamic-versus-static macro loss crossed zero in every definition and encoding. For exact they were 0.00282, 0.00133, 0.00783, and 0.00430; for broad they were 0.00222, 0.00188, 0.00782, and 0.00461. Probabilities of broad dynamic being worse than broad static were 0.75, 0.55, 0.70, and 0.35; these do not establish an adverse gate, especially with only 20 replicates. The prespecified overall classification is `inconclusive_dynamic_fitness`: no supportive variant, but the adverse rule is not met because the dynamic-versus-static macro-worse threshold is not reached in at least two bounded variants for both encodings.

## Interpretation and limits

The strongest supported conclusion is computational: in this frozen eICU snapshot, current Ventilation care-plan receipts are sparse and site-concentrated, and the observed dynamic encodings do not provide robust macro-transportable improvement beyond process/history controls in this execution. The result is not evidence that transitions are clinically useless, because support is limited and the full intended 100 nested replicates were not completed. It is also not evidence of harm. A supportive result would require adequate multisite new/transition support and consistent improvement with all prespecified 97.5th percentiles below zero. An adverse result would require replicated worsening under the stated gate. The present result is inconclusive and should be treated as a feasibility warning.

Clinical adjudication remains essential. Care-plan values and `robust_reduction` are exported structured-documentation states. Stronger claims require blinded chart review, local interface/data-dictionary review, direct time-resolved airway and device evidence, SBT/extubation adjudication, and prospective external validation. Hospitals were fixed, so the computation does not establish transport to unseen hospitals.
