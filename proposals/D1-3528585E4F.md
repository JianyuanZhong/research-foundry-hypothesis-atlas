# Live ICU discharge: selected-population prognosis with explicit exit-selection stress

## Scientific deliverable and substantive successor

The future Harbor solver must newly produce a selection-audited, competing-risk analysis of the live-ICU-discharge transition:

1. a reproducible cohort flow for first eligible live ICU discharges, plus a pooled ICU-stay-hour risk set describing who reaches a live ICU exit and where selection-score overlap is absent;
2. the parent’s twelve-bin late-window physiology and separate observation/testing-process representations, with row-level clinical-time provenance;
3. paired held-out predictions from the same latest-state/context baseline B0, order-blind history model B1, process-only Bmask, and chronological masked GRU, all targeting the same first-transition outcome;
4. an outcome-free live-exit selection score estimated from all at-risk ICU-stay hours, with prespecified overlap-restricted and stabilized selection-weight sensitivity analyses;
5. a negative-control model for near-term live ICU exit itself, testing whether the purported history signal is primarily a discharge-process signal;
6. fixed-workload review/monitoring operating tables, evaluated for R/D event capture and calibration across selection-score, care-unit, and anchor-year strata; and
7. a conclusion audit mapping every claim to computed output and explicitly separating prognostic information from discharge appropriateness, preventability, intervention effect, net benefit, and transportability.

Completion is established by these newly fitted/estimated outputs, their uncertainty and falsifications—not by a favorable metric, readiness, or confirmation of the hypothesis. No fitted result is claimed in this proposal.

This is a substantive child of the assigned assessed parent `[prior hypothesis]`. The material advance is an explicit estimand/selection separation: the primary quantity is prognosis conditional on an observed eligible live ICU exit; an odds-tilt toward the pooled at-risk-hour frame is only a noncausal sensitivity; and post-exit hospital discharge/untimed death are treated as ascertainment boundaries rather than silently folded into “no event.” It also removes stay-end `last_careunit` from every primary feature matrix and requires a time-local transfer-derived unit when one is unambiguous. The clinically important question and fair baseline-versus-chronology comparison are preserved; no fitted result is claimed.

## Unresolved question, strongest supported claim, and hypothesis

The strongest claim supported by the parent design is only an observational one: in the specified MIMIC-IV records, a terminal state plus recent physiology and observation process may contain reproducible information about the first ascertainable post-ICU transition. A history-versus-snapshot improvement, even if observed, does not establish that ICU discharge selected a collider-free population, that instability caused the outcome, that discharge was premature, or that review/monitoring would change it.

The unresolved question is whether a late-history signal at live ICU discharge is robust to the process that selected patients into that index, and whether it can at least serve as a calibrated, workload-constrained candidate for post-ICU review or monitoring. The selection concern is concrete: the primary rows exist only for ICU stays that ended at an observed `outtime`; a late trajectory may be related to the clinician/team decision to exit the ICU, and live discharge changes which future ICU opportunities are observable. Parent coverage and outcome-ascertainment weights do not resolve this index-selection problem.

Prespecified hypothesis:

> Among eligible first live ICU discharges, the final 12-hour trajectory contains information about the 48-hour first competing transition to same-admission ICU rescue (R) or timed in-hospital death (D), beyond terminal physiology, admission/ICU context, and observation process; this information will retain direction and calibration under prespecified live-exit selection stress and will improve R/D case capture at the same fixed review workload. A signal that is explained by the live-exit score, measurement process, or discharge-boundary transition will not satisfy the hypothesis.

The hypothesis has two linked but separable tests. The scientific test is history information for R/D risk. The operational test is whether a frozen score identifies more observed R/D cases at 5%, 10%, or 20% review workload with acceptable held-out calibration and stability. The latter is an operating characteristic only; it is not evidence that a review intervention has benefit.

## Population, temporal boundaries, and labels

### Estimands and index definition

Let A=1 denote an observed eligible live ICU exit and C=1 denote adequate, prespecified pre-index feature coverage. The primary prognostic estimand is the selected-population distribution P(T≤t, J∈{R,D,S} | A=1,C=1), with cause-specific cumulative-incidence functions for R, D, and S through 48 hours after t0, plus derived R-or-D risk. The model comparison estimates held-out operating characteristics for this conditional population; it does not estimate the outcome under an alternative discharge time or policy.

The selection-stress estimand is deliberately different: apply a prespecified, training-only odds tilt to the same A=1,C=1 cases so their measured covariate distribution is less concentrated in high-exit-odds patterns of the pooled at-risk ICU-hour frame. It is a sensitivity family for index-selection fragility, not a causal inverse-probability estimator and not a target population that was actually observed. No conclusion is valid if the tilt has poor overlap or low effective sample size.

### Index population and strict time boundary

Use one index per hospital admission: the first ICU stay in `icu/icustays` that ends in an observed live exit, has valid `intime < outtime`, and satisfies the coverage rule. Call `t0=outtime`. Define an observed live exit only when the stay has an `outtime` and there is no admission `deathtime` at or before `t0`, and a missing `deathtime` is not paired with `hospital_expire_flag=1`; that flag/time combination is unknown and retained in an untimed-death/eligibility audit. A stay ending at or before timed death is not an eligible live exit. Multiple admissions from one subject are allowed, but all admissions from a subject remain in one partition.

All primary prediction values obey a strict pre-index rule: chart/lab observations require clinical time in `[t0-12h,t0)`, interval treatments require overlap with that half-open window and are clipped at `t0`, and no event at exactly `t0` is used as a pre-index feature. The same strict rule is used at candidate hour `h` for the selection model, replacing `t0` with `h`. A separate flow reports all ICU stays, invalid/missing times, timed/non-timed exits, coverage exclusions, and live-exit indices so selection and coverage are not hidden in the denominator.

The primary observation window is `[t0,t0+48h)`. The first transitions are:

- R: a later ICU stay in the same admission, with a different `stay_id` and `intime > t0`, whose `intime` is before `t0+48h`;
- D: timed in-hospital death with admission `deathtime` in `(t0,t0+48h]`, assigned before R if the recorded times tie or death precedes the later ICU `intime`;
- S: `admissions.dischtime` in `(t0,t0+48h]` while no earlier R or D occurred, an ascertainment-boundary transition rather than a safe outcome;
- N: no R, D, or S by 48 hours while the admission remains ascertainable;
- U: unresolved timed-death ascertainment, including `hospital_expire_flag=1` with missing `deathtime`, handled as unknown rather than N.

R/D/S use first-event ordering and cause-specific hazards. The primary scientific contrast is R-or-D, with component R and D reported separately; S and N are retained to expose ascertainment. Report fixed-horizon R-or-D and 7-day analogues only as prespecified sensitivities. Do not use post-`t0` records as prediction features, and do not infer a later ICU stay from an admission-level flag.

## Representations and fair method comparison

Use a deterministic subject-level approximately stratified 70/15/15 train/validation/test split, frozen before outcome-based thresholding. No subject can cross partitions. All scaling, missing-value rules, clipping, feature selection, weight models, calibration, thresholds, and q-score strata are fit or selected on training/validation only.

B0, the transparent baseline, is a penalized discrete-time cause-specific hazard regression with three heads/hazards for R, D, and S. It receives common context:

- `anchor_age, gender, anchor_year_group`;
- `first_careunit`, ICU duration at `t0`, an unambiguous transfer-derived current `careunit` covering `t0` when available, admission type/location, and earlier ICU-stay count in the admission;
- pre-window observation/process summaries;
- terminal-bin values and masks for the core bedside domains;
- terminal lab-testing, Foley/output, and vasoactive process indicators.

B1 is the substantive order-blind alternative: exactly B0’s context and terminal inputs plus all-12-bin summaries (mean, median, minimum, maximum, SD/IQR, abnormal-bin fraction, valid-bin count, observation count/missingness, pressor presence/minutes, and testing/output indicators). It tests whether history adds information without chronology.

Bmask is the mechanism control: B0 plus the complete history of observation masks, observed counts, laboratory testing, Foley charting, and vasoactive interval indicators/minutes, with physiologic values removed. If Bmask matches B1, the result is plausibly documentation/testing intensity rather than recoverable physiology.

The chronological alternative is a small masked GRU over the identical twelve bins, with eight numeric domains, masks, fixed abnormal flags, observed-domain count, and timed process indicators, plus the identical context at the final representation. Use three cause-specific hazard heads, hidden size 32 or 64, validation-only dropout/early stopping selection, and five fixed seeds. B0, B1, Bmask, and GRU share the same outcome, source whitelist, split, held-out metrics, and uncertainty. The GRU can reveal persistence, recovery, reversal, fluctuation, and coupled order that B1’s order-blind summaries lose. Its neural form and any GPU use are not merit criteria.

The primary scientific contrast is B1 versus B0; physiology-specific contrast is B1 versus Bmask; temporal contrast is GRU versus B1. The selection q model and negative-control model are mechanism analyses, not replacements for these contrasts.

## Live-exit selection stress test

The q analysis is an index-selection mechanism audit, not a second clinical estimand. Construct a pooled candidate-hour frame from every valid `icu/icustays` row. Let the candidate grid be `h=intime+k*1 hour`; retain a row when `h >= intime+12h` and `h < outtime`. Use only complete one-hour intervals for negative rows; if a stay terminates during an interval, retain that interval only when it contains an observed live exit, otherwise censor it at termination and do not label it a negative. This prevents administrative truncation from being treated as evidence of no live exit.

Define A_h=1 when a clinically timed live `outtime` falls in `[h,h+1h)` and the stay is not known to have died by that time. Define q(h)=P(A_h=1 | X^-_h, at risk at h). Features X^-_h are strictly before h: the 12-hour physiologic/process history and masks, elapsed ICU time, `first_careunit`, an unambiguous current care unit from `hosp/transfers`, admission type/location, and demographic context. `hosp/transfers` uses `(subject_id,hadm_id,transfer_id,eventtype,careunit,intime,outtime)` and only an interval satisfying `intime <= h < outtime`; ambiguous overlaps become an explicit missing/current-unit category. Exclude `outtime`, `los`, `last_careunit`, `discharge_location`, `dischtime`, `deathtime`, `hospital_expire_flag`, future transfers, and all post-h values.

Fit q on the training subject partition with a pooled discrete-time logistic hazard model and training-only preprocessing. If controls are sampled, record the sampling fraction and use an offset/weight. Evaluate q calibration, quantiles, event flow, and overlap for the actual selected live-discharge rows. For each index, use the grid interval containing `t0`; features are frozen before its start h. A boundary audit must prove that the live-exit label is never used in X^- and that a partially observed terminal interval is not a negative control.

Report three clearly labeled estimands/results: (i) primary selected-population predictions with no q weighting; (ii) q-overlap-restricted predictions over the common training-derived support; and (iii) the sensitivity odds tilt w_sel = [q0/(1-q0)]·[(1-q)/q], where q0 is the training-only q model coarsened to elapsed-time bin, first/current care unit, anchor-year group, and admission type. Report untruncated and prespecified training-derived truncation, overlap, positivity, and subject-level effective sample size. Refit/recompute q and weights within bootstrap replicates where feasible. The tilt asks how conclusions change when selected exits are downweighted in unusually high-exit-odds patterns; it does not recover counterfactual post-discharge risk, correct collider bias, or estimate a discharge policy effect. Extreme weights, poor q overlap, or low ESS make the stress result inconclusive rather than permitting a stronger claim.

### Negative-control selection endpoint

Using the same data-time whitelist and subject split, fit B0/B1/GRU representations at candidate ICU hours to predict live ICU exit within six hours. This endpoint is a discharge-process outcome, not a clinical outcome. A large B1/GRU gain for it, paired with weak or q-unstable R/D gain, supports a process-selection explanation. It does not show that discharge was inappropriate. The negative-control model must not use `outtime` or any label-derived field as a feature; its held-out calibration/discrimination and label-permutation behavior are reported.

## Outcomes, uncertainty, and review operating characteristics

For each primary model report held-out selected-population transition-aware Brier scores for R, D, S, and derived P_N; Brier for A=R-or-D; 12/24/48-hour cumulative-incidence calibration; calibration intercept/slope; AUROC/AUPRC for A and component causes; paired risk differences; and subject-bootstrap 95% intervals. For the observational trajectory association, report cause-specific coefficients and standardized 48-hour CIF contrasts per training IQR only as descriptive summaries; the primary claim is the selected-population prognostic operating characteristic. Repeat every summary under q-overlap and odds-tilt sensitivities with uncertainty. These are not treatment, discharge-appropriateness, or counterfactual policy effects.

Choose review thresholds only in training/validation to flag exactly 5%, 10%, and 20% of live-discharge cases. On held-out data report achieved workload, R/D capture/sensitivity, PPV, calibration in flagged and unflagged groups, risk distributions, and component-event capture for B0, B1, and GRU. Repeat unweighted and under the selection sensitivity. Report rank correlation and top-k overlap between unweighted and selection-weighted predictions, and the same tables within q-overlap strata, first/current care-unit strata, and `anchor_year_group`. These thresholds describe a possible review queue; they do not estimate net benefit, workload harm, or the effect of review/monitoring.

A review candidate is operationally promising only if it improves calibrated held-out R/D capture over B0 at the same workload, has no important calibration degradation, and retains rank/capture patterns across q strata, care units, anchor-year groups, and reasonable ascertainment/coverage definitions. This criterion is deliberately weaker than clinical utility and cannot authorize a care change.

## Falsification, interpretation, and limits

Required checks:

1. Every derived feature has `clinical_time<t0` (or `<h` for q); `last_careunit` and any stay-end field are absent from every primary matrix; input intervals are clipped using `starttime/endtime`; no forbidden post-boundary field enters a matrix; and all joins, duplicate rows, malformed intervals, and partition assignments are audited.
2. Permute live-exit labels within prespecified training risk-set strata and refit q. Selection discrimination/calibration structure and q-derived sensitivity should collapse toward chance; persistence suggests leakage or an evaluation defect.
3. Shuffle preterminal bins 1–11 within held-out subjects while preserving terminal bin, masks, context, and endpoints. A chronology claim should attenuate; all-bin shuffle is secondary.
4. Compare Bmask with B1. A matched Bmask increment makes a physiology-specific interpretation adverse.
5. Repeat valid-bin thresholds 4/6/8, coverage weighting, complete ascertainment, all-eligible weighting, and adverse/non-adverse U bounds.
6. Audit the selected-population estimand separately from q-overlap and odds-tilt results; compare unweighted, q-overlap, and stabilized selection-weight results. A change in target population must not be described as a causal correction. Sign reversal, large calibration loss, or poor overlap/ESS is evidence of fragility.
7. Repeat R/D/S competing transitions with fixed-horizon R-or-D and 7-day sensitivities; a gain confined to S or P_N is not support for adverse residual physiology.
8. Permute outcome transitions within each partition under a fixed seed and refit. Persistent held-out improvement indicates leakage/evaluation failure.
9. Evaluate the negative-control six-hour live-exit model and its label permutation.

Supportive evidence for selected-population prognostic information requires:

- B1 or GRU improves held-out transition-aware Brier and calibrated R/D CIF over its appropriate comparator, with uncertainty excluding a prespecified null improvement;
- the R/D gain is not confined to S or P_N and survives coverage, timed-death, ascertainment, and q-overlap/weighted sensitivities without material calibration collapse;
- B1 exceeds Bmask for a physiology-specific interpretation;
- if chronology is claimed, GRU exceeds B1 and the gain attenuates under preterminal shuffle; and
- for the review candidate, fixed-workload R/D capture and calibration are better than B0 and stable across the prespecified strata.

A strong q/negative-control signal alone is not disproof. But if R/D history gain disappears after q stress, is isolated to S/P_N, tracks care-unit/measurement intensity, or Bmask matches B1, the adverse interpretation is that discharge opportunity or documentation explains the apparent increment. Selection-weight sign reversal, extreme weights, low ESS, shuffle resistance, label-permutation failure, or unstable fixed-workload capture are also adverse or invalidate the corresponding claim.

Results are inconclusive with high U fraction, low R/D counts, sparse valid bins, malformed transfer or interval timing, poor q overlap, extreme weights, low ESS, unstable calibration, contradictory endpoint flow, or intervals too wide to distinguish the prespecified alternatives. Inconclusive means the question was not resolved.

Computationally checkable claims are source/member/schema/item provenance; joins and timing; cohort and candidate-hour flow; q model, sampling offsets and weights; overlap/ESS; labels and U handling; split integrity; feature whitelist; model configurations; held-out predictions; Brier/CIF/calibration; bootstrap intervals; fixed-workload tables; and falsification outputs.

Unavailable or non-identifiable claims include clinician discharge intent, appropriateness of ICU transfer, goals of care/treatment limitations, preventability, ward surveillance, staffing/bed pressure, why tests were ordered or omitted, whether charted oxygen equals delivered physiology, whether missing death time represents a particular course, whether review/monitoring changes outcomes, net clinical benefit, causal effects, and external validity. These require clinical adjudication, stronger external ascertainment, and preferably prospective/interventional evaluation.

## Method selection, alternatives, and resources

The actual deliverable is the newly fitted selection score q, its explicitly noncausal selection-stress estimands, paired B0/B1/Bmask/GRU competing-risk predictions, negative-control exit predictions, fixed-workload operating tables, uncertainty, falsifications, and conclusion audit. The parent’s B0/B1/GRU design remains the fair scientific comparison, with the selected-population estimand frozen before fitting; the new q model is required to test selection robustness.

B0 is selected as the primary baseline because it is transparent, auditable, and directly tests whether the latest discharge state/context is adequate. B1 is the primary substantive alternative because it tests whether order-blind history adds information with the same covariates and target. The GRU is a prespecified secondary alternative because persistence/recovery/order is scientifically relevant and cannot be recovered from B1 summaries. Bmask is necessary to distinguish physiology from observation process. A transformer is deferred: twelve bins already give a direct aggregate-versus-chronology test, and additional capacity has no distinct estimand. A causal discharge-policy model is deferred because policy assignment, intent, ward monitoring, preventability, and intervention data are unavailable. Revisit the transformer only if a robust GRU-versus-B1 chronology signal remains with adequate events and longer dependence is clinically justified; revisit causal policy modeling only with adjudicated discharge decisions and policy/intervention data.

Discovery allowance is 7,200 seconds. The future solver planning envelope is 16 CPUs, 262,144 MiB memory, up to 8 GPUs, and 28,800 seconds. CPU archive aggregation, B0/B1, q fitting, and weighted competing-risk analysis are expected to be feasible but are unmeasured. The small 12-step GRU with five seeds is expected to be CPU-feasible but unverified; an allocated A100-SXM4-80GB may be used for repeated GRU seeds or bootstrap only if profiling threatens the envelope. Ordinary-shell CUDA visibility is not evidence against GPU feasibility. Pooled candidate-hour construction and 1,000 weighted subject-bootstrap replicates are unmeasured; the solver must preserve completed counts and mark execution-limited intervals rather than silently changing the question. No discovery model was fitted.
