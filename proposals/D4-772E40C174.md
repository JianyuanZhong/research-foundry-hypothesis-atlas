# UK Biobank successor: dated two-panel metabolic change and incident cardiometabolic multimorbidity

## Deliverable and hypothesis

The scientific deliverable is a pre-specified, date-bound estimate of whether a second observed metabolic panel adds clinically relevant information beyond the latest metabolic levels for identifying a new transition to cardiometabolic multimorbidity. A future solver must construct the eligible cohort, fit the nested baseline and trajectory models, estimate the trajectory association and its uncertainty, generate locked-test risk predictions, and report calibration, discrimination, and decision-curve contrasts. Completion requires a reproducible cohort/data audit, fitted outputs, uncertainty intervals, and an interpretation classified as supportive, adverse, or inconclusive under the rules below. No conclusion is valid from readiness checks alone.

The strongest claim supported by the inspected evidence is narrower: baseline or cumulative glycaemic/lipid indices and related metabolic measures are associated with later cardiometabolic multimorbidity, and recent UK Biobank work reports modest incremental prediction from composite metabolic indices. A 2026 UK Biobank cohort study of participants with MASLD reported associations of CTI-derived indices with incidence and progression of multimorbidity, and concluded that external validation and clinical-utility assessment were still needed (Tian et al., DOI 10.1186/s12933-026-03260-7, PMCID PMC13508244; acquired and read from the frozen Europe PMC XML). A 2026 analysis of ELSA and HRS reported associations for the HbA1c/HDL-C ratio and trajectory, but it was not an analysis of this UKB rectangular export or its repeated assay fields (Ji et al., DOI 10.3389/fnut.2026.1849520, PMCID PMC13357312; acquired and read). Neither establishes that a date-bound within-person change in the individual UKB assays adds robust information beyond the current level under a participant-level locked evaluation.

The falsifiable hypothesis is:

> In UK Biobank participants aged 40–69 who have no diabetes, major cardiovascular disease, or chronic kidney disease at the end of a valid repeated metabolic measurement interval, a prespecified worsening of HbA1c and triglycerides and/or decline in HDL cholesterol predicts a higher incidence of a second cardiometabolic disease domain than the latest panel alone, after adjustment for age, sex, adiposity, blood pressure, smoking, assessment center, and measurement availability.

This is an observational conditional-association and incremental-prediction estimand, not the causal effect of changing a biomarker and not evidence that a trajectory alert improves care.

### Exposure panel

For each participant, extract from main, joined by eid:

- date at panel 0: 53-0.0
- date at panel 1: 53-1.0
- HbA1c: 30750-0.0, 30750-1.0
- triglycerides: 30870-0.0, 30870-1.0
- HDL cholesterol: 30760-0.0, 30760-1.0
- creatinine: 30700-0.0, 30700-1.0
- cystatin C: 30720-0.0, 30720-1.0

The primary repeated panel requires valid numeric HbA1c, triglyceride, and HDL values at both assay instances and valid ISO dates in both 53-0.0 and 53-1.0. Require 365 <= (date_1 - date_0) <= 3650 days, with date_1 > date_0. The one-year minimum prevents a same-episode measurement difference from being described as a longitudinal trajectory; the ten-year upper bound excludes date/instance contradictions while retaining the expected repeat window. Report the full gap distribution and repeat the analysis with a two-year minimum as a sensitivity analysis.

A biomarker value is valid only when it parses to a finite numeric value and passes the field-specific physical domain check documented before fitting. For the three assay markers, values must be positive; blank cells and non-biological negative sentinel values are excluded as missing for these fields. This is not a global recoding rule: the UKB metadata explicitly says field labels, units, and negative-value meanings are not locally complete, so the solver must preserve raw values, record the exact exclusion counts by field/instance, and run a sensitivity audit in which all negative values are retained as missing without attempting to reinterpret their labels. No assay unit conversion may be invented; the solver must report the units observed in the source or mark unit-dependent clinical thresholds unavailable.

The primary trajectory covariates are date-normalized changes:

- delta_hba1c = (30750-1.0 - 30750-0.0) / gap_years
- delta_tg = (30870-1.0 - 30870-0.0) / gap_years
- delta_hdl = (30760-1.0 - 30760-0.0) / gap_years

Standardize each change using training-partition means and SDs only. Define the prespecified adverse-metabolic direction as positive standardized HbA1c change, positive standardized triglyceride change, and negative standardized HDL change. The primary trajectory score is their equal-weighted mean after reversing HDL change. The analysis must also report marker-specific coefficients; a composite alone cannot establish that all three markers behave consistently.

The latest-panel baseline values are the three instance-1 assays, transformed only as justified by their observed distributions (for example, log triglycerides if that transformation is frozen before fitting). Creatinine and cystatin C are covariates with missingness indicators, not part of the primary trajectory score. A secondary kidney/physical-reserve extension may use 30700-0.0/1.0 and 30720-0.0/1.0, but it must not alter the primary estimand.

## Population, index, and outcomes

Use only the discovery partition defined by the harness: participant-level buckets 0–79 for the study, with buckets 0–59 training, 60–69 validation, and 70–79 locked test. Reserved buckets 80–99 are never used for fitting, tuning, threshold choice, or model selection. The partition is generated from eid by the catalog’s SHA-256 namespace/dataset/eid rule; record the actual namespace and assignment manifest without publishing eids.

Eligibility:

1. population.21022-0.0 is 40 through 69 years inclusive.
2. The participant has a valid two-panel assay trajectory as defined above.
3. No qualifying diabetes, cardiovascular, or kidney-domain diagnosis has an occurrence date on or before date_1.
4. The participant is alive and free of the primary outcome at the end of the washout landmark, defined as date_1 + 365 days.
5. There is no date-order contradiction among panel dates, outcome dates, and death dates.

The index measurement date is date_1 = main.53-1.0; it is not the latest among instances 0–3. The primary risk origin is the 365-day landmark after date_1. Events in the washout are recorded and excluded from the primary risk set; analyses with zero-, two-, and five-year washouts are required to expose reverse-causation sensitivity. Follow-up ends at the first primary event, death, the latest trusted outcome date in the frozen snapshot, or administrative end. The future solver must state how participants who do not survive to the landmark are counted in a flow diagram and cannot treat their exclusion as evidence of low risk.

Clinical phenotype adjudication is unavailable; the outcome is a coded administrative phenotype.

## Estimand and locked analysis

The primary association estimand is the cause-specific hazard ratio for the primary two-domain endpoint per one training-partition SD increase in the trajectory score, conditional on the latest panel values and the prespecified covariates. The comparison is specifically whether the trajectory term remains informative after current levels and measurement-process variables are included. This is not a causal hazard ratio.

The primary prediction estimand is the difference in 10-year risk-prediction performance between a latest-panel baseline and a trajectory-aware alternative on the locked test buckets. Report integrated Brier score, time-dependent C-index/AUC, calibration intercept and slope, calibration plots, restricted mean event-free survival, and decision-curve net benefit at prespecified 10-year thresholds of 5%, 10%, and 20%. Use bootstrap resampling of participants within the locked test set for 95% intervals, preserving all preprocessing and model parameters from the training/validation split. Report subgroup calibration and performance by sex, age 40–54 versus 55–69, and number of available assessment instances.

### Simple baseline

Fit a Cox proportional-hazards model for the same risk origin, endpoint, censoring and eligible participants. Inputs are the latest assay levels (main.30750-1.0, 30760-1.0, 30870-1.0), latest available creatinine/cystatin C, age, sex, BMI, blood pressure, smoking, assessment center, and explicit missingness indicators. Use prespecified restricted cubic splines for continuous latest levels and no outcome-driven feature selection. Estimate robust standard errors and a calibration model at the fixed horizon.

This baseline deliberately compresses the two panels to the current snapshot. It is clinically useful because it approximates a conventional risk assessment and provides the null incremental-information comparator.

### Substantive learned alternative

Fit a compact date-aware longitudinal model on the same participant set and endpoint. Its inputs are the exact same raw exposure/covariate sources, represented as the ordered two-panel vectors (three primary assays, optional kidney assays, observation indicators, and exact gap in years), plus the same baseline covariates. The model may be a small recurrent/temporal-attention discrete-time multi-head hazard model with heads for first diabetes, first cardiovascular disease, first kidney disease, second-domain transition, and death. It must be trained only on buckets 0–59, early-stopped/tuned only on 60–69, and evaluated once on 70–79. The model must not ingest any outcome code/date occurring after the risk origin or any held-out-derived transformation.

The alternative is scientifically substantive because it can preserve the ordering and gap of the two measurements and test whether identical current levels have different implications depending on the direction and speed of prior change and the kidney context. The baseline loses this temporal information. The learned model is not selected for a small predictive gain: the scientific target is whether this temporal information is reproducibly useful and whether its effect is concentrated in clinically interpretable transitions. Report integrated gradients or prespecified perturbation analysis only as descriptive explanation; explanation is not causal mediation.

If the solver cannot justify neural training after the readiness audit, use a prespecified mechanistic alternative instead: a multistate illness-deepening model with states 0 domains, 1 domain (with domain identity), 2 domains, and death, using the same two-panel inputs and date gap as covariates. This alternative can reveal transition-specific associations—e.g., whether metabolic deterioration relates primarily to first diabetes versus progression from one domain to multimorbidity—that a single Cox endpoint loses. The mechanistic alternative is not a license to change the population, outcome, or estimand after seeing results.

## Falsification and interpretation

Supportive evidence requires all of the following:

- the primary trajectory score has a directionally concordant, clinically nontrivial association with the primary endpoint after current levels and measurement availability are included, with a 95% interval excluding the null;
- marker-specific effects are not mutually contradictory without a prespecified explanation;
- the trajectory-aware model improves locked-test calibration or integrated Brier score and shows net benefit at at least one prespecified threshold without materially worsening calibration in either sex, age, or repeat-measurement subgroup;
- the association and performance contrast persist in the zero-, two-, and five-year washout analyses, complete-case/missingness sensitivity analyses, and competing-death sensitivity; and
- the result is not reproduced for a non-cardiometabolic negative-control endpoint built from a frozen code family unrelated to the three disease domains, such as a prespecified injury/fracture phenotype, unless the solver can justify a different control from available codes before fitting.

Adverse evidence is a null or opposite trajectory association after latest levels and measurement intensity are included; materially unstable marker-specific estimates; no improvement or worse calibration on locked buckets; a gain that disappears under participant-level splitting or a longer washout; or a similar gain for the negative-control outcome. These results falsify the proposed incremental-information claim, not the biological harmlessness of metabolic change.

Inconclusive evidence is a failure of the source audit to establish paired date/value correspondence, too few participants with two valid panels or two-domain events for stable intervals, substantial unresolved code/date mismatching, a primary cohort dominated by one repeat-assessment subgroup, or confidence intervals too wide to distinguish clinically meaningful benefit from no benefit. Inconclusive means report the audit, counts, and uncertainty only; do not replace the design with a single-panel analysis and do not claim clinical utility.

Computationally checkable outputs are source/header and join audits, counts at every exclusion step, gap distribution, code/date matching, cohort flow, fitted coefficients, bootstrap intervals, held-out predictions, calibration, Brier/C-index/decision-curve results, and sensitivity estimates. Clinical adjudication or another study is required to establish that coded domains represent clinically confirmed disease, that repeat testing reflects ordinary care, that a monitoring intervention changes outcomes, or that the association is transportable beyond UKB. Medication changes, indications for repeat testing, treatment adherence, socioeconomic confounding, assay-specific units/reference ranges, and external validation are unavailable or incompletely verified here.

## Alternatives, demonstrations, and unavailable branches

The parent’s clinically important question is retained: does observed metabolic deterioration add information beyond the current level? The repair changes only the data representation and temporal claim—from an unverified four-instance biomarker trajectory to a verified two-panel, date-normalized change. No scientific claim is silently upgraded.

Alternatives developed before selection:

1. Current-level Cox baseline. Exact inputs and target are specified above; CPU fit and bootstrap calibration are expected to be feasible within the solver envelope. It is retained as the essential comparator.
2. Date-aware learned sequence model. Same cohort, outcome, split, and metrics; it can reveal speed/direction and transition-specific heterogeneity lost by the snapshot. Full training time and GPU benefit were not measured in discovery. A compact CPU run is the default feasibility path; an allocated A100 (cuda:0 inside the job) is optional only if the solver’s bounded probe shows material benefit.
3. Multistate mechanistic fallback. Same target/split/metrics and same two-panel inputs; reveals first-domain versus second-domain transition structure and is preferred over an arbitrary deep model if the learned model is not stable or informative. It is not a post-result method switch.
4. Proteomic organ-aging or infection-protein direction. Deferred: the UKB catalog has a biological_samples table but does not verify a ready-to-use organ-age indicator or the required labeled proteomic exposure in this snapshot.
5. Fat distribution/imaging. Deferred: no raw imaging dependency is verified. BMI/waist proxies cannot establish visceral fat distribution.
6. CHIP/genetic effect modification. Deferred: genomics is documented as derived/phenotype metadata rather than a verified raw variant/clone-size bundle; gene-specific CHIP claims would exceed the data.
7. Infection, sleep regularity, cancer treatment/staging, and disease-specific PRS branches. Deferred because hospitalization onset/severity, accelerometry-derived regularity, cancer treatment/stage, and exact disease-specific PRS/independent validation fields are not verified.
8. Demonstration dispositions. The Delphi natural-history transformer was read through the locally available article/repository evidence and is adopted only as a bounded conceptual precedent for date-aware sequence learning; no Danish validation or reproduction claim is made. The ALADYNOULLI longitudinal Bayesian method is not reproduced because the original genetic inputs are unavailable; its EHR-only latent structure is considered only as a conceptual mechanistic/longitudinal alternative. The Oncoformer demonstration’s main article and complete STAR Methods remain unavailable locally and the image modality is unavailable; therefore no multimodal reproduction or method claim is made, and a lab-only adaptation is not the direct test of this question. These dispositions follow references/research-ambition/README.md and methods-and-compute.md, which were inspected.
