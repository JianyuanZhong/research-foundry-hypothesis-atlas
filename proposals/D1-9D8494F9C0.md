# Early Post-TACE Hepatic Deterioration: Trajectory Shape vs. Endpoint Composite

## Question
In patients with hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) who undergo transarterial chemoembolization (TACE), does the temporal pattern of hepatic deterioration (trajectory shape: AUC and slope of bilirubin, albumin, and INR over days 1-7) add prognostic information beyond the fixed 7-day endpoint composite, after adjustment for baseline liver function, tumor burden, and post-procedure observation intensity?

## Clinical importance
TACE is a common locoregional treatment for HCC, but a subset of patients experience early hepatic deterioration that may signal a need for escalation to systemic therapy or closer surveillance. The fixed 7-day endpoint composite captures the magnitude of deterioration at day 7, but not the temporal pattern. Two patients with the same day-7 bilirubin level may have very different trajectories: one with a rapid early spike followed by partial recovery, and another with a slow, progressive rise. The trajectory shape may carry prognostic information beyond the endpoint composite, and this distinction could inform early intervention decisions (e.g., whether to escalate to systemic therapy at day 3 vs. day 7).

## Evidence and closest prior work
- [K1] Yang et al. (2026) demonstrate that dynamic liver function monitoring and ALBI grade have potential value in HCC patients, but Child-Pugh scoring has limitations for dynamic monitoring.
- [K2] Lan et al. (2026) demonstrate that dynamic biomarker trajectories (gene expression) can be used for risk prediction in liver disease, supporting the use of trajectory-based features.
- [K3] Tarbaghia et al. (2026) show that response to dobutamine in septic shock with myocardial dysfunction is associated with clinical outcomes, supporting the use of dynamic response measures for prognostic prediction.

## Data bindings
- **Population**: 20,064 TACE patients, ~16,000 with complete early labs, 817 outcome events
- **Procedure**: TACE (transarterial chemoembolization)
- **Laboratories**: bilirubin, albumin, INR (ALT/AST excluded due to zero early rows)
- **Tables**: HCC procedure table, HCC laboratory table, HCC outcome table
- **Join keys**: patient_id, procedure_id
- **Time fields**: procedure_date, lab_date, outcome_date
- **Outcomes**: 90-day hepatic decompensation, treatment failure, or death

## Design
### Baseline model (M0)
Cox proportional hazards model with baseline liver function (Child-Pugh, ALBI), tumor burden (BCLC stage, tumor size, number), and post-procedure observation intensity.

### Endpoint composite model (M1)
M0 + fixed 7-day endpoint composite (bilirubin, albumin, INR at day 7).

### Trajectory model (M2)
M0 + trajectory features (AUC and slope of bilirubin, albumin, and INR over days 1-7; 6 features total).

### Combined model (M3)
M0 + endpoint composite + trajectory features + interaction term between endpoint composite and trajectory features.

### Primary comparison
M3 vs. M1: Does trajectory shape add prognostic information beyond the endpoint composite?

### Incremental prediction metrics
- NRI (Net Reclassification Improvement)
- IDI (Integrated Discrimination Improvement)
- C-index difference

### Falsification criteria
- C-index difference < 0.01
- NRI < 0.05
- IDI < 0.01
- Non-robust to alternative windows (3-day, 5-day, 10-day)
- No decision-curve net benefit
- Non-significant interaction term

## Limitations
1. The trajectory features require complete early labs over days 1-7, which may reduce the effective sample size.
2. The nested Cox model assumes proportional hazards, which may not hold for all trajectory features.
3. The interaction term may be underpowered with 817 outcome events.
4. The decision-curve analysis requires a clinically meaningful threshold probability, which may be difficult to define.

## Method comparison
- **Baseline (M1)**: Fixed 7-day endpoint composite. Simple, interpretable, but loses temporal information.
- **Alternative (M3)**: Trajectory shape (AUC + slope). Captures temporal pattern, but more complex and requires more data.
- **Scientific rationale**: The trajectory shape may carry prognostic information beyond the endpoint composite. For example, a rapid early spike followed by partial recovery may have a different prognosis than a slow, progressive rise, even if the day-7 endpoint is the same.
- **Resource estimate**: CPU-only, ~1-2 hours for the nested Cox models and incremental prediction metrics.
- **Selection reason**: The trajectory-based alternative is scientifically substantive and tests a clinically consequential distinction (early intervention timing). The baseline is retained as a comparison.
