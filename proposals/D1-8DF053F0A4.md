# Episode 62 calibrated-utility successor: is late lactate decision-relevant after transportable recalibration?

## Scope and unresolved question

This is a substantive child of the exact-reconciled Episode 62 proposal (parent [prior hypothesis]). It preserves the audited 1,229-person, 507-death, 722-survivor, 33-hospital cohort, the early pressor landmark t0, L24=t0+1,440 minutes, the asymmetric laboratory revision clocks, all frozen predictors, the hospital-held-out analysis, and the four read-only EICU source files. The change is intentional and limited to the unresolved deployment limitation: the parent’s decision-curve net benefit is not interpretable at probability thresholds unless predicted risks are calibrated in a new hospital and the review action’s benefit-harm tradeoff is clinically validated.

At a fixed 24-hour reassessment after an early recorded norepinephrine/Levophed entry, does adding the released 12-to-24-hour lactate produce a material improvement in mortality-review prioritization value **after nested, hospital-held-out probability recalibration of both the comparator and augmented model**? The experiment also asks whether that mathematical decision-curve result survives a clinically valid threshold/utility definition. The latter cannot be learned from discharge status alone.

**Primary computable hypothesis (Hcal):** in the frozen cohort, the calibrated augmented model R1 has uniformly threshold-averaged net benefit at least **+1 net true-positive-equivalent mortality-review decision per 100 people** over calibrated physiology-augmented R0 across the fixed grid p=0.30,...,0.50, with both calibrated models satisfying the prespecified outer-hospital calibration and site-stability guardrails.

**Separate clinical-utility claim (Hutil):** the fixed p=0.30–0.50 grid represents a clinically meaningful mortality-review action and its implied harm-to-benefit exchange rate. Hutil is not computable from the configured EICU data. It becomes testable only after clinician/patient/stakeholder elicitation defines the review action, acceptable false-positive review burden, and a transportable threshold range. No Harbor result may silently treat Hcal as Hutil.

The primary estimand is therefore:

ΔINBcal = 100/(0.50−0.30) × integral from 0.30 to 0.50 of [NB(calibrated R1,p) − NB(calibrated R0,p)] dp,

evaluated by the trapezoid rule at all 21 fixed thresholds. Treat-all and treat-none are reference strategies; the labs-only late-lactate comparison is descriptive and cannot determine the primary label.

## What is already supported and what remains untested

The strongest available EICU result is the executed Episode 58 analysis: among 1,084 APACHE-IVa-complete people in 31 hospitals, adding observed late lactate to entry lactate and retrospective APACHE-IVa predicted mortality yielded ΔINB +1.982 per 100 (2,000-refit interval +0.483 to +3.351), with improved AUROC, Brier score and log loss and positive fixed-prediction hospital deletions. That supports a positive modeled increment against a sparse, partly untimestamped comparator. It does not establish the locked +1 margin for the present comparator, calibration in an unseen hospital, a useful clinical action, an ordering or treatment effect, or patient benefit.

The exact Episode 62 audit independently reconstructed the stricter cohort and established feasibility, not the Hcal result: 1,229 people, 507 deaths, 722 survivors and 33 hospitals; all three physiology streams exceeded the 80% coverage gate. The parent reports a positive but not yet deployment-interpretable decision-value question; it does not provide transportable recalibration.

Baysan et al. (2022, DOI 10.1097/CCE.0000000000000750; PMCID PMC9444407) was read from frozen full-text XML, source [source checksum], [source checksum]. Its reported 4,440-person sepsis analysis found that adding 24-hour lactate to recalibrated APACHE IV increased the C-statistic from 0.62 to 0.64. This supports modest serial prognostic information, not incremental decision value after the present laboratory-plus-physiology record or transportable calibration. Supplementary material was not inspected.

Ye et al. (2026, DOI 10.1186/s40842-026-00323-2; PMCID PMC13435416) was read from frozen full-text XML, source [source checksum], [source checksum]. Its abstract and relevant methods/results describe a random 7:3 internal validation in 1,394 patients coded with sepsis and heart failure, with lactate, respiratory rate and oxygen saturation among selected predictors. That supports joint prognostic relevance in a different selected population, not this fixed landmark, hospital-held-out calibration, or clinical utility. Supplementary material was not inspected.

Vickers et al. (PMCID PMC6261531) was read from frozen HTML, source [source checksum], [source checksum]. It explains that a decision threshold encodes the relative harm of a false positive and benefit of a true positive, and that the relevant threshold range depends on the clinical action. It does not validate mortality-review thresholds, the +1 margin, or any utility for this EICU question.

The three research-ambition demonstrations and their availability restrictions were inspected through references/research-ambition/README.md. They are not used as evidence. No unavailable main article or supplementary methods are claimed to have been read.

## Frozen population, clocks, outcome, and audit

No row-level sampling is allowed; chunked scans are memory management only.

1. Read patient.csv.gz. Parse age using the frozen rule: literal > 89 and numeric ages above 89 map to 90; retain age >=18. The audit found 200,234 adults from 200,859 rows, including 7,081 older-age mappings.
2. In infusionDrug.csv.gz, among adult stays find the earliest infusionoffset in [0,1,440] whose case-folded drugname contains the literal norepinephrine or levophed. This produced 185,658 qualifying adult infusion rows and 16,299 qualifying adult stays.
3. Per uniquepid, keep the lexicographically smallest (t0, patientunitstayid), yielding 14,786 people; define L24=t0+1,440.
4. Require hospitaldischargestatus in {Alive, Expired} and hospitaldischargeoffset >=L24. The intermediate counts were 14,672 known outcomes and 13,023 stays through L24.
5. Baseline lactate B is a valid row with trimmed/case-folded labname=lactate, labmeasurenamesystem=mmol/L, finite labresult in [0.2,30], labresultoffset in [t0−360,t0], and labresultrevisedoffset <=t0+60. At each collection time retain the greatest eligible revision; invalidate a collection if differing values tie at that final revision; collapse identical ties to the smallest labid; select the latest valid collection. There were 5,077 valid baselines; retain B>=2, leaving 3,117.
6. Follow-up lactate F uses the same analyte, units, range, revision and conflict rules, with labresultoffset in [t0+720,L24] and labresultrevisedoffset <=L24. Select the collection nearest t0+1,080, then the earlier collection, then smallest labid. There were 1,506 repeatedly measured people.
7. Apply the inherited outcome-blind hospital gate: each retained hospital has at least five people with clearance (1−F/B)>=0.20 and at least five below 0.20. Clearance is not a predictor, estimand, subgroup, or rescue analysis.
8. Freeze hospitals and define D=1 for hospitaldischargestatus=Expired, otherwise D=0.

The exact complete-source audit reproduces 1,229 people, 507 deaths, 722 survivors and 33 hospitals. It found one conflicting final-revision baseline collection, which was invalidated, and six identical final-revision duplicate baseline rows, which were collapsed; no final-revision follow-up conflict or duplicate remained. The earlier 1,255-person result caused by using L24+60 as a common revision deadline is prohibited. No person discharged exactly at L24. The endpoint is hospital discharge status after L24; EICU does not provide exact death time or cause.

The selected population is conditional on an early recorded pressor row, survival/continued hospitalization to L24, a clinician-obtained baseline and repeat lactate, data-release timing, and the inherited site gate. It is not a general ICU population.

## Frozen predictors and availability boundary

R0 contains the parent’s complete Episode 59 laboratory-plus-physiology feature set:

- age and age squared; Female and Male indicators with Other/Unknown omitted;
- t0/1,440 and its square; baseline collection lag (t0−B_offset)/360; ln(B) and ln(B)^2;
- follow-up measurement age (L24−F_offset)/720 in both models;
- for each fixed non-lactate key, latest valid value in [t0,L24], its measurement age, and a missing indicator;
- for each of heartrate, respiration and sao2 in vitalPeriodic, the last, median, minimum, maximum, count of unique valid timestamps, and no-valid-observation indicator from [L24−360,L24].

The fixed laboratory keys are potassium|mmol/L, sodium|mmol/L, chloride|mmol/L, BUN|mg/dL, glucose|mg/dL, creatinine|mg/dL, bicarbonate|mmol/L, calcium|mg/dL, Hgb|g/dL, platelets x 1000|K/mcL, WBC x 1000|K/mcL, albumin|g/dL, total bilirubin|mg/dL, paO2|mm Hg, paCO2|mm Hg, and pH with a missing or blank labmeasurenamesystem. R1 adds only ln(F) and ln(F)^2. It does not add clearance, B-by-F interaction, extra measurement counts, feature selection, or outcome-derived transformations.

For labs, coerce finite numeric values, use the fixed keys and apply greatest eligible revision, final-revision conflict invalidation, identical-tie smallest-labid resolution, and latest-valid-collection selection. For vitalPeriodic, accept heart rate 20–250/min, respiration 2–80/min, and sao2 40–100%; at each patient/timestamp invalidate contradictory valid values and collapse identical valid ties to smallest vitalperiodicid. Counts are resolved timestamps, not export rows. For a stream with no valid timestamp, values are missing, count is zero and its indicator is one. No complete-case deletion or stream removal is permitted.

The audit scanned 2,324,317 periodic-vital rows for cohort stays and 86,335 in the six-hour window. Post-QC patient coverage was heart rate 1,218/1,229 (99.10%), respiration 1,126/1,229 (91.62%), and SpO2 1,176/1,229 (95.69%); all exceed 80%. Future nonmissing vital rows were excluded (1,923,391 heart-rate, 1,677,524 respiration, 1,779,054 SpO2). Observationoffset <=L24 is the only available timing boundary: vitalPeriodic has no entry, validation, or display timestamp, so bedside visibility at the exact landmark is unavailable.

## Nested hospital-held-out calibration and models

The base prediction models remain exactly the parent’s models. Use 33 outer leave-one-hospital-out folds. In each outer fold h:

1. On the other 32 hospitals, fit R0 and R1 as unweighted L2 logistic regressions with intercept, C=1, lbfgs, max_iter=10,000, tol=1e−8, no class weighting, seed 20260961. Training-only transformations winsorize continuous predictors at the training 1st/99th percentiles, standardize with training mean and population SD, and mean-impute missing continuous values after scaling to zero; fixed missing indicators are not scaled. A feature with no finite training values or zero post-winsorization SD makes the fold noncomputable; no feature may be dropped.
2. Obtain the outer-hospital raw probabilities p0 and p1, one finite prediction per person.
3. Build inner out-of-fold predictions for every person in the 32-hospital training set using the same full R0/R1 feature definitions, model settings, preprocessing and hospital-held-out logic: each inner hospital is predicted by a model fit on the other inner hospitals. The outer hospital is never used in inner fitting or calibration.
4. Fit a prespecified two-parameter Platt recalibrator separately for R0 and R1 on the inner out-of-fold predictions: unweighted L2 logistic regression of D on (1, logit(praw)), with intercept, C=1, lbfgs, max_iter=10,000, tol=1e−8, no class weighting, seed 20260961. Apply the resulting transformation only to the outer raw predictions:
   qj,h = logistic(ah + bh logit(pj,h)), j∈{0,1}.
   Before every logit operation, clip a finite probability to [1e−15, 1−1e−15]; a nonfinite probability is a failed prediction, not a clipping opportunity.
   A failed or nonfinite inner fit is a calibration noncomputability event, not a reason to revert to raw predictions.
5. Pool the 33 outer sets of q0 and q1 into one honest calibrated prediction per person. Neither outer outcomes nor outer calibration results may influence preprocessing, model fitting, recalibration, thresholds, or feature decisions.

This nested calibration is a transportability test within the EICU hospital network, not external validation. It addresses calibration optimism from fitting and assessing on the same hospitals, but it cannot address a new health system, changed assay, new workflow, or drift beyond this snapshot.

Primary and sensitivity baselines:

- Primary: calibrated R1 versus calibrated R0.
- Required decision default guardrail: calibrated R1 must beat the better of calibrated R0, treat-all and treat-none at every fixed threshold.
- Calibration sensitivity: report raw R0/R1 and calibrated R0/R1; raw-versus-calibrated differences are descriptive and do not change Hcal.
- Descriptive only: the parent labs-only model and its late-lactate comparison; it cannot relabel Hcal.

At p in {0.30,0.31,...,0.50}, compute NB=TP/N−FP/N×p/(1−p). Treat-none is zero and treat-all uses pooled prevalence. Report all threshold cells, AUROC, Brier score, log loss, mean absolute probability change, calibration intercept/slope, and hospital-level calibration results.

## Calibration, utility, uncertainty, and computability gates

The primary calibration guardrails are locked before execution:

- pooled calibration-in-the-large for each qj: absolute risk-scale difference |mean(qj)−mean(D)| <=0.05;
- pooled calibration slope for each qj: 0.80–1.20, estimated by the prespecified logistic regression D ~ 1 + logit(qj) on outer predictions; a nonfinite fit is noncomputable;
- site calibration stability: at least 80% of the 33 hospitals have finite n_h, mean(qj,h), and observed death rate, with |mean(qj,h)−deathrate_h| <=0.10 for each model. This is a risk-scale transportability guardrail, not an assertion that sites are exchangeable;
- the primary ΔINBcal interval is estimated with a 2,000-replicate copied-hospital bootstrap using seed 20260961. Each replicate repeats outer fitting, inner out-of-fold calibration and evaluation; an original hospital and its bootstrap copies remain one held-out group and its copies are excluded from training. At least 95% of replicates must be finite and converged;
- fixed-prediction deletion diagnostics remove each hospital in turn and report ΔINBcal, calibration intercept and calibration slope; at least 80% of finite deletions must retain the direction of the full-cohort ΔINBcal. Deletions are diagnostics, not a rescue of a failed primary guardrail.

Computability additionally requires matching catalog/source hashes and headers; the exact 1,229/507/722/33 cohort; at least 1,000 people, 30 hospitals, 200 deaths and 200 survivors; both outcome values at every hospital; maximum hospital share <=15%; all physiology coverage gates; finite positive training variation for every feature in every fold; exactly one finite q0 and q1 per person; all 21 threshold cells; at least 95% finite bootstrap replicates; and at least 80% finite deletions. Any failure produces Not computable for Hcal. No fallback to raw, labs-only, pooled random split, or altered threshold is allowed.

Clinical utility is a separately gated evidence layer. Before calling any result clinically interpretable, an external blinded clinician/stakeholder exercise must specify:

- the actual action represented by a positive decision (for example, a defined mortality-risk review, not treatment or escalation);
- who performs it, time and resource burden, and what false-positive review displaces;
- the acceptable harm-to-benefit exchange rate or threshold range, with an auditable elicitation protocol and representation from multiple hospitals;
- whether the action is acceptable to patients/surrogates where relevant; and
- prospective order, display, review and action timestamps for validating that qj would be available before the action.

These fields are absent from EICU. Harbor must therefore output a separate `utility_status=not_computable` unless a future adjudication artifact supplies them. A mathematically positive ΔINBcal is not a clinical-benefit or cost-effectiveness result.

## Locked interpretation and falsification rules

Apply rules in this order after source and computation gates:

1. **Not computable (Hcal):** any source, header, cohort, timing, feature, fit, nested calibration, prediction, threshold, bootstrap, or deletion gate fails. Report the failed gate and do not substitute a raw or uncalibrated result. Separately, `utility_status=not_computable` is mandatory when the clinician/stakeholder utility artifact is absent; that status does not erase a computable Hcal result.
2. **Calibrated-model supportive:** Hcal’s copied-hospital 95% interval has lower bound >=+1; both models pass every pooled and site calibration guardrail; calibrated R1 beats the better default at all 21 thresholds; no threshold-specific calibrated ΔNB is below −0.5 per 100; at least 80% of site deletions are positive; and both Brier and log-loss point estimates improve. This supports only a calibrated modeled increment in this selected EICU cohort.
3. **Calibration-failed / clinically uninterpretable:** the calibrated ΔINBcal lower bound meets +1 but either model fails a calibration guardrail. The result is not supportive for a thresholded review model; it is evidence that an apparent modeled increment cannot be safely interpreted at the fixed probabilities in this dataset.
4. **Opposite:** the calibrated ΔINBcal 95% upper bound is <0. This opposes the incremental modeled decision-value claim for the fixed comparator and setting, not every possible model or action.
5. **Adverse to materiality:** the calibrated ΔINBcal upper bound is <+1 but >=0. This refutes the locked +1 materiality claim for this fixed setting, not all smaller increments.
6. **Inconclusive:** none of the preceding result branches applies, including an interval spanning +1, an unstable site pattern without a clean calibration failure, or a failure of the deletion-direction diagnostic.

If Hcal is supportive but utility_status is not_computable, the final conclusion must read **“calibration-supported modeled value; clinical utility unvalidated”**. It must not read “clinically useful,” “should order repeat lactate,” “improves survival,” or “benefits patients.” If a future utility adjudication validates only a subset of thresholds, the supportive label may be applied only to that locked subset and only if the interval and threshold-specific results pass there; a retrospective choice of the most favorable subset is forbidden. A trial or prospective impact study is still required for any claim that acting on repeat lactate changes outcomes, harms, workload or cost.

## Executable outputs, verifier requirements, and evidence limits

The reference executable must write only derived/aggregate files in the workspace: source/header/hash manifest; flow and exact-cohort QC; revision/conflict/tie audit; feature manifest; raw and calibrated fold predictions retained privately; inner-calibration parameters; outer calibration metrics; threshold-by-model NB cells; bootstrap and deletion summaries; utility_status; scalar estimates and intervals; deterministic label; and structured conclusion claims. Source files remain read-only, and no person-level rows or clinical notes are published.

The verifier must recompute hashes, table/column bindings, joins, age mapping, t0 and one-person selection, the asymmetric clocks, exact cohort, outcome counts, feature windows/ranges, timestamp resolution, fold-only preprocessing, base models, nested calibration, probability clipping/logit handling, decision-curve cells and trapezoid integral, defaults, bootstrap, deletions, calibration guardrails, utility status and label. It must include fixtures for supportive, calibration-failed, opposite, adverse-to-materiality, inconclusive, and not-computable results. It must reject numerically correct outputs paired with unsupported statements that repeat lactate improves survival, should be ordered, proves occult hypoperfusion, or was certainly visible to a bedside clinician.

Automatic computation can establish at most an association/prediction result for hospital discharge status in this selected, repeatedly measured population and the behavior of fixed mathematical net benefit after nested within-network recalibration. It cannot establish specimen identity (arterial/venous), actual norepinephrine administration or dose, ordering indication, device validity, clinician view time, intervening fluids/vasopressors/source control, liver or renal handling, tissue perfusion, limitations of care, death time/cause, workflow burden, cost, patient preference, external transportability, or benefit/harm from acting. Clinical informatics adjudication is needed for specimen, device and workflow semantics; clinician/stakeholder review is needed for thresholds/utilities; prospective external validation needs order/view/action timestamps; and a prospective impact study, ideally randomized for any policy change, is required for patient benefit or harm.

## What changed and what remains uncertain

Changed from parent: the primary comparison is now calibrated R1 versus calibrated R0 using nested inner hospital cross-fitting; calibration is a required deployment gate rather than a secondary diagnostic; site-level calibration stability is explicit; calibration failure cannot be rescued by raw predictions or a favorable AUROC/NB result; and clinical utility is a separately reported, currently unavailable evidence layer.

Preserved: exact 1,229-person/33-hospital cohort, 507/722 outcomes, t0/L24 clocks, all frozen features and source bindings, hospital-held-out prediction, copied-hospital uncertainty, fixed +1 margin and threshold grid, treat-all/treat-none default guardrail, noncausal language, four EICU read-only inputs, and all evidence/adjudication limits.

The main remaining uncertainty is whether the calibrated modeled signal is reproducible in a genuinely external health system and whether clinicians would choose the modeled review action at these thresholds with an acceptable burden. This proposal is designed to resolve the former only within the available EICU hospitals and to expose, not conceal, the latter.
