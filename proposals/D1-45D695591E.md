# Discharge-proximal recovery and immediate return after observed TACE in HCC

## Status, question, and hypothesis

Episode 10 successor to `[prior hypothesis]`. This is an observational incremental-prognostic experiment, not a treatment-effect, discharge-policy, mortality, or clinical-utility study.

The strongest supported claim from the inspected external evidence is that hepatic reserve and complications after locoregional HCC treatment are clinically relevant. The inspected frozen full text of Sanai et al. (Scientific Reports 2026, DOI 10.1038/s41598-026-54466-4, PMCID PMC13457603; support source retained by the parent) used clinically adjudicated decompensation components in a different multicentre cohort. It does not establish that a routinely recorded, time-ordered laboratory trajectory in this HCC snapshot adds prognostic information, nor that changing discharge care improves outcomes. The demonstrations were inspected only to the availability stated in `references/research-ambition/README.md`: the natural-history and Bayesian papers have locally available full articles/extractions; the cancer paper's main article and STAR Methods remain unavailable and only its supplement was inspected.

Primary preserved hypothesis: among adults with HCC and a first observed TACE episode who are discharged, does the within-person change in core laboratory measurements from the 14 days before TACE to the first 72 hours after TACE add held-out information about any subsequent inpatient admission within 30 days beyond baseline reserve, prior utilization, demographics, and recorded disease history?

New discharge-aligned nested hypothesis: among that population with at least 96 hours from TACE start to discharge and complete core windows, does the discharge-proximal change (first 72 hours after TACE versus final 24 hours before discharge) add reproducible information about an inpatient admission in days 0–7 after discharge beyond baseline/early labs and a prespecified observation-opportunity vector? The 8–30-day admission outcome is a temporal contrast, not a competing-risk endpoint. The signal is considered more consistent with a discharge-relevant trajectory only if it is not comparable in a non-overlapping late-stay placebo window and is not explained by recording opportunity.

A positive result would motivate prospective validation with adjudicated outcomes, units, stage and scheduling information. A null result would caution against treating routine discharge trajectories as TACE-specific escalation evidence. Neither resolves causality or clinical utility.

## Features, baselines, and analysis

For each assay use medians in half-open windows: baseline [TACE−14d,TACE), early [TACE,TACE+72h], discharge [discharge−24h,discharge). Primary early features are early−baseline; discharge features are discharge−early. Keep assays separate and robust-standardize using training-fold median/IQR. For each assay/window derive valid-row count, distinct timestamp count, span, maximum gap; additionally count core rows/timestamps and distinct pre-discharge encounter keys in [TACE,discharge). This vector P is measurement opportunity, not severity.

Fit patient-level regularized logistic baseline-only versus trajectory models with five-fold patient-level CV in discovery buckets and a 2010–2022 derivation / 2023–2025-12-02 chronological test. Keep imputation, scaling and tuning inside folds. Baseline covariates: age, sex, department, baseline core medians, prior 365-day encounter count, and date-aligned recorded indicators for cirrhosis, portal-vein thrombosis, ascites, diabetes and prior liver surgery. Compare global likelihood ratio and out-of-fold AUROC, AUPRC, Brier, calibration intercept/slope, bootstrap 95% intervals, adjusted odds ratios per fold IQR and representative absolute risk differences. A shallow gradient-boosted model is a bounded functional-form sensitivity, not a claim of clinical utility.

For the nested discharge analysis require >=96h TACE-start-to-discharge and complete core baseline, early and discharge windows. D0 includes baseline/early covariates; D1 adds discharge−early changes. D0+P and D1+P include the identical prespecified P vector. Require >=20 all-cause events before inferential modeling; require >=20 liver-coded first events before any liver-coded model. Otherwise report descriptive counts/components only and call that claim inconclusive. The expanded seven-assay analysis, missingness indicators, inverse-observation weighting and >=2 distinct timestamps per post-TACE assay are sensitivities, not replacements for the primary cohort.

## Falsification and interpretation

Use a non-overlapping late-stay placebo: for the nested cohort, compare the final 24h discharge change with an earlier matched 24h window ending at least 48h before discharge (and require the same available measurement-opportunity controls); a comparable placebo increment weakens discharge specificity. Also retain the parent’s non-overlapping pre-TACE placebo (TACE−42d to TACE−28d versus TACE−28d to TACE−25d) and within-participant assay timestamp permutation plus time reversal with recorded seeds. Run density/gap thresholds, department/calendar selection checks, narrower TACE dictionary sensitivity, and exact-join/no-duplication/leakage assertions. No post-discharge data, post-index orders/medications, later labs, or index encounter may enter features/outcomes improperly.

Supportive early evidence requires LR p<0.05, optimism-corrected out-of-fold Brier improvement CI excluding zero, no chronological direction/calibration reversal, and survival of key density/gap and timestamp checks. Supportive discharge evidence additionally requires the event gate, D1 improvement over D0 with uncertainty excluding zero, consistent D1+P versus D0+P, and no comparable late/pre-TACE placebo gain. If P removes the increment, interpret it as recording opportunity rather than biology. Held-out failure or reversal is adverse/falsifying for the claimed incremental prognostic signal. In-sample AUROC alone is not supportive. Sparse events, failed timestamp/placebo feasibility, unstable dictionaries, or contradictory metrics are inconclusive, not evidence of no association. If early supports but discharge fails, retain only the early claim.

Harbor can verify paths, schemas, partitions, dictionaries, joins, windows, event counts, model outputs, uncertainty, leakage and conclusion-to-output linkage. It cannot establish true decompensation, unplannedness, mortality absence, treatment effect, causal biology, safe discharge thresholds, utility, or transportability. Those require expert adjudication, missing evidence, or another prospective/externally validated study.
