# Cross-marker persistence of renal biomarker discordance in UK Biobank repeat attenders: identified association with assumption-indexed sensitivity only

## Question and repair

The clinically relevant question is whether a baseline cystatin-C value unexpectedly high relative to creatinine is associated with a later creatinine value that remains high after conditioning on baseline creatinine. This is a test of cross-marker persistence in protocol measurements, not a test of measured GFR decline, CKD progression, or clinical utility.

The parent proposal treated a two-wave calibrated no-decline simulation as a gate for identifying a latent decline signal. That claim is not identified: two observations per marker cannot separate person-specific trajectory, stable non-GFR determinants, correlated assay error, and time-varying biological variation without external reliability or measured-GFR information. This child therefore makes the cross-lag coefficient and its uncertainty the primary, identified associational estimand. Measurement-error simulations are retained only as assumption-indexed sensitivity analyses. If the simulated null changes the interpretation, the result is inconclusive rather than calibrated evidence for or against latent decline.

Prior public evidence inspected in the parent supports that cystatin-C/creatinine discordance is common and has non-GFR correlates; it does not establish that discordance predicts later renal deterioration. The unresolved claim tested here is narrower and falsifiable: in eligible repeat attenders, one-SD higher baseline cystatin-C residual conditional on baseline creatinine, age, sex, and center is associated with higher repeat creatinine conditional on baseline creatinine, elapsed interval, sex, age, and center. The symmetric cross-lag is secondary. Neither coefficient is causal.

## Certified-core variables and estimands

For each eligible participant, use natural logs of the four primary laboratory values after positivity and finite-value checks. Because per-field units are absent from the catalog, no eGFR equation is run and no unit conversion is invented. Apply transparent range diagnostics and report exclusions, but do not winsorize.

Within each training fold, estimate the baseline residual exposure

`R_cys|cr = standardized residual of log(30720-0.0) on log(30700-0.0) + centered age + sex + baseline-center fixed effects`.

Positive values mean cystatin-C is higher than expected given the certified core covariates. The symmetric exposure is the standardized residual of log creatinine on log cystatin-C, age, sex, and center.

Primary identified estimand P1 is the coefficient on `R_cys|cr` in an ordinary least-squares ANCOVA for `log(30700-1.0)`, conditioning on `log(30700-0.0)`, centered baseline age, sex, elapsed years, an indicator for changed assessment center, and baseline-center fixed effects. Report coefficient per one training-fold SD, heteroskedasticity-robust 95% CI, n, and partial R-squared. A coefficient of 0.05 SD in repeat log creatinine per SD residual is the provisional smallest scientifically relevant magnitude; it is a reporting threshold, not a claim of clinical importance.

P2 is the symmetric model with repeat log cystatin-C as outcome, baseline cystatin-C as comparator, and `R_cr|cys` as tested exposure. Directional agreement is supportive but not necessary for estimation. Any claim of a shared renal signal requires agreement; a discordant P2 is adverse to that interpretation.

Do not add albumin, CRP, HbA1c, BMI, waist, smoking, blood pressure, medications, or other arrays to the certified-core primary model because their units/codings are not certified in this snapshot. They may be added in a separately labeled sensitivity only after frozen field-level certificates. Their absence leaves confounding by non-GFR determinants unresolved.

## Analysis and negative controls

Use participant-level 10-fold cross-fitting for exposure residualization and all preprocessing. Stratify folds by sex, baseline center, and exposure decile where feasible. The primary core may be fitted without imputation because its required primary fields are complete by cohort definition; report missingness and a complete-case equivalence check. Use robust covariance and baseline-center cluster-robust sensitivity. Compare the primary cross-marker ANCOVA with the prespecified same-marker baseline-only comparator; do not use a naive change score as evidence.

For negative controls, among the same eligible participants with valid LDL or vitamin-D pairs, fit repeat log LDL conditional on baseline log LDL and the same core covariates, and repeat log vitamin D conditional on baseline vitamin D and the same core covariates, each testing the renal `R_cys|cr`. These controls are diagnostic rather than guaranteed nulls. A negative-control association similar to or larger than P1, with compatible uncertainty, is adverse to renal specificity and prohibits a persistent renal-signal interpretation.

Also report a date placebo: regress elapsed years on baseline discordance conditional on baseline age, sex, and center. A material result flags attendance/center structure. A within-center-only sensitivity and leave-one-baseline-center-out analysis are required. A fold-stratified exposure permutation can be reported as a placebo, but it cannot repair the two-wave identification limit.

## Measurement-error sensitivity, not calibration

Do not call any two-wave simulation a calibrated null or use it as an identification gate. If executed, simulate under explicitly stated assumptions about same-marker reliability, cross-marker error covariance, and absence of person-specific decline, preserving the observed four-marker covariance and interval structure. Use a small, prespecified grid of plausible reliability/error fractions and label each output assumption-indexed. The result is supportive only as robustness to that assumed data-generating process; it cannot establish latent decline. If conclusions change over the plausible grid, label the scientific result inconclusive on latent interpretation while retaining the identified association estimate.

## Falsification and interpretation

Support for the narrow associational claim requires a positive P1 CI excluding zero, reasonable interval/center sensitivity, and no material evidence that the result is solely an artifact of a date placebo or gross selection structure. A statistically positive but below 0.05 SD result is detectable but scientifically small, not clinically actionable. Concordant P2 and weaker negative controls support specificity, but still do not establish GFR decline.

An adverse result is a null or negative P1 with precision excluding the 0.05-SD threshold, an equal/larger negative-control association, a center-specific estimate, a failed date placebo, or materially unstable estimates. The appropriate conclusion is that baseline discordance did not provide specific evidence of later cross-marker persistence in these repeat attenders.

An inconclusive result is a wide CI spanning meaningful values, inadequate paired cohort, severe center/interval nonpositivity, uncertified assay identity/range, or dependence of any latent-decline interpretation on the unidentifiable error grid. Neither adverse nor inconclusive results should be rephrased as proof of no biological progression.

The strongest permissible conclusion is an adjusted within-repeat-attender association between baseline protocol biomarker discordance and a later protocol biomarker. This experiment cannot establish measured GFR decline, CKD staging, drug-dosing benefit, causal effects of testing, clinical utility, diagnostic validity, complete medication confounding control, or generalizability beyond UK Biobank repeat attenders. Those claims require field/unit certification, measured-GFR or adjudicated kidney outcomes, external validation, and prospective impact evaluation.
