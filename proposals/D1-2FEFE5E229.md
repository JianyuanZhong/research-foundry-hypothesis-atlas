# Multi-Organ Recovery Trajectory Hypothesis

## Scientific Question

After initial circulatory stabilization in critical illness, patients whose recovery trajectories across multiple organ systems show **asynchronous patterns** have worse outcomes than those with **synchronous recovery**.

This hypothesis extends the fundamental insight from Foy et al. (2022) that WBC-platelet co-regulation defines universal inflammatory recovery trajectories, to a multi-organ framework. While Foy et al. demonstrated that exponential WBC decay followed by delayed linear PLT growth characterizes favorable inflammatory recovery, this proposal tests whether this temporal coordination principle generalizes across multiple organ systems, and whether deviations from synchronous recovery (asynchronous patterns) predict adverse outcomes.

## Clinical Importance

The resolution of this hypothesis has direct clinical implications:

1. **Early Risk Stratification**: Identifying patients with asynchronous organ recovery trajectories could enable earlier intervention before deterioration becomes irreversible.

2. **Precision Risk Models**: Moving beyond aggregate organ scores (SOFA, APACHE) to trajectory-based risk assessment that captures the temporal coordination of organ recovery.

3. **Mechanistic Insight**: Understanding whether organ-specific recovery follows coordinated temporal patterns would reveal fundamental principles of post-critical illness physiology.

## Hypothesis Statement

In patients with severe sepsis or septic shock who achieve circulatory stabilization (defined by cessation of vasopressors and/or normalization of lactate), those exhibiting **asynchronous recovery trajectories** across ≥2 organ systems have significantly worse 28-day mortality compared to those with **synchronous recovery** or **sustained recovery**.

## Data Bindings

### Primary Dataset: MIMIC-IV (3.1)

#### Core Tables:
- **hosp/patients**: Patient demographics (subject_id, gender, anchor_age, anchor_year, dod)
- **hosp/admissions**: Admission details (subject_id, hadm_id, admitting_diag_str)
- **icu/icustays**: ICU stay metadata (subject_id, hadm_id, stay_id, intime, outtime, los, first_careunit, last_careunit)
- **hosp/labevents**: Laboratory measurements (subject_id, hadm_id, charttime, itemid, value, valuenum, valueuom, ref_range_lower, ref_range_upper)
- **hosp/d_labitems**: Laboratory item catalog (itemid, label, fluid, category)
- **icu/chartevents**: Vital signs and clinical events (subject_id, hadm_id, stay_id, charttime, itemid, value, valuenum, valueuom)

#### Key Laboratory Variables:
- **Kidney function**: Creatinine (831), Urine output (from intakeOutput)
- **Respiratory function**: PaO2/FiO2 ratio (from chartevents), ABG measurements
- **Cardiovascular function**: Heart rate (333), Mean arterial pressure (from chartevents)
- **Inflammatory markers**: WBC (35011), Platelets (35012), Procalcitonin (20979)
- **Metabolic**: Lactate (1357), Glucose (1356)

#### Recovery Criteria:
- **Circulatory stabilization**: Vasopressor dose ≤100 ng/kg/min (from medication table) AND lactate ≤4 mmol/L within 48 hours of ICU admission
- **Organ recovery endpoint**: Return to normal reference range for organ-specific markers
- **Asynchronous recovery**: Defined as ≥2 organs failing to recover within 72 hours of stabilization while others recover

### Join Keys:
- Patient identity: subject_id
- ICU stay linkage: subject_id + hadm_id
- Time alignment: charttime (ISO 8601 datetime)

## Statistical Analysis Plan

### Primary Outcome:
- 28-day in-hospital mortality (dod flag in hosp/patients joined to admission via subject_id)

### Primary Predictor:
- Recovery trajectory pattern (synchronous vs. asynchronous vs. sustained)

### Analysis Steps:

1. **Phenotype Definition**:
   - Identify patients achieving circulatory stabilization within 48 hours of ICU admission
   - Define organ-specific recovery using reference ranges from hosp/d_labitems
   - Classify trajectory patterns:
     - *Synchronous*: All monitored organs recover within 72 hours of stabilization
     - *Asynchronous*: ≥2 organs show delayed recovery (>72 hours) while others recover
     - *Sustained*: All organs maintain recovery throughout ICU stay

2. **Cox Proportional Hazards Model**:
   ```
   hazard(t) = hazard0(t) × exp(β1 × Asynchronous + β2 × Synchronous + β3 × Age + β4 × Comorbidities)
   ```
   - Primary test: β1 > β0 (asynchronous vs. baseline)
   - Secondary test: β1 < β2 (asynchronous vs. synchronous)

3. **Sensitivity Analyses**:
   - Different recovery time windows (48h, 72h, 96h)
   - Different organ combinations (2-organ vs. 3+ organ asynchrony)
   - Subgroup by infection source, comorbidity burden, ICU location

4. **Falsification Criteria**:
   - **Supported**: HR for asynchronous recovery ≥1.5 (95% CI excludes 1.0)
   - **Adverse**: HR for asynchronous recovery <1.0 (95% CI excludes 1.0)
   - **Inconclusive**: 1.0 ≤ HR < 1.5 (95% CI includes 1.0)

## Methodological Alternatives

### Baseline Approach:
- **Univariate analysis**: Compare mortality between synchronous/asynchronous groups using logistic regression
- **Limitation**: Cannot adjust for time-varying confounders; susceptible to selection bias

### Learned Alternative:
- **Temporal causal model**: Use causal inference framework with g-formula or inverse probability weighting
- **Information gained**: Can estimate counterfactual outcomes under different recovery patterns while adjusting for time-dependent confounders
- **Resource requirement**: GPU available (8x A100-SXM4-80GB); CPU job for model training (budget: ~2-4 hours)

### Mechanistic Alternative:
- **Dynamic systems modeling**: Fit ordinary differential equations to organ recovery trajectories
- **Information gained**: Could identify whether recovery follows coordinated oscillatory patterns or independent exponential decay
- **Resource requirement**: GPU for ODE solver; CPU for parameter estimation (budget: ~1-2 hours)

### Selection Rationale:
The **Cox regression with time-varying covariates** approach is selected as the primary analysis because:
1. It directly tests the temporal coordination hypothesis without overfitting
2. It provides interpretable effect estimates for clinical translation
3. It can be validated with simple baseline comparisons
4. GPU-intensive alternatives are reserved for the future solver envelope

## Essential Clinical Adjudication

The following clinical judgments are required but cannot be automated:

1. **Indication for organ support**: Distinguishing recovery from treatment effect
2. **Quality of recovery**: Differentiating meaningful improvement from measurement noise
3. **Comorbid disease impact**: Assessing whether organ dysfunction reflects reversible injury or chronic disease
4. **Treatment response**: Determining whether "delayed recovery" reflects biological process or treatment failure

These adjudications require expert review of individual patient charts and cannot be fully captured by EHR data alone.

## Limitations

1. **Data limitations**: MIMIC-IV does not include comprehensive imaging or continuous monitoring data
2. **Selection bias**: Patients who survive to ICU admission may differ systematically from those who die before admission
3. **Time-varying confounding**: Treatment decisions may be influenced by observed recovery patterns, creating feedback loops
4. **Clinical adjudication**: Key judgments about recovery quality and indication for support require expert review
5. **External validity**: Results may not generalize to non-ICU settings or different healthcare systems
