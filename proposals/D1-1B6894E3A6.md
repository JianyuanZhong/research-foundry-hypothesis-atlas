# Temporal state sufficiency for the eICU lab/vital recording-process increment

Status: proposed substantive child of assessed-valid [prior hypothesis]. No cohort count, fitted coefficient, prediction, metric, or clinical result is claimed. This is a descriptive prognostic/transportability experiment, not a causal study of testing, monitoring, physiology, treatment, EOL care, or mortality.

## Scientific opening and unresolved claim

The parent established a clinically relevant but still ambiguous observation: early laboratory and vital recording intensity/gaps may add hospital-held-out ICU-discharge mortality information beyond latest recorded values. Its primary boundary is adults in a first ICU admission who remain beyond 12 hours and have no recorded care-plan-EOL event in the first 12 hours. The remaining rival is evolving patient state. A patient can have the same latest value at 360 minutes while having worsened, improved, or fluctuated during the preceding six hours. If that ordering is absent from the state block, the apparent process increment may simply be an unmodeled temporal patient-state proxy.

The strongest inspected evidence supports narrower premises only. Repeated measurements can be represented as trajectories, but latest-value summaries may still predict as well or better in a disease-specific task [K1]. Information presence/missingness can be predictive and can shift across settings [K2; abstract-only inspection]. Temporal models can be evaluated across ICU databases [K3; abstract-only inspection]. None establishes that this eICU recording process is physiology, a clinician decision, an order, a specimen, an interface artifact, or a cause of death.

The unresolved claim is:

> In P12, does the parent's lab/vital process increment remain above the prespecified negligible margin after adding a fixed early/late/change representation of the same first 360 ICU minutes, with the same hospital-held-out folds, outcome, uncertainty, and process nulls?

The primary falsifiable hypothesis is that the process increment is fully or nearly explained by the temporal state omitted by latest-value summaries: after adding the trajectory block, the held-out process increment will be below the 0.01 AUC negligible margin and below the 0.005 net-benefit margin at the 10% policy threshold. The contrary result is equally meaningful: a process increment that remains above a margin after temporal state adjustment is not explained by this bounded trajectory representation and warrants a workflow/measurement or richer-state follow-up. Neither result proves a mechanism.

This child advances the parent by testing ordering/state sufficiency directly. It does not add a new data source, silently enlarge the population, replace the parent estimand, or use model capacity as evidence.

## What would change

If latest-value plus process improves and the process increment disappears after adding T, the parent's process signal is compatible with an omitted trajectory-state explanation. The appropriate next step would be to report temporal state as a risk descriptor and avoid interpreting the residual process as a distinct clinical signal.

If process remains above the margin after T, especially on P12 external hospitals and with attenuation under the T-aware state-matched null, the fixed early/late representation is insufficient to explain it. That would justify a bounded measurement/workflow audit or a prespecified richer temporal check, not a claim that testing or physiology caused mortality.

If the process increment persists in the T-aware null, is concentrated in one hospital/fold, or is strongest for gaps, documentation/feed/site explanations become more compatible. If the increment vanishes only in P12 but is present in S6/S720, the signal is boundary-dependent and compatible with selection or early-EOL timing. No result licenses a monitoring, treatment, or goals-of-care change.

## Features: same latest state, new trajectory state, and distinct process rows

Let B be the parent's value-only/latest-value block:

- Admission variables: age, gender, ethnicity, admissionweight.
- Training-fold selected labs: up to 30 normalized analytes with at least 1% distinct-stay coverage; deterministic normalized-key tie-breaks. For each, latest finite valid labresult in [0,360], no carry-forward, training-fold median imputation.
- Training-fold selected vitals: fixed numeric candidates from both vital tables; retain channels with at least 1% training-stay coverage and at most 20 by coverage/name tie-break. Latest finite value in [0,360], no carry-forward, training-fold median imputation.

Define the new, small temporal state block T using only the same retained channels and the same valid value rules. For every retained lab or vital channel, calculate:

- E: median finite value in [0,120] minutes;
- L: median finite value in [240,360] minutes;
- C: L-E when both E and L are observed.

Duplicate values at an identical channel/offset are median-collapsed for E/L/C. If a channel has no value in a bin, that bin is missing; impute E and L with the training-fold channel median, and impute C with the training-fold channel-change median. Standardize each T column with training-fold-only location/scale after imputation. Do not interpolate, carry forward, use rows after 360, or use an observation indicator in T. A missingness/observation indicator would be a process feature, not a value feature, and belongs only in P. The fixed 0–120 and 240–360 bins are selected before fitting and are not outcome-optimized. C is a within-window change descriptor, not a physiologic slope or causal response.

For direct comparability, use exactly the parent process block P, independently of row values:

- For lab and combined vital domains, valid rows are finite numeric values with offsets in [0,360], with the parent’s lab revision rule.
- Intensity: log1p(valid row count), distinct exact offsets, distinct 30-minute bins, and retained channels observed at least once.
- Gap: 360 minus last valid offset, largest empty 30-minute-bin run, and fraction of 12 bins without a valid row.
- Duplicate rows count for intensity; exact-offset/bin sets are used for distinct-offset and gap features.
- P is never computed from E, L, C, latest values, or imputed values. A row’s presence and its value remain separate.

## Prespecified model comparison and estimands

For each population and each hospital-held-out evaluation, fit both model families with identical B, T, P, target, folds, preprocessing isolation, null assignments, and uncertainty:

1. Additive baseline: elastic-net logistic regression with B, T and P in named blocks. Report standardized coefficients and block-level prediction contrasts.
2. Capacity-matched learned alternative: histogram gradient-boosted trees with the same input columns, deterministic hyperparameters fixed before fitting, and capacity chosen to match the additive model’s feature/fit budget. It is not a claim that a nonlinear gain is clinically meaningful.
3. Required ablations in both families:
   - B only (parent latest-value state);
   - B+T (temporal-state sufficiency check);
   - B+P (parent process comparison);
   - B+T+P (primary process-after-trajectory comparison);
   - B+T+P_lab and B+T+P_vital, retaining parent lab/vital decomposition;
   - T-only is descriptive only and not a replacement for B.

For metric m and evaluation e, define process increments with “higher is better” orientation:

- Q_P^B(e) = metric(B+P,e) − metric(B,e);
- Q_P^T(e) = metric(B+T+P,e) − metric(B+T,e);
- Δ_T(e) = Q_P^T(e) − Q_P^B(e), the change in process increment after temporal state;
- Q_T(e) = metric(B+T,e) − metric(B,e), the value of ordering beyond latest state.

The primary estimand is Q_P^T on P12 held-out hospitals for AUC, with Q_P^B and Δ_T as paired comparisons. Secondary estimands are AUPRC, Brier, calibration-in-the-large, calibration slope/intercept, alert burden, and decision-curve net benefit at 5%, 10%, and 20% predicted ICU-discharge mortality. Report direct per-hospital effects and fold-pooled effects; orient Brier differences so positive is better. A process increment is not a causal effect.

## Interpretation and falsification criteria

Support for the temporal-state sufficiency explanation requires, on P12 external evaluation, that Q_P^T’s 95% interval be wholly within the negligible region (primary AUC absolute increment <0.01; secondary 10% net-benefit increment <0.005), while Q_P^B was materially positive or the parent process result is replicated. It is strengthened if B+T improves or stabilizes calibration and if the observed process increment is similar to the T-aware state null. This would mean latest values were insufficient; it would not prove the trajectory is physiology.

Support for residual process information requires Q_P^T above the relevant margin with a directionally consistent lab/vital block, across more than one held-out fold, and attenuation under the T-aware state-matched null. This would reject “explained by this bounded T” but not identify testing, monitoring, or interface mechanism.

Support for selection/boundary dependence is a positive Q_P^B or Q_P^T in S6/S720 that falls below the margin in P12/no-EOL, especially with changed prevalence/hospital composition or unstable S6 weights. This does not prove EOL care caused the signal.

Support for documentation/workflow is a process increment that persists in the T-aware null, is strongest for gaps, is concentrated in a site/fold, or survives same-hospital permutation without aligned state distributions. This is compatible with documentation/data-feed behavior, not proof of an outage or clinician practice.

Adverse evidence is a confidence interval wholly below the negligible margin for Q_P^T with no calibration or policy benefit and no technical failure. Inconclusive evidence includes intervals spanning the margin, fewer than five P12 hospitals or 100 P12 events, sparse EOL events, excessive state-cell merging, unstable weights, material feature-selection changes, or strong hospital heterogeneity. An imprecise null is not refutation.

The verifier can check source paths/hashes, schema/column use, joins, offsets, cohort flags, trajectory calculations, fold isolation, null assignments, predictions, metrics, bootstrap intervals, and whether each conclusion points to an output row. Clinical adjudication is required to determine whether a row represents an order, specimen, bedside measurement, device aggregate, interface omission, or EOL decision. A reliable death timestamp, intervention assignment, clinician review, and prospective silent-deployment/impact study would be needed for stronger clinical or causal conclusions.

## Comparable alternatives and selection record

The simple additive baseline is scientifically necessary because it makes the state/process decomposition inspectable: it can show whether early level, late level, or change attenuates a process coefficient and how calibration changes. It may lose nonlinear interactions such as “a late worsening only matters when recording gaps are short” or “a trajectory change has different meaning at different latest values.”

The learned alternative is a capacity-matched histogram GBM with the same B/T/P columns, target, hospital folds, training-only preprocessing, T-aware nulls, and paired bootstrap. It can reveal such state-by-process and early-by-late interactions without pretending that predictive gain explains their cause. Its output is useful only if the scientific interpretation (process attenuation or persistence) agrees with the additive model and is stable across hospitals, calibration, and policy metrics.

A raw-event temporal neural encoder is deferred, not excluded because it is neural or GPU-capable. The current smallest check is whether fixed early/late/change summaries resolve the specific latest-value objection while retaining the parent nulls and estimands. The available schemas expose measurement offsets but not order/specimen/device semantics, and a raw encoder would make the state/process boundary harder to audit. Revisit only if the bounded T comparison is inconclusive or a prespecified diagnostic shows meaningful ordering information beyond E/L/C; then retain the same P12/S720/S6 target, folds, T-aware null, and uncertainty. A hierarchical hospital random-effects model is secondary because it may absorb site behavior rather than test held-out transport. A causal monitoring/testing study is unavailable.

Selection deliverable: the child must newly compute and save T, fit B/B+T/B+P/B+T+P in additive and GBM families, generate observed and T-aware-null predictions, and produce the paired hospital-cluster uncertainty table and conclusion map. Completion is established only when those outputs and leakage/binding checks exist; no fitted result is implied here.

Planned future solver envelope: 16 CPUs, up to 262,144 MiB RAM, and 28,800 seconds, matching the parent’s tabular extraction/five-fold/bootstrap plan; this is an unmeasured estimate. Discovery has not launched a full fit. No GPU is requested: the task is tabular aggregation and tree/logistic fitting, and an A100 would not resolve the scientific rival. If a bounded readiness probe finds the trajectory aggregation or repeated null/bootstrap computation exceeds the envelope, reduce repetitions only through a documented readiness decision or revisit the design; do not silently replace T with latest values.
