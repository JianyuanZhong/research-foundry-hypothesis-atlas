- Under the explicitly measured baseline attendance model and truncation rule, weighted P1 and P2 remain positive and their joint full-bootstrap intervals exclude zero.

Still untested or unsupported:

- renal mechanism or bidirectional renal specificity;
- CKD onset or progression;
- causal effects;
- correction for unmeasured or MNAR repeat attendance;
- clinical utility, action thresholds, calibration for patient decisions, or transportability beyond this export.

Supportive result: both full-bootstrap simultaneous weighted intervals remain above zero, showing the repeat-attender associations are not erased by standardization for the measured baseline attendance model; P1 also exceeds both controls. Adverse result: P2 still does not exceed either control, so a bidirectional renal-specific interpretation fails; the individual 0.005 probability diagnostic narrowly fails. Inconclusive result: these weights cannot recover outcomes for selection driven by unmeasured future health, and two waves cannot distinguish stable person-level marker propensity from biological trajectory, assay effects, treatment, or correlated error.

The weighted observable-persistence hypothesis would be falsified for a direction if a valid full-bootstrap simultaneous upper bound were at or below zero or if the sign reversed materially under the prespecified attendance sensitivity; neither occurred. Practical robustness to the narrow probability gate would have been adverse if the 0.005 floor materially changed a coefficient or interval; it changed neither point because of truncation. Bidirectional renal specificity is already not supported because both P2 matched-control intervals include zero. A stronger renal, progression, causal, utility, or transportability claim requires additional waves or measured GFR, richer selection and treatment variables, laboratory/clinical adjudication of controls and assay processes, external cohorts, and prospective impact evidence.
