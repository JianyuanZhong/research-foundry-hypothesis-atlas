# Provisional hypothesis: discordant renal recovery after AKI

## Proposed relationship
Among adults with an acute kidney injury episode in MIMIC-IV, patients whose creatinine has returned toward baseline but whose urine output and/or fluid balance remain abnormal at a prespecified 48-hour assessment will have a higher risk of subsequent in-hospital death or persistent/recurrent kidney dysfunction than patients with concordant creatinine and urine-output recovery, after accounting for baseline illness severity and treatment context.

This is a hypothesis, not an observed result. Creatinine-based recovery may be a misleading surrogate: creatinine can fall because of dilution, altered production, or recovery while residual tubular dysfunction or fluid overload persists. Resolving this would clarify whether a single creatinine criterion is sufficient for judging renal recovery and whether continued monitoring or renal-risk assessment is warranted.

## Falsifiable test
Fit a prespecified comparison of discordant versus concordant renal recovery at a fixed landmark, using subsequent in-hospital death and persistent/recurrent kidney dysfunction as outcomes. Compare a transparent adjusted model (baseline severity, treatment context, and measured recovery variables) with a trajectory-based alternative using the pre-landmark creatinine/urine-output/fluid trajectory. Report uncertainty and prespecified sensitivity analyses for missingness, treatment timing, and selective measurement. A null or imprecise result would not prove equivalence; it would require a larger cohort or external validation.

## Method comparison
The simple baseline is an adjusted landmark model using prespecified recovery categories and clinical covariates. The substantive alternative is a trajectory model (for example, grouped or spline-based) that preserves the shape and timing of creatinine, urine output, and fluid balance rather than collapsing them to one 48-hour label. It could reveal whether discordance is specific to a residual trajectory pattern, rather than merely a cross-sectional category. Both must use the same population, split, outcomes, uncertainty evaluation, and resource envelope; selection will depend on whether the alternative resolves a scientific uncertainty, not on predictive gain alone.
