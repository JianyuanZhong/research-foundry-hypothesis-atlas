# Successor B: is repeat-lactate availability itself a clinically meaningful care-process signal?

## Decision and unresolved claim

Episode-25 established that its locked 2–4-hour lactate-clearance contrast is not identifiable under its prespecified multi-hospital and event gates. This proposal does not loosen those gates, change its time window, or treat its 62-person result as an estimate.

The new unresolved claim is about measurement rather than a later clearance cutoff: among adults with early recorded norepinephrine/Levophed exposure, baseline lactate at least 2 mmol/L, and a documented opportunity to remain in the ICU through 12 hours, is obtaining at least one valid repeat lactate during hours 4–12 associated with an absolute hospital-mortality risk difference of at least 10 percentage points versus no valid repeat? The clinical decision is whether repeat-lactate status can be treated as a neutral documentation detail, or whether it should trigger a standardized reassessment pathway and be handled as a potentially informative missingness/care-intensity signal.

The hypothesis is deliberately nondirectional: either direction is clinically important. More testing among higher-risk patients would indicate selective clinician concern and warn against naïve complete-case interpretation; less testing among higher-risk patients would indicate a possible missed-risk process. This study does not claim that ordering a test causes either harm or benefit. Its substantive advance is to test whether the measurement process that made the four-hour clearance question infeasible is itself related to outcome, using a fixed 12-hour opportunity window and a 12-hour landmark.

A current full-text eICU transportability study (Yang et al., DOI 10.3389/fmed.2026.1880921, acquired source [source checksum]) reports that eICU complete-case formation was driven largely by repeat-lactate and other measurement availability and was confined to monitoring-dense subsets. That supports concern about selection, not this mortality contrast and not a benefit from testing. The parent’s audited 2–4-hour result likewise supports feasibility failure, not a conclusion about the value of repeat testing.

## Frozen population and temporal boundaries

Use the complete EICU snapshot [source checksum], with every source read-only and no sampling.

1. From adult patient rows, map age >89 to 90 and retain age >=18. For each stay, define t0 as the minimum infusionDrug.infusionoffset in [0,1440] whose case-folded drugname contains norepinephrine or levophed. For each uniquepid, retain the lexicographically smallest (t0, patientunitstayid).
2. Require hospitaldischargestatus exactly Alive or Expired; require both unitdischargeoffset >= t0+720 and hospitaldischargeoffset >= t0+720. This defines a documented ICU/hospital opportunity through 12 hours and excludes discharge/death before the landmark. Define D=1 only when hospitaldischargestatus is Expired. EICU has no exact death time.
3. Construct baseline B using the strict parent rule: labname lactate (case-insensitive), labmeasurenamesystem mmol/L, numeric labresult in [0.2,30], collection labresultoffset in [t0-360,t0], and labresultrevisedoffset <= t0+60. At each collection offset retain the greatest eligible revision; invalidate the collection if eligible values disagree; identical duplicates use smallest labid. Select the latest valid collection and require B>=2.
4. Define M=1 if at least one valid lactate collection exists with labresultoffset in [t0+240,t0+720] and labresultrevisedoffset <= t0+720, using the same range, unit, revision, conflict, and duplicate rules. M=0 means no such valid result. Do not count an order, an unrevised row, a conflicting collection, or a result outside the window.
5. Freeze the population, measurement indicator, and exposure-balanced hospital gate before reading D. Follow-up is the hospital-discharge mortality status observed after the 12-hour landmark; no death-time or cause-of-death claim is made.

A complete-source feasibility scan found 13,111 early-pressor people with the 12-hour ICU/hospital opportunity and 3,246 with baseline lactate >=2. M=1 occurred in 1,887 and M=0 in 1,359, across 114 hospitals. An outcome-blind gate of >=20 people in each M group retains 20 hospitals and 1,881 people (707 M=0, 1,174 M=1). Post-freeze descriptive death counts were 215 and 509, respectively; they are audit output, not an effect estimate and were not used to define the site gate.

## Analysis and fixed falsification rules

The estimand is the standardized hospital-mortality risk difference RD = P(D=1|M=1) - P(D=1|M=0) in the measured-overlap 12-hour landmark target. Fit one prespecified L2 logistic model (C=10) with M, the pre-4-hour covariates, missing indicators, and hospital indicators. Use pooled 1st/99th-percentile winsorization and scaling, normalized overlap weights (1-e for M=1 and e for M=0), standardized risks, and RD in percentage points. Report crude risks separately. This is an association between care-process status and outcome, not a causal effect of ordering a lactate.

Require: >=12 exposure-balanced hospitals, >=500 people, >=50 deaths in each group, propensity scores in [0.02,0.98], group ESS >=40, maximum person-weight share <=5%, hospital-weight share <=35%, every model-column absolute SMD <=0.10, >=95% convergence among 5,000 copied-hospital-then-copied-uniquepid bootstrap replicates, and finite estimates in >=80% of leave-one-hospital-out refits. Use seed 20260924. The hospital gate is M-count-only and is frozen before reading D.

- Supportive for an informative measurement-process signal: every gate passes and the 95% interval for RD lies wholly above +10 points or wholly below -10 points. The sign is reported as part of the process interpretation; neither sign means testing is harmful or beneficial.
- Adverse to the clinically relevant signal claim: every gate passes and the 95% interval lies wholly within [-10,+10] points, supporting no clinically important contrast under this observational design.
- Inconclusive: any population/event/balance/convergence/site-deletion gate fails, the interval overlaps either falsification boundary, or the estimate is unstable.

If supportive, the next study should adjudicate why testing is selective and prospectively test a standardized repeat-lactate/reassessment workflow. If adverse, complete-case lactate analyses may be less threatened by a >=10-point outcome-selection contrast in this target, but assay and clinical-meaning limitations remain. Neither outcome establishes that a repeat-lactate order changes outcome.

## Evidence limits and verification

EICU cannot establish true norepinephrine administration, dose, intent, arterial versus venous specimen, assay indication, fluids, beta-agonists, hepatic clearance, tissue perfusion, source control, treatment limitations, exact death time/cause, or clinical utility. Missing repeat testing is also a composite of clinician concern, workflow, transfer, patient trajectory, and documentation. Clinical adjudication and a prospective process study are needed.

Computationally checkable claims are source hashes/headers, deterministic t0 and person selection, 12-hour opportunity rule, lactate revision/conflict/tie logic, M counts, outcome-blind site gate, pre-4-hour model inputs, and interval interpretation. The verifier must reject a correct M count paired with a causal claim and must test correct interpretations for positive, negative, equivalence-within-margin, and inconclusive synthetic intervals. Reference execution proves computation and feasibility, not clinical truth.
