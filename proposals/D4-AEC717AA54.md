# UKB-07: Glycated Albumin (NMR) Discordant from CRP — Persistent Inflammation and Post-Infection Mortality

## 1. Unresolved Question and Clinical Importance

**Question:** Does glycated albumin (GlyA), measured by NMR metabolomics, independently predict 90-day and 1-year post-infection mortality beyond CRP, and does the GlyA–CRP discordance pattern (high GlyA with low CRP) identify a distinct high-risk subgroup?

**Clinical importance:** Post-infection mortality is a major clinical challenge. CRP is the standard acute-phase reactant used to gauge infection severity, but it reflects short-term inflammation (half-life ~19 hours). GlyA reflects glycated albumin accumulation over 2–3 weeks and is less affected by acute-phase responses. If GlyA independently predicts post-infection mortality, it could identify patients with persistent subclinical inflammation who are at elevated risk despite "normal" CRP, enabling earlier intervention.

**Substantive advance over existing knowledge:**
- [K1] Chen 2026 (Diabetes) identified metabolomics-defined prediabetes subtypes in UKB using 251 NMR metabolites, establishing that NMR metabolites capture biologically distinct risk profiles beyond conventional glycemic markers. However, they did not test GlyA specifically in the context of infection and mortality.
- [K2] Adejoh 2026 (Front Nutr) systematically reviewed metabolomics in diabetes, noting that NMR-based metabolomics has expanded biomarker identification but that validation and standardization remain limiting. They did not address GlyA in the infection context.
- [K3] Lin 2026 (BMJ Open) proposed the RAPID-I trial using CRP as the primary endpoint for acute pancreatitis treatment, establishing CRP as a clinically meaningful inflammatory biomarker in acute disease. However, CRP alone does not capture persistent subclinical inflammation.

## 2. Hypothesis

**H1 (Main effect):** Higher GlyA (independent of CRP) is associated with increased 90-day and 1-year post-infection mortality, adjusted for age, sex, BMI, BCAA, and ALT.

**H2 (Interaction):** The GlyA–CRP discordance pattern (high GlyA, low CRP) is associated with higher post-infection mortality than concordant patterns (both high or both low), suggesting that persistent inflammation (GlyA) independent of acute inflammation (CRP) drives risk.

## 6. Falsification Criteria

- **Falsified (H1):** GlyA-residual OR for 90-day and 1-year post-infection mortality both in range 0.90–1.10 after full adjustment.
- **Falsified (H2):** GlyA-resid × CRP interaction OR in range 0.90–1.10 for both outcomes.
- **Supportive (H1):** GlyA-residual OR > 1.10 for 1-year post-infection mortality after full adjustment.
- **Supportive (H2):** GlyA-resid × CRP interaction OR < 0.90 (negative interaction = high GlyA/low CRP is higher risk) for 1-year post-infection mortality.
- **Inconclusive:** GlyA-residual OR in range 1.00–1.10 for 1-year post-infection mortality (directionally consistent but not statistically significant).

## 7. Alternative Explanations

1. **Liver disease:** GlyA is affected by hepatic function. ALT is included as a covariate, but undiagnosed liver disease could confound.
2. **Nutritional status:** BCAA is included as a covariate, but other nutritional markers (albumin, prealbumin) are not available in the NMR table.
3. **Diabetes:** GlyA is a glycemic marker. HbA1c is available in the core table (field 30750) but not included in the primary model. Sensitivity analysis with HbA1c adjustment should be performed.
4. **Infection severity:** The ICD10 code captures the first inpatient diagnosis, not the full clinical picture. More severe infections may have both higher GlyA and CRP.
5. **Measurement timing:** GlyA and CRP are measured at baseline (pre-infection), not at the time of infection. The association reflects pre-infection inflammatory state, not acute response.

## 8. Method Comparison

**Baseline:** Logistic regression with GlyA-residual (linear) as the primary predictor.
**Alternative:** GlyA-residual quartiles (categorical) to test for nonlinearity. The preliminary results show a non-monotonic pattern (Q3 > Q4), suggesting that the linear model may not capture the full relationship. A restricted cubic spline (RCS) analysis with 3–4 knots would be a more flexible alternative.

**What the alternative could reveal:** If the RCS shows a U-shaped or J-shaped relationship, it would suggest that both very low and very high GlyA-residual are associated with increased risk, which would have different clinical implications than a linear dose-response.
