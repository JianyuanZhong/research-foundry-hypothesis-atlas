## Decision, unresolved claim, and substantive advance

The bedside question remains unchanged: for an adult still invasively ventilated and markedly fluid-positive at ICU hour 48 after a recent recorded vasopressor course and six recorded pressor-free hours, should an ICU-recorded furosemide or bumetanide delivery start during the next 12 hours while the linked ventilation interval remains open, or be avoided? Earlier airway liberation may matter, but creatinine rise, RRT, death, hospice, or acute transfer may outweigh it.

The strongest evidence-supported claim is limited. The 2025 ESICM guideline abstract was inspected in a frozen Europe PMC record (PMID 40828463, DOI `10.1007/s00134-025-08058-x`, source `[source checksum]`, [source checksum]). It conditionally supports protocolized diuretic removal after acute resuscitation but reports limited evidence in specific cohorts. The frozen full XML of Bircher et al. 2026 was inspected (PMCID `PMC13154636`, DOI `10.1016/j.aicoj.2026.100075`, source `[source checksum]`, [source checksum]); it says optimal timing, volume, duration and phenotype-guided removal remain uncertain and individualized. These sources support importance, not benefit, a validated 24-hour recency boundary, or exchangeability.

A six-hour duration has pragmatic precedent but is not a biological shock definition. The inspected full public HTML of the 65 Trial (PMCID `PMC7064880`, DOI `10.1001/jama.2020.0930`, source `[source checksum]`, [source checksum]) required vasopressors expected for at least six hours and defined episode cessation by 24 pressor-free hours, ICU discharge, or death. That prospective expectation cannot be recovered retrospectively here. The inspected full XML of Kotani et al. 2023 (PMCID `PMC9854213`, DOI `10.1186/s13054-023-04322-y`, source `[source checksum]`, [source checksum]) states that norepinephrine-equivalent formulas are not standardized and lack high-quality validation across critical-care practice. Therefore this child uses duration robustness and does not invent a dose threshold.

The parent repaired remote exposure but any positive-duration interval can still qualify. A new treatment- and outcome-blind source audit of the same 764 broad recent-pressor/stable opportunities found 730 with at least one union hour before the stability cut, 645 with at least six union hours, and 609 with an exactly contiguous merged segment of at least six hours. Thus 34 had under one union hour and 119 had under six. The weakness is real but does not justify discarding all eligible patients: most already have sustained records, and downstream feasibility remains unknown.

The unresolved falsifiable hypothesis is:

> In every subject passing the parent’s frozen pre-`t0` eligibility, assignment-compatible A12 follow-up satisfies the parent’s five-point lower-bound airway-improvement and renal/terminal-harm criteria versus B12, and that conclusion is not materially reversed when the complete analysis is rebuilt among subjects with at least six union hours of prior valid recorded pressor support.

This remains an observational record-policy hypothesis. It is not a pharmacologic effect, true shock recovery, durable extubation, renal safety, or a recommendation.

## Why pulmonary congestion is not the successor phenotype

Pulmonary congestion is clinically appealing, and the inspected Bircher review discusses phenotype-guided strategies. MIMIC note 2.1 provides timestamped radiology text but not images. The audit searched only aggregate, private in-workspace text and emitted no row or excerpt. Among the 764 broad opportunities, 560 had a radiology report charted in `(t0-24h,t0]`, 508 had at least one report stored by `t0`, and only 182 had an unvalidated high-specificity positive phrase for pulmonary/interstitial/alveolar edema, vascular/venous congestion, or fluid overload. That 182 is already below the parent’s required 200 final eligible subjects before MAP, balance, urine, potassium, washout, ECMO and multisource-RRT gates.

A congestion-positive modifier would therefore be predictably underpowered and selected by imaging/report availability. The file contains 1,176,597 rows without `hadm_id`; reports are admission-linked rather than stay-linked; images, lung ultrasound and VExUS are unavailable; and absence of a report or term is not absence of congestion. Congestion may be reported descriptively, but it cannot define primary eligibility, a confirmatory modifier, support, or falsification. A clinically interpretable congestion study needs a validated extractor plus blinded imaging/critical-care adjudication.
