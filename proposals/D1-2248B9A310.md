# Asynchronous Organ Recovery in Critically Ill Patients: A Multi-Organ Trajectory Study

## Hypothesis to Test

**Primary Hypothesis**: After circulatory stabilization, patients whose kidney or respiratory function remains impaired are more likely to deteriorate again, independent of baseline severity.

**Formal Statement**: In patients with sepsis or septic shock who achieve hemodynamic stability (defined by cessation of vasopressor requirement and resolution of hypotension within 24 hours), the presence of residual organ dysfunction (persistent elevation in creatinine >1.5x baseline or hypoxemia requiring FiO2 ≥0.40 for ≥6 hours within 24-48 hours post-stabilization) predicts subsequent clinical deterioration (requiring escalation of organ support OR death) within 7 days, after adjusting for baseline SOFA score and confounders.

**Alternative Hypothesis**: The trajectory of multi-organ recovery (kidney + respiratory) provides prognostic information beyond static baseline severity scores in critically ill patients.

## Scientific Rationale

Existing literature demonstrates that disease progression follows temporal patterns rather than static states:

1. **Gerstung et al. 2022 (Nature Communications)** established that WBC-platelet co-regulation dynamics define inflammatory recovery trajectories, with deviation from the mean trajectory associated with 14-22x increased mortality risk in cardiac surgery patients [K1].

2. **Ma et al. 2024 (BMC Med Inform Decis Mak)** showed that timing of diuretic initiation in sepsis differs by perfusion status, highlighting that clinical decisions should be guided by dynamic assessment rather than static markers [K2].

3. **Madotto et al. 2025 (BMC Pulm Med)** used latent class growth analysis to identify distinct biomarker trajectory patterns in COVID-19 ARDS, where patients in hyperinflammatory trajectories had doubled mortality compared to hypoinflammatory patterns [K3].

**The Unresolved Question**: While these studies demonstrate trajectory-based prognostication works, none comprehensively test whether *multi-organ recovery trajectories* (specifically kidney and respiratory function) after hemodynamic stabilization predict subsequent deterioration in sepsis/shock patients. This is particularly important because:
- Current practice relies heavily on single-timepoint SOFA scores
- Organ-specific recovery may be dissociated from hemodynamic stability
- Early identification of patients at risk of deterioration could inform treatment modification

## Study Population and Main Data

### Population
- **Inclusion**: Adults (≥18 years) with confirmed sepsis or septic shock in MIMIC-IV
- **Definition of sepsis**: Sepsis-related ICD-10 codes (B95-B97, R65.20, etc.) with appropriate infection source
- **Temporal boundaries**: ICU admissions from January 1, 2018 to December 31, 2019 (MIMIC-IV stable period)
- **Exclusion**: Patients on vasopressors at admission, dialysis-dependent patients, and those without ≥24 hours of post-stabilization data

### Recovery Criteria Definition

**Hemodynamic Stabilization**: Timepoint when BOTH conditions met within 24-hour window:
- Vasopressor requirement: `VASCULAR_PRESSOR = 0` (or dose <50% of admission dose)
- MAP ≥65 mmHg for ≥6 consecutive hours

**Residual Organ Dysfunction** (assessed 24-48 hours post-stabilization):
- Kidney: Creatinine >1.5x baseline OR rising creatinine trend (≥0.3 mg/dL increase from nadir)
- Respiratory: SpO2 <90% on FiO2 ≥0.40 for ≥6 consecutive hours

**Clinical Deterioration** (within 7 days of stabilization):
- Primary: Death within 7 days
- Secondary: Escalation of organ support (new vasopressor, intubation, renal replacement therapy)

## Scientific Significance

This study advances clinical understanding by:

1. **Capturing recovery dynamics** not represented by single-timepoint severity scores
2. **Identifying patients at risk of deterioration** after apparent stabilization
3. **Informing early treatment modification** - patients with residual organ dysfunction may benefit from:
   - Earlier consideration of renal replacement therapy
   - More aggressive respiratory support
   - Differentiation between "stable but recovering" vs "unstable despite stabilization"

4. **Testing the generalizability** of trajectory-based prognostication beyond the specific conditions studied in prior literature (cardiac surgery, COVID-19)

## Baseline and Alternative Approaches

### Baseline: Cox Proportional Hazards with Static SOFA
- Model: Time-to-event (ICU death or escalation) from admission
- Covariates: Baseline SOFA score, age, comorbidities
- Limitation: Cannot capture post-stabilization recovery dynamics

### Alternative: Gradient Boosting with Multi-Organ Recovery Trajectories
- Model: Gradient boosting (XGBoost/LightGBM) with temporal features
- Features:
  - Time-varying creatinine trajectory (slope, area under curve post-stabilization)
  - Time-varying oxygenation trajectory (time to FiO2 ≥0.40, nadir SpO2)
  - Interaction terms: Baseline SOFA × trajectory features
- Advantage: Can capture non-linear relationships and temporal patterns

### Information Revealed by Alternative
The alternative approach could reveal:
- Whether trajectory slopes are more predictive than endpoint values
- Whether kidney and respiratory trajectories interact (discordant recovery)
- Whether the relationship between recovery and deterioration is non-linear

## Analysis Plan

### Primary Analysis
1. **Define hemodynamic stabilization timepoints** for each ICU stay
2. **Extract recovery trajectories** (kidney + respiratory) for 24-48 hours post-stabilization
3. **Model deterioration risk** using:
   - Primary: Cox proportional hazards (time-to-death/escalation)
   - Secondary: Gradient boosting for comparative performance
4. **Adjust for confounders**: Age, sex, comorbidities ( Charlson index), baseline severity

### Secondary Analyses
1. **Discordant recovery patterns**: Compare outcomes in patients with discordant kidney/respiratory trajectories
2. **Trajectory-based subgroups**: Use clustering to identify distinct recovery patterns
3. **Temporal sensitivity**: Test different post-stabilization windows (24h, 48h, 72h)

### Falsification Criteria
The hypothesis will be falsified if:
- No significant association between residual organ dysfunction and subsequent deterioration (adjusted HR <1.2, p>0.05)
- Baseline SOFA score alone predicts deterioration as well as or better than trajectory features
- The association is entirely confounded by baseline severity (no association after adjustment)

## Expected Clinical Impact

If supported, this study could:

## Limitations

1. **Unmeasured confounding**: Residual organ dysfunction may be influenced by unmeasured factors
2. **Measurement gaps**: Not all patients have frequent laboratory or vital sign measurements
3. **Causality**: Observational design cannot establish that residual dysfunction causes deterioration
4. **Generalizability**: MIMIC-IV population may not represent all ICU populations
