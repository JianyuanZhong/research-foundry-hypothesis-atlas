# Working proposal: discordant visceral fat and thigh muscle fat infiltration

## Scientific opening
UK Biobank body MRI provides simultaneous measurements of visceral fat volume and posterior thigh muscle fat infiltration (MFI), while repeated assessments provide handgrip strength, falls, HbA1c and incident diagnoses. The unresolved question is whether fat distribution identifies two distinct downstream risk pathways: visceral fat as a marker of metabolic disease, and muscle fat infiltration as a marker of functional decline/falls.

The seed hypothesis is: at similar total body fat, higher visceral fat is more strongly associated with metabolic outcomes, whereas higher muscle MFI is more strongly associated with subsequent functional decline and falls. This is not yet established by the available data.

## Why it matters
A person with normal BMI but high visceral fat may need metabolic surveillance, while a person with high muscle infiltration may be at higher risk of disability and falls. Separating these patterns could inform which clinical pathway to prioritize. However, the available data are observational and cannot establish causality.

## Strongest evidence and rival explanations
The strongest currently supported claim is that visceral adiposity and muscle fat infiltration are measurable, distinct body-composition phenotypes associated with adverse outcomes in the literature. The unresolved claim is that their downstream risk pathways are discordant after conditioning on total adiposity and other confounding factors.

The strongest rival is that both measures proxy a common frailty or metabolic state, or that imaging selection and health-care contact create apparent differences. If both measures predict both outcomes equally after adjustment, the discordance hypothesis is not supported.

## Population and temporal boundaries
- Population: UK Biobank participants with body MRI and at least one subsequent assessment or diagnosis date.
- Baseline: the body-MRI assessment date.
- Follow-up: from baseline to first relevant outcome, censoring at death, last known contact, or a prespecified maximum follow-up.
- Repeated assessments: used for time-varying covariates and functional outcomes, with explicit measurement dates.

## Variables and outcomes
- Exposure: visceral fat volume and posterior thigh MFI, standardized within sex and age groups.
- Conditioning: total body fat volume, BMI, age, sex, ethnicity, smoking, alcohol, physical activity, medication use, and imaging-related factors where available.
- Metabolic outcomes: incident diabetes, HbA1c change, and metabolic syndrome components.
- Functional outcomes: incident falls, handgrip strength decline, and activity limitation.

## Analysis and falsification
1. Fit a baseline adjusted model for each outcome with visceral fat and MFI as separate predictors.
2. Compare outcome-specific associations and the visceral-versus-MFI contrast.
3. Use a prespecified alternative model (time-varying or multistate) if repeated assessments support it.
4. Falsification: a null or reversed contrast after adjustment, or equal associations across outcomes, refutes the proposed discordance.

## Method comparison
- Baseline: adjusted Cox or logistic regression with prespecified covariates.
- Alternative: time-varying or multistate model if repeated assessments support it.
- Deferred: neural sequence model, because the primary estimand is a clinically interpretable contrast and the data are sparse longitudinal measurements rather than high-dimensional sequence data.
