# Decision-time stress-tested semantic triage for pathology-defined high-grade MVI (M2) in HCC

## Clinical decision, established evidence, and unresolved claim

The consequential decision is whether an adult who is proceeding to a first eligible liver resection for hepatocellular carcinoma should be sent for multidisciplinary review because the resection specimen may contain pathology-defined high-grade microvascular invasion (M2). The output is a review flag, not a recommendation for wider resection, transplantation, systemic therapy, neoadjuvant therapy, adjuvant therapy, or surveillance. A flag could justify expert review of the operative plan or later risk discussion, but this retrospective source does not contain the review decision, treatment assignment, recurrence, survival, or patient benefit.

The strongest claim supported by inspected evidence is narrower. MVI is a pathology-defined phenotype, M2 represents a more extensive grade in the Chinese M0/M1/M2 scheme, and MVI severity is prognostically relevant. The inspected full-text HCC literature in the inherited candidate support describes concerns about sampling adequacy and weak external validation of preoperative models. The current Europe PMC search also returned a 2026 dual-center retrospective CT-radiomics/serum-biomarker study reporting external validation for binary MVI, and a 2026 retrospective study of LDH and MVI severity; these are abstract/search evidence, not evidence that this HCC snapshot supports an M2 policy (frozen search source [source checksum]). The research-ambition cancer paper's main article remains unavailable in the configured references; only its supplement was inspected, so no claim about its main methods is made. The natural-history paper was inspected as an example of temporally locked longitudinal prediction, not as HCC evidence.

The HCC snapshot establishes that timestamped procedure starts, examination acquisitions and laboratory measurements exist, while pathology narratives are untimed and report-finalization/MDT timestamps are absent. Prior whole-file audits found that a naive anywhere-in-document search for M2 is contaminated by template definitions and that explicit selected-grade ascertainment changes markedly by calendar period. Those are feasibility and endpoint-construction findings, not a validated prevalence, predictive performance, or treatment effect.

The unresolved claim is therefore:

Among all adults meeting a prespecified first eligible liver-resection frame, does semantic information in preoperative CT/MRI findings and assay-specific laboratory values add clinically material, calibrated M2 review-flag value beyond demographic, laboratory-availability, examination-availability, modality and machine/workflow information, and does that incremental value persist when the allowable acquisition-to-operation buffer is changed?

The primary hypothesis is that the semantic extension has positive paired decision value at a fixed review threshold of 0.30 in a locked historical test frame, with the 95% lower bound for incremental net benefit above 0.02, and that the result remains non-harmful under prespecified 0-hour and 72-hour acquisition-buffer sensitivities and in estimable CT and MRI strata. The hypothesis is falsified if the bound is harmful, if a provenance stratum is harmful, if calibration fails, if a workflow-only or label-permutation control performs similarly, or if the result is identifiable only after discarding unknown pathology.

This advances the existing line by testing whether apparent M2 signal is clinical semantic content rather than documentation/ordering process, while treating “available before the decision” as an interval-sensitive evidence limitation instead of silently equating an acquisition timestamp with clinician access.

## Population and temporal boundaries

The target frame contains patients in the HCC snapshot who:

1. have an adult age recorded at the index encounter;
2. have a first recorded eligible liver-resection procedure for that patient in procedures;
3. have no recorded pre-index TACE, ablation, transplantation or other prior tumor-directed treatment under a locked treatment dictionary in procedures, orders, medications and diagnoses; and
4. have a pathology-linked encounter for the index frame or remain in the all-target denominator with an unknown outcome.

The index operation is the earliest procedure for the patient satisfying a locked liver-resection dictionary, including explicit variants of liver/segment/lobe/partial/wedge resection in procedures.surgery (for example, hepatectomy, partial hepatectomy, segmentectomy, lobectomy, wedge resection, left hemihepatectomy, right hemihepatectomy, and explicit HCC resection variants). The dictionary must be frozen before fitting by two independent implementations. It excludes TACE/interventional procedures, ablation, biopsy, drainage, imaging, and transplant-only records. If the dictionary cannot distinguish a combined resection from a non-resection procedure, that row is excluded from the primary index frame and reported as an ambiguity, not resolved by model performance.

The primary operation clock is t0 = procedures.Start time for the selected row. An index encounter is linked only by the composite key (Patient master index, Encounter number) to encounters, examinations, labs, pathology, diagnoses, orders and medications. Duplicate-key audits are mandatory; no many-to-one join may silently multiply patients or rows.

The primary historical eligibility buffer is b=24 hours: an examination or lab is timestamp-eligible only when its source timestamp is no later than t0 - 24 hours, and it must be inside a 30-day pre-index window. Prespecified sensitivity buffers are b=0 and b=72 hours, using the same frozen feature code and no selection of the most favorable buffer. These are acquisition/measurement eligibility proxies. Because the HCC source has no report-finalization time, clinician-view time, or tumor-board time, none of them identifies what was actually known at the decision.

Development uses index years 2015–2018, calibration uses 2019, and the locked patient-disjoint test uses 2020–2021. Index years 2022–2025 are retained for a descriptive ascertainment-drift audit only if selected pathology labels fail the prespecified endpoint-known gate; they are not silently used as a negative or positive validation set. Patient assignment follows the catalog's participant-level discovery partition; no reserved partition is claimed as external validation.

## Outcome construction and missingness

The endpoint is pathology-only high-grade MVI (M2), never a preoperative examination label. All pathology rows linked by the composite encounter key are searched with an anchored state-machine parser that requires an assigned/selected M0, M1 or M2 field/value, rather than a definition or template legend elsewhere in the document. The parser may use pathology.pathology, pathology.examination findings and pathology.examination diagnosis; pathology text is prohibited from predictors. A second independently implemented parser must run on the locked test before the result is interpreted.

Set Y=1 only for an explicit concordant M2 assignment; set Y=0 only for an explicit concordant M0 or M1 assignment; set Y=? for absent pathology, no assigned grade, boilerplate-only text, conflicting assignments, non-resection pathology, unresolved parser disagreement, or any record outside the adjudicated frame. Every eligible operation remains in the primary denominator. No complete-case result can replace the primary all-target result.

The primary estimate is a paired patient-level difference in net benefit at pt=0.30:
NB = TP/n - FP/n * pt/(1-pt), and DeltaNB = NB_S - NB_W,
where W is the workflow/provenance baseline and S is the semantic extension. For each Y=? patient, compute exact sharp lower and upper bounds for the paired contribution to DeltaNB by assigning the unknown outcome the value that minimizes or maximizes that patient's paired contribution. Bootstrap patients, not rows, and report the 95% interval for both bounds, the unknown fraction, and parser-disagreement fraction. A secondary complete/concordant-only estimate is descriptive and cannot override bounds that include harm.

A blinded clinical pathologist should review all parser disagreements and a random sample of each positive, negative and unknown stratum. The computation may enforce that review took place and report agreement; it cannot establish that sampling was adequate, that slides represent the full tumor, or that the local text label is clinically correct. The absence of pathology timestamps means that pathology is an outcome reference, not evidence available at t0.

## Predictors and nested baselines

All predictors are generated from rows with the composite index key and timestamps in [t0-30 days, t0-b]. No clinical-document text, pathology text, post-index row, procedure name, diagnosis name, order status, or medication exposure enters the primary predictor set.

- B: age and sex from encounters.age and encounters.sex; assay-specific laboratory summaries from labs.test, labs.quantitative result, labs.qualitative result, labs.specimen type, labs.test time; and missingness/count indicators. Assays are kept separate because labs.quantitative result has no unit column. No units are imputed and similarly named assays are not pooled without an exact locked dictionary.
- W = B + workflow/provenance: presence, count and recency of qualifying examinations; exact modality family and machine-label availability from examinations.check, examinations.machine model, examinations.start time; and availability/order-process indicators for each assay. Machine and missingness fields are workflow variables, not clinical findings.
- S = W + semantic content: a frozen CT/MRI dictionary applied to examinations.examination; text from examinations.examination findings and examinations.examination diagnosis; and the exact assay-specific laboratory values and within-assay summaries. Text is represented by a fixed, auditable character/word n-gram model with vocabulary learned only in development; a regularized logistic model is preferred for reproducibility.

The primary contrast is S versus W, so an apparent gain from merely having an exam, machine label, or assay ordered does not count as semantic clinical value. W versus B is a secondary workflow-value contrast. A prevalence-only model, a label-permuted model, and a model using only metadata are mandatory baselines.

For each buffer, fit development models only in 2015–2018, fit calibration only in 2019, lock the model, and evaluate once in 2020–2021. The same buffer order and gates are reported for b=0,24,72; no favorable buffer, threshold or calendar interval may be selected after seeing results. Thresholds other than 0.30 are exploratory and cannot rescue the primary claim.

## Falsification, uncertainty and interpretation

Supportive evidence requires all of the following at b=24:

- pooled 95% lower bound for DeltaNB > 0.02;
- the pooled and each estimable CT and MRI stratum has a 95% lower bound no lower than -0.02;
- the result remains non-harmful at b=0 and b=72, with a prespecified robustness summary rather than cherry-picked buffer;
- calibration-in-the-large is within ±0.05 and calibration slope is 0.80–1.25 for S;
- the semantic extension passes overlap, effective sample size, endpoint-known, parser-agreement and patient-disjointness gates; and
- the matched semantic-permutation analysis is compatible with zero while the observed semantic contrast is not.

Adverse evidence is a pooled 95% upper bound below -0.02, a CT/MRI stratum lower bound below -0.02, calibration failure at the review threshold, a harmful buffer sensitivity, or a workflow-only/permuted control that explains the apparent gain. A model that improves AUC but increases false-positive review burden or has bounds crossing harm is adverse for this stated use.

The result is inconclusive—not supportive—if the partial-identification interval crosses both -0.02 and +0.02; there are too few endpoint-known M2 cases (prespecify at least 20 in every claimed CT/MRI stratum and at least 100 eligible patients per stratum); positivity/overlap or effective sample size fails; parser disagreement exceeds 5%; or the locked test cannot be independently maintained. The 2022–2025 ascertainment audit is inconclusive if selected-grade availability collapses, not evidence of low M2 risk.

A supportive result would justify a prospective study with report-finalization and clinician-view timestamps, pathologist-adjudicated standardized MVI sampling, and an observed review action. It would not justify changing resection extent or treatment. An adverse result would discourage deployment of a fixed M2 flag because the apparent signal is non-transportable, workflow-driven or harmful. An inconclusive result would identify the missing evidence needed: timestamped reports and tumor-board decisions, image access or raw images, pathology slides and sampling protocol, and prospective treatment/outcome linkage.

The automatic Harbor verifier can check exact source paths, composite joins, row/patient counts, time windows, feature leakage, locked splits, parser agreement calculations, paired net-benefit arithmetic, sharp-bound arithmetic, calibration, permutation controls, and whether prose matches computed outputs. It cannot establish pathology truth, sampling adequacy, the acceptability of a 0.30 threshold, actual clinician access, whether reviewers act on a flag, treatment effect, recurrence, survival, or generalization outside this snapshot. Those claims require clinical adjudication, additional data, or a prospective study.
