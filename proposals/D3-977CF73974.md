# Residual metabolic reprogramming in treatment-resistant RA macrophages: a distinct regulatory program or scaled inflammation?

**Child of [prior hypothesis]** (D3-M3, E-MTAB-8322).
**Status: PLANNED analysis. A bounded feasibility diagnostic was executed ([research job]); the full inferential analysis has NOT been executed.**

## Scientific opening

The parent candidate (candidate-2edf24fb, top of board, mu 33.69) asks whether
treatment-resistant RA synovial macrophages carry a glycolytic/HIF-1α metabolic program
that distinguishes them from remission RA. Its analysis plan — pathway-level comparison
plus an all-genes vs inflammatory-only ML classifier — tests **separability**: can the
groups be told apart, and do metabolic genes add discriminative information?

But separability does not answer the mechanistic question that matters clinically:
**is the metabolic program a DISTINCT regulatory program, or merely a scaled version of
inflammatory activation?** If resistant macrophages are simply "more inflamed," then
their metabolic gene expression is fully predicted by their inflammatory gene expression
and the therapeutic implication is "suppress inflammation harder." If instead the
metabolic program is decoupled — elevated above what the inflammatory level predicts —
that is a distinct, potentially self-sustaining regulatory state (consistent with the
failed-resolution framework [K1] and the glycolysis–HIF-1α→IL-1β positive-feedback
mechanism [K3]), with a different therapeutic implication (target metabolism directly,
e.g., glycolysis/HIF-1α inhibitors, rather than only anti-inflammatory therapy).

This is the same distinction made explicit in the immunometabolism review: cell-type-
specific metabolic dysregulation as a stratifiable pathotype, not a passive correlate of
inflammation [K2]. The parent's ratio test hints at this but is confounded by using
within-donor ratios of two noisy set means; a regression-residual formulation makes the
"distinct program" vs "scaled inflammation" contrast explicit and testable.

## Hypothesis (falsifiable)

In treatment-resistant RA synovial macrophages, the glycolytic/HIF-1α metabolic program
is quantitatively distinct from what is predicted by the level of inflammatory
activation alone:

**H1 (primary):** After regressing per-donor metabolic score on per-donor inflammatory
score across all independent donors (pooled), resistant-RA donors have a significantly
positive mean residual metabolic score (their metabolic program exceeds the
inflammation-predicted level), whereas remission-RA donors do not.

**H2 (specificity):** The positive residual in resistant donors is not a general RA
effect: naive-RA donors show a residual near zero (or significantly lower than
resistant), i.e., the metabolic decoupling is specific to the resistant state rather
than to any RA.

**H3 (decoupling index, secondary):** The within-group correlation between metabolic
and inflammatory scores is lower in resistant than in remission donors (the two
programs are less tightly coupled in resistant disease), or the resistant group shows a
flatter/shifted metabolic–inflammatory relationship.

## Clinical importance

~30% of RA patients fail to reach remission on current DMARDs/biologics (difficult-to-
treat RA) [K1]. If resistance is associated with a self-sustaining metabolic program
decoupled from inflammatory intensity, then (1) anti-inflammatory therapy alone may be
mechanistically insufficient in these patients, (2) metabolic co-targeting (glycolysis/
HIF-1α inhibition) becomes a rational strategy, and (3) the residual metabolic score is a
candidate stratification biomarker for which patients to add a metabolic agent to.
Conversely, if the metabolic program is fully predicted by inflammatory intensity, the
evidence argues against a distinct metabolic target and against metabolic-biomarker
stratification — an equally decision-relevant negative result.

## Analysis plan (PLANNED)

Gene sets (verified present 21/21 and 29/29 in the diagnostic):
- **Inflammatory (21):** IL6, TNF, IL1B, CCL2, CXCL8, NFKB1, REL, NFKBIA, SOD1, NOS2,
  PTGS2, CXCL10, CCL4, CCL5, CCL11, CCL13, CCL17, CCL19, CCL20, CXCL9, CXCL11.
- **Metabolic (29):** HK2, LDHA, PKM, PFKL, GPI, VEGFA, PDK1, ENO1, SLC2A1, SLC16A1,
  PGM1, ALDOA, TPI1, GAPDH, PGAM1, ENO2, PKLR, LDHB, HIF1A, EGLN1, EGLN2, EGLN3, VEGFB,
  PLVAP, CA9, BNIP3, NDRG1, PHGDH, RPE.
  (The assignment's extended SLC2A2–SLC2A20 list is trimmed to the mechanistically
  coherent 29-gene set; SLC2A1/SLC16A1 are the canonical MCT1/MCT4 transporters.)

1. **Per-donor scores.** For each of the 25 donors, score = mean of log2(count+1) over
   the set's genes (pseudobulk donor level; one sample per donor in this dataset).
2. **Pooled regression.** OLS: metabolic_score ~ inflammatory_score across all 25 donors.
   Extract per-donor residuals (metabolic expression beyond the inflammation-predicted
   level).
3. **Primary test (H1).** One-sample test of resistant-donor residuals vs 0
   (Wilcoxon signed-rank, n=6, with bootstrap 95% CI of the mean residual), and a
   resistant-vs-remission two-sample test (Welch t + permutation, n=6 vs 7).
4. **Specificity test (H2).** Naive vs resistant residual comparison (n=6 vs 6);
   remission vs resistant; healthy vs resistant (descriptive, n=4).
5. **Decoupling index (H3).** Within-group Pearson correlation of metabolic vs
   inflammatory scores per group (resistant, remission, naive, healthy); compare
   resistant vs remission correlations (Fisher z).
6. **Sensitivity checks.** (a) Leave-one-donor-out stability of the pooled slope and of
   the resistant mean residual; (b) alternative score transform (median instead of mean;
   log2(count+1) vs rank-based); (c) trim the metabolic set to core glycolysis-only
   (HK2, LDHA, PKM, PFKL, GPI, PGM1, ALDOA, TPI1, GAPDH, PGAM1, ENO1, ENO2) and to
   HIF-1α-target-only (VEGFA, PDK1, CA9, BNIP3, NDRG1, EGLN1-3) sub-sets; (d) exclude
   GAPDH/TPI1 (housekeeping) variant.
7. **Multiple-testing control:** primary family = {H1 one-sample, H1 group contrast,
   H2 naive contrast}; report Benjamini–Hochberg FDR; all sensitivity analyses are
   labeled exploratory.

## Competing explanations

1. **Inflammatory confounding (the null hypothesis being tested):** metabolic expression
   is a monotone function of inflammatory intensity; resistant patients are simply more
   inflamed. The residual test is designed precisely to separate this; a null result
   supports it.
2. **Cell-type/composition effects:** pseudobulk macrophage means may reflect shifts in
   macrophage subpopulations (e.g., M1-like fraction) rather than per-cell metabolic
   reprogramming. Cannot be resolved without single-cell deconvolution; a limitation,
   not a confound that biases the resistant-vs-remission contrast in a known direction.
3. **Treatment effects:** no per-donor treatment history in design.json; resistant
   patients have by definition received (and failed) therapy, which may itself alter
   metabolism (e.g., JAK inhibitors, steroids). Direction unknown; must be stated, not
   adjusted.
4. **Small sample size:** n=6–7 per group; the one-sample Wilcoxon at n=6 has limited
   power (minimum detectable median residual shift is large). Results must carry CIs;
   a non-significant result is inconclusive, not refuting.
5. **Pseudobulk / batch structure:** one 10x sample per donor; library-prep batch
   effects could correlate with group. Sensitivity: inspect residual vs sample-order
   plots; report but cannot fully control.
6. **Donor-relatedness:** SA149P/UPASA149 excluded per dataset annotation; no other
   relatedness flags in design.json.

## Outcomes

- **Supportive:** resistant mean residual metabolic score significantly > 0 (H1) and
  significantly greater than remission and naive (H2), with bootstrap CIs excluding 0,
  stable under leave-one-donor-out and gene-set sub-set sensitivity checks. Interpretation:
  evidence for a distinct metabolic regulatory program in resistant RA macrophages;
  motivates metabolic co-targeting and residual-score biomarker studies. Does NOT by
  itself establish causality or that the program is self-sustaining (would require
  perturbation: in vitro metabolic inhibition of resistant-derived macrophages).
- **Adverse:** resistant residuals indistinguishable from 0 and from remission/naive;
  metabolic and inflammatory scores remain tightly coupled in all groups. Interpretation:
  the metabolic program is a scaled version of inflammatory activation; argues against
  a distinct metabolic target and against metabolic-biomarker stratification; redirects
  toward inflammatory-intensity mechanisms of resistance.
- **Inconclusive:** small but directionally positive resistant residuals with CIs
  crossing 0; or strong sensitivity-check dependence (e.g., result driven by one donor
  or one gene sub-set). Interpretation: hypothesis-generating only; requires a larger
  cohort (external validation, e.g., independent resistant/remission RA macrophage
  transcriptomic cohorts) and single-cell resolution before any clinical claim.

## Method alternatives considered (record)

- **Parent's ML classifier (all-genes vs inflammatory-only logistic regression,
  LOO-CV):** retained as a supplementary check of separability, but it tests
  classification, not the distinct-program question; a classifier can separate groups on
  any correlated feature. Not the primary test.
- **Within-donor metabolic/inflammatory ratio (parent's ratio test):** noisy (ratio of
  two set means), scale-dependent, and does not model the cross-donor relationship;
  replaced by the regression-residual formulation, which is the ratio test generalized
  to a pooled, donor-level estimand.
- **Variance partitioning (mixed model, donor random effect):** not applicable here —
  one sample per donor, no replicate structure to partition.
- **Single-cell deconvolution / subpopulation analysis:** would address the composition
  confound but requires per-donor cell-level data and is out of scope for this
  cross-sectional check; recorded as the key follow-up if supportive.

## Falsification criteria

- H1 falsified if the resistant mean residual is not significantly > 0 (two-sided,
  alpha 0.05, BH-corrected) and the resistant-vs-remission contrast CI includes 0.
- H2 falsified if naive residuals are as high as resistant (naive ≥ resistant, CI
  excluding a resistant-specific shift).
- H3 falsified if the resistant within-group metabolic–inflammatory correlation is not
  lower than remission's (Fisher z, exploratory).

## What this analysis will and will not establish

- Will establish (if supportive): an association, in a cross-sectional pseudobulk
  cohort, that resistant-RA macrophage metabolic expression exceeds the
  inflammation-predicted level — i.e., a distinct metabolic state at the transcriptomic
  level.
- Will NOT establish: causality (metabolic program → resistance), self-sustaining
  dynamics, single-cell specificity, or clinical utility. Those require perturbation
  studies, longitudinal cohorts, and external validation — recorded as next tests.
