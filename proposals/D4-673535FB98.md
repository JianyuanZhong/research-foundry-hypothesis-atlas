# Executed Direction-A cystatin-C discordance incremental-information experiment

## Question, lineage, and frozen evidence boundary

This executed child of `[prior hypothesis]` tests its unchanged falsifiable question: among the exact UK Biobank selected qualifying first-repeat attenders with baseline creatinine eGFR at least 60, does adding binary baseline cystatin-C discordance (`D = I(eGFRcys_0 < 60)`) to a flexible creatinine-based model provide reproducible held-out information for repeat creatinine eGFR below 60?

The parent’s prior result supported an adjusted association in this selected cohort, not prediction or utility. This execution supports a stronger negative distinction: adding `D` caused small and repeat-stable average held-out loss reductions, but it did not pass the prespecified multiplicity-controlled two-loss gate and it significantly worsened risk concentration at the fixed 10% capacity. The frozen incremental-information claim is therefore classified **adverse/falsified under this protocol**. This is not evidence against biological relevance or every possible use of cystatin C.

All conclusions remain prediction among observed selected repeat attenders only. They are not baseline-deployable because elapsed repeat time is an observed predictor conditioned on qualifying future attendance. No claim is made about CKD diagnosis, chronicity, measured GFR, causality, benefit or harm of cystatin-C testing, clinical utility, or transport to nonattenders. Complete-case selection and the low event count remain limitations.

## Prospectively resolved implementation

The executed models and all preprocessing retained the parent’s information sets:

- **M0:** intercept; separate four-column natural-cubic-spline bases for baseline unrounded `eGFRcr_0`, baseline age, and elapsed years; binary sex.
- **M1:** exactly M0 plus binary `D` and no other cystatin-C feature.

Each natural spline used `patsy.cr` with training-fold empirical 25th, 50th, and 75th percentiles as interior knots, training-fold minimum/maximum boundaries, a centered constraint, and exactly four columns. Basis columns were standardized using training-fold means and population standard deviations. No held-out values informed knots, bases, or scaling.

Both models used sklearn `LogisticRegression`, `solver='lbfgs'`, L2 ridge `C=1`, unpenalized fitted intercept, `tol=1e-10`, `max_iter=2000`, no class weighting, and no tuning. The permitted identically cross-fitted unpenalized sensitivity converged in all 100 model-fold fits.

Ten frozen seeds generated outcome-stratified participant-level five-fold splits by seeded within-outcome permutation and deterministic round-robin assignment. This yielded 50 paired held-out fold prediction sets per model and one prediction per participant per repeat. Test folds contained 3,273–3,275 participants and 61–62 events. Predictions were finite, paired, and complete. Repeated predictions were never treated as independent participants.

For fixed capacity, held-out predictions were ranked separately within each held-out fold. Integer slots were allocated across the five folds by floor plus largest fractional remainder, fixing total selection at 1,637 participants (9.9988%) for 10% and 819 (5.0024%) for 5% in every repeat. Outcomes did not define ranks.

Uncertainty used a paired participant-level prediction bootstrap with all ten repeated predictions carried together: 2,000 successful primary replicates and 1,000 successful secondary replicates. Models were not refit, so all intervals are explicitly conditional on the frozen splits and fits and do not include full model-training variation. The two primary loss p-values used participant-cluster standard errors and Holm adjustment; selected risk and event capture formed a separately Holm-adjusted 10% capacity family. Calibration deterioration was operationalized before the run as an increase in absolute joint calibration-intercept error over 0.10, absolute slope-from-one error over 0.10, or absolute observed-minus-predicted risk error over 0.002, with the corresponding degradation interval above zero. These are computational gates, not clinically validated utility margins.

## Executed aggregate results

### Primary paired held-out losses

M0 mean Brier score was 0.01698955 and M1 was 0.01683293. The paired M1-minus-M0 difference was `-0.00015661` with conditional 95% interval `[-0.00029055, -0.00001844]`; the raw cluster-normal p-value was 0.02779 and Holm-adjusted p-value was 0.05557.

M0 mean log loss was 0.07620191 and M1 was 0.07580523. The paired difference was `-0.00039669`, conditional 95% interval `[-0.00083411, 0.00004479]`; raw and Holm-adjusted p-values were 0.08561.

Both loss point estimates were negative in all ten repeats, but neither passed the frozen two-loss Holm gate. The unpenalized sensitivity was nearly identical: Brier difference `-0.00015679` and log-loss difference `-0.00039355`.

The mean predicted-risk difference was essentially zero (`-0.00000458`, conditional 95% interval `[-0.00016158, 0.00015299]`), while the mean absolute risk change was 0.002319 (0.232 percentage points; interval `[0.0021599, 0.0024743]`). Thus `D` rearranged individual predictions modestly without changing average predicted risk, but this is not a treatment effect or deployable decision threshold.

### Calibration and discrimination

Using each participant’s mean prediction across the ten repeated held-out fits:

- M0 AUC was 0.82450 (conditional interval 0.79951–0.85094); M1 AUC was 0.82503 (0.80008–0.85132).
- M0 joint calibration intercept/slope were -0.04559/0.98572; M1 were -0.04657/0.98533.
- Observed-minus-mean-predicted risk was -0.0000155 for M0 and -0.0000109 for M1.

M1 did not meet the operational definition of materially worse calibration. The AUC difference was negligible and secondary; it cannot rescue failed loss/capacity gates.

### Fixed-capacity concentration

At the primary 10% capacity, M0 selected-risk was 10.3421% and captured 54.7896% of events. M1 selected-risk was 10.1833% and captured 53.9482%. M1 therefore **reduced** selected risk by `-0.0015883` (−0.159 percentage points; conditional interval `[-0.0031068, -0.0003168]`) and event capture by `-0.0084142` (−0.841 percentage points; interval `[-0.0138047, -0.0034159]`). Holm-adjusted capacity-family p-values were 0.02488 for selected risk and 0.00306 for capture. The selected-risk difference was negative in all ten repeats.

At the prespecified 5% capacity, the direction reversed but remained inconclusive: selected-risk difference `+0.0009768`, interval `[-0.0047051, 0.0071936]`, and event-capture difference `+0.0025890`, interval `[-0.0120644, 0.0194565]`. The capacity-specific divergence shows why neither capacity could be selected post hoc.

## Frozen-gate conclusion

The result is **adverse**, not supportive and not merely a null association result:

1. Both loss point estimates favored M1 consistently, but the multiplicity-controlled two-loss gate failed (Holm p=0.05557 and 0.08561).
2. Calibration did not materially deteriorate.
3. The required positive 10% selected-risk interval failed in the opposite direction: adding `D` significantly reduced selected risk and event capture at fixed capacity.
4. All model/fold/bootstrap checks succeeded, so model failure does not make the finding inconclusive.

The strongest supported claim is that, in the exact selected repeat-attender cohort and under the frozen ridge-spline protocol, baseline cystatin-C discordance made small repeat-stable changes that modestly lowered average held-out loss but did not establish multiplicity-controlled incremental prediction and worsened the prespecified 10% triage concentration. This falsifies the parent’s narrow claim of reproducible predictive/decision information under this protocol; it does not negate the parent’s association result.
