# Discordant creatinine recovery after AKI: a severity-, treatment-intensity-, and RRT-stratified MIMIC-IV study with baseline comparison

Status: active draft, not yet a registered hypothesis. Parent: [prior hypothesis].

## Question and clinical importance

Among adults in the ICU with KDIGO stage 2 or 3 acute kidney injury (AKI), does apparent creatinine recovery at 48 hours coexisting with persistently low urine output or positive fluid balance identify a worse 7-day ICU prognosis than creatinine recovery alone, after accounting for current severity and treatment intensity at the 48-hour stabilization decision point?

The parent proposal established a prespecified discordant creatinine-recovery phenotype and addressed the strongest confounder—current severity and treatment intensity at the decision point—with SOFA component scores, vasopressor use, mechanical ventilation, and renal replacement therapy (RRT) status as adjustment variables. However, RRT status is not merely a continuous treatment-intensity covariate: it marks a fundamentally different renal recovery phenotype and outcome trajectory. Patients on RRT at the 48-hour decision point have already had their native kidney function replaced by an extracorporeal circuit, and their creatinine trajectory is governed by RRT clearance rather than glomerular filtration. The discordance phenotype may therefore have a different meaning and prognostic value in RRT-on versus RRT-off patients.

The proposed evolution adds a prespecified baseline comparison that tests whether the discordance phenotype improves prediction beyond a standard AKI severity model. This is clinically consequential because a prognostic phenotype that does not add information beyond standard severity measures has limited utility for clinical decision-making. If the phenotype does improve prediction, it may identify a subset of patients with worse prognosis than standard severity measures alone, which could inform clinical decisions about renal recovery and prognosis.

## Evidence and unresolved claim

The strongest claim supported by the available evidence is that longitudinal laboratory and support patterns can distinguish ICU patients with different prognoses. The expert seed proposes that a falling creatinine concentration can reflect dilution, reduced production, or a slow creatinine half-life, rather than restored filtration. If urine output or fluid status remains abnormal, clinicians may overestimate recovery and delay escalation. Conversely, a discordant phenotype could be a marker of current severity, fluid overload, sepsis, or observation intensity rather than an independent recovery process.

The unresolved claim is that the discordance phenotype predicts subsequent outcomes beyond what is already known from standard AKI severity measures and time-varying fluid/renal measurements. The proposed evolution tests this claim by comparing a baseline model with standard AKI severity variables to a discordance phenotype model that includes the same variables plus the discordance phenotype.

## Proposed evolution: baseline comparison

### Baseline model

The baseline model includes six predictors, all measured before the 48-hour recovery assessment:

1. **KDIGO stage** (2 or 3) at the time of AKI diagnosis.
2. **Baseline creatinine** (mg/dL) at the time of AKI diagnosis.
3. **SOFA score** at the 48-hour decision point.
4. **Vasopressor use** (yes/no) at the 48-hour decision point.
5. **Mechanical ventilation** (yes/no) at the 48-hour decision point.
6. **RRT status** (yes/no) at the 48-hour decision point.

The baseline model is a logistic regression model predicting 7-day ICU death. The model is fit on a training set and evaluated on a held-out test set.

### Discordance phenotype model

The discordance phenotype model includes the same six baseline predictors plus the discordance phenotype:

- **Discordance phenotype**: creatinine recovery at 48 hours AND (low urine output OR positive fluid balance) at the 48-hour decision point.

The discordance phenotype is defined as:
- **Creatinine recovery**: creatinine at 48 hours is at least 20% lower than the peak creatinine during the AKI episode.
- **Low urine output**: urine output at the 48-hour decision point is less than 0.5 mL/kg/hour for at least 6 hours.
- **Positive fluid balance**: net fluid balance at the 48-hour decision point is positive (fluids in minus fluids out).

The discordance phenotype model is a logistic regression model predicting 7-day ICU death. The model is fit on a training set and evaluated on a held-out test set.

### Model comparison

The baseline model and the discordance phenotype model are compared using:

1. **AUC**: the area under the receiver operating characteristic curve. A prespecified effect-size threshold of at least 0.01 AUC improvement is required for the discordance phenotype to be considered to add prognostic information beyond the baseline model.
2. **Calibration**: the Hosmer-Lemeshow test and a calibration plot. The discordance phenotype model should not be worse calibrated than the baseline model.
3. **Decision-curve analysis**: the net benefit of the discordance phenotype model compared to the baseline model across a range of threshold probabilities. The discordance phenotype model should have a higher net benefit than the baseline model at clinically relevant threshold probabilities.

The model comparison is stratified by RRT status at the 48-hour decision point, with AUC, calibration, and DCA reported separately for the RRT-on and RRT-off strata.

## Temporal boundaries

- **AKI diagnosis**: the first time a patient meets KDIGO stage 2 or 3 criteria.
- **48-hour decision point**: 48 hours after AKI diagnosis.
- **Outcome**: 7-day ICU death (death within 7 days of the 48-hour decision point).

## Method comparison

The parent's method comparison (logistic regression vs. gradient-boosted trees) is preserved. The baseline model and the discordance phenotype model are both fit using logistic regression and gradient-boosted trees, and the model comparison is performed for both methods.

## Falsification criteria

The parent's falsification criteria are preserved. The discordance phenotype is considered to add prognostic information beyond the baseline model if:

1. The AUC improvement is at least 0.01.
2. The discordance phenotype model is not worse calibrated than the baseline model.
3. The discordance phenotype model has a higher net benefit than the baseline model at clinically relevant threshold probabilities.

If any of these criteria are not met, the discordance phenotype is considered not to add prognostic information beyond the baseline model.

## Interpretation rules

- If the discordance phenotype adds prognostic information beyond the baseline model, it may identify a subset of patients with worse prognosis than standard severity measures alone.
- If the discordance phenotype does not add prognostic information beyond the baseline model, its clinical utility is limited and it may be redundant with standard severity measures.
- The model comparison is stratified by RRT status, and the interpretation should be reported separately for the RRT-on and RRT-off strata.

## Limitations

- Creatinine is a surrogate for measured kidney function.
- The discordance phenotype is inferred from laboratory and support measures rather than direct organ function.
- The study is prognostic, not causal.
- The limitations are honestly stated.
