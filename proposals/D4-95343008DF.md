# UKB-38: Telomere Length Trade-Off — Vascular Protection vs Cancer Risk

## Unresolved Question and Clinical Importance

Leukocyte telomere length (LTL) is a widely used biomarker of biological aging, but its clinical utility as a risk stratifier remains uncertain. Prior prospective cohorts have reported inconsistent associations between LTL and mortality: some find shorter telomeres predict higher all-cause mortality, while others find null or weak associations. Critically, the existing evidence suggests a possible **dissociation** between vascular and cancer outcomes — a pattern that has not been formally tested as a hypothesis.

The clinical importance is substantial: if longer telomeres genuinely confer vascular protection without increasing cancer risk, LTL could inform risk stratification for preventive cardiovascular interventions (e.g., statin intensity, aspirin use) in a population where competing risks matter. Conversely, if longer telomeres increase cancer risk (as the "replicative capacity" hypothesis predicts), this would argue against using LTL as a general health-aging biomarker and would have implications for telomere-targeted interventions.

**The unresolved question:** In UK Biobank participants with measured LTL, does longer telomere length associate with LOWER incident CAD risk but NOT with lower incident cancer risk — representing a trade-off between cellular replication capacity and disease risk?

## Hypothesis (Specific, Falsifiable)

**H1 (Primary):** In UKB participants with LTL (field 22191, adjusted T/S ratio), after adjustment for age, sex, CRP, ApoB, and albumin, a 1-SD increase in LTL is associated with a **reduced** hazard of incident CAD (HR < 0.95 per SD, 95% CI excluding 1.0) but **not** with a reduced hazard of incident all-cancer (HR 0.95–1.05 per SD, 95% CI including 1.0).

**H2 (Nonlinear):** The telomere→CAD dose-response is **nonlinear** (restricted cubic spline test of nonlinearity p < 0.05), with the strongest protective association at the upper end of the LTL distribution.

**Falsification criteria:** The trade-off hypothesis is falsified if:
- (a) The telomere→CAD association is null after adjustment (HR per SD 0.90–1.10, 95% CI including 1.0), OR
- (b) The telomere→cancer association is also significantly negative (protective, HR < 0.95 per SD, 95% CI excluding 1.0), indicating a general protective effect rather than a vascular-specific trade-off.

## Design

### Primary Analysis: Nonlinear Dose-Response (RCS)
- **Method:** Cox proportional hazards model with **restricted cubic splines** (4 knots at 5th, 35th, 65th, 95th percentiles of LTL) for the telomere→outcome association.
- **Outcomes:**
  - **Primary:** Incident CAD (ICD10 I20-I25 from field 41270)
  - **Secondary:** All-cancer (field 40005), CRC (C18-C20 from 41270), lung cancer (C33-C34 from 41270), all-cause death (field 40000)
- **Adjustments:** age (34-0.0), sex (31-0.0), CRP (30710-0.0), ApoB (30640-0.0), albumin (30600-0.0)
- **Follow-up:** From assessment date (53-0.0) to first event or end of follow-up. Exclude events occurring before assessment date.
- **Exclusions:** Participants with pre-existing CAD or cancer at baseline (identified from 41270 with date before assessment date).

### Baseline Comparison: Cox PH with Linear Telomere
- **Method:** Cox PH with LTL as a continuous linear term (per 1-SD increase).
- **Purpose:** Compare with RCS to determine whether the linear approximation captures the dose-response shape or whether nonlinearity is present.
- **Report:** HR per 1-SD, 95% CI, p-value for linear term; p-value for test of nonlinearity (RCS vs linear).

### Multi-Outcome Trade-Off Comparison
- **Method:** Compare the telomere→CAD HR vs telomere→all-cancer HR.
- **Interaction test:** Fit a model with telomere × outcome-type interaction (CAD vs cancer) to formally test whether the association differs between vascular and cancer outcomes.
- **Report:** HR for CAD, HR for cancer, interaction p-value.

### Sensitivity Analyses
1. **Reverse causation:** Exclude first 2 years of follow-up (events within 2 years of assessment date are excluded).
2. **Unadjusted telomere:** Use field 22192 (unadjusted T/S ratio) instead of 22191.
3. **Smoking:** Not available in the verified fields — noted as a limitation. Smoking is a major confounder of both LTL and CAD/cancer risk.

### Alternative Explanations
1. **Inflammation confounding:** CRP and albumin are included as adjustments, but residual confounding by systemic inflammation is possible. Glei et al. [K2] showed 48% attenuation of the LTL-mortality association after inflammatory marker adjustment.
2. **Reverse causation:** Subclinical disease may shorten telomeres before clinical diagnosis. The 2-year exclusion sensitivity analysis addresses this.
3. **Measurement error:** LTL is measured by qPCR T/S ratio, which has inter-laboratory variability. The adjusted T/S (22191) is preferred over unadjusted (22192).
4. **Competing risks:** Participants who die before cancer diagnosis are censored in the cancer model. Fine-Gray competing risks analysis is a possible extension but not required for the primary hypothesis.
5. **Smoking:** A major confounder of both LTL and CAD/cancer risk, but not available in the verified fields. This is a key limitation.

## Methodological Distinction from Existing Cox-Interaction Designs

This proposal is **methodologically distinct** from the existing 7 Cox-interaction branches in the candidate population:
- **Nonlinear dose-response:** Uses restricted cubic splines (RCS) to characterize the shape of the telomere→outcome association, not just a linear HR. This tests whether the protective effect is concentrated at the upper end of the LTL distribution.
- **Multi-outcome trade-off:** Explicitly compares the telomere→CAD association vs telomere→cancer association with a formal interaction test, rather than testing a single exposure-outcome interaction.
- **Trade-off hypothesis:** Tests a specific biological hypothesis (vascular protection vs cancer risk) rather than a generic interaction.

## What Supportive, Adverse, and Inconclusive Results Would Mean

- **Supportive (H1 + H2 confirmed):** Longer telomeres protect against CAD but not cancer, with a nonlinear dose-response. This would support the trade-off hypothesis and suggest LTL could inform vascular risk stratification. Would not establish causality (observational data, no smoking adjustment).
- **Adverse (H1 falsified):** Telomere→CAD association is null, or telomere→cancer association is also protective. This would falsify the trade-off hypothesis and suggest LTL is either not a useful vascular risk marker or has a general protective effect.
- **Inconclusive:** Telomere→CAD association is borderline (HR 0.95-1.00), or nonlinear test is non-significant. This would not falsify the hypothesis but would not provide strong support. Would require larger cohort or different outcome definition.

## Which Stronger Conclusions Would Require Additional Data

- **Causality:** Would require Mendelian randomization (genetic instruments for LTL) or a longitudinal intervention study.
- **Smoking adjustment:** Would require smoking data (not in verified fields).
- **External validation:** Would require a second cohort with LTL and CAD/cancer outcomes.
- **Mechanism:** Would require tissue-specific telomere measurements or telomere maintenance gene expression data.
