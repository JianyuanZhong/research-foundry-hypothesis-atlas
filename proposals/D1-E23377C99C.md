# Which pulmonary-nodule handoff intervention should be tested first? An all-opportunity discharge-boundary decomposition

## Decision question and substantive advance

For an incidental pulmonary-nodule CT recommendation that is already available to the inpatient team by discharge, two different EHR mechanisms could prevent a usable plan from being present at the transition:

1. **artifact-availability gap:** the canonical discharge artifact is not observed as stored by the actual discharge time; or
2. **action-content gap:** the artifact is observed as stored by discharge, but omits the patient-specific nodule action or carries an incomplete or discordant interval without a documented alternate resolution.

These mechanisms imply different prospective interventions. The first nominates a discharge-time artifact finalization/availability check; the second nominates structured radiology-to-discharge insertion and interval reconciliation. The parent conditioned process inference on artifacts stored by `dischtime + 24 hours` and compared fidelity with an operational 80% target. That design could silently remove the dominant transition-time state and could not determine which intervention to test. This successor makes the actual discharge boundary primary, gives both component risks the same all-eligible denominator, compares them on an absolute scale, and treats delayed artifacts and unresolved data separately.

**Strongest evidence-supported claim.** Published observational evidence and the frozen parent audits support the existence and measurability of a pulmonary-nodule communication problem, not the causal benefit of discharge wording. Kwan et al. reported absent interval imaging in 35/65 patients with explicit follow-up suggestions and an association between discharge-summary mention and timely imaging (J Hosp Med 2019; PMID 30794133; PMCID PMC6625441). A tracking-program report observed follow-up imaging in 34/67 post-implementation versus 16/52 pre-implementation patients (J Digit Imaging 2023; PMCID PMC10287591), while a systematic review found tracking systems promising but heterogeneous and most nonrandomized evidence at serious or critical risk of bias (Chest 2025; PMID 40081655; PMCID PMC12264345). The parent inspected the cited public records/full XML as specified there. These studies motivate a process experiment; they do not show that an original recommendation is appropriate, that stored text was communicated, or that either proposed intervention improves care.

The complete parent audit is feasibility evidence only. It found 1,194 first-subject lexical candidates, with a canonical artifact stored by discharge in only 285, by 24 hours in 770, and by seven days in 1,022. Proxy content labels among the prior +24-hour gated subset were 403 faithful, 188 omitted, and 52 incomplete/discordant. Those proxy counts are not adjudicated outcomes and are not used to claim mechanism dominance.

A new no-sampling timestamp audit of all 331,794 `DS` rows linked them to `hosp/admissions`: 86,609 were stored by discharge, 147,800 first appeared from >0 to 24 hours, 76,977 from >24 hours to seven days, 20,329 after seven days, and 17 had missing `storetime`; 62 rows did not link to a core admission. Every delayed row with an available admission had `charttime <= dischtime` (147,800/147,800 in the 0-24-hour band and 76,977/76,977 in the 24-hour-to-seven-day band). This makes a timestamp or documentation-finalization artifact plausible. It does **not** prove that delayed artifacts were visible, complete, signed, transmitted, or available to patients at discharge: `charttime` is not an availability timestamp, and `storetime` semantics require prospective/local validation.

## Falsifiable primary hypothesis and estimands

The unit is the first established eligible recommendation admission per subject. Let `N` contain every such opportunity with a resolvable report/addendum state and a qualifying recommendation-bearing state stored by `dischtime`, irrespective of discharge-artifact timing or content. For each opportunity define mutually exclusive discharge-boundary states:

- `A=1` (**observed artifact-availability gap**) when the sole canonical `DS` row has known `storetime > dischtime`;
- `C=1` (**action-content gap**) when that row has known `storetime <= dischtime` and blinded adjudication finds either omission or incomplete/discordant action;
- `F=1` when an artifact stored by discharge faithfully contains the nodule, chest/thoracic CT action, and exact 3/6/12-month interval or equivalent due date;
- `R=1` when an artifact stored by discharge documents an explicit alternate resolution; and
- `U_D=1` when artifact timing/content cannot be classified because the row is absent, `storetime` is missing, the admission link is absent/contradictory, or text is unreadable.

Thus `A`, `C`, `F`, `R`, and `U_D` partition `N`; unresolved data are not failures. Omission and incomplete/discordant are also retained separately inside `C`. An artifact stored before the recommendation but still present at discharge is not excluded: if it lacks the action it is a prespecified **stale-artifact sequencing subtype** of `C`, because both report and artifact exist at discharge but the artifact was not updated. Its chronology is reported separately and is not interpreted as a writer having seen information unavailable at artifact storage.

The paired all-opportunity component risks are

`p_A = sum(A)/N` and `p_C = sum(C)/N`,

and the primary decision contrast is the paired absolute difference

`Delta = p_A - p_C = mean(A-C)`.

No risk ratio is primary. The content-failure proportion conditional on an available artifact is descriptive only and cannot replace `p_C`, because conditioning would remove the availability mechanism.

**Directional hypothesis.** The observed artifact-availability component exceeds the content component by a prespecified dominance margin `m=0.05`: `Delta > 0.05`. Five percentage points is not a clinical quality target and is not presented as evidence-derived. It encodes the operational requirement that one intervention address at least five additional observable gaps per 100 eligible opportunities before it is nominated ahead of the other. Report sensitivity at `m=0` and `m=0.10`.

- Nominate the **artifact-finalization/availability intervention** first only if the simultaneous 95% lower confidence limit for `Delta` exceeds `+0.05`, uncertainty/annotation gates pass, and the timestamp-validation caveat is carried into the trial.
- Nominate the **structured action-content intervention** first only if the simultaneous 95% upper confidence limit for `Delta` is below `-0.05`.
- Otherwise neither mechanism dominates sufficiently: test a bundled or 2-by-2 factorial workflow, or obtain more mechanism data, rather than selecting from point estimates.

This hypothesis is falsified if the simultaneous lower limit does not exceed `+0.05`; evidence for the opposite intervention requires the stronger upper-limit criterion below `-0.05`.

## Population, sampling, and temporal rules

The screen scans all 2,321,355 radiology rows; there is no row sampling. Automated lexical rules are high-sensitivity candidate retrieval only. Two radiology-trained reviewers adjudicate every candidate independently while blinded to discharge text and all later data.

Include adults age >=18 with:

- a base CT with `(subject_id, hadm_id)` matching an admission and `admittime - 12 hours <= charttime <= dischtime`;
- chest CT, CTPA/CTA, or abdominal CT including lung bases, based on all detail fields and text adjudication for ambiguous coverage;
- an incidental pulmonary/lung nodule or indeterminate focal nodular pulmonary opacity; and
- an affirmed, patient-specific, unconditional recommendation for chest/thoracic CT at exactly 3, 6, or 12 calendar months, first present in a resolvable report state with `storetime <= dischtime`.

Exclude scheduled lung-cancer screening, an index examination explicitly for nodule surveillance, lung-cancer staging/oncologic surveillance, known actively managed primary pulmonary malignancy, nonpulmonary nodules, conditional/optional recommendations dependent on unavailable risk, explicit no-follow-up recommendations, hospice/comfort-only transitions, in-hospital death, or a pre-discharge CT that explicitly supersedes/satisfies the recommendation. Do not require a “new” nodule, Fleischner eligibility, or guideline concordance because morphology, longitudinal stability, smoking pack-years, and context are incomplete. Abstract size/type/multiplicity/newness/stability when stated.

Select the first eligible admission per subject by base `charttime`, then base `storetime`, then `note_id`, without discharge state or future outcome information. Repeat eligibility at a zero-hour admission boundary and with `edregtime/edouttime`; repeat descriptively among subjects with at least two prior years of same-system radiology trace.

The unconditional screened cascade reports: all lexical candidates; CT metadata-confirmed candidates; adjudication dispositions; `U_R`; established eligible opportunities `N`; recommendation-state availability by discharge; discharge row/link/time data state; and all five discharge-boundary states. No unresolved chain or discharge data state is recoded as `A`, `C`, or success.

## Blinded outcome adjudication

After radiology fields are locked, two transition reviewers independently inspect the canonical discharge text while blinded to report text, exact recommendation, report/discharge chronology, and all later care. They abstract patient-specific nodule content, chest/thoracic CT action, interval/due date, responsible clinician/service when stated, and explicit alternate resolution. Matching occurs algorithmically only after both domains are locked. Consensus or a third reviewer resolves all disagreements.

At each cutoff, classify:

- **faithful:** affirmed patient-specific pulmonary nodule, chest/thoracic CT, and the same exact 3/6/12-month interval or unambiguously equivalent due date;
- **incomplete/discordant:** nodule without action, CT without usable timing, or different action/interval without explicit alternate resolution;
- **omission:** no active patient-specific actionable nodule follow-up content; or
- **explicit alternate resolution:** documented refusal/preference, goals-of-care choice, specialist takeover with a concrete alternative, already scheduled outside-system follow-up, or a documented clinical decision superseding CT.

Negated/history/family-history text, generic “follow up with PCP,” copied guideline boilerplate, or copied report prose without an active patient-specific plan is not faithful. Alternate resolution records documentation only; reviewers do not judge correctness and it is neither success nor failure. Undocumented communication, orders, scheduling, patient receipt, and outside care are never inferred.

Report raw agreement, Gwet's AC1 with subject-bootstrap 95% intervals, and kappa for eligibility, exact interval, and four-state content. Confirmatory decision inference requires complete consensus, raw agreement >=0.80 and AC1 >=0.70 for each domain. If a gate fails, revise the codebook and independently re-adjudicate affected records once; persistent failure permits only reviewer-specific bounds. Automated screening must have sensitivity >=0.95 with lower 95% Wilson bound >0.90 against consensus. Automated labels cannot replace adjudication unless each relevant PPV is >=0.90 with lower bound >0.80.

## Primary analysis, simultaneous uncertainty, and precision gates

Report counts and absolute risks for `A`, omission, incomplete/discordant, their union `C`, `F`, `R`, and `U_D`, all over the same `N`. Calculate `Delta` from paired subject-level indicators; do not compare two conditionally selected cohorts.

Use 9,999 subject-level nonparametric bootstrap replicates with a fixed published seed. In each replicate recompute `p_A`, `p_C`, and `Delta`. Construct a two-sided family-wise 95% max-|t| simultaneous interval across these three prespecified quantities, using multinomial influence-function standard errors; if a component is zero or studentization fails, use a prespecified non-studentized max-deviation bootstrap and report exact multinomial score intervals as a check. Report the full five-state multinomial vector with simultaneous 95% intervals. Because only the first opportunity per subject is retained, subject resampling is also the analysis-unit resampling.

A dominance conclusion requires all of:

- at least 200 established eligible opportunities;
- at least 20 events in each of `A` and `C` (otherwise only exact descriptive bounds);
- no unresolved discharge state exceeding 5% of `N`; if it does, report partial-identification bounds assigning all `U_D` first to `A` and then to `C`, and call intervention selection inconclusive unless the same margin conclusion holds throughout;
- all agreement/screen gates; and
- simultaneous 95% interval width for `Delta` <=0.10 (half-width <=0.05).

Stratify the paired risks descriptively by 3/6/12-month interval, CT coverage, artifact-before-versus-after-recommendation chronology, final medical/surgical service, ICU exposure, number of care transitions, and discharge destination. Report interaction contrasts with simultaneous intervals only as exploratory; no data-dependent subgroup determines the primary intervention.

## Strict discharge primary and delayed-artifact transitions

`dischtime` is the sole confirmatory cutoff. Repeat state construction at `dischtime + 24 hours` and `dischtime + 7 days` only to describe transitions:

- an `A` opportunity remains delayed or becomes faithful, omitted, incomplete/discordant, or alternate resolution when its artifact first appears;
- a `U_D` opportunity may become classifiable if its data problem resolves, but is never retroactively counted as a discharge-time failure;
- content is read from the sole artifact and therefore does not imply that the text existed earlier than `storetime`;
- later report addenda are tabulated separately and cannot alter discharge eligibility.

Publish the complete individual-state transition matrices from discharge to +24 hours and +7 days, along with `charttime-dischtime` and `storetime-dischtime` distributions. The following patterns have bounded meanings:

- A large `A` risk that rapidly transitions to faithful content, especially when `charttime <= dischtime`, is compatible with signing/export latency or timestamp semantics; it does not prove a clinical handoff failure.
- Persistent absence through seven days is stronger evidence of documentation latency but still cannot prove what users saw at discharge.
- Delayed artifacts that appear with omission/discordance indicate both timing and content vulnerabilities; they remain `A`, not `C`, in the strict primary decomposition so mechanisms are not double-counted.

Before deploying an artifact-finalization intervention, a prospective site audit must link `storetime`, signature/finalization time, EHR UI visibility, patient-release/transmission time, and actual physical departure. If that audit shows artifacts labeled delayed by MIMIC were already visible/final at departure, the availability interpretation is falsified as a workflow mechanism even if `p_A` statistically dominates. The MIMIC result would then nominate timestamp validation or data-pipeline repair, not a clinical intervention.

## Falsification tests

1. **Timestamp-artifact challenge:** quantify whether strict-delayed artifacts have `charttime <= dischtime` and whether they transition to faithful by +24 hours/+7 days. MIMIC cannot prove visibility; a prospective timestamp crosswalk is required. Availability-mechanism language is disallowed without it.
2. **Chronology challenge:** report `C` separately when discharge `storetime` precedes versus follows first recommendation-bearing `storetime`. If apparent content failure is confined to stale artifacts, test an update/reconciliation trigger rather than generic author education.
3. **Alternate-resolution challenge:** if most nonfaithful available artifacts contain explicit alternate resolution, they do not support a content-insertion intervention.
4. **Unresolved-state bounds:** if assigning all `U_D` or plausible eligible `U_R` cases to the competing mechanism reverses the dominance decision, the result is inconclusive.
5. **Boundary sensitivity:** repeat with the zero-hour admission rule and ED window. Reversal of the dominance decision means eligibility timing, not a stable workflow component, drives the finding.
6. **Negative feasibility findings remain negative:** dedicated `RECOMMENDATION(S)` headings were present in only six prior proxy-gated cases and `discharge_detail` has no section labels. No heading mechanism may be revived.

## Downstream imaging is retired from intervention selection

Same-system CT does not determine which workflow intervention is nominated. If retained at all, it is a descriptive appendix using the parent's all-eligible observation states: MIMIC-observed recommendation-timed CT, recorded death before window end, no timed CT with continued same-system trace, and no subsequent trace/unknown capture. No adjusted causal model or observed-only restriction is primary.

Preserve the prior negative feasibility result: continued-system trace differed between proxy-faithful and proxy-omitted cases (253/403, 62.8%, versus 99/188, 52.7%); among trace-positive cases timed CT was 44/253 (17.4%) versus 16/99 (16.2%), and any chest CT was 77/253 (30.4%) versus 30/99 (30.3%). In all proxy-gated cases timed CT was 49/403 (12.2%) versus 17/188 (9.0%). These data provide no compelling evidence that discharge wording improves surveillance and show why an observed-only subset is unsafe. No absence of MIMIC CT is called nonadherence or absent outside care.

## Interpretation and intervention-selection matrix

| Result | Supported interpretation | First prospective test | Not supported |
|---|---|---|---|
| Simultaneous lower bound for `Delta` > +0.05; gates pass; `U_D` bounds do not reverse; delay persists or local timestamp crosswalk confirms nonavailability | More established opportunities have no artifact observed stored at discharge than have a stored artifact with failed action content, by at least 5 per 100 | Discharge-time canonical-artifact finalization/availability check, with structured capture of true UI/signature/transmission timestamps | That patients lacked all instructions, clinicians failed to communicate, or finalization improves follow-up/benefit |
| Same statistical result, but most delayed rows become faithful rapidly and local crosswalk shows visibility/finality at departure | MIMIC `storetime` is an unsuitable workflow-availability proxy | Data/timestamp validation or pipeline repair; do not test a clinical availability intervention first | A real artifact-availability bottleneck |
| Simultaneous upper bound for `Delta` < -0.05; gates pass; omission/discordance is not mostly alternate resolution | Action content is the larger directly observable gap | Structured radiology-to-discharge nodule action and exact-interval reconciliation, with stale-artifact update trigger | Appropriateness of the report recommendation or causal benefit |
| Interval lies within [-0.05,+0.05], interval width >0.10, event/agreement gate fails, or unresolved bounds cross either decision | Neither component is demonstrably dominant | More adjudication/mechanism data, or bundled/2-by-2 factorial pilot | Choosing an intervention from point estimates |
| `p_A` and `p_C` are both low and precise, or nonfaithful states are predominantly documented alternate resolutions | These two discharge-EHR mechanisms are unlikely to be the principal bottleneck | Consider active tracking, scheduling, patient communication, and cross-system exchange in a new prospective study | Declaring communication or follow-up complete |
| Content gap concentrated only where artifact predates report | A stale-artifact/update mechanism is plausible | Automated reconciliation/update trigger rather than generic education | That summary authors omitted already-visible content at artifact creation |
| Delayed artifacts frequently appear with content failure | Timing and content vulnerabilities coexist | Bundled/factorial intervention if no margin dominance; preserve strict primary attribution to `A` | Double-counting the same opportunity in both component risks |

A statistically adverse result is scientifically useful: it can rule out one EHR target as the dominant observable bottleneck. An inconclusive result cannot be converted into a clinical recommendation.

## Computable claims, adjudication boundary, and missing evidence

An automatic verifier can check source hashes and archive members; schemas; `(subject_id, hadm_id)` and `(subject_id, note_id)` joins; one-artifact-per-admission logic; reciprocal chain reconstruction; strict `storetime <= dischtime` cutoffs; first-subject selection; mutually exclusive state assignment; common all-opportunity denominators; transition matrices; paired bootstrap/simultaneous intervals; margin and precision gates; unresolved-state bounds; agreement metrics; and whether the stated interpretation matches the computed matrix.

Radiology and transition experts must establish incidental status, unconditionality, exact interval, addendum meaning, CT coverage, faithful content, discordance, and documented alternate resolution. Neither code nor MIMIC can establish guideline appropriateness, true pathology, smoking pack-years, what clinicians or patients saw, whether text was discussed, orders/referrals/appointments, outside-system care, actual departure time, patient preferences not documented, cancer outcome, radiation harm, survival benefit, or intervention causality. Stronger claims require a local timestamp/visibility audit, linked outpatient and outside-system data, expert clinical review, and a prospective randomized, factorial, or stepped-wedge workflow trial.
