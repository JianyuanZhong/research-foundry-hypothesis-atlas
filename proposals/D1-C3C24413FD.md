# Does laboratory surveillance add information after recorded organ-support context?

Status: Episode 45 substantive child of the assessed-valid parent `[prior hypothesis]`. This is a fixed-landmark observational prognostic and measurement-process experiment. It is not a causal treatment study, a physiology study, a treatment recommendation, a hospital-quality ranking, or evidence that changing laboratory or organ-support practice improves survival.

## Unresolved question and clinical importance

The strongest claim supported by the parent and discovery work is limited: in the configured eICU snapshot, revision-safe laboratory result/revision timing, recorded vital trajectories, and later recorded ICU disposition can be joined at the ICU-stay level. Outcome-blind audits establish fields and feasible pre-landmark rows; no model has been fitted here. The parent does not establish that a laboratory-process signal is independent of treatment intensity or organ-support documentation.

The unresolved claim is:

> At ICU-relative minute 360, among adult first ICU stays, does revision-safe laboratory observation history add information about later recorded ICU death beyond admission context, recorded vital level/trajectory, carePlanEOL documentation, and recorded organ-support context through minute 360—and is its incremental information larger when no qualifying organ-support record is present?

This matters for interpretation of a future retrospective review signal. If laboratory-process information remains useful among episodes without documented support, it could identify biochemical or workflow information not represented by the available vital summaries. If the increment disappears after support adjustment or is confined to episodes with support documentation, the apparent laboratory signal is more consistent with severity, support escalation, or documentation intensity. Neither result says that ordering more tests, withholding support, or changing treatment would benefit a patient.

The substantive advance over the parent is a prespecified treatment/organ-support context challenge, including a support-stratum interaction and timestamp-safe source-specific falsifications. It asks a clinically meaningful interpretive question rather than adding an unmotivated model layer. The exact six-hour population, predictor boundary, revision-safe laboratory clock and competing-risk endpoint are retained.

## Evidence-supported versus tested claims

Evidence currently supports only:

- the eICU patient, hospital, laboratory, vital, carePlanEOL, infusion, treatment, respiratory-care, intake/output and medication fields listed below exist in the frozen snapshot;
- longitudinal tables have `patientunitstayid` keys and the source schemas list ICU-relative time fields;
- a bounded first-100,000-row-per-file probe found pre-landmark numeric timestamps in each support table.

The experiment will test the prognostic/interpretive claim, not establish:

- specimen collection, test order, administration, or clinician-intent time;
- that a recorded airway, infusion, medication start, treatment string, fluid-balance row or dialysis value means the intervention was actually delivered or ongoing;
- that absent documentation means absence of support;
- that the parent’s recorded vital trajectory is physiologic recovery;
- causality, treatment benefit, unrecorded death, complete goals-of-care status, or external validity.

The primary directional hypothesis H1 is that the lab-process increment is positive for death-specific held-out-hospital performance after support adjustment, but larger in the no-qualifying-support stratum than in the any-qualifying-support stratum. The neutral hypothesis is no lab increment or no support-stratum difference. A result in the opposite direction is scientifically informative and is not to be relabeled as confirmation.

## Organ-support context and estimand

Construct an outcome-blind recorded-support context from the five support tables:

- airway/ventilation: invasive `airwaytype) documented by `respcarestatusoffset), or an in-window ventilation/airway `treatmentstring);
- vasoactive/inotropic: a qualifying in-window `infusionDrug.drugname), `treatmentstring) or medication-start `drugname);
- renal/fluid support: a qualifying dialysis treatment string or positive numeric `dialysistotal).

For each class retain binary presence, six-bin presence/count channels and source-specific documentation counts. Define `S_any=1) if any class has a qualifying record; `S_none=1) otherwise. The latter means no qualifying record in these tables, never clinical absence. Define a secondary 0/1/2+ class-count stratum only if event and hospital support gates pass. Include total support-table documentation intensity and source indicators so `S_any) is not merely a proxy for one table’s opportunity to record care.

The primary interaction estimand is the difference in lab-process gain between strata:
`G_s = IBS_death(B_C,s) - IBS_death(B_CL,s)`, where positive values favour adding laboratory process and `sin{none,any}); `I = G_none-G_any`. Estimate this as paired held-out predictions, first macro-averaged across hospitals and then across folds. Report the analogous calibration changes and alive-discharge gain. H1 predicts `G_none>0` and `I>0`; the study does not require an arbitrary clinical-effect threshold to call the sign, but requires the confidence interval and support gates below for a supportive interpretation.

## Models and substantive alternative

Create one canonical episode representation containing the parent’s admission context, lab state/process channels, vital state/process/trajectory channels, carePlanEOL channels, and all timestamp-safe support channels. No model receives hospital ID or post-360 information.

- **B_C, transparent support-adjusted baseline:** pooled elastic-net cause-specific logistic hazards for the 12 intervals using admission variables, vital levels/intensity/trajectory, landmark laboratory state, carePlanEOL channels, six-bin support summaries and source-specific support documentation intensity. It has no laboratory timing/process channels other than the fixed observation-set audit.
- **B_CL, transparent primary increment:** B_C plus the six-bin revision-safe laboratory process channels and prespecified lab-process-by-`S_any) interaction. B_C and B_CL must have identical rows, folds, endpoint construction, preprocessing, support features and interval targets.
- **B_result, paired clock sensitivity:** B_CL with `labresultoffset) replacing `availability_offset) on exactly the same retained laboratory rows.
- **B_v and matched support-only controls:** retain the parent baseline without support context to quantify the support adjustment; fit support-only-by-trajectory and vital-process-by-`S_any) controls. If a vital-process increment is as large as the lab increment, the result is not lab-specific.
- **T_CL, learned temporal alternative:** a small one-layer six-bin GRU with separate death and discharge heads, fed exactly the canonical six-bin support/lab/vital/carePlanEOL tensor and the same admission/context channels as B_CL, with no hospital identifier. A parameter-matched non-temporal MLP is mandatory. The GRU can reveal ordered escalation, persistence and nonlinear cross-domain co-occurrence that a transparent additive model loses; it is not evidence of physiology merely because it is temporally expressive.

The primary scientific conclusion is based on B_C versus B_CL and the prespecified interaction. T_CL is confirmatory only if its held-out improvement is uncertainty-compatible, its support-stratum pattern agrees, its order-shuffle falsification is negative, and an interpretable stable pattern summary is saved. A complex model winning without these checks is not supportive.

## Splits, evaluation and uncertainty

Use five outer folds holding out approximately 20% of hospitals. Keep every `uniquepid) in one fold. Within each development fold use a 75/25 hospital-stratified fit/validation split. Learn lexical maps, 64-key lab codebook, field support, scaling, imputation, support strata, regularization, calibration and any threshold only in the fit portion. No outcome-derived feature or lexical category selection is permitted.

Primary evaluation is paired held-out prediction:

- macro-hospital death-specific integrated Brier difference over 360--1800;
- calibration intercept/slope and observed-versus-predicted CIF at 600, 1,200 and 1,800;
- parallel alive-discharge CIF/Brier/calibration;
- macro-hospital primary, with patient-weighted and fold-specific secondary results;
- a fixed-burden retrospective review diagnostic at predicted death-CIF thresholds 0.01, 0.02, 0.05 and 0.10: reviews per 100 episodes, recorded-death capture, PPV, false-review burden and number needed to review. This is label-relative retrospective performance, not net benefit or clinical utility.

Use at least 1,000 hospital-cluster bootstrap resamples of held-out predictions for 95% intervals, preserving each hospital’s paired model predictions; report fold/hospital heterogeneity and event counts. A threshold cell is unsupported unless it has >=200 held-out episodes, >=20 recorded deaths, >=20 alive discharges and >=20 hospitals.

Support gates for the primary interaction are >=20 hospitals, >=500 episodes and >=200 recorded deaths in both `S_none) and `S_any), with at least 20 alive discharges when discharge comparisons are reported. If the support gate fails, report the failure and do not convert a pooled association into a support-stratum claim.

## Falsification and leakage tests

Save every result with a named output and interpretation trace.

1. Permute six laboratory-bin labels within patient while preserving total lab counts, key composition, revision channels and missingness.
2. Reassign laboratory histories within hospital and B_C risk strata, breaking patient linkage while preserving local workflow distributions.
3. Permute `S_any) labels within hospital and B_C risk strata; a remaining lab-support interaction indicates an interaction artifact.
4. Permute support-bin order within patient, preserving support counts, and rerun T_CL; loss of a claimed temporal pattern is required for an ordering interpretation.
5. Fit matched vital-process and vital-process-by-support controls; a comparable or larger increment weakens laboratory specificity.
6. Compare death and alive-discharge increments; an equal or larger discharge increment suggests generic length-of-stay/documentation workflow.
7. Run source-dropout variants (infusion only, treatment/respiratory only, intake/output only, medication only) and report whether the result depends on one sparse or idiosyncratic source.
8. Repeat with carePlanEOL documentation challenge/restriction, keeping missing documentation as unknown.
9. Repeat common-analyte, revision-known-only, interface-stratified, support-stratified and hospital-support-regime analyses; compare availability and result clocks on identical lab rows.
10. Mask the `(300,360]) laboratory, vital and support bins, then mask early bins, to test near-landmark artifacts.
11. Enforce and report zero post-360 predictor rows, no Apache mortality/APS fields, no hospital-ID predictors, no target-fold preprocessing, no outcome-derived support lexicon, no `activeupondischarge), no future respiratory `ventendoffset), no future medication stop, and no text-to-number conversion of `cellvaluetext).
12. Treat any use of offsets outside [0,360] as a separate leakage audit, never as primary evidence.

## Supportive, adverse and inconclusive results

Supportive evidence requires all support gates and: B_CL improves death-specific integrated Brier and calibration over B_C with a 95% interval compatible with improvement; `G_none>0) and `I>0) with stable hospital/fold direction; the discharge increment is smaller; lab-link and bin-order permutations attenuate the increment; no matched vital-process control replaces it; and the pattern is not confined to one support source, interface, hospital or surveillance regime. This supports a support-context-conditioned association with recorded death and a narrower interpretation of laboratory review signals. It does not support occult physiologic deterioration, a treatment effect or benefit from more testing.

Adverse evidence is a null or harmful B_CL increment, `I<=0) with adequate support, unchanged performance after lab-link/order permutation, an equal/larger discharge or vital-process increment, dependence on one hospital/source/interface, or reliance on rows whose information timestamp is post-landmark. These results reject the proposed interpretation even if the model predicts well.

Inconclusive evidence includes failed stratum/event/overlap gates, intervals spanning zero and the prespecified meaningful range, unstable held-out hospitals, excessive missing support timestamps, unreproducible lexical maps, unresolved result-versus-availability discrepancy, or a learned-model gain without a reproducible pattern and falsification support. Sparse or absent documentation cannot be treated as a negative clinical finding.

## Unavailable evidence and revisit criteria

This eICU snapshot does not provide verified specimen collection or laboratory-order timestamps, laboratory units, reliable medication administration records, complete infusion start/stop semantics, complete ventilation intervals, expert-adjudicated organ-support states, continuous waveforms, complete goals-of-care documentation, unrecorded mortality or an external health-system validation set. The experiment must not claim these.

A dynamic landmark or causal support/treatment analysis should be revisited only after a source supplies validated administration/order/collection times, support start/stop semantics, treatment indication and confounder measurement, repeated-landmark estimands and expert review. A mechanistic physiologic state-space model additionally requires unit-aware measurements and adjudicated states. Clinical benefit requires prospective silent-mode impact evaluation or randomization. Harbor can check computation, joins, timestamps, held-out predictions, uncertainty and conclusion/output linkage; it cannot establish those unavailable clinical facts.
