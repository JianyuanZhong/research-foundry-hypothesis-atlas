## Missing-Y partial identification and selection-sensitive heterogeneity

For observed rows use `d_i=(Y_i-p0_i)^2-(Y_i-p1_i)^2`. For missing rows use the exact affine endpoint values

```
d_i(y)=2*y*(p1_i-p0_i)+p0_i^2-p1_i^2
lower_i=min(d_i(L_train), d_i(U_train))
upper_i=max(d_i(L_train), d_i(U_train)).
```

Sum observed and missing endpoints and divide by fixed `N_s` to obtain deterministic `Delta_s_lower` and `Delta_s_upper`; also report all-missing-at-L, all-missing-at-U, and least-favorable-row endpoint scenarios. These are conditional identification regions, never confidence intervals. The global parent bound remains primary; stratum bounds are a predeclared heterogeneity stress analysis and must not be averaged with inverse-observation weights.

For a partition, compute the interval for the heterogeneity contrast by the conservative endpoint arithmetic `[Delta_s_lower-Delta_ref_upper, Delta_s_upper-Delta_ref_lower]`; label it an enclosure, not a sharp joint region, because strata share training fits and range information. A stratum can be called **robustly favorable** only if its Delta interval is strictly positive, its complementary reference is supported, and the heterogeneity interval excludes zero in the predeclared direction. Any endpoint crossing zero is selection-sensitive, even when observed-case G_s is favorable. Missingness fractions and the inherited outcome-blind opportunity strata must be reported by the same partition to show whether apparent heterogeneity tracks recording opportunity.

Retain the parent's common-displacement tipping analysis globally. ## Outcome-blind decision-relevance and falsification controls

Before reading Y, carry forward the parent's selected-event -> B -> P -> BP funnel and mutually exclusive recording-opportunity classes: exam+follow-up, exam only, follow-up only, document-only, none. Flag absolute SMD >=0.5, observation below 80% of the pre-origin rate, low calendar ESS, or arm support failure. A favorable q pattern with a strong observation gradient is selection-sensitive, not supportive.

The compiler/verifier must execute these falsification checks.

* **No-leakage placebo:** recompute all q, crossing, and stratum labels after replacing P by a training-fold-frozen P permutation within broad event-era and arm cells while holding B, core variables, Y, row membership, and patient folds fixed. This is a restricted linkage placebo, not an exact conditional-randomization test. A similar placebo-level movement or heterogeneity pattern weakens attribution to P.
* **Timing placebo:** rerun only the descriptive forecast-movement summaries with P shifted to the prior eligible assay (or, if unavailable, an explicitly missing P), never using a post-event value. It must not alter the primary model. A comparable pattern suggests temporal/linkage artifact; failure to define a valid prior assay is inconclusive, not negative.
* **Stratum-boundary placebo:** shift each prespecified numeric boundary to the adjacent predeclared sensitivity boundary (age 60/70, lead 12/36 hours, interval 30/90 days) without selecting the best result. Concordant qualitative direction is stability only; discordance is heterogeneity instability, not evidence of a subgroup benefit.
* **Opportunity negative control:** compare forecast movement and Y observation by recording-opportunity class, outcome-blind. A large q difference confined to a class with differential observation is a selection warning. The result cannot establish completed follow-up or clinical quality.
* **Leave-one-source-family-out ascertainment check:** recompute opportunity classes omitting examinations, encounters, and documents one family at a time. This checks recording dependence only; it cannot validate assay meaning.

Placebo and boundary diagnostics are not primary outcomes and cannot rescue a failed gate, unsupported range, nonfinite prediction, or nonestimable G.

## Interpretation precedence and falsification criteria

Apply this precedence in the verifier: (1) computationally invalid or unsupported, (2) inconclusive/nonestimable, (3) adverse, (4) selection-sensitive/heterogeneity-unstable, (5) supportive surrogate-level forecast relevance. A result that meets more than one label receives the highest-priority adverse/unsupported/inconclusive label.

* **Supportive, narrowly:** reconstruction and clocks pass; the relevant future block and stratum meet outcome-blind and observed-triplet gates; finite predictions and training range pass; primary G is estimable; the stratum's deterministic Delta interval is strictly positive; its heterogeneity envelope is directionally stable under the declared boundary sensitivity and not reproduced by the P/timing placebo; and no material observation-gradient warning is present. This supports only a selected short-horizon prognostic association and reproducible forecast movement in that calendar mixture. It does not support a subgroup treatment recommendation.
* **Adverse:** M1 is no better or repeatedly worse, the relevant Delta interval is nonpositive, or a placebo reproduces the claimed P-specific pattern under adequate support. This argues against the proposed incremental prognostic signal for that scope, while not proving no clinical value.
* **Selection-sensitive:** observed-case improvement or forecast crossings coexist with a Delta interval crossing zero, strong observed/missing gradients, opportunity-class dependence, or subgroup direction changes under the predeclared boundary sensitivity. This is the expected label for a signal plausibly driven by who receives/has a recorded Y.
* **Heterogeneity-inconclusive:** sparse stratum, failed arm gate, no observed patient clusters, low ESS, unsupported range, outside-range Y, nonfinite predictions, zero SD, or unstable partition/placebo result. Do not pool or relabel it as homogeneous.

## Added support and repeated-patient robustness layer

This layer is a diagnostic and sensitivity analysis only. It is run after the frozen event/B/P/BP construction and alongside, never inside, the parent's model fitting. It must not trim, reweight, refit, redefine, or replace the primary `G` or the global missing-`Y` envelope.

### Training-derived support contract

For each assay and origin, define the training set as the parent's pre-origin observed complete-triplet rows used for the locked fit. Before inspecting any future `Y`, calculate from that training set, separately for each arm where the arm is represented, finite extrema for the continuous pre-event coordinates `B`, `P`, `P_lead_hours`, and `TACE2_to_repeat_days`; use the recorded age coordinate before model imputation and retain an explicit missing-age level. Define a future row as `S0` (M0 support) if its arm and sex category occur in the relevant training set, its age-missingness/category is represented, and each finite M0 coordinate (`B`, age when nonmissing, lead, interval) lies in its corresponding training closed interval. Define `S1` (M1 support) by adding the same requirement for finite `P`. A row is `S0_only` when it passes S0 but fails the P requirement; it is `outside_categorical_support` when an arm/sex/age-missingness level is absent. If any required training extrema are missing or equal, mark that coordinate support undefined rather than silently treating it as supported.

For each continuous coordinate `x`, report the deterministic normalized extrapolation margin

```
e_x = max((L_x-x)/(U_x-L_x), (x-U_x)/(U_x-L_x), 0)
```

where `L_x,U_x` are the pre-origin training extrema; report `e=max_x e_x`, with `e=NA` for an undefined coordinate. The support audit reports counts and fractions of future BP rows in `S1`, `S0_only`, and `outside_categorical_support`, by assay, origin/block, arm, and each existing prespecified stratum. No future outcome is used to define support. The full primary analysis retains all rows; support-warning rows are not deleted and their observed-case `G`, q summaries, and Delta bounds are not relabeled as in-support results.

A support-qualified statement is permitted only for `S1` rows with finite predictions, adequate predeclared gates, and a complete training support contract. A result that is favorable only after excluding support-warning rows is labeled **extrapolation-sensitive**, not supportive. If fewer than 20 S1 rows or fewer than 10 S1 rows in either arm are available for a proposed support-qualified comparison, report it as descriptive/inconclusive. Never infer transportability from this historical support check.

### Equal-patient-influence sensitivity

For each assay, origin, block, and stratum, let `K_g` be the number of BP-eligible rows belonging to patient `g` in that cell, and define `w_i=1/K_g`. For the observed rows, calculate the equal-patient-influence squared-error contrast

```
Delta_cluster = sum_i w_i * d_i / sum_i w_i,
 d_i=(Y_i-p0_i)^2-(Y_i-p1_i)^2.
```

For missing rows, retain the same endpoint construction as the parent, replacing `d_i` by `lower_i` or `upper_i`; this gives `Delta_cluster_lower` and `Delta_cluster_upper`. The primary fixed BP-row-denominator `Delta` and `G` remain unchanged. Report the number of patients, maximum `K_g`, effective patient count `N_patient=(sum_i w_i)^2/sum_i w_i^2`, and the direction of the cluster-weighted point contrast and interval relative to the primary result. Do not use inverse-observation weights: `K_g` is defined from all BP-eligible rows before reading `Y`, so repeated-patient weighting is independent of outcome observation.

When a cell has at least five distinct patients, also compute a deterministic patient leave-one-cluster-out range for the observed point contrast (and for each missing-`Y` endpoint scenario): refit nothing, remove all rows for one patient, and recompute the same fixed predictions and denominator on the remaining rows. If fewer than five patients, mark this influence diagnostic inconclusive. A sign reversal under equal-patient weighting or a single-cluster deletion is a robustness failure/selection warning, not evidence against the primary estimand itself; it prevents a robust forecast-relevance label for that cell.

### Repeated-event and calendar support controls

The support audit must distinguish the number of rows from the number of unique patients in every annual block. A patient may contribute to more than one selected repeat event, but no row may enter a training fold and test block for the same event date; patient-level fold assignment is inherited and remains fixed. Report the maximum and distribution of selected repeat rows per patient in training and future cells. A calendar block with fewer than five patients, or with no represented arm in training, is computationally non-estimable for support-qualified heterogeneity even if its row count reaches the row threshold. The annual block is never merged after inspection.

## Added deterministic falsification and interpretation rules

1. **Support-shuffle negative control.** Preserve every future row, patient fold, arm, calendar block, and `Y`; permute only the support labels using a fixed seed and within assay/block cells. This tests bookkeeping (the observed support-label counts must not change under rerunning the same deterministic label function; a shuffled-label comparison is not evidence of prognostic validity). It cannot validate transport or clinical meaning.
2. **Patient multiplicity negative control.** Recompute the descriptive q and observed-case `d` summaries after retaining the earliest selected repeat event per patient in each future block, without refitting or changing the primary row set. This is a sensitivity control for repeated-event concentration, not a new population. If the direction exists only with the full repeated-event set, label it multiplicity-sensitive.
3. **Support boundary control.** Repeat the support audit using the predeclared inner training 1st and 99th percentiles as a stricter descriptive flag, calculated with the same finite training rows and never selected for performance. This cannot rescue a result failing the closed-interval contract; disagreement between extrema and percentile flags is support uncertainty.

Interpretation precedence is extended to: (1) computationally invalid or unsupported; (2) inconclusive/non-estimable; (3) adverse; (4) extrapolation-sensitive or multiplicity-sensitive; (5) selection-sensitive/heterogeneity-unstable; (6) supportive surrogate-level forecast relevance. A narrowly supportive label additionally requires that the favorable direction and deterministic missing-`Y` interval remain favorable under equal-patient weighting, have no single-patient influence reversal, and are not confined to `S0_only` or categorical-support-warning rows. These checks only strengthen an association/forecast claim. They do not establish units, severity, a patient-important outcome, a treatment effect, safety, utility, benefit, a monitoring policy, or transport.
