# Do laboratory workflow features make early ICU death alerts unsafe to transport?

Status: Episode-6 independent successor of `[prior hypothesis]`; proposed child of the frozen valid parent `[prior hypothesis]`. This is an eICU adaptation of expert seed `[starting question]`, not a reproduction of any demonstration paper.

## Scientific question, evidence boundary, and advance

The strongest claim supported before this experiment is only that the frozen eICU 2.0 snapshot contains multi-hospital ICU stays linked to ICU-relative vital and laboratory observations, laboratory result-revision timestamps, and heterogeneous recorded observation processes. The parent established data bindings and a computable design; it did not fit a model or demonstrate transport failure, workflow causation, clinical utility, or harm.

The unresolved claim is:

> Among adults still observable six ICU hours after an initial ICU admission, does adding early laboratory observation-process information to measured clinical values cause a larger loss of a prespecified early 48-hour ICU-death alert's operating characteristics when the alert is transported to held-out hospitals than when it is evaluated at development hospitals?

“Cause” is not an estimand here; the test estimates an association between recorded process information and transport degradation. The clinical importance is that an escalation, monitoring, transfer, or scarce-resource prioritization list can retain apparent development-site accuracy while missing high-risk patients, generating excess alerts, or becoming miscalibrated in a new ICU. The substantive advance is a threshold- and burden-aware, hospital-held-out transport estimand rather than an AUROC-only comparison. It does not establish that testing practice causes harm, that a testing policy should change, or that the alert is clinically useful.

## Population, landmark, and outcomes

The unit is one eICU `patientunitstayid`. From `patient`, retain rows with numeric age >=18, `unitstaytype='admit'`, `unitvisitnumber=1`, `unitdischargestatus` exactly `Alive` or `Expired`, and finite `unitdischargeoffset > 360`. Deduplicate before any split: among qualifying rows sharing a non-missing `uniquepid`, retain the smallest non-missing `hospitaladmitoffset`, breaking ties by smallest `patientunitstayid`; if all such offsets are missing, retain the smallest ID. Report retained stays with missing `uniquepid`. A non-missing `uniquepid` must have one split role within every fit.

The landmark is ICU-relative minute 360. Predictors may use only clinical/result/observation offsets in the closed interval [0, 360]. A laboratory row is usable only when its finite `labresult` is in that interval and it is revision-available by minute 360: numeric non-empty `labresultrevisedoffset <= 360`, or `labresultoffset` when the revision field is empty. Rows revised after 360 are excluded. No discharge, APACHE, diagnosis, treatment, medication, note, or post-landmark field is a predictor.

The primary binary endpoint is ICU death in (360, 3240], defined as `unitdischargestatus='Expired'` and `360 < unitdischargeoffset <= 3240`. It is not post-discharge mortality.

The mandatory competing-state sensitivity retains the parent labels but makes their complement explicit and exhaustive for finite discharge offsets:

- D: `Expired` with `360 < unitdischargeoffset <= 3240`;
- C: `Alive` with `360 < unitdischargeoffset <= 3240`;
- R: any qualifying status with `unitdischargeoffset > 3240`, hence alive and still in the ICU at minute 3240, even if the eventual discharge status is `Expired`.

Thus a patient who dies after 48 hours is R for this 48-hour estimand, not D. Report the eventual status within R descriptively. Verify mutual exclusivity, exhaustiveness, finite offsets, and the count of late eventual deaths; stop the definitive analysis if this construction fails.

## Baseline, learned alternative, and method choice

Both model families use the same target, eligible rows, folds, threshold rule, uncertainty procedure, and V versus V+P information contrast.

The transparent baseline is paired L2 logistic regression (C=1, deterministic solver) for V and V+P. Numeric imputation/scaling, categorical encoding, and every residualization are fitted inside the relevant outer training data. Logistic regression is primary because the incremental P block, predicted-risk scale, threshold, and calibration behavior are auditable. Its limitation is that first/last/mean/min/max summaries lose temporal order, trajectory shape, recovery/worsening, and irregular timing.

The substantive learned alternative is a one-layer width-64 GRU with dropout 0.1, Adam learning rate 1e-3, batch size 256, weighted binary cross-entropy, at most 30 epochs and patience 5, using twelve fixed 30-minute bins over [0,360]. V sequences contain only within-bin mean and last observed value for each measured channel; empty values use outer-training medians and no observation mask, count, timestamp, or time-since-last feature. V+P adds laboratory observation masks, within-bin counts, capped time-since-latest-result, and fixed early process totals; context is appended to the final hidden state. This is deliberately a values-only versus values-plus-process contrast: the learned branch can reveal temporal order and recovery/worsening that the static baseline loses, while testing whether process information remains portable. It is not selected because it is complex.

As a prespecified representation control, fit V+M and V+P+M logistic models, and the corresponding GRU control if the GRU branch converges, using M only to test whether a laboratory-process transport effect is merely global monitoring intensity. These are secondary decomposition models, not a new endpoint or threshold. A training-only V-risk residualization of P is another diagnostic, not a causal adjustment.

Use five whole-hospital outer folds over the supported hospitals and five fixed repetition seeds 20260913 through 20260917. A hospital enters the fixed supported set only if it has >=200 eligible stays and >=10 D events; use the same set for every model and repetition. Require at least 20 supported hospitals for a definitive transport claim. Within each outer training-hospital set, randomly but deterministically reserve a patient-grouped 20% internal threshold holdout, keeping each non-missing `uniquepid` in one role. Fit each model on the remaining 80%, score the internal holdout for development-site metrics and threshold selection, and apply that unchanged fitted model and threshold to the held-out hospitals. No outer-test row, hospital descriptor, or external outcome enters preprocessing, fitting, thresholding, or model selection.

The 7,200 science-second budget has concurrency 2. Allocate approximately 1,800 seconds to full-source coverage and feature construction, 1,200 to paired logistic fits/predictions and controls, and up to 4,200 to bounded GRU fitting/predictions; request at most one GPU only after a bounded CPU diagnostic demonstrates that the GRU cannot fit within the allocation. Preserve checkpoints and logs. If GRU convergence or compute fails, report the logistic estimand and mark temporal claims deferred; do not replace the cohort or estimand.

## Threshold and transport estimand

For each repetition r, outer fold k, model family f, and representation g in {V,V+P}, use only the internal threshold holdout. Let `t_rkfg` be the highest unique predicted-risk threshold t for which sensitivity for D is at least 0.80, with alerts defined as predicted risk >= t. The highest rather than lowest threshold is a deliberate repair: it minimizes alert fraction subject to the sensitivity target. Ties are not split; all observations at the threshold are alerted. If the internal holdout has no D event or no finite usable predictions, record threshold failure and do not choose an all-alert or favorable substitute. Also report the attained internal sensitivity and alert fraction.

Apply `t_rkfg` unchanged to the held-out hospitals. For each r,k,f,g report internal and external sensitivity, PPV, specificity, NPV, alert fraction, Brier score, and calibration intercept/slope. Primary estimates are the equal-weight mean over the 25 valid repetition-fold cells; report pooled and hospital-macro summaries secondarily. A cell with no D is omitted only from sensitivity/AUROC/AUPRC, not from Brier, calibration when identifiable, PPV/NPV, or alert fraction; every omission and denominator is reported.

Define positive transport deterioration so its sign is unambiguous:

- (L_{sens}=Sens_{internal}-Sens_{external});
- (L_{PPV}=PPV_{internal}-PPV_{external});
- (L_{AF}=|AF_{external}-AF_{internal}|), alert-rate instability;
- (L_{Brier}=Brier_{external}-Brier_{internal});
- calibration loss is the external minus internal absolute deviation from intercept 0 and slope 1, reported separately for intercept and slope.

The primary process transport contrast is (L_{g=V+P}-L_{g=V}) for sensitivity, PPV, and alert-fraction instability. Positive values mean that adding recorded laboratory process is less stable across hospitals. Report the paired internal increments (Metric_{V+P}-Metric_V) so a transport penalty is not presented as a benefit if P never improves development-site performance. Fixed 5%, 10%, and 20% predicted-risk summaries and sensitivity targets of 70%, 80%, and 90% are robustness diagnostics; only 80% is primary and none is a validated bedside threshold. Do not calculate clinical net benefit as a clinical conclusion because no alert action or harm/benefit ratio is observed; alert burden and PPV are the available decision-facing quantities.

Use 2,000 hospital-cluster bootstrap resamples of complete paired internal/external prediction sets, preserving the outer folds and repetitions. Re-select each development threshold within each bootstrap resample from its internal predictions, then recompute the paired contrasts. Report percentile 95% intervals, bootstrap failure counts, and the number of valid cells. Apply a prespecified Holm adjustment across the three primary process contrasts for any confirmatory directional statement; the 0.02 lower-bound check remains a non-triviality descriptor, not a clinical-benefit threshold.

## Falsification and interpretation

Run the following before interpreting a process effect:

1. Permute D labels within hospital and refit; AUROC should approach chance and alert metrics should reflect permuted prevalence. Any reproducible process transport contrast outside the prespecified permutation envelope fails integrity.
2. Permute each patient’s P vector within hospital, preserving P marginals and site structure but destroying patient-process alignment; attenuation supports patient alignment as the source of signal.
3. Shuffle within-stay observation offsets while preserving values and counts; a timing-dependent claim must weaken, while the values-only model should not acquire post-360 information.
4. Compare valid revision-safe lab availability with a revision-ignorant diagnostic build. The latter can reveal sensitivity to leakage but cannot replace the valid build.
5. Compare raw P with training-only P residualized against V-predicted risk, V-risk strata, and V+M/V+P+M controls. Residualization is not claimed to remove acuity or confounding.
6. Verify patient/uniquepid split integrity, no post-360 or revision-after-360 predictors, no identifiers as predictors, no external outcome in preprocessing, and no random patient split used as deployment evidence. A random-split effect absent under hospital holdout is site dependence, not transport evidence.

Supportive results require a positive process transport contrast in at least one of sensitivity, PPV, or alert-fraction instability with a Holm-adjusted 95% interval excluding zero, a nonnegative internal P increment for the affected operating characteristic, no compensating external improvement in Brier/calibration, concordant direction in logistic and GRU when GRU is estimable, and all integrity checks passing. They support only the narrow conclusion that recorded early laboratory process is associated with less portable alert behavior beyond measured values in this eICU snapshot.

Adverse results are no transport penalty, stable or improved external operating characteristics for V+P, or disappearance after M/acuity controls or process permutation. That weakens or falsifies the hypothesis for this panel and cohort; it does not prove universal portability or that process is clinically irrelevant.

Inconclusive results include missing canonical coverage, non-exhaustive D/C/R states, fewer than 20 supported hospitals, threshold failure, unstable site denominators, bootstrap failure, intervals spanning zero, family disagreement, GRU deferral when making a temporal claim, revision/split leakage, or an effect that cannot survive the prespecified integrity checks. Preserve adverse and inconclusive outputs rather than selecting favorable point estimates.

Computationally checkable are source/header bindings, cohort and state construction, joins, time/revision filtering, feature construction, split isolation, fitting, predictions, threshold selection, calibration/Brier/alert metrics, bootstrap intervals, and falsification behavior. Clinical adjudication or another study is required to determine whether a missing row means no order, an unrecorded order, interface loss, delayed result, or de-escalation; whether residualization removes acuity; whether an alert changes care; whether false alerts or missed alerts have clinically acceptable costs; and whether a policy improves outcomes. The snapshot lacks reliable clinician intent, order/cancellation records, interface logs, staffing/capacity, turnaround, treatment indication, prospective alert actions, post-discharge mortality, and external validation outside this eICU snapshot.
