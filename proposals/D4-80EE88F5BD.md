# Provisional hypothesis: fat distribution separates metabolic from functional/fall risk beyond total adiposity

## Proposed relationship

Among UK Biobank participants with second-assessment body-composition MRI, at similar total adiposity, higher visceral fat will be more specifically associated with subsequent metabolic disease, whereas higher posterior-thigh muscle fat infiltration (MFI) will be more specifically associated with subsequent functional decline or falls. This is a hypothesis, not an observed result. The proposed advance is an outcome-specific, temporally ordered test of whether fat distribution contains clinically distinct information beyond total adiposity, rather than another generic health-risk prediction.

## Grounding and evidence boundary

The parent branch and its attached inspected evidence motivate the question: MFI/myosteatosis is associated with metabolic and functional outcomes; IMAT/MFI is heterogeneous across imaging and disease contexts; and UKB abdominal MRI phenotypes including visceral fat have been associated with internal prediction of incident type 2 diabetes. These sources do not establish the proposed divergence, causality, or treatment benefit. No association has been computed in this episode. The expert seed UKB-17 supplies the opening and cautions about overlap, measurement dates, missingness, and event support, not evidence that the hypothesis is true.

## Falsification and interpretation

Supportive: prespecified visceral-fat and MFI contrasts are outcome-specific, stable in held-out/sensitivity analyses, and have uncertainty excluding the null after total-adiposity adjustment. This supports distinct descriptive risk phenotypes, not mechanism or treatment benefit.

Adverse: the composition model adds no stable outcome-specific information, effects reverse, or associations disappear under baseline-severity, timing, selection, and ascertainment diagnostics. This weakens the divergence claim.

Inconclusive: sparse events, poor overlap, missing dates, wide uncertainty, or unstable calibration. This motivates a larger or differently ascertained study, not a positive interpretation.

Causal, mechanistic, and treatment claims require intervention or stronger longitudinal design, clinical adjudication, and additional covariates not guaranteed by these files.
