# Same-current HbA1c history and second cardiometabolic/renal disease: a UK Biobank landmark experiment

Status: prospective observational hypothesis; no fitted result is claimed.

## Unresolved question and advance

The unresolved clinical question is whether two adults with the same current HbA1c have meaningfully different near-term risk because one reached that value after a recent rise. If trajectory history adds clinically material prognostic information, future risk assessment could justify recording prior HbA1c when interpreting a repeat result; if it does not, current HbA1c may be sufficient for this narrow prognostic purpose. Neither result establishes that ordering repeat tests or acting on a trajectory improves health.

The expert seed proposes that metabolic deterioration adds information beyond current levels. This proposal makes that claim falsifiable with one analyte, a delayed-entry repeat landmark, and a prespecified second-disease transition. It separates two non-equivalent estimands: (i) a same-current-value history contrast, primary; and (ii) a fixed-baseline deterioration contrast, secondary. Since C=B+(C-B), conditioning on C changes the meaning of a change term. Current, baseline and change will therefore not be entered as unrestricted covariates in one model.

The strongest available evidence supports only narrower claims. The inherited frozen Europe PMC record for Xue et al. (DOI `10.1186/s12933-026-03255-4`) reports static metabolic/atherogenic indices associated with cardiometabolic multimorbidity in a large prospective UK Biobank cohort; it does not establish that repeated HbA1c history adds information beyond current HbA1c. A current Europe PMC search retrieved a 2026 UK Biobank serum-uric-acid multi-state study (source ID `[source checksum]`), which supports the relevance of transition analyses, not this HbA1c hypothesis. A separate search retrieved acute-care HbA1c/stress-glycemia trajectories in MIMIC-IV, not community repeat HbA1c history. These search records are contextual, not validation of the proposed result.

## Hypothesis and estimands

Let B be baseline HbA1c and C be the strictly later repeat HbA1c, converted to NGSP percentage points only if units are verified. Let L be the repeat date. Let T2 be the time from L to the second distinct qualifying disease family. Death is a competing event.

Primary hypothesis: among participants with the same current HbA1c, a trajectory with B=C-0.5 percentage points has a higher 5-year cumulative incidence of T2 than a trajectory with B=C, after standardization to the eligible landmark cohort.

Primary estimand:
`RD_history = E_X[P(T2<=5 | C, B=C-0.5, X) - P(T2<=5 | C, B=C, X)]`
evaluated only in the empirical joint-exposure support. It is a descriptive, noncausal prognostic contrast, not a hypothetical intervention.

Secondary estimand:
`RD_deterioration = E_X[P(T2<=5 | B, C=B+0.5, X) - P(T2<=5 | B, C=B, X)]`
This holds B fixed and asks about a higher current value. It is reported separately and never relabeled as the primary history contrast. An interquartile-range display is secondary only.

The primary outcome is the second distinct family among inpatient ICD-10 codes: diabetes-coded E11–E14, chronic kidney disease N18.x, and heart failure I50.x. “Diabetes-coded” is deliberate: no adjudicated type 2 diagnosis, outpatient evidence, phenotype, or treatment is available. Repeated codes in one family do not create a second disease; same-day distinct families count as a transition.

## Covariates and analysis

Baseline X is age (restricted cubic spline), sex, BMI, systolic BP and diastolic BP. Baseline HbA1c is part of the exposure history, not a post-L covariate. Creatinine/cystatin C are optional only after field-specific units and a prespecified equation are verified.

Fit, in the identical primary cohort:

- M0: current C + X.
- M1 primary: C plus a prespecified restricted-spline joint surface in (B,C) + X.
- M2 secondary: B and Delta=C-B using the same prespecified spline complexity + X.

Choose knots/complexity from the exposure distribution before outcome fitting; if event support is inadequate, simplify using a rule recorded before outcome fitting or classify the requested contrast infeasible. Standardize M1 to B=C-0.5 and B=C only inside empirical support, without extrapolation. Report cause-specific hazards only as model parameters. Use participant-level bootstrap 95% intervals; imputation and repeat-selection weighting, if used, occur within resampling partitions. Calibration and optimism-corrected incremental prediction are secondary and cannot establish utility.

A sensitivity may use stabilized inverse-probability-of-valid-repeat weights based only on baseline variables, with fixed truncation, positivity, effective sample size and weight diagnostics. It cannot recover deaths, disease before repeat, non-return, or unverified sample timing.

## Feasibility, falsification and interpretation

Write an audit before inference with source/catalog/snapshot/schema hashes, file and row counts, eid uniqueness/overlap agreement, missing/nonnumeric/negative/out-of-range counts, date parsing, sample-date evidence, unit/coding evidence, code-list version, death reconciliation and all selection counts. Source metadata says field-level units/codings are incomplete and negative values have no global interpretation; do not globally recode them.

Hard gates: exact bindings and joins; verified HbA1c units/negative handling; sample-date correspondence; A1>A0 and 1–6-year interval; complete aligned code/date interpretation; disease/death-free ascertainment; verified death semantics/ties; adequate follow-up; at least 100 second-family events overall and 10 in every displayed contrast stratum; joint-exposure support; stable uncertainty and weights if used.

Set clinical materiality before outcome fitting at a 2-percentage-point absolute 5-year risk difference. This is an interpretation threshold, not a treatment threshold.

Supportive: all gates pass, primary estimate is >=+2 points, 95% interval excludes 0, and M1 is calibrated. Stronger material support requires the lower interval bound >+2 points and consistent sensitivity direction. Adverse: a <=-2-point interval-excluding-zero history contrast, or a positive result that disappears under washout/verified-selection sensitivity; this challenges the prognostic interpretation but is not a protective causal claim. Evidence against a material increment: interval wholly below +2 points with calibration; if also wholly within -2 to +2, no material increment was detected in this selected population, not equivalence or universal sufficiency of current HbA1c. Inconclusive: any hard gate failure, sparse events, support failure/extrapolation, unresolved dates/death, or an interval spanning material and trivial effects.

If enough events exist, use aligned K35.x appendicitis as a negative-control outcome. A large comparable association flags utilization/ascertainment confounding; a sparse negative control is reported but cannot invalidate or rescue the primary result.

Computed outputs can establish source availability, selection, date ordering, inpatient-code-defined disease transitions, competing-risk estimates, associations, uncertainty and gate status. They cannot establish outpatient disease, adjudicated diagnoses, stage/severity, treatment effects, representativeness, causal utility, or a management recommendation. Those require validated dictionaries/dates, outpatient and medication data, clinical adjudication, external validation and a prospective testing-and-management study.
