# UKB-38: Does leukocyte telomere length show opposite-direction associations with cancer vs cardiovascular disease?

## Unresolved question
In adults with measured leukocyte telomere length (LTL), does longer LTL associate with lower cardiovascular disease (CVD) risk but not lower (or potentially higher) cancer risk, after adjustment for age, sex, and smoking? The "telomere tradeoff" hypothesis posits that longer telomeres reflect greater cellular replicative capacity, which may protect against vascular aging (lower CVD) while permitting more cell divisions and thus potentially increasing cancer susceptibility. Existing evidence is mixed: some studies report shorter LTL with higher cancer risk, others report longer LTL with higher cancer risk, and the direction for CVD is less consistent. A large-scale, well-powered test in UK Biobank can discriminate between these competing explanations.

## Clinical importance
If LTL shows a consistent opposite-direction association with CVD vs cancer, this would:
1. Support LTL as a dual-purpose biomarker for differential risk stratification.
2. Inform whether telomere-targeting interventions (e.g., telomerase activators) carry cancer risk.
3. Clarify the biological tradeoff between cellular replicative capacity and genomic stability.
4. Guide screening strategies: individuals with longer LTL might benefit from enhanced cancer surveillance even if their CVD risk is lower.

## Design
**Primary analysis**: Cox proportional hazards models for each outcome (any cancer, CVD, all-cause death) with LTL (continuous, per SD) as exposure, adjusted for age (continuous), sex (binary), and smoking (categorical: never/ex/current).

**Key comparison**: The direction and magnitude of the LTL hazard ratio for CVD vs cancer. The hypothesis predicts HR_CVD < 1 (protective) and HR_cancer >= 1 (null or harmful).

**Sensitivity analyses**:
1. Restrict to never-smokers (smoking=0) to reduce confounding.
2. Use 22192-0.0 (Z-adjusted T/S log) as alternative telomere measure.
3. Exclude first 2 years of follow-up to reduce reverse causation.
4. Stratify by sex.
5. Use cancer type-specific models (lung C34, colorectal C18-C20, prostate C61, breast C50) to test whether the direction differs by cancer type.

**Baseline comparison**: A simple logistic regression (cross-sectional) of cancer vs CVD on LTL quintiles, adjusted for age/sex/smoking, as a baseline. The Cox model adds temporal information (time-to-event) that the logistic model loses. The Cox model can reveal whether the LTL effect is stronger for early vs late events, which the cross-sectional analysis cannot.

**Falsification criteria**:
- **Supportive**: HR_CVD per SD LTL < 0.95 (p<0.05) AND HR_cancer per SD LTL > 1.0 (p<0.05), with consistent direction in never-smoker sensitivity analysis.
- **Adverse**: HR_CVD and HR_cancer in the same direction (both <1 or both >1), or neither significant.
- **Inconclusive**: One outcome significant, the other not; or direction consistent but p-values borderline (0.05<p<0.10).

**Power statement**: With n=471,991, 115,882 cancer events, and 69,932 CVD events, the study is well-powered to detect HRs as small as 0.97-1.03 per SD LTL (alpha=0.05, power=0.80).

## Limitations
- Single LTL measurement (no longitudinal telomere attrition).
- LTL is a surrogate for cellular aging, not a direct causal agent.
- Cancer ascertainment via hospital records (40005/40006) may miss early-stage or non-hospitalized cancers.
- CVD ascertainment via ICD10 inpatient codes (41270) may miss outpatient CVD events.
- Smoking is self-reported and may be misclassified.
- UKB selection bias (healthier, more educated participants).
- No CRP or WBC differential available in the frozen data for inflammation adjustment.

## Method exploration
- **Baseline**: Logistic regression (cross-sectional) of cancer vs CVD on LTL quintiles.
- **Learned alternative**: Cox PH model with LTL as continuous exposure, adjusted for age/sex/smoking. The Cox model reveals temporal dynamics (early vs late events) that the cross-sectional model loses.
- **Mechanistic alternative**: Mendelian randomization using genetic instruments for LTL. Not feasible in this dataset (no GWAS summary statistics for LTL available in the frozen parquet tables). Deferred.
- **Selection reason**: Cox PH is the standard for time-to-event analysis and is well-powered in this cohort. MR would provide causal evidence but requires external GWAS data not available here.
