# Scientific Proposal: Proteomic Signatures of Metabolic Inflammation Predict Multimorbidity Through Distinct Biological Pathways

## Unresolved Question

**Primary Question**: Among UK Biobank participants without prior chronic disease at baseline, do proteomic signatures of metabolic inflammation (distinct from traditional cardiovascular risk factors) independently predict incident multimorbidity (≥2 chronic conditions), and are these effects mediated through immune dysregulation and mitochondrial dysfunction pathways?

**Secondary Question**: Can pathway-specific proteomic signatures improve multimorbidity risk prediction beyond traditional clinical risk factors?

## Clinical Importance

### 1. Early Risk Stratification
- Current multimorbidity prevention relies on traditional risk factors that often manifest only after disease onset
- Proteomic biomarkers could enable identification of high-risk individuals years before clinical disease
- Potential for early intervention strategies targeting metabolic-inflammatory pathways

### 2. Mechanistic Insights
- Most research focuses on cardiovascular outcomes; this addresses the broader multimorbidity burden
- Distinguishing between metabolic-inflammatory vs. cardiovascular risk pathways
- Understanding mediation through specific biological mechanisms (immune, mitochondrial)

### 3. Precision Prevention
- Pathway-specific signatures could inform targeted prevention strategies
- Different interventions may be needed for different biological pathways

### 4. Beyond Cardiovascular Focus
- Multimorbidity affects millions; addressing the broader burden is critical
- Current risk scores may miss non-cardiovascular drivers of multimorbidity

## Substantive Advance Over Existing Knowledge

### Existing Knowledge
- Traditional risk factors (age, sex, BMI, smoking, socioeconomic status) predict cardiovascular disease
- Proteomics has been studied for aging clocks and single-disease prediction
- Metabolic syndrome components are known risk factors
- Cardiovascular risk scores exist but focus on single-organ outcomes

### Substantive Advancement
1. **Integration**: Testing whether proteomics add predictive value beyond traditional factors for multimorbidity
2. **Pathway Specificity**: Testing whether metabolic-inflammatory pathways (vs. cardiovascular pathways) drive multimorbidity
3. **Mediation Analysis**: Testing whether proteomic effects are mediated through specific biological mechanisms
4. **Multimorbidity Focus**: Addressing multiple concurrent conditions rather than single diseases
5. **Beyond Cardiovascular**: Expanding beyond the cardiovascular-centric focus of current research

## Hypothesis

### Primary Hypothesis
Among UK Biobank participants without prior chronic disease at baseline:
1. Proteomic signatures of metabolic inflammation independently predict incident multimorbidity (≥2 chronic conditions) after adjustment for traditional risk factors
2. The set of proteins significantly associated with metabolic-inflammatory pathways shows less than 50% overlap with proteins associated with traditional cardiovascular risk factors
3. Proteomic effects on multimorbidity are partially mediated through immune dysregulation and mitochondrial dysfunction pathways

### Formal Statistical Statement
Let:
- P_metabolic be the set of proteins with p < 0.05 in multivariate Cox regression predicting incident multimorbidity, adjusted for traditional risk factors
- P_cardio be the set of proteins with p < 0.05 in multivariate Cox regression predicting incident CVD, adjusted for traditional risk factors

**Test 1**: |P_metabolic ∩ P_cardio| / max(|P_metabolic|, |P_cardio|) < 0.5

**Test 2**: The hazard ratio for multimorbidity associated with metabolic-inflammatory protein signatures remains significant after full adjustment for traditional risk factors

**Test 3**: Mediation analysis shows significant indirect effect through immune/mitochondrial pathway markers

## Statistical Approach

### Primary Analysis

1. **Multivariate Cox Proportional Hazards Regression**
   - Outcome: Time to first incident multimorbidity event (≥2 conditions)
   - Independent variables: Proteomic features (all or pathway-specific subsets)
   - Adjustment: Age, sex, socioeconomic status
   - Effect measure: Hazard ratios with 95% confidence intervals

2. **Pathway-Specific Analysis**
   - Group proteins by biological pathways (immune, metabolic, mitochondrial)
   - Create pathway-level signatures (e.g., mean expression of immune pathway proteins)
   - Test pathway-specific effects on multimorbidity

3. **Overlap Analysis**
   - Compare proteins significantly associated with:
     a) Multimorbidity (proteomic approach)
     b) Traditional cardiovascular risk factors
   - Calculate overlap proportion

4. **Mediation Analysis**
   - Test whether proteomic effects on multimorbidity are mediated through:
     a) Immune dysregulation markers
     b) Mitochondrial dysfunction markers
   - Use causal mediation analysis methods

## Falsification Criteria

### Adverse Results (Reject Hypothesis)
1. Proteomic signatures do NOT predict multimorbidity independently of traditional risk factors (all HRs non-significant after full adjustment)
2. No significant overlap between metabolic-inflammatory proteins and cardiovascular risk proteins
3. No mediation through immune/mitochondrial pathways
4. Traditional risk factors outperform proteomics in prediction (C-index difference < 0.01)

### Supportive Results (Retain Hypothesis)
1. Proteomic signatures predict multimorbidity independently (significant HRs after adjustment)
2. Significant pathway-specific effects (immune, mitochondrial)
3. Moderate overlap (<50%) between metabolic-inflammatory and cardiovascular proteins
4. Mediation analysis shows significant indirect effects
5. Combined model improves prediction over traditional factors alone (C-index increase ≥ 0.02)

### Inconclusive Results
1. Mixed results across pathways
2. Significant findings only in subsets of participants
3. Technical limitations (missing data, measurement error)
4. Requires additional data or refined analysis

## Method Alternatives

### Baseline Method
- Multivariate logistic/Cox regression with traditional risk factors
- Protein feature selection using LASSO or elastic net
- **Limitations**: Assumes linearity, may miss non-linear relationships, doesn't capture feature interactions

### Substantive Alternative
- Machine learning ensemble (Random Forest, XGBoost, Gradient Boosting) with protein signatures
- Pathway-based aggregation
- Mediation analysis using causal inference methods
- **Information Revealed**: Non-linear relationships, feature interactions, robustness to model assumptions

### Alternative Hypothesis
- Test whether proteomics capture distinct biological pathways not captured by traditional risk factors for single diseases (vs. multimorbidity)
- **Rationale**: Multimorbidity may have different etiology than single diseases
