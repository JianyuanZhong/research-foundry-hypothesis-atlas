HYPOTHESIS AND ESTIMANDS
Primary threshold q=60 mL/min/1.73 m2, fixed before analysis. Direction A exposure is baseline eGFRcys<60, computed only from baseline cystatin C, baseline age and sex; outcome is repeat eGFRcr<60, computed only from repeat creatinine, repeat age and sex, adjusted for continuous baseline eGFRcr, age, sex and elapsed years. Direction B reverses the markers: baseline eGFRcr<60 predicts repeat eGFRcys<60, adjusted for continuous baseline eGFRcys and the same prespecified covariates. The primary estimand for each direction is the model-standardized risk difference comparing exposure groups over the observed baseline covariate distribution among eligible first-repeat attenders. The two direction-specific risk differences are co-primary; no claim of coupling-free measurement is made. Shared latent renal state, correlated error, regression to the mean, treatment, and attendance selection remain possible explanations.

Both directions use a single-marker exposure and the other single-marker outcome. The combined creatinine-cystatin-C equation is not a primary endpoint. No q=45, q=30, LDL threshold, or other threshold is added after inspecting results. A threshold crossing is not a CKD diagnosis: chronicity, albuminuria/kidney-damage evidence, measured GFR, medications and clinical outcomes are unavailable.

FALSIFICATION AND RESULT BRANCHES
A supportive result requires an estimable direction, Holm-adjusted co-primary evidence in the prespecified positive direction, simultaneous intervals excluding zero, acceptable overlap/calibration, and stability across unweighted and measured-attendance analyses. It supports incremental prediction of the other protocol biomarker's repeat threshold status among comparable repeat attenders only. An asymmetric result is reported direction-specific; it cannot support bidirectional renal specificity. Adverse results are null/reversed direction, loss after target-level adjustment, weight/floor dependence, poor calibration, or failed threshold support; these falsify that incremental reclassification claim but do not prove absence of renal biology. Inconclusive results are sparse cells, separation, non-overlap, unstable attendance weighting, failed equation/unit/sex certification, or unresolved assay traceability. Additional waves, urine albumin, measured GFR, medication/comorbidity data, laboratory adjudication, external validation and prospective impact studies are required for stronger clinical claims.
