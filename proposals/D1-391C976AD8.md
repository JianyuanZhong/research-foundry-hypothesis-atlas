# Prospective observation-boundary and negative-control audit for frozen P-beyond-B repeat-TACE HCC validation

## Parent, goal, and substantive change

This is a targeted successor of `[prior hypothesis]`, preserving its scientific schema, selected population, event reconstruction, clocks, joins, denominators, annual forward blocks, and noncausal estimand. The unresolved limitation is that the positive 2022 result is conditional on selected repeat-TACE episodes with an observed complete triplet, while 2023/2024 transport is inconclusive because outcome observation and systemic-arm support collapse. This version makes two executable boundaries stricter:

1. Every prospective operation is explicitly origin-embargoed: all preprocessing, feature transformations, imputation, model/alpha selection, observation-selection diagnostics, and negative-control calibration are fit using only pre-origin rows; the future block is then scored once and never used to revise a fit or threshold.
2. Before interpreting any observed P increment, run a prespecified, outcome-blind conditional permutation negative control. It breaks the event-level linkage between P and Y while preserving the observed P distribution, arm, calendar block, and approximate B severity stratum. Re-running the locked nested pipeline on these pseudo-P values gives an executable null distribution for linkage, leakage, and calendar-composition artifacts. It is a falsification of a data-linkage/temporal-artifact explanation, not evidence for a causal effect and not a replacement estimand.

The primary question remains: in the frozen selected population, does contemporaneous pre-repeat assay P improve strictly forward prediction of same-encounter post-event assay Y beyond remote baseline B and the locked decision-time core? The strongest predecessor-supported claim is limited to incremental albumin and bilirubin prediction in the adequately supported 2022 selected calendar mixture. The new analysis tests whether that gain survives an explicit prospective boundary and is distinguishable from an outcome-blind broken-linkage null. It does not recover missing outcomes or establish transport, treatment benefit, mechanism, utility, safety, or actionability.

## Frozen event clocks and endpoint

For each selected repeat event and each assay, preserve exactly:

- `B`: latest valid uncensored assay on TACE2 calendar days −30 through −1.
- `P`: latest valid assay in the selected repeat encounter in `[event_time−72 hours,event_time)`.
- `Y`: selected valid assay in that encounter in `(event_time,event_time+72 hours]`, nearest +24 hours, with deterministic tie handling.

Require `B_time < P_time < event_time < Y_time`, positive P lead, one patient-assay row, and no lab-informed event selection. Do not widen windows, substitute assays, use notes/imaging, or infer missing Y. A complete triplet is exactly the frozen analysis row; a BP-eligible row can lack Y and remains in the risk-set funnel only.

## Prospective experiment and unchanged estimand

Use origins `2021-01-01`, `2022-01-01`, `2023-01-01`, `2024-01-01`; for origin `o`, training events satisfy `event_date < o`, and the test block is `[o,o+365 days)`, with the end exclusive. Blocks are non-overlapping. No event at or after `o` can influence training preprocessing, imputation, variance filtering, scaling, alpha choice, selection model, permutation strata, null quantiles, or interpretation thresholds.

On complete triplets only, retain the locked models:

```
M0: Y ~ B + systemic-record indicator + age + sex indicators
       + TACE2-to-repeat days + P-lead hours
M1: M0 + P
```

Age median imputation (plus age-missingness indicator), sex coding, variance filtering, centering/scaling, inner leave-one-patient-out alpha choice over `[0.01, 0.1, 1, 10, 100]`, and ridge fitting are training-only and unchanged. The primary estimand is assay-specific standardized held-out RMSE gain `[RMSE(M0)−RMSE(M1)]/SD_test(Y)`, with paired arm-stratified patient-frequency bootstrap intervals conditional on each fixed pre-origin fit. MAE, bias, arm-specific errors, drift, and influence remain secondary. No diagnostic changes fitted rows, endpoint, model, or estimand.

A test block is interpretable only after reporting the inherited complete-triplet gate (at least 20 complete rows total and at least 10 in each descriptive arm in both train and test) and the new outcome-blind BP gate (at least 20 BP-eligible rows total and at least 10 per arm in both train and test). Failed gates yield descriptive/inconclusive results, regardless of point estimates.

## Explicit observation-process boundary

Before reading/selecting Y, create for every selected event and assay:

`selected event -> has_B -> has_P -> BP_eligible -> Y_observed -> complete`.

`has_B` and `has_P` use only their frozen clocks and validity rules. `BP_eligible` requires strict clock ordering, positive P lead, and no future outcome information. `Y_observed` is recorded separately using only the frozen post-event window; `complete = BP_eligible & Y_observed`. Report counts and Wilson 95% intervals overall and by arm for each origin's train and test. Fit the pre-origin Y-observation logistic diagnostic only on pre-origin BP-eligible rows using B, P, age, sex, P-lead hours, TACE2-to-repeat days, and arm; apply it to future BP-eligible rows. Report predicted probability range, actual observation fraction, Brier score, observed-case inverse-probability ESS, and future observed-versus-missing standardized mean differences. This is diagnostic only: do not impute Y, reweight RMSE, or assume missing at random.

## Calendar support and prospective transport diagnostics

Among BP-eligible rows, fit the calendar-membership model using only pre-Y variables B, P, age, sex, P-lead hours, TACE2-to-repeat days, and arm. For each origin, all standardization/imputation and model fitting use training rows only; report future propensity range relative to training, fraction outside the training range, normalized training transport-weight ESS, maximum normalized weight, and arm-specific ESS. Report same-arm nearest-neighbor distances in standardized pre-Y space, with the training leave-one-out 95th percentile as the prespecified extrapolation threshold. These are context, not analysis weights.

Flag, without deleting rows, calendar support if any future propensity is outside the training range, any arm ESS is below 10, or >5% of same-arm future rows exceed the training NN threshold. Flag observation selection if future Y observation is <80%, its Wilson interval is clearly below the pre-origin rate, observation ESS is <10, or any observed-versus-missing pre-Y SMD has absolute value ≥0.5. These warnings never convert a sparse block into evidence.

## New executable negative-control / negative-feasibility diagnostic

For each assay and origin, after the BP-eligible and complete-triplet rows are frozen and before fitting the real M0/M1, run 1,000 deterministic-seed permutations (seed `67000 + permutation_index`). This diagnostic uses no Y values to construct or tune pseudo-P; Y is consulted only at final metric calculation, exactly as in the real analysis.

1. Define strata separately within each calendar block (training pre-origin and future test), arm, and B quintile. Compute B quintiles from training BP-eligible B values only; apply those cutpoints to test, with an explicit pooled fallback when a stratum has fewer than 4 training rows. Record the fallback counts.
2. Within each stratum, randomly permute the observed P values and P timestamps jointly across BP-eligible episodes, preserving P availability, P marginal distribution, P-lead/timing pairing, arm, calendar block, and approximate B severity. Do not move P across an origin, patient, or assay. Preserve each row's B, age, sex, event timing, Y, and complete/BP eligibility status. Re-enforce the inherited strict clock condition for pseudo-P; if a jointly moved timestamp violates `P_time < event_time`, redraw within the same stratum, and if no valid donor exists mark that permutation/stratum infeasible rather than silently alter clocks.
3. For every feasible permutation, run the complete locked prospective pipeline from preprocessing through inner patient-level alpha selection and score the unchanged held-out Y rows. Report standardized RMSE gain, by assay and origin, and the fraction of infeasible permutations. The real observed gain is computed once by the same code path.
4. Predeclare the negative-control interpretation: (a) a feasible null distribution centered near zero and with the observed gain outside its two-sided 95% interval is consistent with an event-level P-Y linkage not explained by this broken-linkage/calendar/B-severity null; (b) an observed gain inside the null interval, or high infeasibility, fails to distinguish signal from linkage/composition artifact and is negative/inconclusive; (c) a null gain systematically positive indicates residual model or metric bias and blocks favorable interpretation. This diagnostic does not prove biological relevance, causality, transport, or clinical benefit, and a null result does not prove P is useless.

The negative control is an executable feasibility check as well: report, by origin/assay, the number of complete rows available to the pipeline, number of valid permutations, clock-redraw attempts, and whether the requested 1,000 permutations are feasible. If fewer than 950 valid permutations are obtained, the negative-control result is inconclusive rather than rescued by changing strata, clocks, or denominators. Do not use test Y to choose strata, seeds, thresholds, or a favorable subset.

## Required outputs and interpretation

Produce machine-readable output containing snapshot, exact source paths and hashes, row-scan counts, duplicate handling, frozen denominators/clocks, risk-set funnel, prospective train/test boundaries, all primary/secondary metrics and bootstrap intervals, support/selection diagnostics, negative-control permutation summaries and seeds, and a per-block interpretation status. Emit no identifiers or raw notes.

Supportive evidence requires successful reconstruction, both support gates, no material observation/calendar warning, a locked observed M1 gain whose interval is above zero, and observed gain outside the valid negative-control null interval. It supports only incremental short-horizon prediction in that selected, observed complete-triplet calendar mixture. It does not establish multi-era transport or actionability.

Adverse evidence includes an interval at or below zero in an adequately supported block, repeated M1 degradation, observed gain indistinguishable from the negative-control null, null distributions with systematic positive bias, or material support/observation collapse. This challenges incremental prediction or its interpretation, not a causal mechanism and not the sufficiency of B.

Inconclusive evidence includes failed gates, <950 valid permutations, high permutation clock infeasibility, sparse arms, low Y observation, influential rows, calendar extrapolation, denominator/clock drift, or missing assay units. The already executed predecessor audit found 2022 supported descriptively, while 2023 had 29/43 Y observed (systemic 5/9) and 2024 had 14/70 (systemic 1/9); those later blocks remain transport-inconclusive unless the new audit provides adequate support, observation, and a valid negative-control separation.

Computation can verify hashes, full scans, literal filters, joins, duplicate handling, clocks, boundaries, prospective fit isolation, funnel counts, support diagnostics, permutation mechanics, and numerical metrics. It cannot validate laboratory units/reference ranges, clinical severity, tumor burden, imaging response, repeat-TACE rationale, medication administration, transfusion/fluid exposure, external-care capture, mortality, clinical adjudication, causal effects, clinical utility, or actionability. Stronger claims require expert review and independent temporal/external/prospective data with validated assay metadata and clinically meaningful outcomes.
