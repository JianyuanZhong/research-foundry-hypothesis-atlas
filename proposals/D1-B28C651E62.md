# Proposal: A boundary-validated, noncausal prognostic experiment for first NE/VP stop

## Substantive child and clinical advance

This child combines the clinically direct stopping question from `[prior hypothesis]` with the executable observed-stop cohort and endpoint pipeline from `[prior hypothesis]`, while removing the unsupported causal and underpowered claims in both directions. The completed repeated-landmark audit found only 3.49% NE-first and 2.76% VP-first actions among 6,040 MAP-eligible landmarks, so the assigned-strategy emulation fails its prespecified 5% positivity gate. The 84-versus-53 observed-stop arms also fail the direct-association parent’s own minimum arm/event requirements, particularly the 24 failures in the VP-first arm. Neither failure is repaired by lowering a gate, treating no-action landmarks as controls, or indexing eligibility backward from a future stop.

The frozen Harbor experiment is therefore strictly prognostic. Among adults at the first documented non-simultaneous stop of one of two concurrently active low-dose pressors, it asks: **(1) can physiology measured before the stop predict exactly six-hour observed hemodynamic failure, and (2) does the observed first-stop order add transportable prognostic information beyond those pre-stop features?** The clinical advance is a strategy-neutral, prospectively testable enrollment-enrichment phenotype for a future randomized discontinuation-order study, together with an explicit test of whether the charted action is merely a marker of latent clinician information. It cannot recommend which drug to stop, estimate a counterfactual risk, establish a treatment effect, or justify bedside treatment selection.

Public evidence is compatible with equipoise but not a universal order. The inspected individual-patient meta-analysis (PMID 30893494; DOI 10.1002/phar.2265) reported less clinically significant hypotension with NE-first but no clear short-term mortality or length-of-stay difference and substantial heterogeneity. A later weighted cohort was null, and the inspected systematic-review result reported discordant observational and randomized directions. This discordance motivates prospective randomization and is not evidence for an order recommendation. The research-ambition demonstration papers were methodological references only; no unavailable main article or supplementary method is claimed.

## Population, time zero, and no-back-indexing rules

Use one episode per `subject_id`: the first qualifying stop in the subject’s first ICU stay, with the first ICU stay defined by earliest `icu/icustays.intime` and `stay_id` as tie-breaker. Adult age is calculated using the MIMIC convention `patients.anchor_age + year(admissions.admittime) - patients.anchor_year`; values above 89 remain de-identified age values and are not reinterpreted as literal ages.

Reconstruct positive infusion intervals symmetrically for NE and VP from `icu/inputevents`. Normalize only supported rate units, retain dose-audit counts, and reject nonpositive/invalid intervals and implausible normalized doses. Union overlapping rows. Bridge a positive gap of at most five minutes only when `orderid` or `linkorderid` continues and the preceding row is not explicitly stopped, paused, or cancelled. A candidate stop is the end of an active reconstructed interval after at least six hours of NE/VP overlap, with no positive row for that same drug beginning within the next ten minutes. The comparator must be active at the exact stop. Exclude if the two stop times differ by at most five minutes. These rules are mirror-symmetric for NE and VP.

Eligibility uses only data available at or before time zero (the stopped interval end). Require the patient to be alive and in the ICU at time zero; both doses positive, with NE <=0.20 mcg/kg/min and VP <=0.04 U/min; neither dose increasing during the preceding 30 minutes; no epinephrine, phenylephrine, or dopamine active in the preceding two hours; and the pre-stop MAP rule below. Do not require future absence of either study drug, no restart, future comparator persistence, future MAP coverage, survival, ICU residence, or a favorable endpoint. Restart and subsequent instability are outcomes, not eligibility conditions.

The primary exposure is the observed order (`NE_first` or `VP_first`). Simultaneous stops are excluded from the two-arm model but retained in the flow table. The action label is not an intervention assignment and is never described as treatment received under a causal estimand.

## Exact six-hour outcome and ascertainment

The primary outcome is the binary **observed six-hour hemodynamic failure**, beginning at time zero and ending at six hours, ICU exit, or death. It is positive at the earliest of:

1. death at or before six hours;
2. a positive supported interval restarting or re-initiating the stopped study agent after time zero;
3. initiation of epinephrine, phenylephrine, or dopamine not active in the preceding two hours;
4. confirmed NE escalation, defined as a subsequent valid NE dose at least 0.05 mcg/kg/min above the last pre-stop NE dose, sustained in a positive interval or confirmed by a second valid dose record within 30 minutes;
5. two valid MAP values below 60 mmHg in separate 30-minute bins, 5–120 minutes apart, without carrying a value across a gap greater than 15 minutes; or
6. ICU exit before six hours with death, an active study/rescue pressor, a `continueinnextdept` continuation signal, or a prior failure component.

MAP uses `icu/chartevents` item 220052 (arterial) and 220181 (non-invasive), accepting physiologically valid values 20–200 mmHg and preferring arterial at duplicate `charttime`. Pre-stop eligibility requires at least three MAP values spanning at least three of the four 30-minute bins in the preceding two hours, including one in the final 30 minutes, median MAP >=65, and minimum MAP >=55. For a failure-free survivor, classify endpoint ascertainment as indeterminate unless post-stop coverage includes at least four represented 30-minute bins and at least one MAP in hours 4–6. A monitoring-independent failure remains observable even without complete MAP coverage. Report failure, failure-free, and indeterminate counts; all component counts; the monitoring-independent composite; and best-case/worst-case bounds treating indeterminate failure-free survivors as failures versus successes.

The inherited no-sampling feasibility artifact reports 137 unique episodes, 136 determinate endpoints, 73 observed failures, 59 restarts, 2 competing-pressor events, 19 NE escalations, 20 MAP failures, no deaths, and no ICU-exit failures. These are computability counts, not adjudicated clinical results. The stricter confirmed/sustained NE-escalation rule above must be applied in the final implementation rather than the permissive feasibility shortcut.

MCS is not assumed absent. Before any MCS-restricted interpretation, freeze a dictionary from `icu/d_items` for IABP, Impella, ECMO, VAD/RVAD/LVAD and related support, then scan timestamped `chartevents`, `datetimeevents`, and `procedureevents` records through time zero. Classify each episode MCS-positive, MCS-negative, or MCS-unknown. The inherited selected-proxy exclusion removed one of 138 pre-MAP candidates and found 20 proxy-positive candidates before exclusion (17 procedure, 18 chart, 20 either), but these are not adjudicated active-device intervals. Positive and unknown timelines require blinded critical-care/pharmacy review; missing records cannot be coded negative. If MCS remains unresolved, results are descriptive and the enrichment rule is not advanced.

## Primary prognostic experiment

Fit three nested models on determinate episodes only, with the one indeterminate episode carried into ascertainment sensitivity analyses:

- **M0:** intercept-only reference.
- **M1:** compact pre-stop physiology model.
- **M2:** M1 plus one binary observed-order term (`NE_first` versus `VP_first`).

The prespecified M1 feature set is deliberately limited to six effective continuous predictors, all defined before time zero:

1. age;
2. log-transformed NE dose at one second before the stop;
3. log-transformed VP dose at one second before the stop;
4. log-transformed hours of reconstructed NE/VP overlap;
5. median pre-stop MAP; and
6. pre-stop MAP slope, calculated by ordinary least squares on valid MAP observations against minutes before time zero.

MAP minimum and MAP observation count are mandatory eligibility/audit variables and secondary model extensions, not additional primary degrees of freedom. This protects against fitting a high-dimensional model to 73 failures. Dose and overlap transformations, centering/scaling, and any imputation rule are frozen before model fitting. No diagnosis, discharge code, post-stop variable, boundary-quality flag, endpoint component, future coverage, or future survival enters M1 or M2. Care unit and calendar group are validation strata, not primary predictors; adding them is an explicitly secondary sensitivity and cannot replace transport validation.

Use penalized logistic regression, with the penalty selected within each training set only. Do not use data-driven feature selection, interactions, tree ensembles, or flexible splines in the primary analysis. The effective degrees of freedom must remain <=8, including the action term in M2; if the selected procedure cannot satisfy this constraint, report the model as infeasible rather than relaxing the constraint. Missing primary features are not silently imputed; the primary analysis uses the prespecified complete-feature set, and a secondary missingness-indicator/imputation analysis is labeled exploratory. The model estimates observed endpoint probability, not risk under a hypothetical order.

The primary predictive estimand is out-of-sample Brier score and calibration for M1 versus M0. The primary incremental question is the paired out-of-sample Brier difference M2 minus M1, with calibration intercept, calibration slope, observed-to-expected ratio, AUROC and AUPRC as supporting summaries. Use subject-grouped bootstrap optimism correction and percentile or BCa 95% intervals where valid. All preprocessing, penalty selection, and any enrichment threshold selection occur inside training folds.

## Frozen transport validation and enrichment rule

The audit found the following complete-cohort calendar counts: 2008–2010, n=31/failures=15; 2011–2013, n=21/9; 2014–2016, n=27/17; 2017–2019, n=36/20; and 2020–2022, n=22/12. The latest group fails the predeclared n>=30 and >=10-failure gate because n=22. Therefore the formal chronological holdout is the latest *qualifying* later group, 2017–2019: train on 2008–2016 and test once on 2017–2019. The 2020–2022 group is a prespecified descriptive stress-test holdout, not a claim of adequately powered temporal transport. Also perform leave-one-anchor-year-group-out validation, reporting uncertainty and labeling sparse folds exploratory.

Perform leave-one-first-care-unit-out validation. Formal unit transport claims are limited to holdouts with at least 20 episodes and 10 failures: MICU (n=42, 21 failures) and MICU/SICU (n=28, 20 failures). CVICU, CCU, SICU, TSICU, and smaller units are reported with intervals as descriptive stress tests; units are never pooled or redefined after seeing model direction. For every holdout report n, determinate outcomes, failures, arm composition, Brier score, calibration, and missingness/monitoring density.

Within each training fold, select a candidate strategy-neutral enrichment threshold targeting predicted failure probability <35%, then freeze it for the held-out fold. Report the number retained, observed failure risk and exact interval, sensitivity, specificity, and retained proportion. Call the rule provisionally transportable only if pooled validation has at least 40 low-risk episodes, the upper 95% confidence bound for low-risk failure is <50%, calibration slope is 0.7–1.3, calibration-in-the-large is within +/-0.10 on the probability scale, and no adequately sized unit or calendar holdout exceeds 50% observed failure. These are planning gates, not proof of safety. The threshold cannot use observed order for treatment selection.

M2 is considered incrementally informative only if its paired validation Brier improvement over M1 is at least 0.01, calibration does not worsen, and the direction is stable across the formal temporal and adequately sized unit holdouts. Even then, the interpretation is that documented order contains residual information about clinician assessment or unmeasured state. If M1 transports but M2 does not, advance only an order-neutral enrichment phenotype. If M1 and M2 both fail, retain only endpoint/boundary feasibility and recommend expert-defined prospective enrollment without an EHR score.

## Falsification, adjudication, and interpretation

Before viewing arm-specific endpoint results, run: pseudo-stops six hours before the actual stop while both agents remain active; prediction of a pre-stop negative-control outcome using the observed action label; monitoring-density and arterial-line comparisons; 0-, 5-, 10-, and 15-minute bridge windows; 5-, 10-, and 15-minute no-new-row windows; 2-, 5-, and 10-minute simultaneous windows; MAP thresholds 55/60/65; arterial-only MAP; rescue-only versus full endpoint; and leave-one-unit/year-group analyses. Strong action-label prediction of pre-stop instability, arm-linked observation density, boundary sensitivity reversal, or calibration reversal falsifies the intended transport interpretation rather than confirming it. Endpoint components must be reported separately so a composite driven by documentation intensity is not treated as physiologic support.

A fixed blinded review should sample at least 25 boundaries per observed arm, oversampling non-explicit boundaries and early restarts. Reviewers classify likely true stop, rate/bag transition, transfer continuation, indeterminate, and likely endpoint failure. Require boundary PPV >=0.80 in each arm, arm PPV difference <0.10, and endpoint PPV >=0.80 before using results to plan a prospective study. Automated computation cannot establish true bedside cessation, clinician intent, exchangeability, perfusion, ethical equipoise, or clinical importance.

Supportive results are a calibrated, reproducible M1 enrichment rule that passes the frozen validation gates and remains stable under boundary, monitoring, MCS, and ascertainment analyses. A stable M2 increment supports only the latent-information interpretation. Adverse results include failure of the low-risk rule, excess failure in an ostensibly low-risk holdout, an action increment that reverses across strata, or more death/rescue/restart/indeterminate outcomes in an apparently favorable order group. Inconclusive results include sparse validation, wide bounds crossing clinically relevant benefit and harm, MCS uncertainty, differential endpoint ascertainment, or failed boundary adjudication. None supports a bedside order recommendation.
