# Episode 82 successor: cross-practice transport of numeric repeat-lactate queue value

## Unresolved question and clinical importance

At a fixed 24-hour reassessment after an early recorded norepinephrine entry, a repeat lactate can be present because one ICU measures it more often than another. The selected parent tests a stricter pooled-within-hospital question: whether adding the repeat value to an observation-process-adjusted model improves a fixed-capacity seven-day mortality-review queue in each of two outcome-blind hospital testing-practice strata. That is valuable, but its hospital-held-out fits train on both strata. A positive result could therefore still depend on learning a mixed distribution and need not show that a rule trained in one testing environment transfers to a different one.

The clinically consequential unresolved claim is narrower and more actionable: can a numeric-repeat-lactate ranking rule be trained in one measurable testing-practice environment and used in another without losing a material queue increment? If not, local recalibration or validation would be required before using such a rule across ICUs.

Primary falsifiable hypothesis H-transfer: for each directed transfer (low-availability hospitals trained, high-availability hospitals tested; and high trained, low tested), and each capacity c in C={0.05,0.075,0.10,0.125,0.15,0.175,0.20}, the held-out target-hospital fixed-slot contrast Delta7(a->b,c) = [events in Q_full,target(c) - events in Q_process,target(c)] / K_target(c) is at least 0.03 deaths per occupied slot, equivalently 3 additional D7 deaths per 100 occupied slots. Let tau7=min over directed transfers and capacities of Delta7. Support requires the two-sided 95% lower bound for tau7 to be at least 0.03, with an eventual-death no-tradeoff guard and target-stratum calibration checks. The 0.03 anchor is an operational materiality threshold, not validated utility or preventability.

This is a substantive change to the parent: the primary model-training fold is now the opposite practice stratum, not a mixture of both. The parent's ordinary leave-one-hospital-out, within-stratum result is retained as a secondary diagnostic for distinguishing transfer failure from general signal failure.

## Evidence already supports, and what it does not

The inspected full-text Baysan et al. article (2022, DOI 10.1097/CCE.0000000000000750, PMCID PMC9444407; frozen XML [source checksum]) reports a modest increase in recalibrated APACHE-IV C-statistic from 0.62 to 0.64 after adding 24-hour lactate in a measured sepsis cohort. That supports incremental association in that selected setting, not value after modeling the observation process and not cross-practice transport.

The inspected full-text Sisk et al. review (2020, DOI 10.1093/jamia/ocaa242, PMCID PMC7810439; frozen XML [source checksum]) supports treating presence/observation patterns as potentially informative and motivates missingness/observation summaries. It does not validate EICU summaries as clinician attention, establish the practice proxy's cause, or establish transportability.

The strongest EICU-specific evidence before this experiment is feasibility, not H-transfer: a complete-source audit reproduced 2,302 people in 30 hospitals, 1,163 eligible repeat lactates, 607 D7 deaths, and 779 eventual deaths. The outcome-blind median split yields low availability 15 hospitals/1,011 people/229 D7 events/782 non-events and high availability 15 hospitals/1,291 people/378 D7 events/913 non-events. Both directed transfers therefore satisfy the inherited minimum source/target support gates. These counts do not test the hypothesis.

## Estimands, uncertainty, and falsification

For target stratum b and target hospital h, k_h(c)=ceil(c n_h). Use equal-cardinality top-k queues from R_full and R_process. For each directed transfer a->b, Delta7(a->b,c)=[sum_h(events in Q_full,h(c)-events in Q_process,h(c))]/K_b(c), where K_b(c)=sum_h k_h(c); define tau7=min over a->b,c of Delta7(a->b,c). Use the same scores to calculate tauInf with Dinf and report capture, false positives, unflagged events, overlap, additions/removals, ties and denominators.

The primary uncertainty procedure is 2,000 copied-hospital refit replicates, sampling hospitals independently within each source and target stratum and preserving directed labels. Each copied original is one exclusion group: a target copy is never scored by a model trained on itself or its source-stratum copy. Repeat source-only preprocessing, both directed fits, target scores, all capacities, minima and decompositions. Save joint tuples for every directed capacity contrast, tau7, tauInf, and target calibration metrics. Require >=1,900 finite replicates per required functional and two-sided percentile 95% intervals. M=0 sets fixed-slot Delta to zero and leaves substitution yield undefined.

Before interpreting queue gains, require each target stratum's held-out R_process and R_full predictions to pass a source-independent calibration audit: finite predictions, observed target outcome support, calibration slope/intercept estimable, and absolute calibration-in-the-large difference <=0.05 for both models in each target stratum. A failed calibration gate is inconclusive, not a reason to recalibrate using target outcomes. Report Brier score and calibration slope descriptively; no predictive metric replaces the queue estimand.

Run fixed-design leave-one-original-hospital-out deletion within each directed transfer. Support requires lower bound(tau7)>=0.03, lower bound(tauInf)>=0, both directed target-stratum calibration gates, positive tau7 in >=70% of represented deletions in each direction, and no directed capacity with an upper 95% bound for Delta7<0. The inherited parent pooled/equal-hospital checks are secondary diagnostics.

Ordered states:
1. Not computable: source/hash/header/schema mismatch; flow or counts differ; changed clocks/features/capacities; outcome or target support failure; leakage; nonconvergence/nonfinite fit; failed calibration gate; fewer than 1,900 finite bootstrap replicates; missing artifacts; or queue/algebra failure.
2. Adverse opposite direction: any directed capacity's upper 95% bound is <0.
3. Adverse transfer failure: a directed transfer has upper 95% bound for tau7 <0.03 while the reverse direction clears 0.03; this falsifies symmetric portability and suggests local validation is needed, without identifying why.
4. Adverse eventual-death tradeoff: tau7 clears 0.03 but upper bound(tauInf)<0.
5. Adverse below materiality: upper bound(tau7)<0.03 in both directions.
6. Supportive cross-practice transfer: all gates pass and both directed transfer minima clear 0.03, tauInf is nonnegative, deletion criteria pass, and no opposite-direction guard triggers.
7. Inconclusive: all other computable patterns, including intervals crossing zero/materiality, calibration failure, deletion instability without a contradictory bound, or favorable within-stratum results with failed transfer.

Support means only that the numeric repeat value improves a modeled equal-capacity D7 ranking when the model is trained in the opposite EICU measurement-practice stratum. It does not show that a test should be ordered, a result was visible/released/reviewed, review changes care, deaths are preventable, or the practice proxy represents staffing or biology. Adverse means the specified transfer/materiality claim fails, not that lactate has no prognostic value. Inconclusive is not equivalence.
