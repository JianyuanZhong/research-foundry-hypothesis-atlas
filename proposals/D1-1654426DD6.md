# Day-42 HCC recorded-assay transport: auditable support, workflow separation, and an adjudication gate

Status: proposed substantive child of [prior hypothesis]. No cohort has been fitted in this branch and no clinical result is claimed.

## Scientific question and the new boundary

The unchanged clinical question is whether recorded assay state at day 42 adds stable, reusable information for a later operational repeat-TACE event among patients who are observable at the landmark. The landmark, outcome, temporal windows, and exact source bindings below are inherited unchanged.

The parent correctly identified two unresolved interpretations:

1. An apparent assay increment may be a workflow signal: patients with more visits, orders, measurements, or planned retreatment have both more assay values and more recorded repeat TACE.
2. A large set of transport strata can produce selective reporting, unsupported cells, or an apparent heterogeneity claim that is not auditable.

This child makes the smallest useful repair. It does not convert the endpoint into imaging response, infer treatment intent, or add a new subgroup. It defines a support gate before outcome interpretation, a single multiplicity-controlled reporting family, and a two-part state/workflow decision summary.

The strongest evidence-supported claim is only that recorded longitudinal information may predict a locally recorded repeat-TACE event in this HCC snapshot. The unresolved claim is narrower and falsifiable:

> On the held-out calendar test set, does adding day-42 recorded assay state to the matched observation-and-care-process model improve calibrated prediction of the operational repeat-TACE endpoint in the supported target population, with no material transport loss across prespecified opportunity, order, encounter, care-process, calendar, and horizon axes?

A positive result supports a transportable recorded-state signal for an operational event. It does not establish tumour response, treatment benefit, treatment necessity, assay causality, or mechanism. A workflow-dominated result is still clinically informative: it says the next data acquisition should prioritize scheduling/intent and outside-care linkage rather than treating the assay value as a portable state marker.

## Three inspected works and the substantive advance

[K1] Dai et al. was inspected through the attached full-text excerpt. It supports the strongest nearby predictive evidence: treatment-adjacent CT/radiomics information predicted outcomes in a selected multicenter repeat-TACE cohort, while sample size, scanner differences, and generalization remained limitations. This proposal adds a routine longitudinal EHR transport test and an explicit workflow-versus-state decomposition; it does not reproduce the imaging study.

[K2] Tsurusaki et al. was inspected at the abstract level. It bounds interpretation because HCC treatment response is assessed with necrosis-aware imaging criteria such as mRECIST/RECIC, which are absent here. The present operational endpoint therefore cannot be called response.

[K3] Minh et al. was inspected at the abstract level. Its documented early imaging-response comparison illustrates that treatment selection, ascertainment, and undocumented imaging can alter an apparent treatment-adjacent association. This child tests whether observable recording opportunity and care process explain the HCC signal, without claiming to solve those unmeasured confounders.

These three works are distinct; no exhaustive novelty search is claimed. Branch-local UTF-8 excerpts/provider responses and their receipts are attached in key-references.json; K1 is full-text excerpt inspection and K2/K3 are explicitly abstract-only.

## Population, landmark, outcome, and timing stress test

Index is the first dated locally recorded procedure row with trimmed Unicode-case-folded '手术 == tace', requiring nonmissing '患者主索引', '就诊号', and '开始时间'. “First” is first in this HCC snapshot, not first-ever TACE. Exact duplicate patient/start rows are collapsed for event counting but retained in the raw duplicate audit. Chinese or combined procedure strings are not merged into the primary endpoint. Patients with an undated strict-TACE row are flagged and a prespecified sensitivity excludes them.

Eligibility requires all-source observation through 'index_start + 42 days', where 'obs_end' is the maximum valid timestamp across the sources listed below. Exclude any later exact-code TACE at elapsed time 0 < Delta < 43 days; retain its time and counts in 'early_repeat_audit.csv'. The primary event is the first later unique patient/start exact-code TACE with 43 <= Delta < 181 days. An untimed repeat is not a timed negative.

Follow-up ends at the first primary event, all-source 'obs_end', or day 181. Person-days are at risk only when 'obs_end' reaches the start of that interval. The primary horizons remain [43,90] and [91,181) elapsed days (integer day bins 43–90 and 91–180). A complete-180-day binary outcome is sensitivity only. No feature uses 'obs_end', future events, future counts, or post-index exact-code TACE.

Timing stress tests are fixed before scoring:

- primary day-42 cutoff and 43–181-day event window;
- remove all process/assay records in the last 7 days before the cutoff while retaining the landmark;
- construct process features only through day 28 and compare early and late horizons;
- alternate valid 'obs_end' definitions and exclude undated strict-TACE histories;
- preserve, rather than relabel, early repeats and untimed repeats.

A signal appearing only in late care marks, only in the early horizon, or only with records immediately before day 42 is compatible with planned-retreatment/lead-time workflow and is adverse to a portable state interpretation.

## Features and matched alternatives

All features are fold-fitted and use only information available by the specified cutoff.

- 'M0': pre-index age, sex, department, encounter/admission history, diagnosis counts through matched encounters, procedure counts, and exact-assay summaries in [-365,0).
- 'S': the eight exact assay labels in (0,42], with numeric values only after scale audit, qualitative/inequality flags, and assay-specific change from a valid pre-index observation in [-365,0). No request or missingness indicator is in 'S'; absence belongs to 'P_obs'.
- 'P_obs': dated encounter, examination, medication/order and lab-observation counts, distinct dates/gaps, assay-label request/result counts, and availability/missingness indicators in (0,42], with no assay values or qualitative results.
- 'P_care': dated non-TACE procedure tokens, non-drug-order tokens, medication starts/ends, department/visit process marks, and timing in (0,42], with no assay values.

The primary transparent baseline is elastic-net pooled-logistic discrete-time hazards for days 43–180 with fixed day bins or low-degree time spline, comparing 'M0' against 'A=M0+P_obs+P_care' and 'B=A+S'; matched ablations include 'M0+P_obs', 'M0+P_care', and 'M0+P_obs+S'. The primary state increment is 'B' versus 'A', and the matched workflow increment is 'M0+P_obs+P_care' versus 'M0+P_obs'. These are predictive comparisons, not causal adjustment.

The learned alternative is a compact time-aware GRU over the same -365 through +42 event stream, with elapsed-time decay, event category/label, audited numeric value, qualitative/inequality flags, relative time, time since prior event, and channel tag. It receives the same ablations, target, split, IPCW, observation weights, metrics, and three fixed development seeds. The GRU is a matched sensitivity/trajectory representation, not a reason to prefer complexity. It may reveal nonlinear timing or state trajectories lost by the baseline; it cannot resolve missing imaging, intent, units, or outside care.

Selection rationale: retain the elastic-net hazard as the primary analysis because its channel ablations and loss contrasts are auditable. Retain the GRU because a nonlinear temporal trajectory could be scientifically relevant to state-versus-workflow discrimination. Defer any larger transformer, extensive hyperparameter search, or neural deployment: it would add compute and flexibility without resolving the identified clinical ambiguity.

## Split, censoring, observation support, and uncertainty

Use one patient-level calendar split: earliest 80% of eligible index dates for development, latest 20% untouched test, frozen before outcomes are scored; no patient in both. A patient-hash random split is a sensitivity. Within development use five grouped folds for dictionaries, scaling, imputation, censoring/observation models, hyperparameters, interactions, and controls.

All-source right-censoring weights are fitted inside development folds from information available before each risk interval, with a frozen tail rule and effective-sample-size audit. For 'S', cross-fit day-42 assay-observation weights using 'M0', pre-day-42 'P_obs', 'P_care', and the relevant stratum. Report ordinary and observation-weighted results separately. No extrapolation is permitted.

Primary metrics are weighted log loss, weighted Brier at day 180, integrated Brier, calibration slope/intercept, with AUROC/AUPRC secondary. Bootstrap untouched-test patients, retaining all person-days, weights, strata, and paired predictions, for 95% uncertainty intervals. All outputs record source/catalog hashes, cutoff, split, cutpoints, weight rule, model/seed, and primary/sensitivity/exploratory status.

Resource estimates are planning estimates, not measured results. The elastic-net fits, audits, weights, and bootstrap are expected to fit a CPU job with 4 CPUs and 16 GiB RAM in approximately 1–4 hours, depending on row counts and bootstrap count. The GRU is a bounded optional task-specific fit: one allocated A100 GPU, 4 CPUs, 16 GiB RAM, approximately 2–6 hours for three fixed seeds and early stopping. Ordinary shell CUDA absence is not evidence against GPU feasibility; if the allocated probe or GRU cannot run, report the transparent result and defer only the learned sensitivity.

## Prespecified strata and support gate

Compute strata after eligibility and before outcome fitting; cutpoints use development only and are frozen before test scoring:

1. 'G_meas': zero, low-positive, high-positive distinct elapsed dates with any valid encounter/exam/lab/medication/order timestamp in (0,42], positive split at the development median.
2. 'G_order': zero, low-positive, high-positive distinct dates with valid '开立时间' in (0,42], positive split at the development median.
3. 'G_enc': zero, low-positive, high-positive distinct composite encounter IDs with a valid timestamp in (0,42], positive split at the development median.
4. 'G_care': zero, low-positive, high-positive dated de-duplicated non-TACE process marks in (0,42], positive split at the development median.
5. 'G_cal': three equal-frequency index-date bins from development timestamps.
6. 'G_hor': fixed early [43,90] versus late [91,181) interval.

The primary analysis estimates each axis one at a time; the only joint secondary is the supported 2x2 low/high 'G_meas' by 'G_care' table. Zero/positive categories remain separate. No neighboring strata are collapsed.

A stratum is claim-supported only if, before looking at held-out model differences, it has at least 15 primary events, assay observation/censoring support, effective sample size >=100 for the relevant weighted estimate, and no observation/censoring weight beyond the frozen development-fold tail rule after permitted truncation. The overall study gate is at least 50 events and completed label/calendar/duplicate/obs_end audits. Unsupported strata remain in 'transport_stratum_counts_support.csv' with an explicit reason and contribute to no transport claim; they are not silently pooled.

This makes coverage part of the result. If only a narrow subset is supported, the correct conclusion is “transportability unresolved outside supported coverage,” not a positive or negative claim about excluded cells.

## Multiplicity-controlled state and transport decision

The single confirmatory reporting family contains the prespecified axis-level and horizon-specific test-set contrasts. Use a max-|t| patient-bootstrap distribution over all reported primary contrasts, with the same paired resamples and fixed strata, to provide simultaneous 95% intervals. The same family-wise correction applies to the state increment, the workflow increment, their difference, and axis pairwise contrasts; exploratory learned-model and random-split results are labeled separately and never rescue a failed primary gate. A Holm-adjusted one-sided test is a secondary tabulation, not a reason to select favorable cells.

Define, in the test set:

- 'D_state,g = loss(A) - loss(B)' (positive favors adding recorded assay state);
- 'D_workflow,g = loss(M0+P_obs) - loss(A)' (positive favors adding care process);
- 'D_state-vs-workflow,g = D_state,g - D_workflow,g';
- 'D_transport,g = D_state,g - D_state,overall_supported', with partial-pooling shrinkage only for estimation, never to create support.

The overall supported-population state result is supportive only if the simultaneous lower 95% bound for standardized 'D_state' is >0, the primary model is calibrated, and its direction is stable under ordinary and observation-weighted scoring. Transport is supportive only if the simultaneous upper 95% bound for the maximum absolute supported-stratum contrast is below the predeclared 0.005 weighted-log-loss margin (and the secondary Brier margin is 0.010), while coverage is explicitly reported. These are computational separation margins, not clinical MCIDs.

Interpretation is a two-axis table, not a single favorable label:

- **State-consistent:** 'D_state' is positive after 'P_obs+P_care', persists under the 7-day lead-time control and observation weighting, and is not reproduced by assay-value/time permutations. This supports recorded-state information, not biology.
- **Workflow-consistent:** 'D_workflow' is positive and comparable to or larger than 'D_state', or 'D_state' vanishes after lead-time/process controls. This supports an operational workflow explanation; it does not prove intent or eliminate residual severity.
- **Mixed:** both increments remain positive. Report both; workflow and state information can coexist.
- **Transport-heterogeneous:** the state increment varies beyond the margin or is present only in supported high-opportunity/high-care/calendar cells. Do not call it reusable across the target population.
- **Inconclusive:** support, calibration, weight, event, or precision gates fail. An imprecise null is not refutation.

No conclusion is based on “every stratum is positive,” which would make sparse-cell multiplicity and missing support indistinguishable from evidence. No post hoc pooling, cutpoint selection, or alternative endpoint rescues a failed gate.

## Smallest decision-facing summary

The solver must create 'decision_facing_summary.csv' with one row per supported axis/stratum/horizon and these predeclared fields:

- supported flag and exact support failure reason;
- observed event count, person-days, assay-observation rate, censoring/observation ESS, and coverage fraction;
- calibrated held-out 180-day operational repeat-TACE risk from 'A' and 'B', each with bootstrap 95% interval;
- paired risk difference 'risk_B - risk_A', weighted log-loss and Brier increments with simultaneous intervals;
- 'D_workflow', 'D_state-vs-workflow', lead-time-control direction, and interpretation class;
- adjudication status: 'eligible_for_next_study', 'workflow_priority', 'defer_for_support', or 'inconclusive'.

The only escalation-to-adjudication criterion is population/stratum-level: a supported stratum is 'eligible_for_next_study' if the primary state gate, calibration gate, lead-time gate, and multiplicity-controlled transport gate pass. It is not a patient-level treatment rule. A patient-level clinical utility threshold cannot be invented because the snapshot lacks mortality, harms, costs, treatment intent, outside-care events, imaging response, assay units/reference ranges, and expert adjudication. If a future imaging/intent study is funded, it should sample the eligible and workflow-priority strata and adjudicate response, intent, and outside-care status.

## Revisit and abandonment rules

Continue to imaging/intent/outside-care linkage if the supported state gate passes and coverage is broad enough to matter. Prioritize scheduling, intent, and outside-care data if workflow evidence dominates or support is concentrated in high-opportunity cells. Defer the transport claim if event/support gates or scale audits fail. Abandon the assay-state validation direction after adequately supported repeated controls show that process or permutation features match the assay increment, or if the increment disappears under leakage-safe timing. A failed GRU alone never refutes the assay question.
