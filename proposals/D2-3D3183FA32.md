# D2-T1: Intervention-associated transcript response in macrophage–fibroblast context

## Question
Does the intervention-associated transcript response in synovial fibroblasts depend on the presence and phenotype of co-cultured macrophages?

## Hypothesis
The intervention-associated transcript response in synovial fibroblasts is context-dependent: it differs when fibroblasts are co-cultured with macrophages versus cultured alone, and the magnitude and direction vary by macrophage phenotype.

## Scientific value
If intervention responses are context-dependent, single-cell-type screening may miss effects that depend on the microenvironment. This would affect prioritization of fibroblast versus macrophage targets and the interpretation of drug response in RA synovium.

## Evidence and closest inspected work
The available GSE95588 and E-MTAB-8316 sources contain macrophage–fibroblast coculture and fibroblast condition measurements, but they are not independent donor-level validation sets. The closest inspected works establish that macrophage–fibroblast crosstalk is relevant to synovial disease and fibrotic remodeling, while leaving open whether an intervention-associated transcript response is specifically context-dependent.

Its inspected article reports that macrophage–fibroblast communication contributes to disease-associated remodeling, but it does not establish a causal intervention-specific transcript response in the available coculture assays.

[K2] supports the use of macrophage-conditioned or coculture systems to study fibroblast activation. It provides a mechanistic framework for macrophage-derived signals, but it does not test whether the same intervention produces different transcriptomic effects across macrophage phenotypes.

[K3] provides a broader evidence base for macrophage–fibroblast interactions in fibrosis and tissue remodeling. It bounds the claim by showing that crosstalk can be disease-context dependent, while not establishing the specific donor-aware intervention contrast proposed here.

## Falsifiable test
Compare intervention-associated transcript changes in fibroblasts with versus without macrophage coculture, and compare macrophage phenotype-defined contexts where the assay registry supports them. Support requires reproducible donor-aware differences in direction or magnitude after accounting for repeated measurements. Adverse evidence is absence of context-specific differences or a pattern explained by donor, batch, or composition. Inconclusive evidence includes insufficient verified donor mapping, unavailable phenotype labels, or no independent validation set.

## Competing explanations
- Donor or batch composition may explain apparent context effects.
- Macrophage phenotype may be incompletely resolved in the available metadata.
- The intervention-associated response may reflect indirect medication effects rather than direct target action.
- Coculture r1/r2 includes earlier GSE57723 data and cannot be treated as independent validation.

## Method alternatives
A simple donor-aware paired contrast is the baseline. A mixed-effects model with donor and condition random effects is a substantive alternative that can reveal whether context effects persist after accounting for repeated measurements and donor dependence. Neither method establishes causality without perturbation or target-specific validation.
