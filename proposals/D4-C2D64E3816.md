# CBC discordance: a delayed first-CBC cancer-type contrast, with repeat observation as a selection stress test

Status: proposed episode-3 child of [prior hypothesis]; planned experiment, not an executed result.
Parent repair reason: the repeat-landmark hematologic count is sparse, and baseline-only inverse-probability weighting cannot identify unmeasured clinical testing indication. The child therefore changes the primary estimand to a delayed first-CBC contrast and makes the near-diagnostic contrast and repeat change explicitly secondary tests of competing explanations.

## Scientific opening and hypothesis

Prior evidence supports an association between anaemia/CBC patterns and CRC, including clinically relevant near-diagnostic haemoglobin information [K1], and supports dated CBC trajectories before CRC diagnosis [K2]. It does not establish that a platelet-haemoglobin discordance is specific to CRC rather than hematologic cancer, that it persists beyond the diagnostic work-up period, or that a repeat-assessment change is not an artefact of who is re-observed. Prediction-model evidence also warns that discrimination without calibration, appropriate population definition and external validation is insufficient for clinical use [K3].

The strongest evidence-supported claim is therefore: CBC measurements can associate with CRC outcomes in selected clinical datasets, sometimes before registry diagnosis. The unresolved claim is narrower and falsifiable:

> In adults cancer-free at a complete first research-assessment CBC, is the relative platelet-high/haemoglobin-low association stronger for CRC than for hematologic cancer during years 2–5 after that CBC, and is it materially different from the 0–2-year association?

The leading explanation is a type-specific, temporally persistent phenotype. The consequential rival is near-diagnostic or testing-context selection: symptoms, clinician concern or work-up may produce a CBC pattern and an imminent diagnosis, making the 0–2-year contrast look clinically specific even if it does not persist. A second rival is repeat-observation selection: change scores may be informative only among survivors who return for another UKB assessment. The data can test timing and measured re-observation patterns; they cannot observe symptoms, indication, test ordering or community healthcare contact and therefore cannot adjudicate that context directly.

The clinical advance is a boundary on interpretation: a persistent delayed contrast would justify external validation of a single-CBC cancer-type risk comparison, while a near-diagnostic-only or repeat-only contrast would argue against treating it as a general longitudinal clinical rule. Neither result establishes a referral threshold, mechanism, treatment effect or clinical utility.

## Population, timing and outcomes

### Primary first-CBC population

Include nonmissing `eid`, parseable T0=`53-0.0), and nonmissing instance-0 values of all four CBC fields. Exclude any qualifying cancer date on or before T0. Do not condition on a future repeat CBC.

Follow from T0 to the first of event, T0+5 years, or 2022-12-31. The first event is CRC (ICD-10 C18/C19/C20), hematologic cancer (C81–C96), other primary malignant cancer (C00–C97 excluding CRC and C81–C96 and excluding secondary C77–C79), death, or censoring. Treat death as competing. Report denominators, missingness, event counts, person-time and exclusions before fitting.

### Timing estimands

Fit prespecified piecewise cause-specific hazards for 0–2 and 2–5 years. The 0–2 estimate is the near-diagnostic/timing-sensitive contrast. The primary estimand is the 2–5 contrast among participants still event-free and alive at T0+2; this conditioning is explicit and is not a claim about an unconditional 5-year population.

For cancer class c, let beta_P,c,w and beta_Hb,c,w be the platelet and haemoglobin coefficients in window w. The window-specific cancer-type discordance is:
`Delta_w = (beta_{P,CRC,w}-beta_{P,Heme,w}) - (beta_{Hb,CRC,w}-beta_{Hb,Heme,w})`.
Positive Delta means relatively higher platelets and lower haemoglobin are more CRC-associated than hematologic-associated. The key timing comparison is `D=Delta_0-2-Delta_2-5`. Use robust 95% intervals and a prespecified 0.20 log-hazard contrast as a practical magnitude for timing attenuation; do not call an imprecise difference evidence of persistence or refutation. A 5–10-year analysis is exploratory only if administrative follow-up supports it.

### Repeat landmark, secondary only

Define R=1 if `53-1.0` is parseable and strictly later than T0 and all four instance-1 CBC values are present; R=0 otherwise. For R=1, exclude cancer on or before T1=`53-1.0` and death on or before T1, then follow from T1 to T1+5 or 2022-12-31. Use baseline z values, four changes `d_j=z_{j,1}-z_{j,0}), and assessment gap T1-T0. CRP `30710-1.0` is sensitivity-only.

Estimate the repeat change contrast with the same cancer classes, both unweighted and with stabilized baseline-only inverse-probability-of-repeat weights. The repeat model for R uses only baseline CBC, sex, age, BMI, smoking, CRP, first-assessment year and eligibility indicators; numerator uses sex and first-assessment year; trim at fixed 1st/99th percentiles and show balance. Weighting is a measured-selection stress test, not causal repair. It cannot remove unmeasured symptoms, healthcare contact, indication, treatment or survival selection. Because the inherited repeat early hematologic count is 27, any repeat estimate with fewer than 50 held-out hematologic events is automatically inconclusive and cannot support the main hypothesis, regardless of its point estimate. Report repeat results by gap strata and use instance 2 only as a sensitivity cohort.

## Methods compared before selection

Both methods use exactly the same first-CBC population, features, competing outcomes, 0–2/2–5 windows, temporal split and held-out uncertainty evaluation.

### A. Transparent primary: piecewise cause-specific Cox

Fit separate cause-specific Cox models for CRC and hematologic cancer, with other primary cancer and death retained as competing events in the risk sets. Predictors are the four instance-0 CBC z values, age, sex, BMI, smoking, CRP and first-assessment year, with prespecified interactions between each CBC term and the 0–2 versus 2–5 window. Estimate `Delta_0-2`, `Delta_2-5` and `D`; report coefficient intervals, event counts, proportional-hazard/time-varying-effect diagnostics and Aalen–Johansen cumulative incidence at 2 and 5 years for all outcome classes. Use penalization only after documented separation or convergence failure.

This is primary because it directly answers an auditable coefficient-contrast question and makes the delayed-risk-set estimand visible. It loses nonlinear thresholds, cell-line interactions and flexible time-varying hazards.

### B. Substantive learned alternative: discrete-time multitask gradient boosting

Fit a CPU gradient-boosted discrete-time competing-risk model with separate hazards for CRC, hematologic cancer, other primary cancer and death and an interval indicator for 0–2 versus 2–5 years. Inputs are exactly the A inputs; no registry field, later CBC or repeat-attendance indicator is available to the first-CBC model. Tune a small fixed grid only in rolling development folds.

The learned model can reveal nonlinear low-haemoglobin/high-platelet regions, interactions among WBC/RDW/platelets/haemoglobin, and time-window-specific type hazards that the Cox contrast averages away. To place it on the same scientific question, calculate on the final holdout the standardized CRC-versus-hematologic cumulative-incidence contrast for a prespecified +1 SD platelet/−1 SD haemoglobin profile versus the development-set sex-specific mean profile separately in each window; also report finite-difference marginal effects and calibration. This is an analogue of `Delta_w`, not a mechanistic estimate and not permission to choose a favorable profile after seeing test outcomes.

Evaluate both methods on the latest temporal 30% holdout after sorting by T0; earliest 70% is development. Participants remain intact. Rolling development folds fit standardization, imputation rules (if any), tuning, calibration and repeat-observation weights. No test outcomes, test dates or test normalization enter fitting. Compare target-specific calibration intercept/slope, calibration plots, 2- and 5-year cumulative-incidence Brier/log scores and participant-bootstrap intervals; AUC/C-index is secondary. Use 500 participant bootstrap replicates for held-out predictions, `Delta` and `D`, preserving convergence failures. If the held-out hematologic count is below 50, label all type-specific learned metrics imprecise and do not replace the temporal split with a random split.

Expected solver budget: 8–16 CPU cores, 32–64 GiB RAM, approximately 2–4 hours for the selected-column read, person-period expansion, two model families, predictions and bootstrap; no GPU is required for this tabular model. This is an estimate, not a measured full fit. A transformer or latent GP/state-space model is deferred because only two broadly complete repeat CBCs are available and repeat hematologic events are sparse; its extra flexibility would not identify clinical indication. It should be revisited only if richer dated clinical tests or substantially more repeat events become available.

## Falsification and interpretation

Support for a persistent type-specific signal requires all of the following: (1) Delta_2-5 has the prespecified positive direction with informative uncertainty; (2) Delta_0-2 is not the only window carrying the direction, with `D` not showing a large prespecified attenuation; (3) the pattern is more CRC-versus-hematologic-specific than the corresponding comparison against other primary cancers; (4) the learned model's nonlinear type-specific pattern is stable across development folds and improves held-out calibration or cumulative-incidence scores rather than only AUC; and (5) the repeat stress test does not show a large measured-selection change, while its sparse-event limitation is reported.

Evidence for the near-diagnostic/context alternative is a positive or precise Delta_0-2 with attenuation, reversal or a null Delta_2-5, especially if the learned model also loses calibration outside 0–2. Evidence for repeat-observation selection is a repeat-only change signal, a large weighted-versus-unweighted change, or a pattern confined to narrow assessment-gap strata. A precise reversal or a signal shared by other cancers is adverse to cancer-type specificity. A wide 2–5 interval, fewer than 50 held-out hematologic events, unstable dates/gaps, or failed convergence is inconclusive, not a refutation.

The cancer registry dates are diagnosis dates, not test-order dates. The table lacks symptoms, ferritin/iron, reticulocytes, FIT, medications, transfusion context, smear, marrow/pathology adjudication, stage, diagnostic work-up, laboratory indication/source and complete healthcare-contact history. Therefore the experiment can computationally check temporal association, competing-risk contrasts, selection sensitivity, calibration and falsification controls; it cannot determine whether the CBC was clinically indicated, establish a mechanism, estimate symptomatic-patient diagnostic accuracy, or support a referral rule. Those claims require clinical adjudication, richer linked records, external validation and a prospective decision-linked study.

Required negative controls and audits: preserve date/code pairing; check duplicate eids, invalid dates and implausible gaps; compare complete-CBC participants with all eligible first-CBC participants; permute CRC versus hematologic labels among development event dates while preserving event timing and verify the interaction collapses toward zero; report other-cancer specificity; do not impute missing CBC as normal; and do not use future R in the first-CBC model.

Completion requires conclusions linked to these outputs. A readiness probe or a fitted model alone is not completion.

The transparent model is primary for estimand auditability and sparse-event interpretability. The gradient-boosted model is retained because it can reveal clinically meaningful nonlinear threshold and timing structure on the same question; it is not selected for a small predictive gain. A latent GP/state-space or transformer is deferred for the documented two-observation/sparse-event identifiability problem. A model using future R as a predictor is rejected as leakage. Cross-namespace augmentation is rejected because no authorized mapping exists. Revisit the deferred alternatives only with richer dated clinical context or enough repeat hematologic events to make their added structure identifiable.

## Key claim discussion

[K1] Hamilton et al. supports the haemoglobin/CRC premise in UK primary-care records, but is case-control, CRC-only and not a repeated or cancer-type-specific UKB analysis. This child adds a hematologic comparator and an explicit delayed-versus-near-diagnostic estimand.

[K2] Sala et al. supports examining longitudinal CBC trajectories and a 24-month prediagnostic boundary, but is a CRC-only Spanish cohort and does not establish type specificity or remove observation/context selection. This child tests whether the contrast survives beyond that boundary in a UKB first-CBC population.

[K3] Hampton et al. supports calibration, temporal/appropriate-population validation and restraint before clinical adoption because discrimination often lacked calibration and external validation. This child makes those checks part of the held-out evaluation and does not claim clinical utility.

## Limitations retained from the parent

The delayed 2–5 estimand conditions on being alive and event-free at two years; it is not an unconditional natural-history effect and cannot establish causality. Repeat weighting covers only measured baseline predictors and cannot identify clinical indication. Hematologic events remain sparse, especially after temporal splitting. UKB research-assessment participation and registry ascertainment may not represent symptomatic clinical populations. The proposed result remains a bounded descriptive/predictive adaptation, not a reproduction of any cited paper.
