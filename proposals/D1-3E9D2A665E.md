# POD1 multiorgan dynamics and later new organ support after HCC resection

Status: proposed, reference-complete, not executed. Aggregate event support was audited; no predictive model was fitted.

## Clinical opening, strongest evidence, and hypothesis

After HCC resection, the practical POD1 question is not simply whether discharge will be delayed or whether pathology will show microvascular invasion. It is whether a patient who is not already receiving selected organ support is beginning a trajectory toward a consequential respiratory, circulatory, or renal rescue event over the next six days. This could affect monitoring intensity, level-of-care review, imaging, fluid/bleeding reassessment, and escalation readiness; it is not a treatment-effect question.

The strongest evidence already supported is limited. Severe postoperative organ failure after HCC hepatectomy is clinically important and has been associated with preoperative vulnerability, but it requires adjudicated physiological and complication data [K1]. In major hepatectomy, POD1 bilirubin plus clinical factors predicted adjudicated grade B/C liver failure better than lactate alone, showing that a simple POD1 snapshot can be strong [K2]. Routine-EHR observation intensity can itself be prognostic and must not be confused with physiology [K3].

**Falsifiable hypothesis:** among adult first HCC resections in 2016-2025, patients still hospitalized with no selected support start recorded from admission through T0+24h, a multiorgan latent-state model fitted only to information recorded by T0+24h will improve locked 2023-2025 prediction of a first new recorded invasive/non-invasive ventilation, IV vasoactive infusion, or renal-replacement start during (T0+24h, min[T0+168h, discharge]) over a transparent POD1 landmark model, with adequate calibration and with at least half of its incremental proper-score gain lost when within-patient value-time pairing is destroyed but observation times are retained.

This hypothesis tests added predictive information and a bounded physiology-versus-observation explanation. It does **not** assert occult organ failure, causal benefit of escalation, ICU transfer, or a biological mechanism.

## Scientific advance and rivals

Closest inspected work models adjudicated PHLF after major hepatectomy using clinical factors and POD1 laboratory snapshots [K2]. The proposed advance is a different clinical decision and outcome: later incident multiorgan support after the first postoperative day, in an HCC resection population, while explicitly asking whether multiorgan dynamics add information beyond both a strong snapshot and care-process signals.

Leading explanation: coordinated POD0-1 deterioration across oxygenation/perfusion, renal clearance, coagulation, hepatic function, and hematology carries information about latent loss of physiological reserve that precedes later organ support.

Strongest rivals:

1. **Current-state severity:** the latest POD1 values, age, operative burden, and liver reserve contain essentially all useful information; trajectory adds no meaningful gain.
2. **Observation/treatment-process artifact:** sicker patients are tested more often, and support orders reflect clinician practice; timing and missingness—not biological path—drive apparent gain [K3].
3. **Era/coding artifact:** medication/order adoption and thresholds changed materially after 2015; a model learns year or documentation style.
4. **Outcome heterogeneity:** ventilation, pressors, and dialysis arise from distinct mechanisms; a composite gain may hide poor or opposite component behavior.
5. **Selection/competing events:** excluding patients discharged before the horizon and requiring POD1 measurements conditions on care and severity.

Different predictions: a physiological trajectory signal should beat both latest-state and timing-only baselines, degrade materially after value-time scrambling, remain directionally useful across outcome components and years, and not be explained by year. A process artifact predicts timing-only performance near the full model, little loss under value permutation or value-time scrambling, and marked era instability.

## Exact computable design

### Population and clock

- Exact cohort: age >=18; encounter diagnosis `诊断名称` matches `肝细胞癌|原发性肝癌|原发性肝恶性肿瘤`, excluding `疑似|个人史`; timestamped procedure `手术` contains both `肝` and `切除` and excludes transplant/donor/biopsy/ablation/TACE/embolization/infusion terms; first qualifying resection per `患者主索引`; T0 within recorded admission-discharge.
- Use calendar years 2016-2025 for the primary analysis because audited IV vasoactive records are absent before 2016. Years 2012-2015 are an era sensitivity, not training data.
- **T0 is earliest qualifying resection `开始时间`.** Do not call it surgery end: 24,301/25,071 audited procedures lacked a distinct usable later end.
- Landmark L=T0+24h. Require `出院时间 >= L`.
- Exclude a patient if any selected ventilation, IV vasoactive, or renal-replacement support starts in [admission,L]. This defines an incident-support question and avoids predicting continuation of immediate peri-anesthetic support.
- Primary outcome: first high-specificity start in (L, min[T0+168h, `出院时间`]) of:
  - mechanical ventilation: selected `医嘱(非药品)`, non-null `开始时间`, `医嘱状态='已停'`, excluding negative durations;
  - IV norepinephrine, dopamine, dobutamine, or vasopressin from `用药`, route `用药方式 in ('静推微泵','静滴')`, non-null `开始时间`;
  - renal replacement from selected CRRT/hemodialysis orders or medications with non-null `开始时间`, or matching timestamped procedure.
- Report each component and overlap separately. Do not include blood products in the primary outcome.

The tightened incident-event audit found N=20,401 and 201 outcomes during 2016-2025: train 2016-2020 N=7,891/events=105; development-recalibration 2021-2022 N=3,735/events=38; locked test 2023-2025 N=8,775/events=58. Component totals were ventilation 60, vasoactive 169, renal 22, with 42 multi-component patients. Expanding the exclusion from [T0,L] to [admission,L] removed nine patients and three later outcomes that otherwise would not be first recorded support starts. These are planned-cohort support counts, not fitted results. Mechanical ventilation has the cleanest single-event timestamp semantics but only 14 locked-test events; the composite is the only endpoint with workable support and must remain labeled a recorded support-start proxy until adjudicated.

### Predictors available by L

Static: age, sex, year; resection-name flags learned/frozen from training vocabulary (laparoscopic/open, named hemi/segmental/partial, multivisceral/co-procedure); operation-start time and documented duration/missingness; admission-to-T0 interval; preoperative diagnosis flags for cirrhosis and comorbidities, acknowledging diagnosis timing is unavailable.

Longitudinal labs in [T0-24h,L], with explicit training-only label mapping and no cross-label pooling without demonstrated equivalence: hemoglobin, platelet count, PT/INR, fibrinogen, lactate, creatinine, bilirubin, albumin. For each value retain label, numeric value, specimen, and hours from T0. Record number of tests, first/latest/min/max, time since latest, and missingness. The audit found 0-24h numeric coverage of 9,011 for hemoglobin, 7,906 INR, 7,891 fibrinogen, 5,460 acute lactate, 5,121 acute creatinine, 5,070 acute bilirubin, and 5,069 acute albumin; incomplete observation is intrinsic. A separate exact-risk-set audit found at least two distinct POD0-1 selected-lab times in 2,607/7,891 training patients (81/105 events), 923/3,735 development patients (27/38 events), and 4,203/8,775 test patients (36/58 events). Repeats in all three domains occurred in 2,019 training patients (69 events), 431 development patients (11 events), and 1,307 test patients (29 events); this supports a temporal comparison but also exposes major era-varying observation intensity.

Exclude any support-outcome event, blood-product order, or post-L information from predictors. A sensitivity may add explicit 0-24h RBC/blood-product order indicators, but it changes the question toward clinician action and must not be in the primary models.

## Matched methods on the same question

### B0: transparent POD1 landmark baseline

Penalized logistic regression with restricted cubic splines for age, documented operation duration, admission-to-T0 interval, and latest available value before L for the prespecified lab families; sex, operation flags, cirrhosis, year, label-specific missing indicators, time since latest measurement, and 0-24h measurement counts. Fit preprocessing and penalty using training only. This is the primary comparator because [K2] shows a clinically strong POD1 snapshot can outperform an isolated dynamic biomarker.

Secondary B-process uses only static predictors plus measurement counts, missingness, and collection times; no laboratory values. This quantifies the care-process channel.

### T1: substantive multiorgan latent-state alternative

Fit one constrained irregular-time linear-Gaussian state-space model on training sequences only, without outcome labels. Training-set robust z-scores are emitted with lab-label-specific intercepts and residual variances; explicit time gaps update three prespecified one-dimensional latent domains: oxygen delivery/hematology (hemoglobin, platelets), perfusion/renal (lactate, creatinine), and hepatic/coagulation (bilirubin, albumin, PT/INR, fibrinogen). Estimate shared mean-reversion and process-noise parameters by maximum likelihood with a Kalman filter; freeze them before validation. For each patient, pass the posterior state mean, 0-24h change, and posterior variance for each domain at L to a ridge-logistic outcome head together with the complete B0 feature vector, including latest values and process features. Select the ridge penalty by prespecified rolling-origin validation wholly within 2016-2020; after refitting on all training years, update the calibration intercept only on pooled 2021-2022. Do not fit a calibration slope on only 38 development-era events.

This nine-feature latent summary is deliberately low-dimensional for 105 training events. What T1 can reveal that B0 loses is whether **cross-domain order, rate, persistence, and uncertainty** before POD1 contain reproducible information beyond latest levels and surveillance intensity. It cannot establish a biological mechanism. A GRU-D sequence model was considered but deferred: with only 201 total and 105 training events, its extra flexibility is not scientifically necessary unless a larger adjudicated cohort later demonstrates adequate support.

Controls:

- T1-values-only: remove counts/times except gaps required for interpolation.
- T1-timing-only: values replaced by training medians while retaining labels/times/missingness.
- Within training-year and operation-stratum, permute numeric values across patients while preserving each patient's labels and timestamps.
- Within patient, scramble early/latest value-time pairing while retaining latest state and observation times.
- B0 plus simple first-to-latest deltas for a transparent trajectory comparator.
- Outcome-label permutation sanity check. Each feature perturbation is generated independently within each temporal split under frozen seeds; its model is refitted on perturbed training data, intercept-updated on perturbed development data, and compared once on the corresponding perturbed locked test. Outcome labels are permuted in training only.

### Fitting, splits, uncertainty, and resource envelope

- Train 2016-2020; freeze label mapping and scaling within each fold and choose both B0 and T1 ridge penalties by mean Brier score over rolling-origin folds (train 2016-2017/validate 2018, train through 2018/validate 2019, train through 2019/validate 2020). Refit on all five training years; perform an intercept-only calibration update for each model on pooled 2021-2022; open 2023-2025 once for final evaluation.
- No random patient split. Patients are unique by design.
- Primary metric: Brier score. Secondary: log loss, AUROC, AUPRC, calibration-in-the-large, calibration slope, and 10-bin reliability; report component-specific AUROC/AUPRC/calibration descriptively with exact event counts.
- Use 2,000 patient bootstrap replicates on the locked test for paired differences and calibration uncertainty; state that a frozen-model bootstrap omits fitting uncertainty. If feasible, add 200 full-pipeline stratified bootstrap refits on train/development.
- Rare-event calibration is central. Do not optimize or select on accuracy. Report precision/recall and decision curves at clinician-specified action thresholds; in their absence, use 0.5%, 1%, 2%, and 5% risk only as descriptive thresholds, not proof of utility.
- Approximate future solver budget: 12 CPUs, 64 GB RAM, no GPU required, <=4 hours for extraction, state-space maximum-likelihood fitting, controls, and inference; B0 alone <=30 minutes. These are unmeasured planning estimates. One A100 could accelerate a later GRU-D sensitivity, but that method is deferred for inadequate event support rather than hardware constraints.

## Prespecified falsification and interpretation

Support requires all of:

1. locked-test T1 Brier improvement over B0 >=0.001 absolute and 95% paired CI entirely below zero; this is 10% of audited 2016-2025 prevalence and about 15% of locked-test prevalence and is intentionally harder than a trivial rank gain;
2. calibration slope 0.8-1.2 and calibration-in-the-large within +/-0.002 absolute risk after pooled 2021-2022 intercept-only recalibration;
3. T1 improves AUPRC over B0 with 95% paired CI above zero;
4. value-time scrambling removes >=50% of T1's Brier gain, while timing-only explains <50% of that gain;
5. component-specific effects have no strong reversal: for each component with >=20 locked-test events, T1 Brier is not worse by >0.001 and AUROC is not worse by >0.03;
6. annual 2023-2025 calibration does not show a monotonic absolute risk error >0.005.

Adverse evidence: adequate test events and precision but T1 fails Brier/AUPRC gain, timing-only retains most gain, scrambling does not attenuate it, or component behavior reverses. This favors current state/process or endpoint heterogeneity and stops escalation to a complex model.

Inconclusive: fewer than 50 composite test events (audit suggests 58), calibration/effect intervals include clinically useful and null effects, marked outcome-code drift, inadequate lab overlap, or adjudication shows low/unstable positive predictive value. Inconclusive does not refute physiology; it blocks deployment claims and calls for adjudicated/external data.

Continue only if support gates pass and blinded endpoint adjudication has acceptable, stable PPV across components/eras. Revise toward a component-specific endpoint if the composite alone fails coherence. Abandon this dataset for the clinical claim if timestamp semantics or adjudication are poor.

## Required adjudication and claim limits

Before clinical interpretation, conduct blinded manual review of a stratified random sample of at least 50 operational positives (oversampling each component/year) and 50 matched negatives, using clinical documents and full chart context. Two clinicians should determine actual delivery, indication, post-anesthesia continuation versus new rescue, elective dialysis, organ failure, local surgical complication, and onset; resolve disagreements and report kappa and PPV with confidence intervals. The available document table has no document timestamps, so it cannot safely enter prediction.

Automatically checkable: source hashes/headers, cohort rules, joins, T0/L clocks, vocabulary, timestamps, exclusions, split isolation, counts, features available by L, model code, metrics, calibration, bootstrap contrasts, shuffle/permutation controls, and conclusion-output links.

Not automatically establishable: true organ failure, ICU transfer, intervention indication, administration truth for EHR orders, biological mechanism, actionability, net clinical benefit, generalizability, or treatment effect. These require adjudication, clinician review, external validation, and ultimately a prospective decision study.
