# Research Proposal: Discordant Multi-Organ Recovery Trajectories Predict Subsequent Treatment Escalation and Mortality in ICU Patients

## Scientific Question

**Primary Hypothesis:** In critically ill patients requiring organ support, discordant recovery patterns (where one organ recovers while another remains impaired) are independently associated with subsequent treatment escalation and mortality, beyond what is captured by single-organ recovery metrics or composite severity scores.

**Unresolved Question:** What specific combinations of organ-recovery trajectories (kidney, respiratory, cardiovascular) predict the need for escalated support (additional vasopressors, mechanical ventilation, renal replacement therapy) and mortality, and do these patterns differ by the underlying clinical phenotype (sepsis vs. shock vs. cardiac)?

## Clinical Importance

1. **Current Limitations:** Clinical practice relies heavily on single timepoint severity assessments (e.g., SOFA scores calculated at admission or daily) that fail to capture the temporal dynamics of multi-organ recovery. [K1] demonstrates that temporal recovery dynamics predict outcomes, but focuses on inflammatory markers rather than organ function.

2. **Consequential Decision:** Identifying discordant recovery patterns could enable:
   - Earlier escalation of supportive care when recovery is asynchronous
   - De-escalation of therapy when multi-organ recovery is synchronized
   - Personalized prognostication that better stratifies patients for clinical trials or resource allocation

## Substantive Advance Over Existing Knowledge

| Existing Approach | Limitation | Proposed Advance |
|-------------------|------------|------------------|
| Single timepoint SOFA scores | Misses temporal dynamics of recovery | Continuous recovery trajectory modeling [K2] confirms data availability for this approach |
| Single-organ recovery metrics | Ignores inter-organ relationships | Multi-organ discordant pattern detection |
| Phenotype-agnostic analysis | Fails to account for phenotype-specific recovery patterns | Phenotype-stratified recovery analysis |
| Cross-sectional comparisons | Cannot inform prospective treatment decisions | Prospective risk prediction for treatment escalation |

### Organ Recovery Metrics

**Respiratory Recovery:**
- Mechanical ventilation days (from ICU stays)
- Ventilator weaning events (from chartevents with ventilator-related items)
- Oxygenation indices (PaO2/FiO2 from chartevents)

**Renal Recovery:**
- Serum creatinine trajectory over ICU stay
- Urine output (from chartevents with urine output items)
- Renal replacement therapy initiation (from chartevents with CRRT items)

**Cardiovascular Recovery:**
- Vasopressor intensity (from emar/medication)
- Systolic blood pressure (from chartevents)
- Lactate clearance (from chartevents with lactate items)

**Circulatory Stabilization:**
- Time from vasopressor initiation to dose reduction by 50% or discontinuation
- Time from admission to hemodynamic stability (SBP > 90 mmHg or MAP > 65 mmHg without vasopressors)

## Study Population

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Adult patients (≥18 years) admitted to ICU in MIMIC-IV
- Initial vasopressor requirement (within first 24 hours)
- ICU stay ≥48 hours
- At least one laboratory measurement of creatinine and/or lactate during ICU stay

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Transfers to ICU from outside hospital (to avoid pre-ICU confounding)
- Patients with end-of-life status at admission (dod=1 within 24 hours)
- Missing critical variables for recovery assessment

**Population Size:** Approximately 20,000-30,000 ICU patients with vasopressor use (based on MIMIC-IV 3.1 characteristics)

## Statistical Analysis Plan

### Primary Analysis: Discordant Recovery Patterns

1. **Define Recovery Trajectories:**
   - Calculate time to each organ's "recovery" (defined as: creatinine < 1.5× baseline, lactate clearance >50%, or vasopressor discontinuation)
   - Classify recovery as: synchronized (all organs recover within 7 days of each other) vs. discordant (at least one organ recovers >7 days after others)

2. **Identify Discordant Patterns:**
   - Pattern A: Kidney recovers first, then respiratory
   - Pattern B: Respiratory recovers first, then kidney
   - Pattern C: Cardiovascular recovers first, then kidney and respiratory
   - Pattern D: Asynchronous recovery with no clear sequence

3. **Outcomes:**
   - Primary: Time to treatment escalation (additional vasopressors, CRRT, or mechanical ventilation)
   - Secondary: 28-day mortality, 90-day mortality

### Secondary Analysis: Phenotype Stratification

- Stratify by primary diagnosis (sepsis, shock, cardiac, respiratory failure)
- Test for interaction between recovery pattern and phenotype

### Baseline Models

1. **Univariate Analysis:** Compare outcomes by recovery pattern (chi-square for categorical, log-rank for time-to-event)

2. **Multivariable Cox Proportional Hazards:**
   - Adjust for: age, gender, comorbidities, severity at admission, initial treatment intensity
   - Test: Discordant pattern vs. synchronized pattern

3. **Machine Learning Baseline:**
   - Random forest classifier for treatment escalation
   - Features: admission severity, initial treatment, recovery trajectory features

### Advanced Analysis (if computational resources permit)

1. **Latent Class Analysis:** Identify subgroups of patients with distinct recovery trajectories

2. **Time-varying Cox models:** Account for time-dependent covariates (recovery status changes over time)

3. **Causal inference:** Target-trial emulation to assess effect of recovery pattern on treatment escalation

## Falsification Criteria

The hypothesis will be falsified if:
1. Discordant recovery patterns show no significant association with treatment escalation or mortality (p > 0.05 in multivariable analysis)
2. Discordant patterns are equally predictive across all clinical phenotypes (no significant interaction)
3. Synchronized recovery patterns are as predictive as discordant patterns

## Limitations

1. **Data Limitations:**
   - Urine output may be incompletely recorded
   - Lactate measurements may be selective (only drawn when clinically indicated)
   - Vasopressor dosing may be imprecisely recorded

2. **Temporal Limitations:**
   - Recovery definitions are based on arbitrary thresholds
   - Some recovery may occur outside ICU (not captured)
   - Treatment decisions may be influenced by unmeasured factors

3. **Causal Inference:**
   - Observational design cannot prove causation
   - Confounding by indication may remain despite adjustment
