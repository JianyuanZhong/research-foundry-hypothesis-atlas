# Renal Function Trajectory and Insulin-Associated Hypoglycemia in ICU: A Multi-Hospital eICU Cohort Study

## Unresolved Question

Among ICU patients receiving insulin, does worsening renal function (rising creatinine) independently increase the risk of hypoglycemia (glucose <70 mg/dL) beyond what is explained by insulin intensity and illness severity?

**Leading claim:** At the same insulin exposure, patients whose renal function is declining (rising creatinine trajectory) have a higher incidence of hypoglycemia than patients with stable or improving renal function, because impaired renal clearance prolongs insulin half-life and reduces endogenous glucose production.

**Strongest rival:** The association between rising creatinine and hypoglycemia is confounded by illness severity (sicker patients get more insulin AND have worse kidneys) and by concurrent factors (hepatic dysfunction, sepsis, malnutrition) that independently drive both renal impairment and hypoglycemia. After adjustment for APACHE score, insulin dose, and concurrent organ failure, the independent effect of renal trajectory on hypoglycemia disappears.

**A third explanation (measurement artifact):** More frequent glucose monitoring in sicker patients (who also have rising creatinine) increases the detection probability of hypoglycemic episodes, creating a surveillance bias rather than a true causal association.

## Clinical Importance

Hypoglycemia in the ICU is a potentially preventable adverse event associated with increased mortality, prolonged ICU stay, and neurological injury [K1]. The NICE-SUGAR trial established that tight glucose control protocols that induce hypoglycemia increase mortality [K2]. Current insulin dosing protocols in most ICUs do not systematically account for real-time renal function changes. If worsening renal function independently increases hypoglycemia risk at the same insulin dose, this would support:
1. Protocol modifications that reduce insulin doses when creatinine is rising
2. More frequent glucose monitoring in patients with declining renal function
3. Risk stratification tools that incorporate renal trajectory

This is a **potentially preventable adverse event** question with direct implications for insulin dosing safety in a common ICU scenario (AKI occurs in ~25-30% of ICU patients).

## Scientific Advance over Existing Knowledge

- [K1] Fehér 2026 (244 patients, single center): showed hypoglycemia <70 associated with mortality, but did not examine renal function as a modifiable risk factor; small single-center sample
- [K2] Gunst 2026 (review): synthesized NICE-SUGAR and subsequent trials, identified that hypoglycemia risk varies by context but did not quantify renal function as an independent predictor in a large multi-hospital cohort
- [K3] Jiang 2026 (592 patients, single center): showed CGM reduces severe hypoglycemia, but did not examine patient-level risk factors for hypoglycemia
- **This proposal adds:** A large multi-hospital (200+ hospitals) eICU cohort examining whether renal function trajectory (not just baseline creatinine) independently predicts hypoglycemia at the same insulin exposure, with hospital-level random effects and held-out hospital validation. No prior study has tested the renal trajectory hypothesis in a multi-hospital ICU cohort.

### Temporal Design
- **Index time:** First insulin administration (drugstartoffset from medication or infusionoffset from infusionDrug)
- **Exposure window:** 0-24h after index time (insulin intensity, creatinine trajectory)
- **Outcome window:** 0-72h after index time (any glucose <70 mg/dL)
- **Censoring:** ICU discharge, transfer, or death before 72h

### Join Keys
- All tables join on `patientunitstayid`
- Time alignment via offset columns (hours from ICU admission)

## Estimand

Among ICU patients receiving insulin with adequate glucose and creatinine monitoring, the association between renal function trajectory (rising vs stable/improving creatinine in the first 24-48h) and the probability of hypoglycemia (glucose <70 mg/dL within 72h of insulin initiation), adjusted for insulin intensity, APACHE score, age, gender, and hospital.

## Analysis Plan

### Primary Analysis
1. **Multilevel logistic regression** (hospital random intercept):
   - Outcome: Hypoglycemia (binary, glucose <70 in 72h)
   - Primary predictor: Creatinine trajectory (continuous: % change in creatinine 0-48h; and categorical: rising >20% vs stable ±20% vs declining >20%)
   - Covariates: Insulin intensity (continuous, log-transformed), APACHE score, age, gender
   - Random effect: hospitalid
   - Report: Adjusted OR with 95% CI, hospital-level ICC

2. **Interaction analysis:** Creatinine trajectory × insulin intensity (does the renal effect differ at low vs high insulin doses?)

### Sensitivity Analyses
1. **Nutrition adjustment:** Restrict to 8,149 stays with nutrition records; add nutrition status as covariate
2. **Monitoring density adjustment:** Add number of glucose measurements per 24h as covariate (addresses surveillance bias)
3. **IV insulin only:** Restrict to patients with IV insulin infusion records (more precise dosing)
4. **Severe hypoglycemia (<54):** Repeat primary analysis with stricter threshold
5. **Time-varying creatinine:** Use creatinine measured closest to each glucose measurement (rather than 48h trajectory)

### Method Comparison
- **Baseline:** Univariable logistic regression (no adjustment) → multivariable logistic regression (no random effects)
- **Primary model:** Multilevel logistic regression with hospital random intercept
- **Robustness check:** Gradient-boosted trees (XGBoost or LightGBM) with hospital-level cross-validation (5-fold by hospital) to assess whether the renal trajectory signal is captured by nonlinear interactions that linear models miss
- **Rationale:** The multilevel model is the primary analysis because it properly accounts for hospital-level clustering and provides interpretable ORs. The tree model tests whether the linear assumption is adequate; if the tree model shows substantially better discrimination (ΔAUC > 0.02) with renal trajectory as a top feature, this suggests nonlinear interactions worth further investigation.

### Validation
- **Held-out hospital validation:** Split hospitals into training (70%) and validation (30%) sets; refit model on training hospitals, evaluate on validation hospitals
- **Metrics:** AUC, calibration slope, Brier score on validation set
- **Success criterion for validation:** AUC degradation < 0.03 between training and validation

## Falsification Criteria

| Outcome | Interpretation |
|---------|---------------|
| **Supportive:** Creatinine trajectory OR > 1.3 (95% CI excluding 1.0) for hypoglycemia, sustained in held-out hospitals, with ΔAUC ≥ 0.005 when adding renal trajectory to insulin-only model | Renal function decline independently increases hypoglycemia risk; supports protocol modification |
| **Adverse:** Creatinine trajectory OR ≈ 1.0 (95% CI includes 1.0) after adjustment for insulin intensity and APACHE | Renal trajectory does not independently predict hypoglycemia; the association is confounded by severity/insulin dose; no protocol change supported |
| **Inconclusive:** OR directionally >1 but CI wide (e.g., 0.9-1.5) due to small effect or measurement noise; or surveillance bias sensitivity analysis eliminates the association | Cannot distinguish true effect from confounding; requires prospective study or more precise renal function measures (eGFR, cystatin C) |

## Power Considerations
- Expected hypoglycemia rate in insulin+glucose+creatinine population: ~15-25% (based on 30,683/192,812 overall, likely higher in insulin-treated)
- With 54,465 stays and ~20% hypoglycemia rate: ~10,900 events
- Detecting OR=1.2 with 80% power requires ~1,000 events in the exposure group (rising creatinine ~30% of population = ~16,000)
- **Adequately powered** for OR ≥ 1.15

## Limitations
1. **Retrospective design:** Cannot establish causality; residual confounding by illness severity, sepsis, hepatic dysfunction
2. **Creatinine as renal function proxy:** Does not capture GFR directly; acute changes may reflect hemodynamic shifts rather than true renal injury
3. **Insulin dosing imprecision:** medication.dosage field is largely non-numeric (only 4,180/187,251 parseable); IV infusionDrug drugrate is more reliable but not universal
4. **Nutrition sparsity:** Only 8,149/54,465 stays have nutrition records; cannot fully adjust for caloric intake
5. **Surveillance bias:** Sicker patients (higher creatinine) may be monitored more frequently, increasing hypoglycemia detection
6. **Single time-point APACHE:** Does not capture evolving severity during the outcome window
7. **No continuous glucose monitoring:** Intermittent glucose measurements may miss hypoglycemic episodes

## What Would Require Additional Data
- **Causal inference:** Would require prospective interventional study (e.g., protocol modification trial reducing insulin in rising-creatinine patients)
- **Mechanistic confirmation:** Would require insulin pharmacokinetic measurements (serum insulin levels)
- **External validation:** MIMIC-IV or other ICU datasets for transportability
- **eGFR/cystatin C:** More precise renal function measures not available in eICU
