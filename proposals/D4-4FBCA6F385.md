# Scientific Proposal: Proteomic Biomarkers Predict Long-Term Multimorbidity Trajectories

## Unresolved Question

**Primary Hypothesis**: Pre-illness proteomic profiles measured at UK Biobank assessment center predict distinct long-term multimorbidity trajectories that differ from those predicted by traditional clinical risk factors alone.

**Specific Testable Claim**: Among UK Biobank participants without major chronic disease at baseline, baseline proteomic signatures (from the biological_samples table) predict the incidence and temporal pattern of incident multimorbidity (≥2 chronic conditions) more accurately than age, sex, BMI, and traditional cardiovascular risk factors alone.

## Clinical Importance

1. **Early Risk Stratification**: Proteomic biomarkers could enable earlier identification of individuals at risk for developing multimorbidity, potentially years before traditional risk factors manifest clinically.

2. **Mechanistic Insights**: The differential prediction of proteomic signatures vs. traditional risk factors could reveal novel biological pathways driving disease progression, distinguishing between inflammatory, metabolic, and other mechanisms.

3. **Precision Prevention**: Understanding which proteomic profiles predict which disease combinations could inform targeted prevention strategies for specific disease clusters.

4. **Clinical Decision Making**: Early identification of high-risk individuals could justify more intensive monitoring or preventive interventions, potentially reducing the burden of multimorbidity.

## Substantive Advance Over Existing Knowledge

**Current State**: Traditional risk stratification relies on age, sex, BMI, blood pressure, cholesterol, smoking, and diabetes status. These factors predict individual disease risk but do not capture the dynamic biological processes that drive multimorbidity development.

**Proposed Advance**: This study will test whether:
1. Proteomic signatures provide *independent* predictive value beyond traditional risk factors
2. Proteomic profiles predict *distinct disease combinations* (e.g., cardiovascular-metabolic vs. inflammatory-metabolic)
3. Proteomic signatures capture *temporal dynamics* of disease risk that traditional factors miss

**Key Innovation**: Moving from static risk factor assessment to dynamic proteomic profiling for predicting multimorbidity trajectories.

## Study Population and Temporal Boundaries

**Population**: UK Biobank participants (n≈500,000) who:
- Were recruited between 2006-2010
- Underwent assessment center visit with proteomic measurements
- Did not have major chronic disease at baseline (no diabetes, no CVD, no CKD, no cancer)
- Have follow-up data through health_outcomes table

**Temporal Boundaries**:
- Baseline: Assessment center visit (2006-2010)
- Follow-up: Up to 15 years post-assessment
- Outcome assessment: Incident multimorbidity events

## Statistical Approach

### Primary Analysis

**Outcome**: Time-to-first incident multimorbidity (≥2 conditions from predefined set)

**Exposure**: Baseline proteomic signature (multivariate model)

**Covariates**: Age, sex, BMI, socioeconomic status, smoking status

**Model**: Cox proportional hazards regression with:
- Main effect of proteomic signature
- Adjustment for traditional risk factors
- Interaction terms (proteomic × traditional factors)

### Secondary Analyses

1. **Disease-specific trajectories**: Separate analysis for different multimorbidity clusters
2. **Proteomic feature importance**: Identify top predictive proteins
3. **Calibration**: Compare predicted vs. observed risk across deciles
4. **Net reclassification improvement**: Compare proteomic+traditional vs. traditional alone

### Baseline Comparison

**Null Hypothesis**: Traditional risk factors alone adequately predict multimorbidity trajectories

**Alternative Hypothesis**: Adding proteomic signatures significantly improves prediction

## Falsification Criteria

**Study will be falsified (rejected) if**:
1. Proteomic signatures do not predict multimorbidity risk after adjusting for traditional factors (p > 0.05 for proteomic main effect)
2. No significant improvement in prediction metrics (AUC, NRI) when adding proteomics to traditional factors
3. Proteomic associations are entirely confounded by traditional risk factors (all proteomic effects disappear after full adjustment)

**Study will be supported if**:
1. Proteomic signatures predict multimorbidity risk independently of traditional factors
2. Adding proteomics to traditional models improves prediction metrics (e.g., AUC improvement ≥0.02)
3. Proteomic associations persist after full adjustment for traditional factors

**Study will be inconclusive if**:
1. Proteomic associations are weak but directionally consistent
2. Results vary substantially by disease cluster
3. Missing data or measurement error prevents definitive conclusions
