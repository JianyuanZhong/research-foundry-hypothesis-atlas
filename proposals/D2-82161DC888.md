# MerTK signaling on protective macrophages mediates suppression of the proinflammatory synovial fibroblast program in rheumatoid arthritis

## Domain
Therapeutic Target Prioritization (D2). Develops expert seed D2-T2 (protective vs pathogenic macrophage states) and D2-T1 (context-specific intervention response) on the E-MTAB-8873 MerTK co-culture assay.

## Question
Is the protective (MerTK+/CD206+ "Mpos") macrophage→synovial fibroblast (FLS) context in rheumatoid arthritis (RA) mediated by MERTK activity? Specifically: does pharmacologic MerTK blockade on Mpos macrophages convert the FLS transcriptome toward the proinflammatory/tissue-damaging (MerTK−/CD206− "Mneg") program?

## Why it matters (clinical consequence)
RA is driven by a feed-forward stromal–myeloid circuit in which pathological FLS and proinflammatory macrophages sustain synovitis and joint destruction [K3]. A large fraction of patients are difficult-to-treat precisely because this circuit is not resolved. MerTK+ CD206+ macrophages are the pro-resolving, efferocytic, "protective" subset [K2, K3], yet in active disease they can acquire pro-arthritogenic functions [K2]. If the protective FLS-suppressive effect of Mpos macrophages is MERTK-dependent, then (a) MERTK is a therapeutic target whose engagement on the protective subset preserves tissue-protective crosstalk, and (b) loss of MerTK signaling (genetic or inflammatory) is a mechanistic route by which the protective circuit fails. This directly informs whether MerTK-targeted or resolution-restoring strategies should preserve vs block MERTK on the Mpos subset — a consequential, currently unresolved therapeutic decision.

## Closest inspected work and the remaining question
- [K1] Sheridan et al., Arthritis Rheumatol 2026 (DOI 10.1002/art.70123): in an RASF–macrophage co-culture, inhibiting FLS amidation (PAM) skews macrophages toward proinflammatory **MerTK−** phenotypes and increases FLS invasiveness. Establishes that FLS–macrophage crosstalk sets MerTK state, but does NOT test whether MERTK activity on the protective macrophage is required to suppress the FLS inflammatory program.
- [K2] Huang et al., Immunity 2026 (DOI 10.1016/j.immuni.2026.01.019): patient-derived **SELENOPhi MerTK+ CD206+** macrophages persist in RA synovium yet acquire pro-arthritogenic (C1q/SARM1/PANoptotic) functions. Establishes the identity and plasticity of the protective subset, but not the direction of the macrophage→FLS protective signal nor its MERTK dependence.
- [K3] Wei & Xu, Front Immunol 2026 (DOI 10.3389/fimmu.2026.1935792): frames difficult-to-treat RA as "failed resolution" of a self-sustaining stromal–myeloid circuit; motivates resolution-restoring, crosstalk-directed therapy. Provides the therapeutic frame, not a test of MERTK dependence.

**Unresolved claim (this study):** whether MERTK activity on Mpos macrophages is *necessary* for suppression of the FLS proinflammatory program — i.e., whether blocking MERTK on the protective subset removes its FLS-suppressive effect. No inspected work performs this directional, target-specific test on the FLS readout.

## Data and design (bound to E-MTAB-8873)
Source: `E-MTAB-8873.counts.tsv.gz` (33 FLS pseudobulk samples × 445-gene targeted panel; features are `GENE|NM_..|end` rows, samples are columns) + `E-MTAB-8873.design.json` (sample→donor, condition, treatment, cell count). Registry: `assay-registry.json` (E-MTAB-8873, 33 samples, 445 features, `independent_donors_not_assumed=true`, `verified_single_donor_ids=[]`).

Design (from design.json): RA FLS (type B synovial cell) from donor clusters R1 (SA256A), R2 (SA257A/SA258R), R5 (SA263R/SA266A), co-cultured with MerTK/CD206+ (Mpos) or MerTK/CD206− (Mneg) macrophages; macrophages pre-treated with none / LPS / MerTK inhibitor. `FLS_only` = not co-cultured.

**Primary estimand (mechanistic):** paired, donor-matched contrast `log2(Mpos + MerTK-inhibitor) − log2(Mpos)` for the FLS inflammatory program (IL6, IL1B, TNF, CXCL10, CXCL9, CXCL11, IL1RN, SPP1, CCL2, CCL20, MMP13, MMP1, COL1A1, COL1A2, VEGFA). Available inhibitor pairs: R1a (SA259A Mpos), R1b (SA260R Mpos), R2 (SA257A/SA266A Mpos), R5 (SA266A/SA277A Mpos).

**Discriminating convergence test:** does `Mpos+Inhibitor` collapse toward the `Mneg` state? Compare `(Inhib − Mneg)` vs `(Mpos − Mneg)` per FLS-donor pair (R1 SA259A has all three arms; R2/R5 have Mpos/Mneg/Inhibitor where present). Convergence (Inhib−Mneg ≈ Mpos−Mneg, i.e., inhibitor removes the Mpos advantage) supports MERTK dependence; absence of convergence (Inhib ≈ Mpos, no shift toward Mneg) is adverse.

**Baseline (descriptive, for comparison):** paired `log2(Mpos) − log2(Mneg)` per FLS-donor pair, with `FLS_only` as the no-context anchor. This baseline establishes that the two macrophage states differ on the FLS readout but CANNOT attribute the difference to MERTK. The mechanistic contrast adds the causal read the baseline loses.

**Controls:** `FLS_only` (no co-culture) anchors the context effect; `preLPS` arms control for macrophage activation state; donor-matching controls for donor. Donors are reused across conditions → use paired/donor-aware contrasts, NOT an ordinary donor t-test (registry limit).

## Executed discovery probe (this episode)
Donor-aware paired log2 contrasts ([research job]; `work/lead/probe_8873_result.txt`):
- Mpos vs Mneg: **inconsistent** across 5 donor pairs (IL6 +0.70 R1 vs −3.20 R2a; CXCL10 −1.22 R1 vs +0.36 R5a) → donor-dependent, weak as a primary estimand.
- Mpos + MerTK-inhibitor vs Mpos: **consistent suppression** of FLS inflammatory genes across all 4 clusters (IL6, IL1B, TNF, CXCL10, IL1RN, SPP1 mostly down).
- Convergence (R1 SA259A): Inhib−Mneg more negative than Mpos−Mneg for IL1B/TNF/IL6/IL1RN/SPP1 → inhibitor pushes FLS toward/below the Mneg state.
- FLS_only anchor: co-culture strongly upregulates MERTK/MRC1/IL1RN/SPP1 (context effect present).
This grounds the leading claim and motivates the inhibitor contrast as the primary estimand. It is exploratory (small n, no multiple-testing correction); the full solver study applies the formal analysis below.

## Analysis (solver)
1. Load 8873 matrix + design; parse features to gene symbols (first `|` field); map samples to donor cluster + condition + treatment.
2. For each FLS-donor pair with Mpos & Mneg & (Inhibitor where present), compute paired log2 contrasts on the FLS inflammatory panel.
3. Primary: `Inhib − Mpos` per pair; aggregate across pairs with a sign-consistency / permutation test accounting for donor (paired permutation of the within-pair contrast, or a mixed model with donor as a random effect). Report per-gene effect, direction, and cluster-level consistency.
4. Convergence: per pair, `Δ = (Inhib − Mneg) − (Mpos − Mneg) = Inhib − Mpos`; test whether the Mpos advantage over Mneg is abolished (Δ ≈ 0 for the protective genes) vs retained.
5. Baseline: `Mpos − Mneg` per pair for comparison.
6. Multiple-testing correction (BH) across the panel; sensitivity analysis excluding the high-cell-count R2/SA258R samples and the `preLPS` arms.

## Falsification / outcome interpretation
- **Supportive:** Mpos+Inhibitor suppresses the FLS inflammatory program relative to Mpos AND converges toward the Mneg state (Mpos advantage over Mneg abolished) across ≥3/4 donor clusters, with BH-significant effects. → MERTK activity on Mpos macrophages is required for FLS suppression; MERTK is a viable therapeutic target to preserve on the protective subset.
- **Adverse:** Mpos+Inhibitor does NOT shift the FLS program toward Mneg (Inhib ≈ Mpos; no convergence), or the effect is confined to one cluster. → MERTK is not the mediator of the protective context; the protective signal is MERTK-independent (e.g., ligand-independent or via another receptor), redirecting target selection away from MERTK.
- **Inconclusive:** effects are small, inconsistent across clusters, or underpowered (n=4–5 pairs). → report honestly; the design is a screening-level check, not a confirmatory study. Additional donors/replicates or a wet-lab MerTK-knockdown co-culture would be needed.

## What this does and does not establish
- Establishes (if supportive): a *drug-associated, target-specific* association that MERTK blockade on Mpos macrophages removes the FLS-suppressive context. It is an in-vitro, targeted-panel, small-n screening result.
- Does NOT establish: direct MERTK target engagement (the inhibitor is a drug; off-target effects possible), in-vivo relevance, or clinical efficacy. Mpos/Mneg identity is by surface marker (MerTK/CD206), not transcript-verified in the FLS. Confirmatory claims require wet-lab MerTK loss-of-function, additional donors, and clinical adjudication.

## Alternatives / competing explanations
- LPS activation state of macrophages (preLPS arms) could drive part of the FLS response → controlled by using non-LPS arms and sensitivity analysis.
- Donor biology / co-culture density differences → controlled by donor-matched paired contrasts; R2/SA258R (high cell count) excluded in sensitivity analysis.
- Inhibitor off-target effects on FLS directly (not via macrophages) → the FLS_only+Inhibitor arm (R1 SA256A FLS_Inhibitor) tests direct FLS effect; if FLS alone responds to inhibitor, the macrophage-mediated claim weakens.
- Mpos vs Mneg baseline differences confound the state contrast → the inhibitor contrast (same Mpos macrophages, ± inhibitor) is the cleaner causal read.

## Deliverable (what the solver must produce)
Per-gene paired contrasts for the three arms (Mpos−Mneg, Inhib−Mpos, convergence Δ) across all available FLS-donor pairs; donor-aware permutation/mixed-model p-values; BH-corrected significance; a cluster-consistency summary; and a stated supportive/adverse/inconclusive verdict with the numeric evidence. No model training required; CPU-only.
