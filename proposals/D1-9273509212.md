# A real-time landmark audit of live ICU-discharge transition risk

## Scientific deliverable

The future Harbor solver must newly produce a data-audited prognostic study of the first observed live ICU exit. Completion requires:

1. an all-eligible cohort flow and exact first-live-exit index audit;
2. a one-hour pre-exit landmark matrix whose primary features were available by both clinical time and recording time;
3. first-event competing-risk labels through 48 hours after ICU exit, including an explicit unknown category for untimed deaths;
4. paired held-out predictions from a latest-state/context baseline (B0), matched order-blind history model (B1), process-only model (Bmask), and chronological masked GRU;
5. an outcome-free ICU-hour live-exit score q used for overlap and selection-stratum audits, not misdescribed as causal inverse-selection correction;
6. chart-time-only versus available-by-landmark sensitivity results, chronology and observation-process falsifications, subject-bootstrap uncertainty, and fixed 5/10/20% review operating tables; and
7. a conclusion ledger tying every claim to computed output and separating prognostic operating characteristics from discharge appropriateness, preventability, intervention effect, net benefit and transportability.

No fitted result is claimed here. A favorable score, readiness check, or hypothesis-confirming result is not completion.

This is a substantive child of `[prior hypothesis]`. It preserves the parent’s MIMIC-IV competing-risk question and fair baseline-versus-chronological comparison while tightening the landmark and selection estimands. The parent’s inverse-selection weighting is not promoted to the primary analysis because post-exit outcomes are not observed for ICU hours at which no exit occurred.

## Unresolved question and clinical importance

The strongest claim supported by existing evidence and the parent design is only that late ICU measurements and their observation pattern may predict a subsequent recorded ICU readmission or in-hospital death among patients who leave the ICU alive. Even a reproducible history increment does not show that the measurements caused the event, that discharge was premature, or that review would improve care.

The unresolved question is narrower and clinically interpretable:

> Among patients at their first observed live ICU exit, does a twelve-hour pre-exit trajectory, measured using information actually available by a landmark one hour before exit, improve prediction of the first 48-hour post-exit transition to ICU readmission or timed in-hospital death beyond the latest available state, admission/ICU context, and observation intensity?

This matters because a post-ICU review queue would need to operate before the transition boundary, and a retrospective chart entry made after the clinical measurement but near the exit cannot be assumed available to a bedside reviewer. The advance over the parent is an auditable operating-characteristic estimand that does not claim to reconstruct outcomes under an unobserved discharge policy. It directly separates physiologic history from documentation intensity and tests whether conclusions survive a realistic availability rule.

Prespecified hypothesis: B1 will improve held-out calibration and transition-aware prediction of A=R-or-D over B0, and the improvement will remain directionally present in q-overlap strata and under the available-by-landmark feature rule. A physiology-specific interpretation additionally requires B1 to outperform Bmask. A chronology interpretation additionally requires GRU to outperform B1 and for that increment to attenuate when preterminal bins are permuted. If improvements are confined to q strata, S, or the chart-only matrix, the primary hypothesis is adverse or unresolved rather than supported.

## Population, index and estimand

Use one index per admission: the first ICU stay in `icu/icustays` with a valid `intime<outtime`, observed live ICU exit, and at least one hour between the landmark and `outtime`. Join `hosp/admissions` on `(subject_id,hadm_id)` and `hosp/patients` on `subject_id`. Exclude reversed/missing intervals and non-live or untimed endpoint cases from the timed primary estimand, but retain an all-eligible flow and report exclusions. Do not use `dod`.

For index exit time t0=`outtime`, define the prediction landmark tL=t0-1 hour. The 12-hour feature window is [tL-12h,tL]. No prediction feature may use t0, `outtime`, `los`, `last_careunit`, `discharge_location`, `dischtime`, `deathtime`, `hospital_expire_flag`, a future transfer, or any row after tL. The one-hour buffer is part of the estimand, not an optional post hoc exclusion.

The primary population is therefore “first observed live ICU exits with an available-by-landmark feature record,” and the estimand is predictive, not causal: held-out risk discrimination/calibration and fixed-workload event capture for the observed-exit population. Report the all-eligible and coverage-restricted flows separately so missing feature coverage is not mistaken for biologic absence.

Define first transitions in (t0,t0+48h]:

- R: a different `stay_id` in the same `hadm_id`, with later `intime>t0` and `intime<=t0+48h`;
- D: `admissions.deathtime` in the interval, ordered against R when both are possible;
- S: alive hospital discharge at `admissions.dischtime` before R or D, an observation-boundary competing transition rather than a safe outcome;
- N: no R, D or S by 48 hours while the admission remains ascertainable;
- U: `hospital_expire_flag=1` with missing `deathtime`, or another prespecified unresolved timed-death case.

Primary outputs are the 48-hour cumulative incidence for R and D, their composite A=R-or-D, and transition-aware Brier/calibration metrics with S competing and U excluded from the timed primary analysis. Report complete-ascertainment and adverse/non-adverse U bounds. A fixed-horizon composite and a 7-day sensitivity are secondary; a gain confined to S or N is not evidence for adverse residual physiology.

This estimand describes risk among observed live exits. It does not estimate risk under “discharge now” for every ICU patient, the effect of discharge, or the appropriateness of the observed exit.

## Observation-process control

Construct an available-by-landmark matrix using both clinical and recording timestamps. For chartevents/labevents/outputevents, require the event’s clinical time and `storetime` <= tL. For inputevents, require `storetime<=tL` and `starttime<=tL`; derive cumulative started-therapy and valid pre-landmark duration without using an endtime after tL. For transfers, use only the latest record with `intime<=tL`; no future `outtime` is used. Preserve missingness, counts, time since last observation, test-order/measurement indicators, Foley charting, and vasoactive start/presence indicators as process features; missing output is not zero.

Fit the same models on a chart-time-only sensitivity (`charttime<=tL` or `starttime<=tL`) while reporting the fraction and identities of rows newly admitted by that rule. Divergence in B1/B0 increment, calibration, or q-stratum capture is adverse to real-time availability. This is an observation audit, not proof of clinical irrelevance or of why a measurement was recorded.

## Models and fair comparison

Freeze a deterministic subject-level 70/15/15 train/validation/test split, approximately stratified by endpoint and anchor-year group; no subject crosses partitions. Fit imputation, scaling, clipping, feature selection, calibration and review thresholds on training/validation only.

B0 is the transparent baseline: penalized discrete-time cause-specific hazards for R, D and S. Inputs are anchor age/gender/year group; admission type/location; first/current care unit when available; ICU elapsed duration at tL; earlier ICU-stay count; observation summaries; and terminal-bin values/masks/process features from the allowed primary matrix.

B1 is the substantive order-blind alternative: exactly B0’s context and terminal inputs plus all 12 bins’ means, medians, minima/maxima, SD/IQR, abnormal-bin fractions, valid-bin counts, measurement counts/missingness, test/output indicators, and pressor start/presence summaries. It tests whether history adds information without attributing gain to chronology.

Bmask is B0 plus the complete observation mask/count/testing/Foley/vasoactive process history with physiologic values removed. If Bmask approaches B1, the apparent history gain is plausibly observation intensity.

The chronological alternative is a small masked GRU over the identical 12 bins, with the same numeric domains, masks, abnormal flags, observed-domain count, process indicators, context, outcome, split and primary availability rule. Hidden size 32 or 64, five fixed seeds, validation-only early stopping/dropout selection. It can reveal persistence, recovery, reversal, fluctuation and coupled order that B1’s summaries lose. GRU versus B1 is secondary; complexity or GPU use is not a merit criterion.

## Exit selection audit q

Build a pooled ICU-stay-hour frame from every valid `icu/icustays` row, with candidate landmarks h from 12 hours after `intime` through the last complete one-hour interval before the observed end. A positive is live ICU exit in (h,h+1h]. Use only features available by h under the same clinical/recording-time rule. Exclude `outtime,los,last_careunit,discharge_location,dischtime,deathtime,hospital_expire_flag`, future transfers and post-h rows from q features. Candidate death/untimed-death/administrative-boundary rows are not live-exit positives; retain and report their counts.

Fit q only in training subjects as a pooled logistic one-hour exit hazard; preserve any sampling fraction as an offset/weight. Calibrate on validation, evaluate on held-out risk-set hours, and assign q to each observed exit at its one-hour pre-exit landmark. Report q calibration, quantiles, positivity, the number of exits outside prespecified q-overlap regions, and effective counts. Use q deciles/overlap bands as descriptive selection strata and repeat all primary metrics there. Do not inverse-weight or call q a recovered discharge-policy counterfactual: outcomes after non-exit candidate hours are not defined under the observed data. A future study with adjudicated counterfactual outcomes would be required for that claim.

## Outcomes, uncertainty and operating characteristics

Report held-out transition-aware Brier scores for R, D, S, N and A; 12/24/48-hour cumulative-incidence calibration, calibration intercept/slope, AUROC/AUPRC for A and component causes, paired B1-B0 risk and metric differences, and subject-bootstrap 95% intervals. Report complete-ascertainment and U-bound sensitivities. Bootstrap at subject level; refit preprocessing, q and thresholds inside each replicate where feasible.

Choose thresholds only in training/validation to flag exactly 5%, 10% and 20% of observed live exits. Test-set tables report achieved workload, R/D capture, PPV, calibration in flagged/unflagged groups, component-event capture, q-stratum performance, care-unit performance and anchor-year-group performance for B0, B1 and GRU. These are candidate review operating characteristics, not clinical utility, workload harm, net benefit, or evidence that a review causes a different outcome.

## Falsification and interpretation

Required checks are:

1. row-level provenance proves no feature has clinical or recording time after tL; interval clipping, duplicate/malformed joins and split integrity are audited;
2. q live-exit labels are permuted within prespecified risk-set strata and refit; q discrimination and q-stratum structure should collapse;
3. preterminal bins 1–11 are shuffled within held-out subjects while preserving terminal bin, masks, context and labels; a chronology claim should attenuate;
4. Bmask is compared with B1; convergence is adverse to a physiology-specific interpretation;
5. chart-only versus available-by-landmark results are compared; a large primary-to-sensitivity change is adverse to real-time availability;
6. valid-bin thresholds 4/6/8, complete/all-eligible ascertainment, U bounds, fixed-horizon and 7-day sensitivities are reported;
7. transition labels are permuted within partitions with a fixed seed; persistent improvement indicates leakage or evaluation failure;
8. q-overlap bands, first/last care-unit and anchor-year-group stability are reported; sparse strata and wide intervals are inconclusive.

Support requires an incremental B1 or GRU improvement over B0 in held-out A=R-or-D calibration/Brier with uncertainty excluding a prespecified null, no material loss under available-by-landmark data, no dependence on S/N alone, and adequate q overlap. Physiology-specific support additionally requires B1>Bmask; chronology support additionally requires GRU>B1 plus attenuation under bin shuffle. Adverse evidence includes Bmask≈B1, chart-only-only gains, q-stratum instability, q label-permutation failure, label-shuffle resistance, poor coverage, or large calibration degradation. Inconclusive evidence includes low R/D counts, high U, poor overlap, sparse valid bins, unstable calibration, or intervals too wide to distinguish these alternatives.

Computationally checkable claims include source/member/schema/item provenance, joins, timing, availability rule, cohort flow, labels/U handling, split integrity, model inputs, q construction, held-out predictions, calibration/Brier/CIF, bootstrap intervals, fixed-workload tables and falsifications. Clinician discharge intent, appropriateness, goals of care, preventability, staffing/bed pressure, why measurements were ordered or omitted, whether charted oxygen equals delivered physiology, ward surveillance, intervention effects, net benefit, causal discharge effects and external validity are unavailable or non-identifiable here. They require clinical adjudication, stronger ascertainment, external validation, and preferably prospective/interventional study.

## Alternatives and resources

The actual scientific deliverable is the landmark-audited observed-exit estimand, newly fitted B0/B1/Bmask/GRU predictions, q selection strata, availability sensitivity, fixed-workload tables, uncertainty, falsifications and claim ledger.

B0 is selected as the transparent baseline. B1 is the primary substantive alternative because it holds context and terminal state constant while testing whether late history adds information. Bmask is necessary to distinguish physiology from observation process. The GRU is retained as a scientifically substantive secondary alternative because order may encode recovery/reversal lost by aggregates. A transformer is deferred: twelve bins and a small GRU directly test the scientific chronology question; revisit only if GRU>B1 remains robust with adequate event support and a clinically justified longer dependence. A causal discharge-policy model is deferred because the data lack discharge intent, counterfactual post-exit outcomes for non-exits, ward monitoring, intervention and preventability. Weighting is not a primary alternative for the same reason; revisit only with those missing outcomes and a defensible identification strategy.

Discovery allowance is 7,200 seconds. Inputs specify a future solver envelope of 16 CPUs, 262,144 MiB memory, up to 8 GPUs and 28,800 seconds. CPU archive aggregation, B0/B1/Bmask and q are expected feasible but unmeasured; the small GRU is expected CPU-feasible but unverified. An allocated A100-SXM4-80GB may accelerate repeated GRU seeds/bootstrap if profiling requires it; ordinary-shell CUDA visibility is irrelevant. Pooled-hour construction and bootstrap duration are unmeasured. No model was fitted in discovery.
