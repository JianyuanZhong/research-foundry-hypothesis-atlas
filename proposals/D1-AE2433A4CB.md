# Prespecified coverage-gated test of lactate-by-perfusion effect modification at live ICU exit

## Scientific deliverable

The future Harbor solver must newly construct a leakage-controlled, competing-risk prognostic study and determine whether the 48-hour prognostic association of persistent hyperlactatemia differs by the direction of measured perfusion immediately before an observed live ICU exit. It must also determine whether that interaction remains interpretable after an outcome-independent audit of phenotype coverage and the measurement process.

Completion requires:

1. a flow from all eligible first live ICU exits through each technical coverage requirement;
2. a frozen primary interaction estimand on the strict phenotype-complete population;
3. a separate event-support gate, with sparse-cell outcomes reported as inconclusive rather than repaired after looking at outcomes;
4. exact source/member/item/unit/time provenance and counts for missingness, invalidity, duplicate handling, test intensity, and delayed storage;
5. matched held-out predictions from B0, B1, Bmask, Bdisc and learned M2 sensitivity;
6. competing-risk cumulative-incidence estimates, uncertainty, fixed falsification analyses, and a conclusion ledger; and
7. explicit separation of computationally checkable findings from clinical claims requiring adjudication or another study.

The solver must newly fit the models, estimate the interaction and competing-risk contrasts, generate coverage and measurement-process audit outputs, quantify uncertainty, and run the stated falsifications. Readiness or successful fitting is not a clinical result. This proposal claims no fitted result.

## Unresolved question and strongest supported evidence

The inspected expert seed proposes that persistently elevated lactate may have different meaning when measured perfusion is improving versus worsening. The configured MIMIC-IV v3.1 source and dictionaries verify that repeated lactate, MAP, vasoactive-support intervals, urine output, organ-marker labs, ICU stays, admissions, demographics, transfers and dual clinical/recording timestamps are available in the named tables. The database documentation and local metadata do not establish that these measures diagnose microcirculatory dysfunction, that repeated testing is nonselective, or that the strict phenotype is prevalent or event-supported. No fitted evidence was inspected or generated here.

The unresolved, falsifiable claim is:

> Among first eligible live ICU exits with repeated pre-landmark lactate and paired hemodynamic measurements, is the excess 48-hour risk associated with persistent hyperlactatemia smaller when measured perfusion is improving than when it is worsening?

The directional hypothesis is a negative lactate-by-perfusion interaction. The primary 48-hour estimand is:

`[CIF(R/D | persistent, improving) - CIF(R/D | nonpersistent, improving)] - [CIF(R/D | persistent, worsening) - CIF(R/D | nonpersistent, worsening)]`.

The clinically meaningful null region is -2 to +2 percentage points. Report the point estimate and 95% interval; an estimate inside that region is not primary support. The null region is a decision-relevance convention for this study, not a universal utility threshold.

The advance over a lactate-only or generic-history analysis is a prespecified effect-modification test with an auditable, strict two-domain perfusion phenotype, an explicit indeterminate category, and a measurement-process/event-support gate. A supported interaction would show that the prognostic interpretation of a lactate threshold depends on concurrent measured hemodynamic trajectory. A null result would argue against this distinction for retrospective risk stratification. Neither result identifies lactate etiology, diagnoses microcirculatory dysfunction, or recommends fluids or discharge policy.

## Population, landmark, and temporal boundaries

Use one index per `hadm_id`: the first `icu/icustays` row with valid `intime < outtime`, `t0 = outtime`, and at least 13 hours between `intime` and `t0`. Require an admissions join on `(subject_id, hadm_id)`. Exclude invalid/reversed intervals, missing admission links, and exits with `admissions.deathtime <= t0`; count these failures. Rows with `hospital_expire_flag=1` and missing `deathtime` remain an unresolved ascertainment category; exclude them from the timed primary estimand unless an adverse bound is supplied.

Set `tL=t0-1 hour`. The primary feature window is `[tL-12h,tL]`, split into early `[tL-12h,tL-6h]` and late `(tL-6h,tL]`. A primary measurement is available only when both clinical and recording timestamps precede the landmark:

- `chartevents`, `outputevents`, and `labevents`: `charttime <= tL` and `storetime <= tL`;
- `inputevents`: valid `starttime <= tL`, valid interval, `storetime <= tL), and interval contribution clipped at `tL`; no future `endtime` information;
- no feature uses `outtime`, `los`, `last_careunit`, future transfers, endpoint fields, notes, or any row after `tL`.

A chart-time-only availability analysis is a fixed sensitivity, not a replacement. Missingness stays missing and is represented by prespecified masks/counts; it is not imputed to normal or zero.

## Outcome-independent phenotype coverage diagnostic

Run and freeze this diagnostic before attaching any post-`t0` outcome. Its denominators and required outputs are recorded in `coverage-gate-lactate-perfusion.md`.

D0 is the eligible live-exit landmark denominator after index validity, linkage, timing and known-death-at-`t0` checks. D1-lactate requires at least one compatible, numeric lactate in each half. D1-MAP requires a valid MAP summary in each half. D1-pressor requires a valid vasoactive support summary in each half. Dstrict requires all three D1 requirements, before looking at post-`t0` R/D/S/N/U labels.

The diagnostic must report counts and proportions at every stage; failure reasons; raw-row and unique-time counts; duplicate-resolution counts; invalid/non-numeric values; incompatible units; clinical-to-store lag; late-store exclusions; half-specific gaps; and fixed pre-landmark strata by anchor-year group, current unit from a transfer interval covering `tL`, and measurement intensity. These strata are descriptive and cannot be selected using outcomes.

The primary strict phenotype is deliberately not selected by outcome:

- persistent lactate: early and late median lactate >=2.0 mmol/L;
- nonpersistent lactate: the complementary status among complete lactate trajectories;
- improving perfusion: late minus early median MAP >=5 mmHg and late pressor-support minutes <= early pressor-support minutes;
- worsening perfusion: late minus early median MAP <=-5 mmHg and late pressor-support minutes >= early pressor-support minutes;
- all other perfusion trajectories: indeterminate, never forced into either primary stratum.

Within Dstrict, report the four primary cells (persistent/nonpersistent × improving/worsening), the indeterminate fraction, continuous changes, and measurement intensity before interpreting outcomes. The fixed 4.0 mmol/L lactate threshold is a sensitivity. MAP-only, arterial-MAP-only, pressor-only, continuous standardized changes, normalized Foley output as secondary corroboration, and 4/6/8-bin availability requirements are fixed sensitivities. No definition is chosen after seeing held-out outcomes.

Coverage diagnostics are feasibility evidence, not clinical evidence. A small strict fraction, high indeterminate fraction, large delayed-store fraction, poor positivity across measurement intensity, or strong dependence on testing frequency is a measurement-process threat that must be reported.

## Event-support gate and prespecified sparse-phenotype resolution

Only after Dstrict and all phenotype labels are frozen, attach the mutually exclusive first transitions in `(t0,t0+48h]`:

- R: another `icu/icustays` row for the same `hadm_id`, different `stay_id`, with `intime > t0` and `intime <= t0+48h`;
- D: `hosp/admissions.deathtime` in the interval;
- S: alive `hosp/admissions.dischtime` in the interval before R or D, an ascertainment-boundary transition rather than a safe outcome;
- N: no R, D or S while the admission remains ascertainable through 48 hours;
- U: unresolved timed-death ascertainment, including hospital-expire rows with missing death time.

At equal timestamps, D precedes R; S is assigned only while alive and before the other event. Report R, D, S, R/D and N with cause-specific hazards and cumulative incidence. The primary analysis treats R/D with S competing; R and D separately are secondary. Provide 7-day and complete-ascertainment sensitivities and adverse/non-adverse U bounds.

For each primary phenotype cell report n, R, D, R/D, S, N and U. A cell is event-supported only if n>=30 and R/D events>=10. An interaction interval wider than six percentage points is inconclusive. If any strict cell fails this gate, retain the strict interaction as the primary prespecified result but classify it as sparse/inconclusive. Do not merge cells, relax cutoffs, replace strict labels with a learned cluster, or choose a more favorable endpoint after seeing outcomes.

The fixed resolution is to publish the full coverage/event table, then run the declared MAP-only, pressor-only, continuous, cutoff, bin-availability and timestamp sensitivities with the same estimand family and split. These sensitivities can show whether sparsity is specific to strict concordance or whether the measurement process dominates. They cannot turn a sparse strict analysis into confirmatory evidence. If no prespecified definition is event-supported and sufficiently covered, the appropriate next study requires richer perfusion, treatment-intent, fluid-responsiveness, or adjudicated microcirculatory data.

## Transparent baseline and substantive learned alternative

Freeze a deterministic participant-level 70/15/15 split before any outcome-based thresholding, stratified only by endpoint and anchor-year group; no subject crosses partitions. Every model uses Dstrict and the same split. Fit scaling, imputation, calibration, mixture assignments, q-overlap summaries and thresholds using training/validation only.

B0 is a penalized discrete-time cause-specific complementary-log-log model for R, D and S using admission/demographic/current-ICU context, terminal-bin physiology, terminal missingness and observation-process variables. It is the transparent latest-state baseline.

B1 adds order-blind summaries in twelve one-hour bins across the same physiology, lactate, organ-marker, output, pressor-presence and observation-process fields. It asks whether the primary interaction adds information beyond generic late history and measurement intensity.

Bmask retains context, counts, masks, test indicators, Foley charting and pressor-presence history with physiologic values removed. If Bdisc does not exceed Bmask, a physiology-specific interpretation fails.

Bdisc is the transparent, clinically substantive alternative: B1 plus persistent lactate status, strict improving/indeterminate/worsening perfusion, early/late lactate, MAP/pressor changes and their interaction, organ markers and separate availability indicators. It can reveal an interpretable cross-domain interaction that B0’s terminal state and B1’s order-blind summaries lose; it does not establish causality.

M2 is a training-only two-component diagonal-covariance Gaussian mixture over standardized early-to-late changes in lactate, MAP, pressor minutes and secondarily output/organ markers. Pass posterior probabilities into the same cause-specific model. Label a component improving/worsening only if training means meet the fixed direction rules; otherwise leave it unlabeled. M2 could reveal nonlinear coupled trajectories and heterogeneity lost by hand thresholds, but is sensitivity only because selective testing may destabilize mixture structure and because a learned cluster is not a clinical perfusion label.

The parent’s chronological masked GRU is deferred because chronology versus order-blind history is already tested by B1, while this successor’s uncertainty is strict cross-domain phenotype coverage and process selection. A causal fluid-response model is deferred because required intent, responsiveness, confounding and counterfactual evidence are unavailable. These are evidence-bound deferrals, not bans on neural networks, GPUs or structured data.

The coverage diagnostic and small tabular fits are expected to be CPU-feasible but were not benchmarked. The future solver envelope remains 16 CPUs, 262,144 MiB, up to 8 GPUs and 28,800 seconds. If repeated fitting materially benefits from acceleration, request an allocated A100 and explicitly use `cuda:0`; an ordinary shell has no CUDA allocation and cannot establish GPU absence. No discovery fitting or proposer/solver training was run.

## Outcomes, analysis, uncertainty, and falsification

Report held-out 48-hour CIF and transition-aware Brier scores for R, D, S, R/D and N; calibration intercept/slope; paired Bdisc-B1 and B1-B0 differences; the primary interaction and fixed sensitivity interactions; fixed 5%, 10% and 20% predicted-risk operating tables; and subject-bootstrap 95% intervals. Threshold tables report reviewed count, events captured, sensitivity, PPV and false negatives. They are operating characteristics, not net benefit, staffing impact, patient preference, or actionability.

Required audits and falsifications are:

1. verify dictionary labels/linkages/units/ranges, dual timestamps, interval clipping, joins, duplicates, first-exit selection, prohibited fields and row-level provenance;
2. permute outcomes within partitions; held-out gains should disappear;
3. permute early/late assignments within subject while preserving counts and availability; a trajectory interaction should attenuate;
4. compare Bdisc with Bmask and a process-only phenotype model; similar gains without physiological values support documentation/selection rather than physiology;
5. compare dual-time availability with chart-time-only availability; gains requiring later storage are adverse for real-time interpretation;
6. repeat fixed MAP/pressor/output/cutoff/bin/timing definitions and U bounds without choosing after test outcomes;
7. report R, D and S separately; a gain confined to S does not support adverse physiology;
8. fit the outcome-free six-hour live-exit negative-control model; a much stronger process signal than R/D supports selection explanation;
9. audit measurement-intensity overlap/ESS, current-unit and anchor-year strata, phenotype prevalence, indeterminate fraction, delayed-store fraction and held-out calibration.

Supportive evidence requires event-supported cells, adequate technical coverage, an interaction below -2 points with its 95% interval entirely below the null region, lower persistent-lactate R/D CIF in improving than worsening perfusion, calibrated Bdisc improvement over B1, Bdisc exceeding Bmask, and stability across the fixed measurement/process sensitivities. M2 may corroborate but cannot validate a proxy alone.

Adverse evidence includes a null or reversed interaction, Bdisc matching B1 or Bmask, process-only or chart-time-only explanation, instability under definitions or measurement intensity, gains confined to S/N, or outcome-permutation resistance. The result is inconclusive when any strict cell is sparse, the indeterminate or U fraction is high, units are incompatible, overlap/ESS is poor, calibration is unstable, or the interaction interval is wider than six percentage points.

Computationally checkable claims include source provenance, flow counts, timing, units, joins, phenotype coverage, event support, split integrity, fitted outputs, uncertainty, calibration, falsifications and the claim ledger. Clinical adjudication or another study is required for microcirculatory dysfunction, lactate etiology, fluid responsiveness, clinician intent, treatment effect, preventability or appropriateness of ICU discharge, monitoring benefit, net utility, staffing, external transportability and bedside recommendations. The study cannot recommend fluids or a discharge policy.
