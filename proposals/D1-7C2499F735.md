# Is the PIVKA-II signal clinicians act on in HCC partly an iatrogenic vitamin-K artefact?

**Dataset island:** HCC (single-centre Chinese tertiary liver-cancer cohort)

## 1. The clinical setting and the opening

PIVKA-II, also called des-gamma-carboxy prothrombin (DCP; Chinese lab name 异常凝血酶原), is not
a research assay in this cohort. It is ordered 152,951 times in 36,289 patients (OBSERVED), and it
is a guideline-endorsed tumour marker for hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) diagnosis, post-treatment
surveillance and response assessment. Clinicians act on it: a rise triggers imaging, a diagnosis of
recurrence, or a change of therapy; an early post-operative fall to normal is read as evidence that
the resection was complete.

DCP is not a tumour antigen. It is prothrombin that failed hepatic vitamin-K-dependent
gamma-carboxylation. HCC cells produce it because their carboxylation machinery is defective — but
so does *any* perturbation of the vitamin K cycle, in anyone. This is textbook biochemistry and is
acknowledged as a caveat in reviews. What is not established is the thing that matters clinically:
**how often, and by how much, routine inpatient care moves a patient's DCP across the thresholds on
which decisions are actually made.**

The opening is a concrete and surprising observation in this cohort's own prescribing record, not a
conjecture about biology:

- **OBSERVED.** 40,344 vitamin K1 administration records in 15,767 patients. **10,362 patients
  received vitamin K1 within −3 to +7 days of a liver resection or ablation** — i.e. in precisely
  the curative-intent window where early DCP normalisation is used as evidence of complete tumour
  clearance.
- **OBSERVED.** The hospital's perioperative vitamin K1 practice changed abruptly. Share of liver
  resection/ablation encounters with peri-procedural vitamin K1, by year of procedure:

  | 2013 | 2014 | 2015 | 2016 | 2017 | 2018 | 2019 | 2020 | 2021 | 2022 | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | 0.021 | 0.010 | 0.025 | **0.650** | 0.652 | 0.542 | 0.464 | 0.356 | 0.408 | 0.404 | 0.458 | 0.372 | 0.247 |

  (denominators 1,335–4,651 encounters/yr). A ~62 percentage-point jump between 2015 and 2016, then
  a slow decay. Use is also strongly department-dependent: 肝胆肿瘤与肝移植外科 0.421 and 肝肿瘤外科
  0.473 versus 介入手术室 0.027 and 肝肿瘤内科 0.071.
- **OBSERVED.** The hospital also uses, at scale, cephalosporins whose N-methyl-thio-tetrazole
  (NMTT) side chain inhibits vitamin K epoxide reductase: cefoperazone/sulbactam (7,929 records),
  latamoxef (4,809), cefamandole (4,568) — alongside non-NMTT cephalosporins that share the
  infection indication but not the vitamin-K mechanism: cefuroxime (13,935), ceftizoxime (11,221).

So the same cohort contains, as routine care, both a **DCP-suppressing** exposure concentrated in
the curative-intent window and a **DCP-elevating** exposure with a mechanism-matched active
comparator. That is an unusual opportunity to measure the iatrogenic component of a marker directly
rather than assume it away.

## 2. What existing evidence already supports, and what it does not

**Strongest claim the existing evidence supports.** DCP/PIVKA-II is a guideline-endorsed HCC marker
with purpose-specific cutoffs — the JSH 2021 consensus recommends 40 mAU/mL and states its use for
"HCC surveillance, diagnosis, evaluation of treatment response, and follow-up for recurrence",
giving **no** drug-exposure caveat and no correction method [K1]. Separately, non-tumour causes of
PIVKA-II elevation are established: in patients *without* HCC, PIVKA-II is significantly raised in
obstructive jaundice and sepsis, correlates with PT and INR, and does not differ in concentration
from HCC [K3]. The NMTT/vitamin-K-epoxide-reductase mechanism of certain cephalosporins is also
established pharmacology [K2]. **"PIVKA-II has non-tumour determinants" is therefore NOT this
proposal's contribution** and must not be presented as such.

**What the closest prior work does and does not settle.** [K2] is the nearest design to H1: a
propensity-score-weighted EHR cohort running exactly the cephalosporin side-chain contrast, and it
reports a **null** for PT-defined coagulopathy with cefoperazone-sulbactam (OR 0.88, 95% CI
0.67–1.15), with only an INR > 1.2 excess. Our own clean-subset null (§2b(ii)) converges with it.
Two gaps remain. First, no inspected study uses DCP/PIVKA-II as the *outcome* of these exposures, so
the tumour-marker misclassification question is untested even where the coagulation question is
answered. Second, and decisively for this proposal, **[K2] excludes "receipt of vitamin K within
three days before or after the index date" by design** — it removes precisely the exposure that H2
is about. [K3] likewise records no drug exposure at all and is cross-sectional with no surveillance
outcome. Targeted searches for vitamin K administration with PIVKA-II as the outcome returned no
relevant studies; that search was **not exhaustive** (Chinese-language and perioperative-anaesthesia
literature were not systematically covered), so this is a statement about what we inspected, not a
proof of novelty.

**What is not established, and is the target here:**
1. The *magnitude and duration* of the iatrogenic DCP excursion in actual treated HCC patients, net
   of the clinical state that prompted the drug.
2. Whether that excursion is large enough to cross the decision thresholds in use (commonly 40
   mAU/mL; relative-change rules for "progression").
3. **The masking direction, which to my knowledge is essentially unstudied:** whether routine
   peri-operative vitamin K1 lowers DCP enough that *early post-operative DCP normalisation loses
   its meaning as evidence of complete resection*, and whether DCP-based surveillance is
   systematically desensitised in exposed patients.

Point 3 is the consequential one. A false alarm costs an extra scan. A false reassurance costs a
missed residual or early recurrent tumour in a patient who has just had potentially curative
surgery.

## 2b. OBSERVED: bounded diagnostics run during this episode

Two diagnostics were executed today on the read-only sources. They are **feasibility/mechanism
probes, not the proposed study**, and they changed the proposal.

### (i) Lead diagnostic — resection-only cohort, the decisive rival controlled

The obvious rival to any "vitamin K1 lowers DCP" finding is **differential marker kinetics**: DCP's
half-life (~40–72 h) is shorter than AFP's (~5–7 d), so after tumour removal DCP falls faster than
AFP for purely pharmacokinetic reasons, and vitamin K1 is given mostly around surgery. I therefore
restricted to patients who **all** had a liver resection (first resection per patient; pre-op DCP
and AFP in −30..−1 d; post-op DCP and AFP in +3..+10 d; n = **8,705**) and compared
vitamin-K1-exposed with unexposed *within* that cohort, using the within-patient
difference-in-differences Δlog DCP − Δlog AFP:

| vitamin K1 doses in encounter | n | median Δlog DCP | median Δlog AFP | median (Δlog DCP − Δlog AFP) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 (unexposed) | 4,667 | −0.400 | −0.645 | **+0.205** |
| 1–2 | 2,959 | −0.823 | −0.735 | −0.104 |
| 3–5 | 1,011 | −1.273 | −0.903 | **−0.393** |
| ≥6 | 68 | −1.851 | −0.936 | **−0.749** |

Exposed vs unexposed difference in the DID: **−0.378 (bootstrap 95% CI −0.421 to −0.337)**,
Mann–Whitney p ≈ 9 × 10⁻¹³⁸. On the ratio scale, vitamin K1 exposure is associated with an
**additional ~31% fall in post-operative DCP beyond what the patient's own AFP trajectory
explains** (exp(−0.378) = 0.685), with a **monotone dose-response** across 0 → 1–2 → 3–5 → ≥6 doses.
Both arms underwent resection, so tumour removal and DCP's shorter half-life are held constant;
AFP moves only modestly between arms (−0.645 vs −0.773) while DCP moves by more than twice that.

**Two leading rivals tested and surviving.** (a) *Regression to the mean*: exposed patients do start
higher (median pre-op DCP 210.5 vs 114.0 mAU/mL; AFP 36.0 vs 15.2), so the fall could be mean
reversion. Stratifying on baseline-DCP decile, the exposed−unexposed DID gap persists in **all ten
deciles** (−0.305, −0.344, −0.266, −0.266, −0.269, −0.166, −0.330, −0.294, −0.330, −0.222; median
−0.282), i.e. it is flat across the entire baseline range rather than concentrated where reversion
would act. (b) *Resection extent*: the gap holds separately in major resections (−0.300 vs +0.093,
n = 1,125/1,097) and minor resections (−0.121 vs +0.238, n = 2,913/3,570). Neither rival explains
the signal, though neither is fully excluded by stratification alone.

### (ii) Branch-b diagnostic — NMTT cephalosporins vs active comparator

Triple difference (Δlog DCP − Δlog AFP), exposure vs non-NMTT cephalosporin active comparator:

| subset | NMTT − comparator | vitamin K1 − comparator |
|---|---|---|
| all episodes (n = 9,684 / 12,161) | −0.185 (−0.234, −0.147) | −0.274 (−0.304, −0.247) |
| no co-exposure (n = 5,245 / 5,832) | **−0.051 (−0.112, +0.009)** | −0.383 (−0.429, −0.339) |
| ≥3 doses, no co-exposure (n = 1,051 / 1,071) | **+0.015 (−0.099, +0.105)** | −0.554 (−0.722, −0.434) |

**H1 is not supported in the clean subsets.** Once co-exposure is removed, the NMTT-versus-comparator
contrast is null (CIs covering zero), and the apparent signal in the pooled set is attributable to
co-administered drugs. This is a genuine negative finding and it is retained, not discarded: the
NMTT-cephalosporin false-alarm concern, which is the version of this worry that the literature
tends to raise, does **not** reproduce at scale here. Threshold-crossing rates did differ for NMTT
(e.g. 29.1% vs 21.2% crossing above 40 mAU/mL in the no-co-exposure subset), but with a null
continuous contrast this is more plausibly baseline-distribution difference than a drug effect, and
H1 is accordingly demoted to a secondary, exploratory question.

### (iii) Three limitations the diagnostics themselves exposed — recorded, not hidden

1. **The AFP negative control is weaker than assumed, and its weakness is not uniform.** Regressing
   Δlog DCP on Δlog AFP in the comparator arm gives λ ≈ 0.52–0.68 with **R² only 0.11–0.15**. AFP and
   DCP do not track tumour burden tightly enough for AFP to absorb all tumour-driven DCP movement.
   The §2b(i) design mitigates this by holding the procedure constant across arms, but the negative
   control should be described as *partial*, and the solver must report sensitivity across λ. See
   §2b(iv) for a follow-up measurement in the primary cohort that revises this item materially.
2. **Exposure is overwhelmingly peri-procedural and short.** 97% of exposure episodes sit within
   −7..+21 d of a procedure, median 2 administrations. The H1 null therefore means *"no
   NMTT-attributable DCP inflation above roughly +12% under short peri-procedural courses"* — **not**
   "NMTT cephalosporins do not inhibit VKOR". [K2]'s design required >7 consecutive days; this
   cohort may be structurally unable to show a long-course effect.
3. **An unplanned measurement finding that may matter more than either hypothesis.** Among patients
   with pre-exposure DCP < 40 mAU/mL, **16–38% crossed above 40 within 2–21 days in *every* exposure
   group**, against ~0.1–0.2% for AFP crossing 20 ng/mL. Part of this is genuinely procedure-related
   (tumour necrosis, hepatic injury) rather than assay noise, but either way the short-term
   reliability of a *single* DCP value near the guideline 40 mAU/mL cut-off [K1] appears poor in the
   peri-treatment window. This is carried forward as a pre-specified secondary aim.

### (iv) OBSERVED: λ re-measured in the *primary* cohort, with the decision rule fixed first

The method deferral in §7 rested on λ measured in the **cephalosporin comparator arm**, whereas the
primary analysis set is the **resection cohort** — a real gap, since the shared tumour covariance
could easily be stronger where every patient has a tumour being removed. I therefore re-measured it
there (`work/lead/lambda_resection.py`, results frozen to `lambda_resection_results.txt`, n = 8,705),
and **wrote the interpretation rule into the script before reading the output**: R² ≥ 0.40 in the
unexposed arm would mean the tumour factor is plausibly identifiable and the deferral *must be
revisited*; 0.15–0.40 ambiguous; < 0.15 deferral confirmed.

| stratum | n | λ (OLS slope, 95% CI) | R² |
|---|---|---|---|
| all resections | 8,705 | 0.810 (0.755, 0.864) | 0.139 |
| **vitamin K1 unexposed (the decision stratum)** | 4,667 | 0.657 (0.585, 0.734) | **0.090** |
| vitamin K1 exposed | 4,038 | 0.744 (0.667, 0.816) | 0.139 |
| major resection | 2,222 | 0.774 (0.656, 0.897) | 0.114 |
| minor resection | 6,483 | 0.768 (0.710, 0.830) | 0.131 |
| baseline DCP tertile — low | 2,910 | **0.166 (0.118, 0.211)** | 0.020 |
| baseline DCP tertile — mid | 2,893 | 0.409 (0.334, 0.486) | 0.065 |
| baseline DCP tertile — high | 2,902 | 0.772 (0.686, 0.863) | 0.160 |
| both markers above assay floor (DCP>40 & AFP>20) | 3,734 | 1.213 (1.114, 1.307) | 0.248 |
| … unexposed subset of that | 1,780 | 0.927 (0.786, 1.068) | 0.153 |

Two consequences, one confirmatory and one unplanned:

- **The deferral is confirmed in the set that actually matters.** R² = 0.090 in the unexposed
  resection arm, below the pre-set 0.15 threshold and *lower* than the comparator-arm figure the
  deferral was originally based on. The objection I raised against my own candidate is resolved in
  the direction that strengthens the decision, not weakens it. In the floor-restricted subset R²
  rises to 0.153 — just inside the "ambiguous" band — so the deferral is recorded as **confirmed but
  conditional**, with the floor-restricted subset named as the stratum to re-check first if the
  latent model is ever revived.
- **Unplanned and consequential: λ is not a constant.** It rises monotonically from 0.17 (low
  baseline DCP) to 0.77 (high), and reaches 1.21 when both markers are clearly above their assay
  floors. λ is therefore substantially a *dynamic-range* phenomenon: near the floor the two markers
  barely co-move. This matters because AFP-differencing is **weakest exactly where the 40 mAU/mL
  decision [K1] is made** — in the low-DCP stratum the DID is close to a raw Δlog DCP, so the
  negative control provides little protection there. This cuts both ways and the proposal does not
  resolve it: near the floor there is also less tumour signal available to confound. Accordingly the
  primary H2 analysis now **pre-specifies a baseline-DCP-tertile-stratified sensitivity analysis**,
  and the headline estimate must be reported with λ fitted *within* stratum rather than pooled. A
  pooled λ is now known to be a misspecification, not a conservative simplification.

**Net effect on the proposal:** the mechanism premise for H2 is now grounded by our own computation
rather than assumed, and has survived the differential-half-life, regression-to-the-mean and
resection-extent rivals; H1 is demoted after an informative null that converges with [K2]; the
partial-negative-control and peri-procedural-exposure limits are explicit; and the unresolved,
consequential claim is narrowed to the **clinical-decision arm of H2** (§3) plus the DCP
short-term-reliability secondary aim.

## 3. Hypotheses (falsifiable)

Let DCP be analysed on the log scale; AFP (甲胎蛋白) is the **negative-control outcome** and
PT/INR (凝血酶原时间 / 国际标准化比值) the **positive mechanistic control**. The vitamin-K mechanism
must move DCP and PT/INR and must *not* move AFP. True tumour change moves DCP and AFP together and
need not move PT/INR.

**Already established by §2b(i), no longer the hypothesis under test:** peri-operative vitamin K1 is
associated with a dose-dependent, AFP-negative-control-adjusted extra fall in early post-operative
DCP. I treat this as a *supported premise*, not as the contribution.

- **H2 (primary, UNRESOLVED — the clinical-consequence claim).** Because of that suppression, **early
  post-operative DCP normalisation is a materially worse indicator of residual/recurrent disease in
  vitamin-K1-exposed patients than in unexposed patients.** Operationally: among patients with
  pre-operative DCP > 40 mAU/mL undergoing curative-intent resection, the sensitivity of
  "failure of DCP to normalise by day 30" for 180-day imaging-confirmed recurrence is lower, at
  matched specificity, in exposed than unexposed patients; equivalently, the exposure × DCP-
  normalisation interaction in a recurrence model is non-null. *This is what §2b does not show.* A
  marker can be shifted pharmacologically and still rank patients correctly; whether the shift
  destroys the decision rule is a separate, testable, and clinically decisive question.
- **H1 (false alarm — demoted to secondary/exploratory after the §2b(ii) null).** NMTT cephalosporins
  raise DCP relative to non-NMTT cephalosporins. The clean-subset contrast was null; the residual
  question is only whether threshold-crossing differences reflect anything beyond baseline
  distribution.
- **H3 (within-patient washout — the sharpest test).** The DCP-lowering effect of vitamin K1 is a
  *pharmacological transient*: within the same patient, the vitamin-K1-attributable DCP depression is
  present in values drawn within ~2–14 d of the last dose and absent in values drawn ≥30 d after it,
  while AFP shows no such time-since-dose pattern. A dose-response across the recorded 10 / 20 / 30 mg
  doses and across the number of doses should accompany it.

  *Why H3 replaced an earlier plan.* I had intended a regression discontinuity at the 2015→2016
  practice break (exposure jumped 0.025 → 0.650). **I checked and abandoned it:** the DCP assay only
  begins 2015-08-13 in this snapshot, so the pre-break period contributes 25 eligible patients versus
  146 in 2016 (OBSERVED). The sharp RD is not estimable and is dropped rather than quietly weakened.
  The residual secular decline (exposure 0.774 in 2016 → 0.333 in 2025 within the analysis set,
  n = 146–2,499/yr) is retained only as a *supporting* time-varying-propensity analysis, explicitly
  confounded with other secular change (surgical technique, systemic-therapy era, assay drift), and
  is not load-bearing. Identification rests instead on: within-patient crossover, the mechanism-matched
  active comparator, the AFP negative-control outcome, and the H3 washout/dose-response pattern — none
  of which require between-patient comparability.

**Pre-specified decision thresholds.**
- *Supportive:* the exposure × DCP-normalisation interaction is non-null and the **absolute drop in
  sensitivity at matched specificity is ≥10 percentage points** (95% CI excluding 0), with the
  washout and dose-response patterns of H3 present and absent for AFP.
- *Adverse / refuting:* the interaction CI covers the null and **excludes a ≥10-point sensitivity
  drop**, and corrected DCP does not beat raw DCP out of sample. Interpretation: vitamin K1 shifts
  the DCP *level* but does not degrade the *decision rule* — clinicians may keep using DCP
  normalisation as they do. This is a genuinely useful negative, because it would convert a
  currently unexamined assumption into a tested one, and it is a live possibility: a uniform
  multiplicative shift can leave discrimination intact.
- *Inconclusive:* CI wide enough to include both no effect and a ≥10-point drop, or recurrence
  adjudication accuracy too poor (κ < 0.6 against the adjudicated sample) to support the
  sensitivity comparison. Then the claim narrows to "the level shift is established (§2b) but its
  decision impact is unquantified", and the next step is a prospective study with directly measured
  vitamin K status and protocolised imaging.

An imprecise null would not refute H2; the ≥10-point exclusion requirement is what distinguishes a
refutation from an uninformative result.

## 4. Why the rivals do not trivially explain H1–H3

| Rival explanation | How the design separates it |
|---|---|
| **Confounding by indication.** Cefoperazone is used for biliary sepsis; cholestasis itself raises DCP. | Active comparator (non-NMTT cephalosporin, same infection indication); adjust concurrent total/direct bilirubin, ALP, GGT; within-patient crossover among patients exposed to both at different times. |
| **Severity / sicker patients progress.** | AFP negative-control outcome: severity-and-tumour explanations predict AFP moves too; the vitamin-K mechanism predicts it does not. |
| **Reverse causation** (progressing tumour → infection → antibiotic). | Restrict to exposures with a pre-exposure DCP within 30 d; landmark design; AFP control again. |
| **Vitamin K1 is given to sicker/more coagulopathic patients** (so exposure marks worse livers). | The 2015→2016 discontinuity: a ~62-point exposure jump driven by institutional practice, not by individual patient biology. Also the department contrast. |
| **Calendar-time confounding.** | The secular-trend arm is explicitly non-load-bearing (see H3). Any period analysis must show the shift is **DCP-specific**: no concordant shift in AFP normalisation or in recurrence. |
| **Vitamin K1 exposure marks coagulopathic/sicker patients.** | If so it should predict *worse* outcomes and *less* marker normalisation; observing *more* DCP normalisation with *no* excess AFP normalisation is the wrong sign for confounding. Reinforced by the H3 within-patient washout, which compares a patient to themselves at different times since dose. |
| **Measurement/observation artefact** (sicker patients tested more often). | Fixed measurement windows in a landmark design; report testing intensity by group. |
| **Differential marker half-life.** DCP (~40–72 h) clears faster than AFP (~5–7 d), so after tumour removal DCP falls faster for purely kinetic reasons, and vitamin K1 is given mostly around surgery. | **Already addressed in §2b(i):** the comparison is made *within* the resection cohort, so both arms had tumour removal; the exposed–unexposed DID gap (−0.378) cannot be a shared kinetic property. The solver must retain this restriction. |
| **Resection extent.** Larger resections may attract both more vitamin K1 and greater tumour-burden reduction. | Adjust for procedure extent, codable from `手术` text (肝叶切除 vs 肝部分切除 vs 特殊肝段切除 vs 腹腔镜下肝部分切除), operative duration (`开始时间`→`结束时间`), and peri-operative transfusion/PCC exposure. **This is the leading unaddressed confounder of the §2b(i) estimate and must be handled before any causal reading.** |
| **DCP half-life makes windows matter.** | Pre-specify windows (−30..−1 d pre; +3..+10 d and +21..+40 d post) and show sensitivity to them. |

### Outcome adjudication — stated honestly as a limitation

**OBSERVED feasibility probe (Lead, today).** Filtering `检查` to abdominal/liver studies yields
**118,762 reports in 35,161 patients**, of which **99.96% have a non-empty `检查诊断`** conclusion
field (median length 25 characters — these are terse structured impressions, not free prose).
Recurrence vocabulary is explicit: 复发 4,134 reports, 癌栓 3,185, 转移 3,317, 活性 3,356, 新发 515.
Crucially, a naive keyword rule would be badly biased: applying a clause-level negation/uncertainty
rule to the 6,384 reports mentioning 复发/新发灶/残留/活性灶 classifies them **82.9% affirmed,
10.2% negated (未见明确复发灶 …), 6.9% uncertain (可疑复发灶 …)** — so ~17% of keyword hits are not
assertions of recurrence. This is good news for feasibility and a hard requirement for rigour.

Imaging-confirmed recurrence must therefore be derived from the narrative `检查诊断` / `检查所见` fields of
abdominal contrast CT/MRI (`上腹部平扫+增强＋DWI+MRCP`, 24,573), liver CEUS (12,148) and liver/portal
ultrasound (12,845). Example text is informative (e.g. "肝左外叶占位-考虑MT可能大，门脉左支栓子可能，
肝硬化，脾肿大，少量腹水"). **The snapshot metadata states the only bundled text tool is an
unvalidated lexical cirrhosis flag; there is no validated Chinese radiology phenotyper here.** The
solver must therefore (a) write and publish its own rule set, (b) report agreement against a
manually adjudicated random sample (target ≥300 reports, stratified by modality and era), and
(c) treat the recurrence outcome as *measured with error of quantified magnitude*, propagating that
into the sensitivity/specificity comparison. Full RECIST/mRECIST adjudication is **not possible**
and is not claimed. There is **no mortality linkage**, so survival endpoints are out of scope.

## 6. Design

**Population.** Adults in the HCC cohort with ≥1 DCP measurement. Two analysis sets:
- *Set A (H1, exposure-response):* exposure episodes = first in-encounter start of an NMTT
  cephalosporin or a non-NMTT cephalosporin, with a DCP in (−30,−1) d and ≥1 DCP in (+2,+21) d.
- *Set B (H2/H3, curative-intent):* first liver resection or ablation per patient, with a
  pre-operative DCP (−30..−1 d), ≥1 post-operative DCP in (+2..+90) d, and ≥180 d of subsequent
  in-system contact. Index = procedure date. Exposure = any vitamin K1 in −3..+7 d.

**Temporal boundaries.** DCP assay available from 2015-08 (OBSERVED first DCP 2015-08-13), so Set B
spans 2015-08 → 2025-12 with a complete-follow-up cut at 2025-06 for the 180-day outcome window.
The 2015/2016 practice break is the ITS cut point for H3.

**Estimands.**
- H1: within-patient (fixed-effects) difference in Δlog DCP, NMTT vs non-NMTT, with the same
  contrast estimated for Δlog AFP (negative control) and ΔINR (positive control). Plus the
  risk difference in crossing 40 mAU/mL from below.
- H2: the **interaction** between vitamin K1 exposure and early post-operative DCP normalisation in
  predicting 180-day imaging-confirmed recurrence; expressed as the difference in sensitivity at
  fixed specificity (and in time-dependent AUC) of DCP normalisation between exposed and unexposed.
- H3: within-patient association between log DCP and time since last vitamin K1 dose (binned
  2–14 d / 15–29 d / ≥30 d), and across recorded dose (10/20/30 mg) and dose count, with the
  identical model fitted to log AFP as negative control. The artefact signature is a monotone
  washout in DCP with none in AFP.

**Measured analysis-set sizes (OBSERVED).** 25,144 patients have a first curative-intent liver
procedure (20,687 resection / 4,457 ablation). 12,593 have a pre-op DCP and ≥1 post-op DCP;
8,705 have paired pre/post DCP *and* AFP in the tight +3..+10 d window used in §2b(i). For the H2
outcome analysis, which additionally requires adjudicable imaging: **6,509 patients have ≥2
post-procedure DCP and ≥1 adjudication-grade liver imaging report within 24 months** (≤2022: 3,441;
2023–2025: 3,068 — a usable temporal split), falling to 2,073 if ≥2 imaging studies are required.
Within the eligible set there are 178,307 distinct lab timepoints in −30..+730 d (median per
patient: 5 DCP, 5 AFP, 7 PT, 6 bilirubin) — adequate density for the longitudinal decomposition.
**6,509 is the honest primary-analysis denominator for H2**, not the larger marker-only counts.

**Secondary aim (from §2b(iii)).** Estimate the short-term test–retest behaviour of DCP around the
40 mAU/mL threshold in the peri-treatment window, with AFP as the comparator marker, separating
procedure-attributable movement from residual variability by using measurement pairs at varying
distance from any procedure.

## 7. Two methods on the same question (baseline and substantive alternative)

**Baseline — within-patient difference-in-differences / landmark logistic regression.** Linear model
for Δlog DCP with patient fixed effects, exposure indicator, and concurrent bilirubin, albumin,
PT/INR, log AFP; cluster-robust SEs. For H2, a landmark logistic/Cox model for 180-day recurrence
with DCP-normalisation × vitamin-K1 interaction. Cheap (minutes, CPU), transparent, and directly
answers "is there an average artefact and does it modify the decision rule".

**Substantive alternative — multivariate latent state-space decomposition of the marker
trajectory.** Model each patient's joint trajectory of (log DCP, log AFP, PT/INR, bilirubin) as the
sum of (i) a slow *tumour-burden* latent process shared by DCP and AFP and (ii) a fast
*vitamin-K-state* latent process shared by DCP and PT/INR and forced by the drug exposures;
fit by EM/Kalman smoothing (linear Gaussian) or as a Bayesian joint model, with a GRU/neural-ODE
variant as a flexible comparator. Output: a per-timepoint **corrected DCP**.

*What the alternative reveals that the baseline loses.* The DiD uses 2 of a median ~23 lab
timepoints per patient, gives one average effect, cannot output a corrected value for an individual,
cannot estimate the perturbation time constant 1/κ, and is blind to *uncoded* vitamin-K perturbation
(dietary, nutritional, biliary) — which matters because 46–58% of pre-exposure baselines are
themselves within a prior exposure's shadow, biasing the baseline toward the null. The decomposition
yields the variance partition, the time constant, and a per-timepoint corrected DCP that can be
*evaluated* out of sample.

**Selection decision: the latent model is SPECIFIED but DEFERRED, on measured grounds.** Three
pre-conditions were measured during this episode and all are unfavourable. (i) λ = Δlog DCP /
Δlog AFP in the comparator arm is 0.52–0.68 with **R² only 0.11–0.15** — there is little shared
tumour covariance for the tumour factor to capture, which is precisely the structure the model
relies on. (ii) The NMTT input coefficient is measured null (§2b(ii)), so the *only* identified
driver of the vitamin-K factor would be vitamin K1 itself — which is prescribed *in reaction to*
coagulopathy, making the correction potentially circular. (iii) **The obvious objection to (i) was
that λ had been measured in the wrong cohort; it was re-measured in the primary resection cohort
against a rule fixed in advance and came out R² = 0.090 in the decision stratum — worse, not
better** (§2b(iv)). The deferral therefore survives the strongest available challenge to it.

The same follow-up, however, revealed that λ is **strongly heterogeneous** (0.17 → 1.21 across the
marker dynamic range), which changes what the deferred model would have to do: a single global
vitamin-K factor with a constant loading on AFP is now known to be misspecified. Reviving the latent
model would require either a λ that varies with marker level or an explicit assay-floor censoring
model. That is recorded as a design requirement on any future revival, not a reason to attempt it now.

Accordingly the solver should run a **cheap intermediate first**: a linear mixed model of log DCP on
contemporaneous INR, bilirubin, albumin and log AFP plus a spline in days-since-procedure, yielding
a liver-function-adjusted DCP. Pre-specified stopping rules:
- If that mixed model reaches **≥90% of the state-space model's gain** in sensitivity at 90%
  specificity on validation, publish the cheap version and report the complex model as unnecessary.
- If the estimated vitamin-K factor is **better explained by bilirubin than by the exposure series**,
  the recovered object is a liver-function-adjusted DCP and **the vitamin-K interpretation must be
  dropped** — a falsification of the mechanistic reading, not a tuning failure.
- Identifiability (zero restrictions a_AK = 0, a_PT = 0) must be verified by simulation before any
  corrected DCP is reported.

This is a deliberate choice not to buy complexity the measured data cannot identify; the full model
remains specified so it can be revisited if a vitamin-K-status assay or an exogenous exposure
instrument becomes available.

*Resources.* **Measured:** the whole discovery diagnostic suite (exposure extraction, episode
building, lab pairing, negative controls, robustness, adjudication availability) ran in <15 min of
single-CPU shell time with 0 GPUs. **Estimated, unverified by timing run:** the DiD baseline
~1–2 h on 8 CPUs; the continuous-time linear-Gaussian state-space over 6,509 patients × ~760 days
× 4 channels ≈ 16 CPU × 10 h, with an optional GPU variant ≈ 1 A100 × 8 h. The primary fit is
CPU-only and **no GPU is required for the primary claim**; device choice here is a tradeoff, not a
merit criterion. These sit well inside the solver planning envelope (16 CPUs / 8 GPUs / 8 h).

*Infrastructure note, separate from scientific feasibility:* `job_submit` rejected branch-b's
declared inputs ("Use this workspace or a configured read-only source file") even when only the
source CSV was declared, so the diagnostics were run as chunked shell steps instead. This is a
harness/packaging issue for the compiler to resolve, not a limitation of the science.

*Deferred alternatives, recorded so they can be revisited.* (a) Direct measurement of vitamin K
status — not in this snapshot, and the single piece of evidence that would most sharpen the
mechanism; revisit if an assay appears. (b) The NMTT-cephalosporin arm (H1) as a primary design —
deferred after the §2b(ii) null; revisit if a larger within-patient crossover (currently 886
patients, 238 with episodes >30 d apart) can be assembled or if co-exposure can be modelled
explicitly. (c) The 2016 regression discontinuity — abandoned on data grounds (§3, H3), revisit only
if earlier DCP measurements are released.

## 9b. What the solver must actually produce

A completed study delivers, at minimum: (1) the adjudicated recurrence phenotype with its measured
agreement statistics against the manually reviewed sample; (2) the fitted baseline interaction model
with the exposure × DCP-normalisation estimate and CI, and the sensitivity-at-matched-specificity
difference against the pre-specified ±10-point band; (3) the fitted latent decomposition, its
variance partition between the tumour and vitamin-K processes, and an identifiability check by
simulation; (4) the out-of-sample comparison of corrected versus raw DCP on both a patient-level and
a temporal (fit ≤2022 / test 2023–2025) split; (5) the resection-extent-adjusted re-estimate of the
§2b(i) effect; (6) **the primary H2 estimate stratified by baseline-DCP tertile with λ fitted within
stratum**, since a pooled λ is now measured to be a misspecification (§2b(iv)) — if the exposure ×
DCP-normalisation interaction is confined to a single tertile, that is a materially weaker and
differently actionable finding than a uniform one, and must be reported as such. Readiness or
reference execution establishes that these compute, not that H2 is true.

## 8. What this would and would not establish

- *Supportive:* DCP values in routinely treated HCC patients carry a quantified iatrogenic
  component large enough to cross decision thresholds, and post-operative DCP normalisation is
  partly a pharmacological artefact. Immediate implication: record vitamin K1 and NMTT-cephalosporin
  exposure when interpreting DCP; do not read early post-operative DCP normalisation as evidence of
  complete resection in exposed patients. This is checkable computationally.
- *Adverse:* current face-value interpretation of DCP is defensible in this setting — a useful
  negative that removes a plausible source of error.
- *Not established by any result here:* that correcting DCP improves patient outcomes; the correct
  threshold for any corrected marker; generalisation beyond one Chinese HBV-dominant tertiary centre
  with its own assay platform and perioperative culture. Those need external replication, direct
  measurement of vitamin K status, and prospective evaluation. Recurrence adjudication quality is a
  hard limit here and requires clinical review of the sampled reports.

## 9. Unresolved objections (UNRESOLVED)

1. ~~Sharp 2016 regression discontinuity~~ — **resolved and dropped**: DCP assay starts 2015-08, so
   the pre-break analysis set is 25 patients. See H3. Residual risk: assay/platform drift across
   2015–2025 could still distort the supporting secular analysis; the solver must check reporting
   continuity (value distribution, censoring strings, `标本类型`) before using any period contrast.
2. Whether vitamin K1 at the doses recorded (10/20/30 mg, OBSERVED) actually shifts DCP measurably
   at +2..+21 d, or whether the effect is too brief relative to DCP's half-life. **This is the single
   diagnostic that decides whether the branch continues** and is what branch-b is measuring.
3. Whether recurrence can be adjudicated from narrative reports at acceptable accuracy — the binding
   constraint on H2/H3.
4. ~~Whether the literature already answers H1~~ — **resolved**: [K2] substantially pre-empts H1 and
   reports a null, which our §2b(ii) reproduces. Contribution is accordingly narrowed to H2/H3.
5. Resection extent as a confounder of the §2b(i) estimate (see rivals table) — the leading
   unaddressed threat to a causal reading of the mechanism premise.
6. Assay-species caveat raised by [K3]: if the PIVKA-II produced by HCC differs from that produced
   by cholestasis/vitamin-K deficiency, the assay may not measure one quantity, which would
   complicate any "corrected DCP". Not resolvable in this snapshot; requires assay-level work.
