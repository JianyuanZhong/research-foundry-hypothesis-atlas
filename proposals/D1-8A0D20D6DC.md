# Target-trial emulation: stop empiric intravenous vancomycin at the 72-hour culture landmark in ICU patients without evidence of invasive Gram-positive infection

## Clinical question and falsifiable hypothesis

For adults still in intensive care 72 hours after empiric intravenous vancomycin plus an antipseudomonal beta-lactam was started and whose blood cultures have not identified an organism requiring vancomycin, does stopping vancomycin at that reassessment point, compared with continuing it, reduce acute kidney injury without increasing death or clinically important treatment failure?

**Untested hypothesis.** Among patients meeting the above 72-hour decision-state criteria, a strategy of stopping IV vancomycin during hours 60–72 will lower 7-day stage 2/3 acute kidney injury (AKI) risk and will not increase a prespecified 14-day composite of death or rescue anti-MRSA treatment. This is a causal strategy hypothesis; MIMIC-IV can support a careful observational target-trial emulation but cannot establish clinical noninferiority or prove that an individual infection was safely excluded.

**What is already supported.** Current sepsis guidance encourages daily antimicrobial de-escalation, says it may be appropriate even with negative cultures, and explicitly characterizes the underlying evidence as very low quality and in need of randomized trials (Evans et al., 2021, PMID 34605781, DOI 10.1097/CCM.0000000000005337; inspected full public text at `https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8486643/`, frozen source `[source checksum]`). That recommendation does not answer whether stopping empiric vancomycin at a culture-informed 72-hour landmark is safe for ICU patients who remain ill. The proposed advance is to align eligibility, observable microbiology, treatment assignment, confounding measurements, and follow-up at the actual de-escalation decision.

## Population and temporal design

Unit is the first eligible ICU admission per patient (later eligible admissions excluded to avoid correlated treatment decisions).

1. Age at least 18; first ICU stay in the admission.
2. Empiric regimen initiation: first qualifying systemic IV vancomycin administration accompanied by systemic cefepime, piperacillin-tazobactam, ceftazidime, meropenem, imipenem-cilastatin, or aztreonam within a symmetric 12-hour co-initiation window. Both must occur from 6 hours before through 24 hours after ICU `intime`. Define regimen start as the earlier first administration.
3. At least one true bacterial `BLOOD CULTURE` specimen collected from 24 hours before through 24 hours after regimen start. Do not classify every specimen whose description merely contains “blood”: explicitly exclude serology, viral antibody/PCR, malaria, and mycobacterial/fungal-only tests from the primary bacterial-culture rule.
4. At hour 60 after regimen start, patient is alive, not discharged, remains in ICU, and received IV vancomycin during hours 36–60. This establishes treatment opportunity and avoids immortal-time bias.
5. No information available by hour 60 indicating a compelling need for continued vancomycin: exclude blood-culture organism reported by `storetime <= landmark` that is *S. aureus*, beta-hemolytic streptococcus, *Enterococcus* with susceptibility supporting vancomycin, or another organism adjudicated a priori to require Gram-positive coverage; exclude any non-blood sterile-site culture with such an organism; exclude a vancomycin-susceptible organism for which definitive therapy remains indicated. Coagulase-negative staphylococci are not automatically contaminants: primary analysis excludes them as indeterminate; sensitivity analyses classify them by number of positive specimens and timing.
6. Exclude chronic systemic vancomycin already present >24 hours before ICU, comfort-measures-only status before landmark when computably documented, pre-landmark RRT, missing baseline creatinine sufficient to stage AKI, or an eMAR gap that prevents classifying administration.

**Time zero (landmark):** 60 hours after regimen start. The 12-hour grace period assigns a strategy without using future outcomes: stop arm has no systemic vancomycin administration after hour 60 and through hour 72; continue arm receives at least one systemic dose in hours 60–72. Clone-censor-weighting is preferred: clone each eligible person to both strategies at hour 60, censor a clone when observed administration deviates during the grace period, and weight for adherence. A simpler grace-period assignment will be a sensitivity analysis and starts outcome counting at hour 72 to avoid immortal-time bias.

**Strategies:** (A) discontinue empiric IV vancomycin by hour 72 while other antibiotics remain clinician-directed; (B) continue IV vancomycin beyond hour 72. Vancomycin restart after initial adherence is allowed and counted as rescue; because this is an intention-to-treat analogue, later switching does not reassign exposure. A per-protocol analysis censors at strategy deviation and uses time-varying inverse-probability weights.

## Outcomes and estimands

Primary benefit outcome is **stage 2/3 AKI within 7 days after hour 72**, using KDIGO creatinine and urine-output criteria: creatinine at least 2 times baseline, creatinine at least 3 times baseline or >=4.0 mg/dL with acute rise, urine output thresholds when measurement coverage is adequate, or new RRT. Baseline creatinine is the lowest valid value from 24 hours before regimen start through landmark; sensitivity analyses use first value and a 7-day pre-ICU value when available. Death before ascertainment is handled as a competing event and in a composite sensitivity outcome, not coded as “no AKI.”

Primary safety outcome is **14-day death or rescue anti-MRSA therapy**, defined as all-cause death (`deathtime` or `dod`) or restart/initiation after a 24-hour vancomycin-free interval of systemic vancomycin, linezolid, daptomycin, ceftaroline, or telavancin. Report death and rescue separately; rescue is a process proxy, not proof of missed infection.

Secondary outcomes: 14-day mortality; new RRT; subsequent blood/sterile-site culture growing MRSA or another organism requiring Gram-positive coverage; vasopressor-free days and ventilation-free days through day 14; ICU-free and hospital-free days through day 28 (death assigned zero); 7-day change in creatinine; and antipseudomonal beta-lactam days, to detect whether “vancomycin stop” merely tags broader de-escalation.

Estimands are standardized 7- and 14-day risks under each strategy, risk differences and risk ratios in the eligible landmark population. This is not a noninferiority trial. For the safety composite, present the estimate and two-sided 95% CI against a clinically specified harm margin of +3 absolute percentage points; crossing the margin is inconclusive/adverse rather than evidence of safety.

## Confounding control and analysis

Pre-specify baseline (pre-regimen) and updated landmark covariates, never post-landmark values: age, sex, race/insurance as social/context variables, anchor-year group, ICU/service, emergency admission, comorbidity history, surgical status, suspected source proxies, prior MRSA/Enterococcus cultures, culture count/site and whether antibiotics preceded culture, baseline and landmark SOFA components, lactate, temperature, WBC, creatinine/eGFR, bilirubin, platelets, oxygen/ventilation, vasopressor agents/dose, urine output, fluid balance, trajectory/slope and missingness of vitals/labs over 0–24 and 24–60 hours, antimicrobial agents, vancomycin administration count, time since last dose, and culture result state available by `storetime` (no growth/comment, pending, probable contaminant, organism/susceptibility). Treatment-level values or future-coded diagnoses must not enter the model.

Use a doubly robust analysis: stabilized inverse-probability weights from an ensemble propensity model plus weighted outcome regression/TMLE, truncating weights at predeclared 1st/99th percentiles. Report overlap plots, effective sample size, extreme weights, standardized mean differences before/after weighting, missingness, and positivity within prespecified strata. Use patient-level bootstrap confidence intervals. Multiple imputation or missingness indicators are allowed only from pre-landmark data. Estimate subgroup effects only with interaction CIs: shock/pressor at landmark, pneumonia proxy, culture sampled before versus after antibiotics, and baseline renal dysfunction.

Baselines: unadjusted landmark comparison; conventional multivariable regression; high-dimensional propensity approach; and an active-comparator timing analysis among patients whose antipseudomonal beta-lactam continues, reducing confounding by global recovery/de-escalation.

## Falsification, negative controls, and robustness

* **Pre-exposure negative-control outcome:** maximum creatinine and platelet decline during hours 0–24 after regimen initiation. A material adjusted association with the later stop decision indicates residual severity/confounding or leakage.
* **Future-exposure falsification:** among patients receiving vancomycin through hour 96, label future stopping during hours 96–108 and test whether it predicts outcomes occurring before hour 96. It must not.
* **Active-comparator negative control:** stopping a non-nephrotoxic empiric companion agent at the same landmark should not reproduce a highly specific creatinine benefit if the proposed mechanism is vancomycin toxicity; interpretation is cautious because indications differ.
* Re-run using prescription `starttime/stoptime` rather than eMAR only, stricter “Administered” eMAR status, IV-route-confirmed administrations, 48-hour and 72-hour landmarks, blood cultures sampled before first antibiotics only, complete cases, no urine-output criterion, alternative baseline creatinine, exclusion of early deaths, and quantitative E-value/bias analysis for unmeasured confounding.
* Audit random stratified samples of 100 encounters each from stop/continue arms and every unexpected post-landmark invasive Gram-positive culture. Clinical reviewers, blinded to outcome when possible, must check whether medication events represent systemic dosing, culture result availability was reconstructed correctly, and an indication for vancomycin was missed. This adjudication is essential before clinical inference.

## Feasibility audit actually run

The reproducible script `[internal dataset path]` streamed the exact archive members without modifying sources; output is `[internal dataset path]`. This broad screen found 85,242 adult first ICU admissions, 9,189 with administered-name matches for vancomycin plus an antipseudomonal beta-lactam from ICU hour -6 to +24, 4,859 with a broadly matched blood specimen from -24 to +24 hours, and 2,365 whose first ICU stay lasted at least 72 hours. Among those, a deliberately crude eMAR continuity rule requiring vancomycin at hours 36–60 classified 478 as stopped (none at 60–120 hours), 781 as continued (administration at 72–96 hours), and 248 as ambiguous. These are feasibility counts, not the analytic cohort: the screen over-included oral/local vancomycin and serology specimens, used ICU `intime` rather than regimen start, and did not apply organism/indication, survival, RRT, route, patient-deduplication, or complete covariate criteria. The exact build must report a CONSORT-style attrition table and may narrow considerably. Source headers and example rows were directly inspected for all central tables.

## Decision rules and evidence limits

**Supportive:** adjusted AKI risk is lower with stopping; the 95% CI for the 14-day safety risk difference lies wholly below +3%; estimates are directionally stable across route-confirmed, culture-timing, active-comparator, and landmark sensitivity analyses; balance/positivity diagnostics pass; and falsification tests are null. This supports equipoise and a prospective stewardship trial, not automatic stopping or causal certainty.

**Adverse:** the safety composite, mortality, or subsequent invasive Gram-positive infection is higher and its interval excludes zero or materially exceeds the +3% harm margin, especially consistently across robust analyses. This argues against adopting the strategy for the studied decision state and motivates adjudication of missed indications.

**Inconclusive:** poor overlap/effective sample size, failed negative controls, conflicting definitions, excessive missing creatinine/urine output, materially divergent estimates, or a safety CI that crosses both no effect and the +3% margin. A null P value is not evidence of safety.

Computationally checkable claims include cohort assembly, temporal ordering, administration/culture rules, covariate balance, effect estimates, CIs, diagnostics, and falsification results. MIMIC cannot reliably determine bedside intent, source control adequacy, true infection versus contamination, clinician knowledge before `storetime`, outpatient antibiotic use, antimicrobial resistance arising after discharge, or whether rescue therapy represented genuine failure. Notes can assist blinded adjudication but must not be treated as a validated infection label. Establishing noninferiority, generalizability beyond one deidentified center, or a clinical stopping protocol requires infectious-disease/pharmacy review and ideally a prospective randomized trial.
