## Unresolved question, importance, and evidence boundary

After pressor-treated shock has resolved, should a markedly fluid-positive, invasively ventilated adult with adequate MAP, urine output, and potassium begin an ICU-input-system recorded furosemide or bumetanide delivery during the next 24 hours **while the endotracheal airway is still present**, or avoid such delivery until extubation or the end of that window? The possible benefit is a clinically material increase in durable, record-observed liberation; possible harms are early recorded creatinine rise, RRT, death, or unfavorable terminal disposition. Resolving this association could identify a decision point worth randomizing and could falsify a common assumption that post-resuscitation diuresis is benign.

The strongest currently supported clinical claim is narrower. The indexed abstract of Silversides et al. 2017 (DOI `10.1007/s00134-016-4573-3`; frozen search `[source checksum]`; full paper not inspected) reports 11 heterogeneous randomized conservative/deresuscitative trials (2,051 patients), 1.82 more ventilator-free days (95% CI 0.53–3.10), and imprecise mortality (RR 0.92, 95% CI 0.82–1.02). The indexed RADAR-2 abstract (DOI `10.1007/s00134-021-06596-8`; frozen search `[source checksum]`; full paper not inspected) reports that a 180-patient bundled strategy reduced day-3 balance but was underpowered for clinical outcomes and had a mortality point estimate compatible with harm. The inspected full XML of Orieux et al. 2026 (PMCID PMC13218125; DOI `10.1016/j.aicoj.2026.100083`; frozen source `[source checksum]`) calls renal congestion hypothesis-generating and states that interventional evidence and clinical utility of congestion targeting remain unestablished. None establishes the effect or renal safety of a single recorded-loop decision in this phenotype.

The inspected full XML of Maringe et al. 2020 (PMCID PMC7823243; DOI `10.1093/ije/dyaa057`; frozen source `[source checksum]`) supports cloning, artificial censoring, and inverse-probability weighting when treatment initiation and follow-up do not coincide; it supplies no clinical effect evidence and does not remove unmeasured confounding. The three ambition papers are methodological demonstrations only. No unavailable cancer main text is cited or claimed read.

Early airway exits before any loop are legitimate common policy outcomes; they must remain identical in both compatible clones and must not later be reassigned by a post-exit dose.

## Recorded-loop classifier and corrected dynamic strategies

A qualifying positive segment is `inputevents.itemid` 221794, 228340, or 229639; valid matching encounter keys and interval; `endtime>starttime`; ultimate status `FinishedRunning`, `ChangeDose/Rate`, or `Stopped`; and either positive `amount` with `amountuom=mg` or positive `rate` with `rateuom=mg/hour`. Collapse exact duplicates and use `linkorderid/orderid` to prevent segmented courses from masquerading as new starts. Paused, Bolus, unknown unit, contradictory key/time, nonpositive, order/cancellation, the two known dose-unit rows, and intervals beyond admission are ambiguous or sensitivity-only. The effective onset is retrospectively verified `starttime`, never `storetime` or `endtime`. Because route and actual bedside receipt are unavailable, call this ICU-input-system recorded delivery, never proven IV receipt.

Let `G=t0+24h`. Let `X` be the earliest post-t0 airway-exit start time from generic extubation 227194, explicitly unplanned extubation 225468/225477, or tracheostomy 225448/226237 in `procedureevents`; let `T` be the inherited resolved terminal death/discharge time and `E` index-ICU exit. The shared strategy boundary is `C=min(G,X,T,E)`.

- Strategy A: begin the first qualifying recorded-loop segment in `(t0,C)` while still invasively ventilated. If generic or unplanned extubation, tracheostomy, terminal event, or ICU exit occurs first, the strategy stops assigning treatment and requires no later initiation.
- Strategy B: have no qualifying recorded-loop start in `(t0,C)`. The same closures stop B.
- A qualifying loop strictly before `C` satisfies A and artificially censors B immediately before onset. If `G` occurs first, B is satisfied and unsatisfied A is censored at G. If `X,T,` or `E` occurs before loop/G, both clones close without asymmetric censoring. A loop after closure is allowed common subsequent care, reported as a co-intervention, and cannot alter clone assignment.
- Same-minute loop/airway-exit ties and unresolved event order are ambiguous: report separately, assign the airway state `[0,1]`, and censor both from the primary contrast at the tied time; treatment-first and airway-first tie rules are sensitivities only.
- Once a candidate airway event occurs, freeze its event time for treatment adherence. Its later 48-hour durability classification may use allowed future outcome data, but no treatment after the event may retroactively satisfy/censor a clone.

This last rule supersedes every parent sentence allowing post-extubation delivery to satisfy A or censor B. For renal and mortality outcomes, follow-up continues to the fixed endpoint after shared airway closure under unrestricted common post-closure care; report post-closure loop use by original compatible strategy. The estimand is therefore a dynamic policy effect that stops intervening once the airway decision is no longer relevant.

## Falsification and result meanings

Supportive evidence requires the one-sided 95% lower confidence limit of the AA-E48-D5 lower-bound risk difference above +0.05, at least 75 effective subjects per arm after corrected closure, adequate treatment-before-airway events, acceptable calibration/balance/weight tails, no partition leakage, successful negative controls, less than 5% unresolved treatment/airway ordering and less than a 5-point arm imbalance in unknown renal state, plus every inherited contamination, observation, and sensitivity gate. The strongest permitted conclusion is: assignment to the ICU-recorded loop-while-ventilated policy was associated with at least a five-point increase in the record-defined airway outcome in measured-overlap patients. A lower renal bound below its harm margin means only that the prespecified recorded harm margin was not detected.

Adverse evidence is an airway upper confidence limit at or below 0, an all-eligible RCR48 lower limit at or above +0.03, observed-RRT7 lower limit at or above +0.05 under both date brackets, or inherited death/unfavorable-terminal harm. Margin-falsified is an airway upper limit at or below +0.05. These findings argue against advancing this policy without stronger contrary evidence; they do not prove biological harm.

Inconclusive/infeasible includes too few final subjects or treatment-before-airway events, ESS below 75 per arm, positivity/balance/control failure, wide airway bounds, renal unknown above 15% or imbalanced by more than 5 points, unresolved ordering at least 5%, material grace/store/status/item/linkage/readout/date-bracket reversal, or incompatible broad-versus-final attrition. Null is not equivalence; unsupported favorable prose must be rejected.
