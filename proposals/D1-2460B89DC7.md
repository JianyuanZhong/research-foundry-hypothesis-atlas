# Explicit nutrition interruption during charted IV insulin and short-horizon hypoglycemia in eICU

## Status, scientific opening, and deliverable

This is a substantive repair of expert seed `[starting question]` / parent `[prior hypothesis]`. It is a planned observational experiment, not an executed result. The original all-insulin claim is not supportable: eICU `medication` records active orders and explicitly does not guarantee administration. The repair restricts exposure to repeated charted IV insulin rate states in `infusionDrug`, restricts nutrition comparison to explicit timestamped `nurseCare` state transitions, and never treats missing nutrition documentation as cessation.

The strongest evidence-supported claim is that inpatient insulin is a high-alert therapy, decreasing intake/NPO status changes insulin need and warrants monitoring [K2]; AKI was strongly associated with hypoglycemia in an insulin-treated two-ICU cohort [K1]; and a single-center cohort receiving continuous enteral nutrition had infrequent level-1 and level-2 hypoglycemia across compared subcutaneous regimens [K3]. These works do not establish whether an explicit nutrition interruption during charted IV insulin creates a reproducible short hazard window beyond current glucose, insulin rate, renal trajectory, severity and monitoring.

The unresolved hypothesis is:

> Among adult eICU stays at an explicit nutrition-state transition while a positive, unit-standardized IV insulin rate has been charted within 120 minutes, transition from documented carbohydrate-providing nutrition to documented NPO is followed by a higher six-hour hazard of glucose <70 mg/dL than documented nutrition continuation at otherwise comparable dynamic states; the excess is larger when creatinine has risen in the preceding 48 hours and is attenuated when insulin is promptly reduced or stopped.

This is a risk-window/decision-support hypothesis, not a causal effect of withholding nutrition. The leading rival is worsening illness: procedures, shock, organ failure or declining glucose may jointly prompt NPO, insulin adjustment, denser testing and dextrose. Documentation/interface timing is a second rival. The clinical advance is to decide whether a simple safety trigger—explicit NPO transition plus active IV insulin, especially with renal worsening—merits prospective protocol evaluation. A supported retrospective association alone does not authorize a bedside alert or insulin change.

The future solver must construct the cohort and all transitions; fit the transparent and temporal models below; estimate absolute and relative six-hour risk with uncertainty; execute reverse-timing, monitoring, rescue, severity, hospital and documentation checks; and emit auditable tables, predictions and conclusion labels. Confirmation is not required for completion.

## Population, anchors, and temporal boundaries

Use adults age >=18 (`> 89` -> 90), first ICU visit `unitvisitnumber == 1`, with transition time `T >= 360` minutes, `unitdischargeoffset >= T+360`, a pre-anchor glucose >=70 available in [T-120,T], and at least one glucose in (T,T+360]. Keep repeated anchors; group all rows for a `uniquepid` in one split and cluster inference by patient and stay.

A nutrition state is a `nurseCare` row where `celllabel='Nutrition'` and `cellattribute='Nutrition'`, using `nursecareoffset` as clinical time and requiring `abs(nursecareentryoffset-nursecareoffset)<=60`. Freeze a clinician-reviewed exact state map before outcomes. Primary carbohydrate-present states are enteral, enteral and IV, regular diet, clear liquids, full liquids, calorie controlled, diabetic diet, renal diet, low sodium diet, and cardiac diet. TPN/PPN are excluded from primary analysis because composition/delivery is uncertain; include in a separately adjudicated sensitivity. Unmapped state values are unknown.

An interruption anchor is a carbohydrate-present state followed 1–720 minutes later by explicit NPO. A continuation anchor is a carbohydrate-present state followed 1–720 minutes later by another carbohydrate-present state. The later row defines T. Do not infer state between distant rows beyond 12 hours; no row, late entry, or a gap is unknown—not NPO. A secondary high-specificity analysis uses enteral/enteral-and-IV only.

Active insulin requires the last `infusionDrug` row at or before T to have `drugname` containing insulin and an explicit `units/hr` label, finite `drugrate >0`, and age <=120 minutes. Require a second insulin chart within the prior six hours to reject isolated entries. Exclude labels containing bolus, TPN, units/kg, ml/hr, mg or mcg. A zero rate is an observed stop, never a dose. The primary comparison is restricted to common support of prior glucose (70–300), insulin rate, time since last rate, ICU day, and renal trajectory.

Primary endpoint: first `labname in {'glucose','bedside glucose'}`, mg/dL, <70 in (T,T+360]. Severe endpoint: <54. Report 0–2, 2–4 and 4–6-hour cumulative incidence. A result is available only at `max(labresultoffset, nonmissing labresultrevisedoffset)`; the clinical event time remains `labresultoffset`. Exclude implausible <=10 or >1000 values and exact duplicate `labid`; retain simultaneous lab/bedside conflicts for adjudication sensitivity (minimum versus venous-only).

Renal trajectory uses all numeric creatinine values available by T in [T-2880,T]. Primary continuous features are latest value, 48-hour slope, absolute change and ratio from first to last. The prespecified modifier is >=0.3 mg/dL absolute rise or >=50% relative rise with >=2 values. This is an observed creatinine trajectory, not KDIGO AKI: baseline, urine output and dialysis timing are incomplete.

## Transparent baseline and estimand

Primary hospital: hospital meeting support thresholds selected without outcomes. For the prespecified baseline estimand, use all eligible primary-hospital anchors; no outcome-driven variable selection is allowed. Estimate coefficients and uncertainty with a patient-cluster bootstrap, and evaluate optimism with 10-fold grouped cross-fitting by `uniquepid`, stratified by ICU day and transition type. The temporal alternative uses the same ten frozen folds for out-of-fold predictions, tuning only within each training fold. Repeated anchors are allowed; a first-eligible-anchor-per-stay sensitivity removes informative repetition.

Fit pooled discrete-time complementary-log-log hazards in six one-hour intervals. Covariates available at T: interruption, hour since T, their interaction; restricted cubic splines for latest glucose, glucose slope and time since glucose; insulin rate, rate slope/AUC and time since rate; creatinine latest/change/slope; ICU day; age/sex; diabetes evidence; APACHE; ventilation/dialysis; corticosteroid and vasopressor evidence; nutrition state; hospital/site is fixed by primary restriction. Include interruption×renal-rise and interruption×post-T insulin-reduction time-varying interactions. The principal estimand is standardized six-hour risk difference and ratio for interruption versus continuation over common-support anchors, with 95% patient-cluster bootstrap CIs. The renal interaction is secondary and must report stratum-specific absolute risks.

A patient fixed-effect conditional logistic/self-controlled sensitivity compares interruption and continuation anchors among the 288 audited stays having both transition types, conditioned on stay and matched on ICU day, prior glucose and insulin rate. It controls time-invariant patient factors but not time-varying severity or monitoring.

Do not condition the primary model on post-T insulin reduction, glucose testing or rescue; they are responses/mediators. Use them only in stratified process analyses.

## Substantive temporal alternative

Fit a landmark discrete-time joint observation/outcome model on the identical anchors, splits, six-hour horizon and inputs. A 16–32-unit GRU (or causal temporal convolution if it wins development log loss) ingests 30-minute bins from T-12 h through T+6 h. Up to T it receives glucose/creatinine values and masks, insulin unit/hour rate and rate-change mask, explicit nutrition state, vitals, vasopressor/corticosteroid indicators and static covariates. After T it receives only insulin rate/stop, nutrition state/resumption and measurement masks—not post-T glucose values—to avoid leaking the outcome. Two heads estimate (a) interval hypoglycemia hazard and (b) glucose-measurement hazard. Inverse-intensity weighting, truncated at 10, is learned from the measurement head; an unweighted model and a joint shared-frailty sensitivity bound informative ascertainment. Five seeds, hidden sizes {16,32}, dropout {0,.2}, learning rates {3e-4,1e-3}, <=100 epochs/patience 10; tune only development/calibration log loss and calibration.

This alternative is scientifically useful only if it improves cross-fitted integrated Brier score/calibration and changes the inferred timing or renal/insulin-adjustment interaction, because the baseline loses ordered rate changes, nonlinear clearance-like accumulation and informative glucose-check timing. A merely higher AUROC does not justify it. Mechanistic PK simulation is deferred: eICU lacks formulation/concentration confirmation, delivered dose and nutrition carbohydrate rate needed to identify insulin action/clearance. Those data would justify revisiting a mechanistic model.

## Bias, falsification, and interpretation

- **Order-versus-delivery:** primary insulin comes only from explicit unit/hour `infusionDrug`; `medication` insulin orders are a negative-control exposure. If order-only associations equal primary associations, reject delivery-specific interpretation.
- **Nutrition documentation:** require observed fed→NPO or fed→fed transitions and timely entry. Repeat with <=15-minute entry lag, enteral-only states, corroborating Route-Status/intake rows, and excluding simultaneous contradictory states. Missing remains unknown.
- **Reverse timing:** exclude any glucose <70 or dextrose/glucagon order/infusion in [T-120,T]; test pseudo-anchors at T-6 h and T+6 h. An effect preceding NPO or disappearing after entry-lag restriction supports reverse timing/documentation artifact.
- **Monitoring:** report testing probability, delay and counts by group; use measurement-hazard weighting and best/worst-case bounds for unobserved outcomes. If interruption predicts testing but not hypoglycemia under supported ascertainment, reject the biological hazard claim.
- **Renal severity:** adjust dynamic creatinine continuously; report APACHE/dialysis/urine and shock sensitivities. The interaction must be directionally stable and not wholly driven by dialysis or baseline severe illness.
- **Insulin response:** report insulin reduction/zero within 30/60/120 minutes. A hazard concentrated where insulin stays positive is supportive of a preventable mismatch; attenuation after prompt reduction is not causal proof because clinicians select actions.
- **Rescue:** medication dextrose/glucagon is order-only and D10/glucagon infusion capture is sparse. Post-T rescue is a positive-control consequence; pre-T rescue triggers exclusion. No claim of rescue effectiveness.
- **Hospital concentration:** primary is within hospital 420 selected by support. Other sites are descriptive. Failure to reproduce direction in any adequately supported future site is adverse to generalization.

Support requires: cross-fitted standardized six-hour risk higher after interruption with a two-sided 95% CI excluding zero; no pre-anchor/pseudo-anchor association; acceptable overlap and calibration; direction maintained under timely-entry, enteral-only and monitoring bounds; and renal stratum estimates compatible with a larger excess under rising creatinine. The renal interaction CI may include zero and is then inconclusive, not disproved.

Adverse results include no excess with sufficiently narrow CI (exclude >=3 percentage-point risk difference), an effect before rather than after T, disappearance under timely/corroborated documentation, order-only equivalence, or an association explained by measurement probability. Any of these rejects the corresponding hazard/interpretation even if another model predicts well.

Inconclusive results include fewer than 10 primary interruption outcomes overall, CI spanning both zero and +3 points, positivity failure, unstable hospital support, high model-seed variation, or discordant ascertainment bounds. The audit suggests sparsity is likely; severe <54 is descriptive unless support increases.
