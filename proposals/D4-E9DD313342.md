# Provisional hypothesis: fat-distribution divergence survives rival explanations

## Proposed relationship

Among UK Biobank participants with valid second-assessment MRI body-composition measurements, visceral fat and posterior-thigh muscle fat infiltration (MFI) may identify different subsequent health trajectories: visceral fat may be more specifically associated with incident metabolic disease, while MFI may be more specifically associated with later functional decline or falls. The test is whether these outcome-specific contrasts remain after adjustment for total adiposity and prespecified baseline severity, measurement timing, MRI-selection characteristics, and outcome-ascertainment limitations.

This is a provisional hypothesis, not an observed result. No association, effect size, or clinical conclusion has been computed in this branch.

## Evidence and uncertainty

The assigned parent links three inspected works: [K1] supports associations between myosteatosis/MFI and metabolic and functional outcomes; [K2] supports the broader but heterogeneous relevance of IMAT/MFI across metabolic, cardiovascular, and oncologic contexts; [K3] supports that UKB abdominal MRI phenotypes, including visceral fat, improve internal prediction of incident type 2 diabetes. These sources motivate the question but do not establish the proposed divergence, causality, or clinical utility. The parent’s bounded feasibility diagnostics report 36,320 complete second-assessment MRI/total-fat participants, 4,557 with any third-assessment grip, and 4,564 with third-assessment falls; these are feasibility counts, not hypothesis results.

The strongest rival explanations are: (1) baseline disease severity or frailty already differs between fat-distribution groups; (2) the MRI and outcome measurements are not comparable because of timing and assessment instance; (3) selection into MRI is related to health and follow-up; and (4) outcomes are ascertained through different sources, ascertainment windows, and missingness patterns. The child must test these rivals rather than treating adjustment as proof of a mechanism.

## Falsifiable test

Use a prespecified baseline index at the second assessment. Compare a simple total-adiposity model with a composition model adding visceral fat and posterior-thigh MFI, with outcome-specific contrasts for incident metabolic disease and later functional/fall outcomes. Require temporal ordering, exclusion of prevalent disease and baseline functional limitation where measurable, a one-year metabolic lag with no-lag and longer-lag sensitivities, and explicit missingness/ascertainment diagnostics. Evaluate held-out calibration/discrimination and, more importantly, direction, magnitude, uncertainty, and stability of the visceral-versus-MFI contrasts.

Supportive evidence would be stable, outcome-specific contrasts with uncertainty excluding the null and robustness to timing, severity, selection, and ascertainment checks. Adverse evidence would be disappearance, reversal, or instability of the contrasts under these checks. Inconclusive evidence would include sparse events, poor overlap, missing dates, or wide uncertainty. None of these outcomes establishes causality or treatment benefit.
