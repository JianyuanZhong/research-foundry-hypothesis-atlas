# Scientific Proposal: Temporal Laboratory Trajectories Predict Pre-hospitalization Deterioration

## Unresolved Question

Current clinical practice relies heavily on admission laboratory values to assess patient risk and guide initial management. However, admission values represent a single snapshot that may not capture the dynamic nature of disease progression. Patients may have subtle but concerning laboratory trajectory patterns in the days/weeks before admission that are missed by static assessment, potentially leading to inadequate pre-hospitalization risk stratification.

## Clinical Importance

This question has direct clinical importance because:

1. **Early warning opportunity**: Identifying patients at high risk before hospitalization could enable earlier intervention and potentially prevent admission
2. **Risk stratification**: Static admission values may misclassify patients who have concerning trajectory patterns despite normal admission labs
3. **Resource allocation**: Early identification of high-risk patients could inform triage and resource allocation decisions
4. **Understanding disease progression**: Temporal patterns may reveal pathophysiological mechanisms of deterioration that static values miss

## Substantive Advance Over Existing Knowledge

Existing literature focuses on:
- Static admission laboratory values as prognostic markers
- Cross-sectional comparisons of lab abnormalities
- Single-time-point risk scores

This proposal advances by:
1. **Temporal granularity**: Using longitudinal laboratory measurements before admission to define trajectory patterns rather than single values
2. **Trajectory-based phenotyping**: Identifying patients with specific lab trajectory patterns (e.g., accelerating renal decline, rising inflammatory markers)
3. **Early prediction**: Testing whether pre-admission trajectory patterns predict deterioration better than admission values alone
4. **Mechanistic insight**: Understanding which laboratory trajectory patterns are most predictive and potentially actionable

## Hypothesis

**Primary hypothesis**: In patients with acute illness requiring hospitalization, those exhibiting specific pre-admission laboratory trajectory patterns (particularly accelerating decline in renal function, rising inflammatory markers, or worsening hepatic enzymes) have significantly higher risks of subsequent clinical deterioration (ICU admission, readmission within 30 days, or death) compared to patients with stable or improving trajectory patterns, even after adjustment for admission laboratory values.

**Secondary hypothesis**: Specific trajectory patterns (e.g., accelerating vs. stable vs. improving) provide incremental predictive value beyond static admission laboratory values.

## Population

- **Source**: HCC hospital dataset
- **Inclusion**: Patients with documented acute illness requiring hospitalization (diagnoses suggesting acute conditions like pneumonia, heart failure, acute kidney injury, sepsis)
- **Exclusion**: Patients with <14 days of pre-admission laboratory data, patients who die within 24 hours of admission, patients with missing outcome data
- **Study period**: All available HCC admissions
- **Sample**: Approximately 5,000-10,000 patients meeting criteria

## Baseline Comparison

**Baseline model**: Static admission laboratory values
- Uses admission laboratory values (first lab on admission day) to assess patient risk
- Does not capture pre-admission trajectory patterns
- May misclassify patients with concerning pre-admission trends despite normal admission labs

**Proposed model**: Pre-admission laboratory trajectory patterns
- Defines trajectory patterns based on longitudinal lab measurements before admission
- Identifies patients with specific trajectory patterns (accelerating, stable, improving)
- Provides earlier risk stratification
- Captures dynamic disease progression

## Analysis Plan

1. **Population selection**:
   - Identify patients with acute illness requiring hospitalization using diagnosis codes
   - Exclude patients with <14 days of pre-admission laboratory data
   - Exclude patients who die within 24 hours of admission

2. **Trajectory pattern definition**:
   - Group laboratory tests by type (renal, inflammatory, hepatic, etc.)
   - Calculate trajectory slope and acceleration for each test type
   - Define trajectory patterns: accelerating (increasing slope), stable (minimal change), improving (decreasing slope)
   - Create composite trajectory scores for each organ system

3. **Outcome analysis**:
   - Cox proportional hazards models comparing trajectory pattern groups
   - Adjust for admission laboratory values, age, sex, comorbidities
   - Calculate hazard ratios for each trajectory pattern

4. **Incremental value assessment**:
   - Compare predictive performance of trajectory patterns vs. admission values
   - Calculate net reclassification improvement (NRI)
   - Calculate C-statistic improvement

## Falsification Criteria

The hypothesis would be **falsified** if:
1. Pre-admission trajectory patterns show no significant association with subsequent deterioration (p > 0.05 after adjustment)
2. Static admission laboratory values perform equally well or better than trajectory patterns (NRI < 2%, C-statistic difference < 0.02)
3. Trajectory patterns are equally common across all risk subgroups (suggesting no selective risk)

The hypothesis would be **supported** if:
1. Specific trajectory patterns significantly increase risk of subsequent deterioration (adjusted HR > 1.5, p < 0.001)
2. Trajectory patterns provide incremental predictive value beyond admission values (NRI > 5%, C-statistic improvement > 0.03)
3. Specific trajectory patterns (e.g., accelerating renal function) show distinct risk profiles

The hypothesis would be **inconclusive** if:
1. Trajectory patterns show weak associations (HR 1.1-1.3) that may be clinically meaningful but not statistically significant
2. Results are confounded by hospital-level factors or data collection practices
3. Trajectory patterns are rare (<5% of population) limiting statistical power

## Method Comparison

### Baseline: Static Admission Laboratory Values
- **Approach**: Use admission laboratory values as proxy for patient risk
- **Limitations**: Single time-point, ignores pre-admission trajectory, may misclassify patients with concerning trends
- **What it reveals**: Overall organ dysfunction burden at admission

### Proposed: Pre-admission Laboratory Trajectory Patterns
- **Approach**: Define longitudinal trajectory patterns before admission; identify specific patterns
- **Advantages**: Captures disease progression, identifies high-risk patients earlier, provides actionable monitoring framework
- **What it reveals**: Which organ systems are deteriorating before admission, when risk increases

### Scientific Value
The proposed approach provides mechanistic insight into pre-admission disease progression that the baseline cannot capture. While both approaches predict overall outcomes, only the trajectory pattern approach can inform pre-admission risk assessment and potentially enable earlier intervention.
