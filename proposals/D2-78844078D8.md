## 2. Unresolved question and clinical importance

RA drugs are chosen for inflammatory suppression, but the macrophage compartment also carries a protective program: MERTK+/LYVE1+/MRC1+ macrophages enriched for negative regulators of inflammation, with a gene cassette shared with RA drug-free remission synovium [K2]. Kuo et al. showed that most RA medications target HBEGF+ inflammatory macrophages but "in some cases, medication redirected them into a state that is not expected to resolve inflammation" — an unquantified, per-drug-unknown fate [K1]. Fibroblast-conditioned niches instruct macrophage cluster identity [K3], so drug effects on macrophage state may further depend on fibroblast crosstalk.

**Unresolved question: when clinically used RA drugs suppress the harmful macrophage inflammatory module in fibroblast crosstalk, do they concurrently erode the pro-resolving module (non-selective co-suppression), and does this dominance profile differ by drug and by fibroblast context?** K1 reported aggregate medication effects but no per-drug pro-resolving fate; K2 defined the protective module but tested no drugs; K3 predicts context-dependence but in development/organoids, not drug response. Clinically: a drug that co-suppresses both programs achieves inflammation control without a resolution program — consistent with drug-tolerant persistent disease — whereas a benefit-dominant drug would prioritize differently (suppression plus pro-resolving preservation). This directly informs which mechanisms to pair with which drugs.

## 3. Hypothesis (testable claim)

**H2:** In GSE95588 RA macrophage cocultures, drug suppression of the harmful inflammatory module occurs together with proportional erosion of the pro-resolving module — non-selective co-suppression, D_drug,ctx ≈ 0 for most drugs — with any drug-level heterogeneity in D dominated by co-suppression degree rather than selective benefit.
**Rival H2′:** specific drugs (e.g., tofacitinib, methotrexate, leflunomide) are benefit-dominant (d_H<0, d_P≥0, D>0).
**Secondary:** D differs between MTF and MT contexts (drug × context interaction), motivated by [K3].

## 5. Analysis design and falsification

**Primary (MTF context):** per-sample library-size-normalized log module scores → donor-paired deltas per drug vs the same donor's untreated MTF baseline → D = d_P − d_H per drug. (a) Omnibus drug heterogeneity in D via donor-blocked permutation (Friedman-type) across drugs with ≥2 donors; (b) per-drug classification: benefit-dominant (d_H<0, d_P≥0), adverse-dominant (d_P<0, d_H≥0), non-selective co-suppression (both <0, |D| small), no-signal; donor sign-agreement required for any class call; FDR across drugs.
**Secondary (context):** paired D_MTF − D_MT per drug per donor (drugs present in both contexts), donor-blocked test of drug × context interaction.
**Baseline vs alternative (method comparison):** baseline = transparent paired module-score deltas above; alternative = donor-blocked empirical-Bayes gene-level DE (limma-style) on module genes with FDR, revealing whether module-level dominance is driven uniformly or by a few genes — gene-level attribution the baseline loses. With 4 donors a learned model would overfit; the DE model is a pre-specified attribution check at CPU-minutes cost; no GPU needed. Both are fit to the same splits; module-score method is primary because per-drug n=2–3 donors makes gene-level power low.
**Falsification criteria:**
- *Supportive of H2:* majority of drugs classified non-selective (d_H<0, d_P<0, |D| small) with omnibus heterogeneity null, or heterogeneity driven by co-suppression degree not direction.
- *Supportive of H2′:* ≥2 drugs donor-consistently benefit-dominant with significant omnibus heterogeneity.
- *Adverse:* any drug donor-consistently adverse-dominant → deprioritize/monitor for pro-resolving erosion.
- *Inconclusive:* donor inconsistency dominates, or deltas indistinguishable from noise (explicit small-n caveat).
Known HBEGF/NOTCH3/PGDH findings are background, not rebranded as novel.
