# Timestamp-robust early laboratory trajectories and competing liver-directed pathways in HCC

## Decision and substantive repair

The incumbent has a clinically consequential temporal-validity flaw that is repairable with the same read-only HCC sources. It lets a laboratory observed at exactly 48 hours be a predictor of a procedure whose recorded start is exactly 48 hours, although the procedure table contains a large mass of `Start time = 00:00` timestamps. In the first-index HCC population, a bounded source audit found 22,267 R-family and 15,504 E-family relevant procedure rows at 00:00. This is evidence of coarse or convention-generated procedure timing, not proof that every midnight timestamp is imputed. The original 48-hour boundary therefore cannot ensure that the last laboratory precedes the clinical/procedural decision.

This child changes the temporal estimand and landmark rather than merely adding a sensitivity analysis. The primary cohort has no valid liver-directed procedure in [0,72) hours and is still inpatient at 72 hours. The primary laboratory history ends at 24 hours, and the primary outcome is the first eligible pathway in [72,144) hours. This creates a 48-hour recorded-time buffer between the last primary predictor and the procedure window, making the direction of prediction robust to a procedure date being recorded up to approximately one day coarsely. The original [0,48) laboratory / [48,120) outcome is retained only as a labelled bridge sensitivity and cannot overturn the primary interpretation.

## Scientific deliverable and falsifiable hypothesis

The deliverable is a newly fitted, locked, pathway-specific competing-risk prognostic analysis with:

1. a frozen adult first-observed HCC-coded admission cohort at a 72-hour landmark;
2. a versioned exact procedure-family dictionary and timestamp-resolution audit;
3. a transparent admission/pre-admission baseline B0 and an early-summary baseline B1;
4. an irregular coupled hepatic-reserve/renal-stress alternative fitted to the same dated assays and split;
5. held-out E/R/X/D predictions, cause-specific and cumulative-incidence calibration, standardized pathway contrasts, patient-bootstrap uncertainty, and all falsification outputs.

The hypothesis is:

> Among adults with a first observed HCC-coded inpatient admission, no recorded liver-directed procedure in the first 72 hours, and continued inpatient observation at 72 hours, laboratory information recorded in the first 24 hours contains reproducible pathway-differential prognostic information for the first subsequent recorded embolization/interventional (E) versus resection/ablation (R) procedure in hours 72–144. Specifically, a prespecified worsening hepatic/renal-reserve pattern has a larger standardized association with E than with R after admission and pre-admission information.

“Larger” is tested as the prespecified contrast in the two family-specific cause-specific models and in standardized 72-hour post-landmark CIF contrasts; it is not assumed. The endpoint is a local recorded procedure pathway, not treatment indication, equal-eligibility treatment choice, progression, treatment failure, benefit, or necessity.

The strongest available evidence supports only feasibility: the source catalog contains dated encounters, diagnosis-linked HCC lexical proxies, dated labs, and dated procedure rows. A bounded audit under provisional strings found 42,722 first-index encounters, 22,723 with no provisional liver-directed procedure in [0,72), 20,819 still inpatient at 72 hours, and 11,593 provisional first E/R events in [72,144) (1,501 E and 10,092 R). These are discovery diagnostics, not locked study results. The unresolved claim is whether a temporally insulated early laboratory signal differentially predicts the recorded pathway and survives observation-intensity, timestamp, and procedure-dictionary falsification.

A positive result could motivate prospective review of physiologic reserve when multidisciplinary teams plan or triage pathway evaluation. It cannot select E over R, establish eligibility or resectability, or support a treatment effect.

## Population, time origin, outcome and competing risks

Use participant buckets 0–59 for fitting, 60–69 for selection, and 70–79 for the locked internal test. Buckets 80–99 are inaccessible; no external-validation claim is allowed.

1. Construct candidate encounters from `encounters` joined to `diagnoses` on the exact pair (`Patient Master Index`, `Visit Number`). Normalize only whitespace/encoding. Require age `Age >= 18`, parseable native `Admission Time` and `Discharge Time`, `Discharge Time >= Admission Time`, admission in [2011-01-01, 2026-01-01), and at least one frozen lexical HCC diagnosis string. The final diagnosis dictionary must be frozen after an audit of exact strings; diagnosis type is descriptive only.

2. Select the earliest eligible encounter per `patient master index`, ordered by native `admission time` and then `encounter number`. Report duplicate encounter keys, multiplicity and `diagnosis type`, unmatched diagnosis keys, invalid/ongoing times, overlaps, and earlier HCC-coded encounters. Do not silently substitute a later encounter for an invalid earliest one.

3. The primary landmark is admission plus 72 hours. Exclude any candidate with a valid-start procedure in the frozen liver-directed dictionary during [0,72), and require native `Discharge Time >= Admission Time + 72 hours`. A missing/invalid procedure start is not evidence of no procedure; report its count and perform a predeclared unknown-time sensitivity. This entry criterion is a cohort definition, not a predictor.

4. Primary predictors are dated numeric laboratory tokens in [0,24) hours from native `admission time`. The endpoint window is [72,144) hours from admission. A procedure is linked only by exact (`patient master index`, `encounter number`) and onset is native `start time`, never `end time`. Retain rows with `start time <= discharge time`; after-discharge procedure rows are ineligible.

5. Define first-event competing risks:
   - E: exact frozen embolization/interventional therapeutic terms, including explicit hepatic-artery embolization, TACE/chemoembolization and therapeutic hepatic-artery catheter intervention.
   - R: exact frozen hepatic resection or liver-lesion ablation terms, including hepatic resection and explicit liver-tumor/lesion radiofrequency, microwave, cryoablation or percutaneous ethanol ablation.
   - X: ambiguous/mixed liver-directed therapeutic strings or another included liver-directed therapeutic family that cannot be defensibly assigned to E or R. Same-time E+R evidence is X unless an exact composite rule is frozen before modelling.
   - D: discharge before an E/R/X event in [72,144). The subject is at risk at 72; discharge is an observed loss of local inpatient procedural opportunity, not a non-event.
   - administrative censoring: no E/R/X/D by 144 hours.
   
   For a cause-specific E model, R/X/D are competing failures; for R, E/X/D are competing failures. A procedure in [0,72) excludes the subject from this primary landmark rather than being relabelled as a later event.

The primary estimands are (mathrm{CIF}_E(72)) and (mathrm{CIF}_R(72)) from the 72-hour landmark to 144 hours, and the difference between standardized E and R risk contrasts for the frozen worsening-reserve pattern, (Delta_{E-R}). Estimate cause-specific hazards in six-hour bins with X and D as competing causes, then derive CIFs by standardizing over the competing process. Do not interpret a cause-specific hazard ratio as a treatment effect or as a marginal probability.

The primary direction is (Delta_{E-R}>0), with a two-sided 95% interval and a clinically relevant absolute threshold of 1 percentage point in the standardized 72-hour CIF contrast fixed before locked-test inspection. If the scale is unstable because of event support, report the estimate and interval without a binary directional claim. The former 48–120-hour estimand is a bridge sensitivity only.

## Transparent baseline, substantive alternative and selection record

B0 is a transparent cause-specific pooled-logistic model in six-hour bins using age, sex, admitting department, calendar era, time of admission, diagnosis-type descriptors, prior encounter/inpatient counts, prior local liver-directed procedure counts, and exact-assay pre-admission nearest value, recency, count, missingness and slope when supported. No post-admission lab, procedure, order, medication, examination, document, pathology, discharge or length-of-stay information enters B0.

B1 adds only summaries from [0,24): first and last valid value, difference, elapsed time, count, distinct timestamp count, missingness/availability, and slope when at least two timestamps exist, separately for each exact assay. No cross-assay arithmetic is used without identical units (units are absent, so cross-assay contrasts are normally prohibited). B1 is the minimum adequate transparent comparison; it prevents a learned model from claiming novelty over a weak admission-only comparator.

The substantive alternative is a low-dimensional irregular continuous-time latent model over the same early assay tokens, assay identity, elapsed time, qualitative-result/missingness indicators and measurement density. It fits separate latent hepatic-reserve and renal-stress states with patient shrinkage, assay-specific offsets/noise, irregular gaps and cause-specific E/R/X/D heads. Landmark outputs are latent level, recent slope, coupled-state contrast and posterior uncertainty, plus B0 covariates. The states are fitted representations, not diagnoses of hepatic failure or acute kidney injury. This alternative can reveal coupled movement, nonlinear timing and uncertainty that first/last summaries lose, while making observation-process dependence explicit.

Fit only in buckets 0–59; select dictionary, assay semantics, state dimension, regularization and observation-process specification in 60–69; lock buckets 70–79. Patient IDs cannot cross splits. A one-layer GRU/temporal-attention model over identical inputs is deferred, not prohibited: it may reveal nonlinear order interactions, but the latent alternative answers the current trajectory question with lower compute and more auditable state outputs. Revisit requires a new scientific child if it changes the estimand. Text/image models are deferred because local examinations are narrative CSV rather than images, documents have no native times, pathology has no native time, and expert labels are unavailable. No method is rejected because of neural or GPU implementation.

Use paired patient bootstrap 95% intervals with at least 500 resamples for B1−B0, latent−B1, CIF contrasts and (Delta_{E-R}). Report absolute risks, event prevalence and uncertainty, not only relative metrics.

Predeclare these falsifications:

1. Repeat the primary analysis after adding/removing measurement count, missingness, time gaps and observation-process terms. Disappearance after these adjustments is surveillance/selection evidence, not organ-state evidence.
2. Permute lab timestamps within patient and exact assay while preserving values and assay identity. A timing claim should attenuate; unchanged performance indicates value or density rather than trajectory timing.
3. Use pre-admission timestamps as a negative timing control and verify no early token is outside [0,24).
4. Repeat with exact same-assay/same-timestamp aggregation and after duplicate-row exclusion.
5. Compare the timestamp-robust [0,24)→[72,144) design with the incumbent [0,48]→[48,120) bridge and with removal of procedure rows recorded at 00:00. If the association exists only in the boundary-sensitive design, label it timing/recording artifact.
6. Retain X as a competing event and separately report an X-excluded estimand. If conclusions depend on forcing mixed or ambiguous strings into E or R, the pathway claim fails.
7. Leave out one exact procedure string or low-frequency family at a time, reporting row/patient/encounter contribution; freeze exclusions before looking at locked performance.
8. Use central-line/venipuncture-only workflow terms as a negative-control endpoint. Comparable discrimination implicates care intensity rather than pathway physiology.
9. Stratify by admitting department, calendar era, assay availability and measurement density; these are robustness descriptions, not causal effect modification.
10. Use early orders only in a labelled planned-care/leakage audit. They cannot be called intent labels or entered in the primary model.
11. Compare the competing-risk model with a naive model that ignores discharge/competing events as a negative analysis; do not use the naive estimate as primary.
12. Preserve the parent composite endpoint as a bridge only.

## Interpretation and failure criteria

Supportive computational evidence requires a coherent frozen E/R/X dictionary; adequate events (at least 100 for each primary cause after locked exclusions); a nonzero, directionally coherent (Delta_{E-R}) whose two-sided interval and 1-point threshold satisfy the preregistered criterion; calibrated held-out B1 predictions over B0 for at least one pathway without major failure in the other; and, if the latent model is claimed as a substantive advance, a prespecified calibrated/CIF or risk-stratification improvement over B1 with uncertainty. The primary direction must persist after the 48-hour buffer and not be explained by timestamp permutation, measurement intensity, one era/department/assay/string, X classification or the negative-control endpoint. This supports external validation and clinical adjudication, not treatment selection.

Adverse evidence includes a null/opposite pathway contrast, poor or unstable CIF calibration, no information beyond B0/B1, disappearance after observation-process adjustment, persistence after timestamp permutation, comparable negative-control performance, or dependence on midnight timestamps or one procedure string. A negative result is informative about the limits of this EHR signal.

Inconclusive evidence includes inadequate event support, inability to freeze exact semantics, excessive missing/invalid procedure times, substantial X events, incomplete discharge ascertainment, insufficient [0,24) assay support, nonconvergence, unstable CIF weighting, or intervals too wide to distinguish a meaningful difference from no difference. Inconclusive is not evidence for the hypothesis.

Computationally checkable claims are hashes, row/join audits, exact dictionaries, cohort flow, timestamp distributions, split integrity, leakage absence, model convergence, predictions, calibration, CIFs, bootstrap intervals and falsification outputs. These sources cannot establish validated HCC diagnosis, pathology, radiographic stage, tumor burden, vascular invasion, treatment intent, scheduling, multidisciplinary recommendation, resectability, procedure necessity, benefit, toxicity, mortality, transplant eligibility, recurrence, progression, organ failure, AKI, or complete outside-hospital capture. Those claims require expert review, acquisition-timestamped imaging/pathology and laboratory units/reference ranges, indication/scheduling fields, mortality/transplant linkage, external validation, and a prospective clinical or causal/implementation study.
