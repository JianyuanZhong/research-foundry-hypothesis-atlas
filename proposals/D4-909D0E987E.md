# UKB panel-change after established single-domain disease: selection-aware landmark progression

## Successor purpose and scientific deliverable

This targeted child preserves the assigned two-panel UKB question and repairs a material estimand problem in the parent. The parent excluded second-domain events and death during the year after panel 1, then began follow-up. That makes the primary comparison conditional on a future outcome-free year and can remove precisely the early progression that a panel-change signal might predict. The repaired primary analysis starts follow-up at the panel-1 date; the one-year lag is retained as a clearly labeled reverse-causation sensitivity, not silently built into the main estimand.

The future solver must newly construct and freeze the source/header audit, exact ICD code/date phenotype, panel dates and gap, stable single-domain cohort, repeat-panel selection diagnostics and weights, administrative censoring date, event flow, matched competing-risk models, locked predictions, uncertainty, phenotype and lag sensitivities, falsification tests, and a conclusion classified as supportive, adverse, or inconclusive. Completion is the computed comparison and its uncertainty-linked interpretation, not a successful fit, a positive coefficient, or readiness alone.

## Unresolved question and hypothesis

Available evidence supports associations between metabolic measurements and later cardiometabolic disease, and the local UKB snapshot contains two dated biomarker instances for a subset of participants. It does not establish whether a change between those panels adds route-specific prognostic information after the latest level and an already established domain are accounted for. Administrative code/date fields also do not establish clinical onset.

Primary prognostic hypothesis:

> Among UKB participants aged 40–69 at recruitment who have exactly one administrative cardiometabolic domain (diabetes, cardiovascular disease, or chronic kidney disease) at both biomarker panels, are alive and free of a second domain at panel 1, and have complete paired HbA1c, triglyceride, and HDL measurements, worsening between panels adds information about the first subsequent transition to a second domain beyond panel-1 levels, existing-domain identity, disease duration, and prespecified covariates. The added information may differ by route, but should not be comparable for acute appendicitis.

This is a conditional, internal prognostic estimand among participants with an observed repeat panel and a stable one-domain state at panel 1. It is not a causal effect of changing a biomarker, a claim about biological onset, a treatment recommendation, or evidence that monitoring improves outcomes. The strongest available conclusion remains association/prediction from coded administrative outcomes.

## Phenotype, time boundaries, and estimands

Before fitting, normalize code case/whitespace and freeze this codebook:

- diabetes: ICD-10 E10–E14; ICD-9 250;
- cardiovascular disease: ICD-10 I20–I25, I50, I60–I69; ICD-9 410–414, 428, 430–438;
- chronic kidney disease: ICD-10 N18/N19; ICD-9 585;
- negative-control acute appendicitis: ICD-10 K35; ICD-9 540.

Scan every listed suffix. Pair a code and date only at the same suffix and require a valid parsed date; a code-only or date-only value is audited but creates no event. A domain's first date is the minimum valid paired date, not the first array position. These are first coded/recorded dates, not adjudicated onset dates.

Use participants with recruitment age `21022-0.0` 40–69 inclusive, valid `31-0.0`, valid main and assessment panel dates `d0=53-0.0` and `d1=53-1.0` with `d0<d1` and a 365–3,650 day gap, and agreement of main/assessment dates after normalization. Require complete finite values for the three core markers at both main instances 0 and 1. At d0 and d1 there must be exactly one of the three domains, the same domain, and its first coded date must be no later than d0. Death by d1 excludes entry. Define duration as `(d1-first_domain_date)/365.25`; include it in every model. No biomarker instances 2/3 are inferred.

Primary time zero is d1. Follow to the first newly coded second domain, death, administrative censoring, or five years after d1. Death is an absorbing competing event; a same-day domain/death tie is assigned to death, with the reverse tie rule as sensitivity. Do not remove events or deaths occurring in the first year after d1 from the primary analysis. After the source audit, compute and freeze `C_admin` as the latest finite date in the audited outcome/death date fields (`41280-*`, `41281-*`, `40000-*`) within the snapshot; report its value and date-range audit before splitting. This is an administrative observation limit, not proof of complete follow-up.

The one-year delayed-entry analysis is secondary: among primary-cohort participants who remain alive and without a second coded domain through d1+365, start follow-up at d1+365 and use the same five-year horizon from d1+365. It addresses reverse causation but is explicitly a survivor/landmark estimand. Additional lag (0, 2, and 5 years), gap (182–5,475 days), and stable-domain definitions are sensitivity analyses, not replacements for the primary estimand.

For marker j, compute annualized change `g_j=(x_j1-x_j0)/((d1-d0)/365.25)`; reverse HDL so worsening is positive. Standardize levels and changes with fit-partition parameters only. Panel-1 values are the current level; duration and gap are measured at the landmark.

## Matched baseline and substantive alternative

All models use the same primary cohort, time origin, routes, death hazard, split, censoring, weights, preprocessing, uncertainty, and locked evaluation.

**B0, matched current-level baseline.** Fit route-specific cause-specific Cox hazards for each existing-domain to each new-domain route and an explicit death hazard. Inputs are panel-1 HbA1c, triglycerides, HDL, existing domain, disease duration, age, sex, panel gap, BMI, systolic/diastolic BP, smoking, and center. Use prespecified fit-frozen restricted cubic splines for continuous variables; no change variables enter B0. A creatinine/cystatin-C extension is secondary because their exact fields are available in main but may have poorer support.

**M1, marker-specific temporal alternative.** Use the identical hazards and inputs as B0 and add the three annualized changes, with change-by-existing-domain interactions only if specified before locked testing and route support is adequate. This directly tests whether dated history adds information beyond the current level and disease duration.

**M2, mechanistic coordinated-deterioration alternative.** In the fit partition, standardize HbA1c, triglycerides, and reverse-HDL and define a fixed equal-weight metabolic deterioration score `S_t=mean(z_HbA1c,t,z_TG,t,z_reverseHDL,t)`; define `deltaS=S_1-S_0`. Fit the same route/death Cox architecture as B0 with panel-1 individual levels plus `deltaS` and prespecified `deltaS × existing-domain` terms. M2 tests a substantive alternative scientific explanation: progression may be signaled by coordinated worsening of a common metabolic burden rather than by one marker or a freely estimated set of three changes. It can reveal common-axis route heterogeneity that B0 loses; comparison with M1 distinguishes common coordinated change from marker-specific information. The score is a mechanistic summary, not a validated clinical index.

A two-panel recurrent/temporal-attention model is deferred. It would use the same six marker values, covariates, target, split, uncertainty, and metrics and could reveal nonlinear cross-marker/route interactions, but two observations provide little sequence structure and added capacity cannot resolve repeat-testing indication or coded-onset uncertainty. Revisit only after at least three verified dated assay panels or a bounded locked diagnostic shows stable value beyond M1/M2. The four-instance slope/mixed-effects branch is not feasible because the verified main header has no assay instances 2/3. This is a scientific adequacy decision, not a neural-network or GPU ban.

Refit the selection model and full pipeline inside each bootstrap where feasible. Evaluate hypothetical review thresholds 5%, 10%, and 20% with alert rate, sensitivity, specificity, PPV, number needed to review, calibration, and decision-curve net benefit. These thresholds have no validated action, burden, cost, or external validation and cannot establish clinical utility.

**Supportive:** In a route with prespecified minimum support (at least 20 total route events and at least 5 locked-test events), worsening change has a 95% interval excluding the null in the prespecified direction and the locked test shows an uncertainty interval for incremental integrated Brier score below zero for M1 or M2 versus B0; the result does not reverse under the weighted or one-year-lag sensitivity, and acute appendicitis does not show a comparable signal. This supports incremental internal prognostic information only.

**Adverse:** the change association is null/reversed with informative precision, incremental locked prediction is worse, the signal disappears after measured duration/selection adjustment, acute appendicitis shows a comparable association, or within-stratum change permutation produces a comparable signal. This falsifies the proposed incremental prognostic claim in the selected population, not all biology or causal effects.

**Inconclusive:** fewer than the route-support minimum, inadequate repeat-panel positivity/effective sample size, convergence or calibration failure, intervals spanning meaningful benefit and no benefit, unstable administrative dates/code pairing, or a supportive primary result that reverses under a key lag/selection/phenotype sensitivity. A threshold result without an expert-approved action remains inconclusive for policy value.

Computationally checkable claims are hashes and headers, joins, code/date rules, cohort flow, dates/gaps/duration, selection diagnostics, fitted models, predictions, uncertainty, calibration, Brier, decision curves, permutations, and sensitivity outputs. Confirmation of disease onset, treatment/adherence effects, reasons for repeat testing, clinical utility, transportability to non-repeat-tested people, and policy validity require clinical adjudication, additional evidence, or another study.
