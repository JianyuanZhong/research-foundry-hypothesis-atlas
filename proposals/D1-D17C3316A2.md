## The unresolved question and why it matters

ICU patients are monitored by repeated lab draws, and clinicians decide how often to draw.
[K1] Agniel 2018 (269k/669,452-patient cohorts) showed the *presence and timing* of a test
order — not only its value — carries survival signal (order presence significant in
233/272 tests; timing beat the value in 118/174). Monitoring intensity therefore encodes a
care process. The consequential, still-unresolved question for this multi-hospital cohort is:

> When a patient's own lab-draw rate **falls** in hours 12–24 while they remain clinically
> unstable, is that a **patient-level leading indicator of imminent deterioration (H1)**, or
> does it reflect a **hospital-level surveillance fingerprint (R2)**, **clinical improvement /
> recovery (R3)**, or **pending discharge / end-of-life planning (R4)**?

This matters because if H1 holds, reduced monitoring is a modifiable red flag; if a rival
holds, treating draw-rate drops as alarms would misdirect attention. The parent framed H1 and
the R2 (between-hospital) rival with a multilevel random-slope discriminator and leave-one-
hospital-out (LOHO) transport. This child adds the two tests that can actually separate H1
from its strongest rivals on this data.

## Leading claim vs strongest rival, and the discriminating observations

- **H1 (leading claim, patient-level leading indicator):** within a person, a lower draw-rate
  ratio predicts higher (24,48]h deterioration, and the effect is **concentrated** in patients
  who are still physiologically unstable at the hour-24 landmark.
- **R2 (strongest structural rival, practice fingerprint):** draw-rate ratio is a hospital/
  unit surveillance habit; between-hospital variance dominates and LOHO transport collapses.
- **R3 (recovery/severity rival):** the drop tracks concurrent improvement and lower severity;
  the crude association disappears (or reverses) after conditioning on the measured trajectory.
- **R4 (disposition rival):** the drop precedes discharge/EOL, so outcomes are administratively
  determined, not clinically.

Discriminating observations (pre-specified):
1. **Shape test:** H1 predicts a monotone gradient (lowest ratio → highest (24,48]h risk).
   R3 predicts a flat or **inverse** pattern (bigger drops → lower risk) because de-escalation
   marks recovery; R-rev (clinician-driven escalation) predicts risk highest at the *high* end.
2. **Negative control:** under H1 the drop's risk signal is ~null in the *stable* subgroup
   (low hour-24 instability) and present in the unstable subgroup (ratio × instability
   interaction). Under R2/R3/R4 the drop predicts similarly in both strata.
3. **Transport:** under H1 the within-hospital patient-level effect survives LOHO; under R2
   discrimination/penalties collapse under LOHO.

- Crude (24,48]h mortality by draw-rate-ratio quartile (Q1 = largest drop, Q4 = largest rise):
  **Q1 1.04%, Q2 1.25%, Q3 2.33%, Q4 1.21%** (n≈29.7k each).

**This crude pattern is non-monotone and does NOT support naive H1** (the biggest drop, Q1,
has the *lowest* mortality). That is exactly why the shape and negative-control tests above
are needed: the data may favor R3 (de-escalation = recovery) or R-rev (escalation = instability)
rather than H1. This child therefore treats the shape verdict as a first-class outcome and
pre-commits to reporting a **falsification of H1** as a valuable result.

## Population, estimand, outcomes, baselines (frozen)

- **Population:** adult eICU unit stays, age ≥16, `unitdischargeoffset` ≥ 1440 min, decisive
  unit outcome, restricted to hospitals with ≥200 eligible stays. Join key
  `patientunitstayid`; hospital via `patient.hospitalid`.
- **Landmark:** hour 24 (`unitdischargeoffset` > 1440 min and observed ≥ 2880 min or in-window
  death; alive discharges/transfers before 48h evaluated only for the escalation outcome).
- **Primary exposure:** person-centered `R = draws(720,1440] / draws(0,720]` (draws from
  `lab.csv.gz` by `labresultoffset`, uniquely by `labid`).
- **Primary outcome:** (1440,2880] min unit deterioration = in-unit death (`unitdischargestatus`
  = Expired within `unitdischargeoffset`≤2880) or escalation (new vasopressor in
  `infusionDrug.csv.gz`/`treatment.csv.gz` while still admitted).
- **Estimand:** patient-level effect of `log R` on the outcome, with hospital random intercept
  + random slope; reported as a continuous dose–response plus pre-specified quintiles.
- **Baselines:** (i) SIMPLE — fixed-effect logistic regression on measured hour-24 vitals+labs
  (MEWS-like score, creatinine, BUN, WBC, Hgb, platelets, Na, K, Cl, bicarbonate, glucose, Ca,
  Mg, anion gap, APACHE IVa); (ii) SUBSTANTIVE ALTERNATIVE — multilevel random-slope model
  (and a counting-process/point-process model of draw times), which can separate a
  patient-level within-person slope from between-hospital practice variance — information the
  pooled baseline confounds. Compare by change in discrimination, calibration (O:E, slope),
  Brier and net benefit with uncertainty; a small AUC gain alone is not the criterion.
- **Transport:** leave-one-hospital-out; pre-specified collapse thresholds.
- **Falsification:** supportive = monotone patient-level gradient surviving LOHO with the
  instability interaction; adverse = flat/inverse gradient or LOHO collapse (H1 refuted);
  inconclusive = underpowered/non-identifiable (wide CIs, EOL-driven).
