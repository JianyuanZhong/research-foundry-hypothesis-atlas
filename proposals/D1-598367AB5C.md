# Residual instability before ICU-to-ward transfer: separating signal, materiality, observation process, and endpoint meaning

Status: complete experiment proposal; no outcome model has been fit and no hypothesis result is claimed. Parent: `[prior hypothesis]`. Dataset: MIMIC-IV 3.1 core.

## What is changed, and what is fixed

This child preserves the parent's scientific question, population, landmark, exposure window, outcome horizon, exact MIMIC bindings, endpoint-snapshot baseline, interpretable trajectory model, measurement-process controls, and GRU-D alternative. It changes the interpretation contract and component analysis because the parent's conjunctive supportive/adverse labels could incorrectly equate:

1. any statistically detectable incremental information;
2. a prespecified clinically material prediction gain;
3. decision-curve utility;
4. value-dependent physiology-compatible information rather than observation-process information; and
5. a composite whose ICU-return and death components have the same clinical meaning.

Those are now separate reported axes. A real but smaller-than-threshold gain is not called endpoint sufficiency. Failure of one materiality threshold does not erase a result on another axis. An observation-dominant pattern does not erase prediction but narrows its interpretation. The observed endpoint is called **48-hour ICU return or in-hospital death**, not “deterioration,” because MIMIC does not label whether an ICU return was unplanned or whether death reflected a treatment limitation.

Fixed by design:

- first eligible adult ICU-to-general-ward transfer per hospital admission;
- landmark `t0 = icustays.outtime`;
- exposure window exactly `[t0-24h,t0)`;
- outcome horizon exactly `(t0,t0+48h]`;
- the frozen general-ward destination set;
- S0 versus T1 as the primary nested comparison and compact GRU-D L1 as the substantive longitudinal alternative;
- all source archive members, columns, joins, item IDs, and plausibility rules below.

## Clinical opening, evidence boundary, and falsifiable hypothesis

The strongest inspected evidence supports three bounded claims. A recent MIMIC-IV/external-hospital study found better seven-day ICU-readmission/death discrimination with GRU-D++ than SWIFT, but one-day averaging could lose short-term variation; operational factors and DNR status were not fully handled; and the authors explicitly cautioned that readmission and death represent different clinical trajectories [K1]. A systematic review found that stronger ICU-readmission models often used clearly defined outcomes and longitudinal routine predictors, while outcome windows, composite definitions, and risks of bias varied substantially [K2]. EHR test timing and frequency can be more prognostic than measured values because recorded data reflect healthcare processes as well as physiology [K3].

These works do not establish whether persistence or volatility in the final 24 hours before a specific ICU-to-general-ward transfer adds clinically material 48-hour information beyond the final state. They also do not establish whether any gain comes from measured values, observation practices, discharge selection, planned procedures, or treatment-limited care.

**Primary falsifiable hypothesis.** Among adults making their first eligible ICU-to-general-ward transfer in a hospitalization after at least 24 hours in the ICU, prespecified final-24-hour persistence and volatility features from heart rate, blood pressure, respiratory rate, SpO2, temperature, FiO2, oxygen flow, and oxygen-device charting add held-out predictive information for the observed 48-hour composite of ICU return or in-hospital death beyond the same channels' last pre-transfer measurements, recent organ support, measured charting intensity, code-status documentation, and transfer context.

The hypothesis of **incremental information** is distinct from these additional questions:

- Is the gain at least the prespecified materiality threshold?
- Does it improve net benefit at 2%, 5%, or 10% risk thresholds?
- Do measured values add beyond detailed observation timing and masks?
- Is the association carried by ICU return, death, or both?
- Does it persist when PACU-proxied returns and documented treatment limitations are handled separately?

This remains prognostic and measurement-discriminating, not causal. It does not test whether postponing transfer or adding ward monitoring prevents an event.

The leading explanation is unresolved physiologic or oxygen instability. The strongest rivals are observation intensity, discharge selection, treatment-limited care, planned or procedure-associated ICU return, and endpoint sufficiency. The study discriminates them as far as MIMIC permits: nested value models test incremental information; observation-only and permutation analyses test value dependence; component models and sensitivities locate the signal; but planned-return status, goals-of-care truth, and mechanism remain unidentifiable without adjudication.

## Population and fixed landmark

The unit is a hospital admission's **first eligible ICU-to-general-ward transfer**. A subject may contribute more than one hospitalization, but all admissions for one `subject_id` remain in one split and bootstrap cluster.

For every row in `icu/icustays`, set `t0 = outtime`. Join a successor `hosp/transfers` row on exact `subject_id, hadm_id` and `transfers.intime = icustays.outtime`. Eligibility requires:

- age at ICU admission `anchor_age + year(icustays.intime) - anchor_year >= 18`;
- ICU `los >= 1.0` day;
- alive at `t0`: `admissions.deathtime` is null or greater than `t0`;
- exact successor destination in the frozen general-ward set;
- earliest eligible transfer by `outtime, stay_id` within `hadm_id`.

The frozen primary general-ward set is `Medicine`, `Neurology`, `Med/Surg`, `Medicine/Cardiology`, `Transplant`, `Vascular`, `Med/Surg/GYN`, `Surgery/Trauma`, `Med/Surg/Trauma`, `Surgery`, `Cardiac Surgery`, `Medical/Surgical (Gynecology)`, `Surgery/Pancreatic/Biliary/Bariatric`, `Cardiology`, `Thoracic Surgery`, `Oncology`, and `Hematology/Oncology`.

The primary cohort excludes successor ICUs, PACU, emergency/observation, discharge lounge, psychiatry, obstetric/nursery, unknown, and intermediate/step-down locations. A prespecified sensitivity adds, as separate destination strata, `Cardiology Surgery Intermediate`, `Hematology/Oncology Intermediate`, `Medicine/Cardiology Intermediate`, `Neuro Intermediate`, `Neuro Stepdown`, `Surgery/Vascular/Intermediate`, and `Surgical Intermediate`.

The exposure is exactly `[t0-24h,t0)`. Values at or after `t0` are forbidden. The snapshot uses the last valid observation before `t0` and an indicator of freshness within four hours. Post-transfer measurements, future transfers, outcomes, and discharge summaries cannot enter predictors.

## Primary three-state outcome and component estimands

### Primary three-state target

At 48 hours, assign exactly one state:

1. **Observed composite event:** an ICU return in the same `hadm_id` with `icustays.intime > t0` and `<= t0+48h`, or in-hospital death with `admissions.deathtime > t0` and `<= t0+48h`. If both occur, the earlier time defines event chronology, but the primary class remains positive.
2. **Live hospital discharge before event and before 48 hours:** `admissions.dischtime <= t0+48h`, no earlier composite event, and no in-hospital death at discharge.
3. **Alive, in hospital, and event-free at 48 hours.**

The reported primary probability is state 1. State 2 is a competing observed state; it must never be described as 48 hours of ward stability. Death outside the hospital after live discharge is unavailable. Ties are audited: an ICU-return timestamp equal to death time is labeled a same-time composite for chronology, and exact discharge/event ties are reported rather than silently ordered.

### Mandatory component estimands

The composite is decomposed without pretending the components are interchangeable:

- `R48`: probability of any observed ICU return by 48 hours before live hospital discharge. Death before return is a competing event.
- `D48`: probability of in-hospital death by 48 hours, regardless of whether ICU return occurred first. Live hospital discharge is a competing event.
- first-transition counts and cumulative incidences for ICU-return-first, death-before-return, live discharge first, and event-free in hospital at 48 hours;
- the number with both ICU return and death by 48 hours and their temporal order.

Secondary S0 and T1 component models use identical frozen features, split, preprocessing, and penalty selection. They report paired binary Brier differences for `R48` and `D48`, component calibration, and cause-specific cumulative incidence by trajectory-burden quartile. These outputs locate composite performance; they are not separately optimized primary hypotheses.

A gain confined to `R48` is reported as ICU-return information. A gain confined to `D48` is reported as in-hospital-death information. Opposite component directions make the composite interpretation endpoint-heterogeneous; they do not mathematically invalidate its predictive score.

## Planned-return and treatment-limit ambiguity

MIMIC has no validated planned/unplanned ICU-return label. A bounded audit of the exact fixed cohort found 2,002 ICU returns within 48 hours. Of these, 1,653 had no intervening transfer start between `t0` and return, 349 had an intervening transfer start, and 75 had `careunit='PACU'` as the immediate pre-return location. No broader operating-room label appeared as an immediate pre-return `careunit` in this audit. Therefore:

- the narrow **PACU-proxied return** sensitivity removes an ICU-return event when a `hosp/transfers` row has `t0 < intime < return_time` and the latest such row has exact `careunit='PACU'`; a qualifying death remains a death event;
- a pathway table separately reports direct ward-to-ICU returns, returns after any intervening transfer row, and all exact intervening `careunit,eventtype` pairs;
- PACU proximity is a proxy for possible procedure association, not proof of a planned return. Planned direct returns can remain, and unplanned deterioration can occur after PACU.

Definitive planned-return classification requires clinician review of pre-transfer plans, operative/procedure records, and notes. Discharge summaries are not predictors and cannot retrospectively repair the primary label.

Treatment limitation uses the last `chartevents.itemid=223758` value in the index ICU stay with `charttime < t0`, preserving exact text before mapping to full code, DNR/DNI, comfort-measures-only, other, or undocumented. The multinomial model permits class-specific code-status effects. Required analyses are:

- primary: all eligible transfers, with code-status category included;
- no documented limitation: exclude DNR/DNI and comfort-measures-only, retain and explicitly label undocumented;
- documented full-code only;
- component results stratified as documented full code, documented limitation, and undocumented when event support permits.

“Undocumented” is never treated as full code. The audit found code-status charting in the final 24 hours for only 10,191 of 49,157 transfers, so full-code analyses are selected-documentation sensitivities, not a replacement target. If gain is confined to death among documented limitations or disappears in documented full-code patients, the conclusion must name this heterogeneity; it cannot call the composite generic physiologic deterioration. True goals of care, appropriateness of ICU return, and whether death was expected require clinical adjudication.

## Measurements and frozen feature construction

All numeric values undergo fixed plausibility checks; rejected counts are reported: heart rate 20–250/min; SBP 40–300 mmHg; MAP 20–200 mmHg; respiratory rate 4–80/min; SpO2 50–100%; temperature 30–43 °C after Fahrenheit conversion; oxygen flow 0–100 L/min; FiO2 0.21–1.0 after dividing values >1 and <=100 by 100. No outcome-informed winsorization is allowed.

Create twelve left-closed two-hour bins in `[t0-24h,t0)`; aggregate continuous values by median. For BP prefer invasive over noninvasive within a bin and retain source. Preserve masks, record count, distinct chart hours, time since last observation, median/IQR `storetime-charttime`, and distinct `caregiver_id` count.

Prespecified abnormal states are heart rate <50 or >110/min; SBP <90 or MAP <65 mmHg; respiratory rate <10 or >24/min; SpO2 <92%; temperature <36.0 or >38.3 °C; and elevated oxygen support defined by FiO2 >0.30, oxygen flow >4 L/min, or an exact observed device value mapped before model fitting to high-flow, reservoir/face-mask, noninvasive positive-pressure, or invasive-airway support. Missing oxygen documentation is unknown, never room air. Before opening test outcomes, freeze `oxygen_device_dictionary.csv` containing every observed item 226732 value, count, exact normalized mapping, and unmapped values; fuzzy substring expansion is forbidden.

For each channel calculate:

- abnormal observed-bin fraction;
- longest consecutive abnormal run, with missing bins breaking a run;
- normal-to-abnormal and abnormal-to-normal transitions;
- 90th–10th percentile range and median absolute successive difference, scaled using training data;
- Theil–Sen slope and early-12-hour minus late-12-hour median;
- worst value and final four-hour value;
- observed-bin count, record count, time since last observation, and missing-bin fraction.

Features needing fewer than four observed bins are missing with an indicator. Report persistence lower/upper bounds treating missing bins as normal/abnormal as a measurement-uncertainty diagnostic only.

The frozen trajectory-burden score is the mean across six domains—heart rate, BP, respiratory rate, SpO2, temperature, oxygen support—of training-empirical percentile ranks for four equally weighted quantities: abnormal-bin fraction, longest abnormal run/12, median absolute successive difference, and transition count/11. It requires at least four covered domains; training-derived quartile cut points are frozen.

Recent treatment context includes interval overlap with the last 24 hours and time since end for invasive ventilation and selected vasopressors. Exclude `statusdescription='Rewritten'`; require positive `rate` or `amount` for input events. Include destination, source ICU, latest service at/before `t0`, ICU LOS, prior ICU count in the admission, admission type, transfer hour, and night (19:00–06:59). Do not use weekday because subject-specific date shifts invalidate cross-subject calendar meaning. ICU occupancy, discharge intent, ward staffing, rapid-response availability, and transport delay are unavailable.

## Models on the same frozen question

### S0: endpoint-snapshot and process-aware comparator

Fit ridge-penalized multinomial logistic regression with age, sex, admission type, source/last ICU, destination, latest service, ICU LOS, prior ICU count, transfer hour/night, each channel's last value and four-hour freshness, last GCS components, recent ventilation/vasopressor state, code-status category, and measurement-density/recency/store-lag/caregiver variables. Continuous terms use training-fitted restricted cubic splines where prespecified. Impute with training medians plus indicators.

### T1: interpretable trajectory model and primary comparison

T1 adds only the frozen persistence, run, transition, volatility, slope, early-late, and worst-value features to S0. Select penalty on validation multinomial log loss. The primary scientific contrast is paired test `T1 versus S0`.

### O1 and M: observation-process controls

M uses static transfer context plus counts, masks, recency, store lag, and caregiver counts, but no measured physiological values. O1 adds to S0, for each channel, the twelve two-hour observed/not-observed indicators, elapsed time since the preceding observation at each bin, longest missing-bin run, early-versus-late record-count difference, and count of observation-state transitions; it adds no measured-value trajectory. Ridge penalization and validation selection are identical to T1. O1 is the matched observation-process expansion for T1.

### L1: substantive longitudinal alternative

Fit a compact GRU-D to the same twelve bins with values, masks, and elapsed-time channels plus identical S0 static context and the same three-state target. It can reveal nonlinear duration, cross-channel lag, and late relapse lost by summaries. This is an adaptation, not a reproduction of [K1].

Freeze before test evaluation: one or two recurrent layers; hidden size {32,64}; dropout {0,0.2}; Adam learning rate {1e-3,3e-4}; validation multinomial-log-loss early stopping; five fixed seeds. Calibrate on validation data with multinomial temperature scaling. Open the test set once.

Required L1 comparisons are masks-only L1; values with fixed two-hour spacing and no masks in the dense stratum; and within-stratum sequence-value permutation while retaining each patient's mask/timestamps, stratified by destination, S0 risk decile, and measurement-density quintile. L1 is scientifically preferred only if calibrated and materially better than T1 and if measured values account for the gain. Otherwise T1 is preferred. Gradient-boosted summaries remain a fallback if T1 gains but GRU optimization is unstable.

## Split, estimation, and uncertainty

Assign `subject_id`, not rows, 60% train, 20% validation, 20% test with seed `20260923`, stratified at subject level by any qualifying composite event and dominant destination group. Keep all admissions for a subject together. Fit preprocessing, splines, imputers, penalties, mappings, model selection, and calibration without test outcomes.

Define primary paired test-set improvement:

`delta_B = Brier_composite(S0) - Brier_composite(T1)`.

Also estimate the standardized 48-hour composite-risk difference between top and bottom frozen trajectory-burden quartiles, standardized to the test distribution of S0 covariates. Report `Brier(T1)-Brier(L1)`, multinomial log loss, AUROC/AUPRC for the composite class, calibration intercept/slope and plots, component metrics, and decision-curve net-benefit differences `NB(T1)-NB(S0)` at 2%, 5%, and 10%.

Use 2,000 subject-cluster bootstrap replicates for paired 95% intervals, rerunning test-set standardization but not tuning. Report failed replicates. Destination and component heterogeneity are prespecified secondary analyses. No univariate predictor screening is allowed; use Benjamini–Hochberg for secondary coefficient families while retaining effect sizes and intervals.

Dense observation requires heart rate, BP, respiratory rate, and SpO2 in at least eight distinct hours and at least one oxygen-support observation. Report channel-specific support. Additional frozen sensitivities are no documented treatment limitation, documented full-code only, daytime only, general ward plus step-down with destination interaction, first eligible transfer per subject, and PACU-proxied-return removal.

## Separated result and falsification contract

The solver must produce a result vector, not one conjunctive label.

### Axis A: incremental prognostic information

- **Detected:** lower 95% bound for `delta_B` is >0.
- **Adverse:** upper 95% bound is <=0.
- **Unresolved:** otherwise, including intervals that cross 0.

This axis answers whether T1 predicts the observed composite better than S0. It does not answer clinical magnitude, mechanism, or utility.

### Axis B: prespecified materiality

For Brier improvement:

- **Material gain demonstrated:** lower 95% bound for `delta_B` is >=0.002.
- **Material gain ruled out:** upper 95% bound is <0.002.
- **Materiality unresolved:** otherwise.

For the standardized top-versus-bottom burden risk difference, independently use the analogous 2-percentage-point threshold. Report both. A statistically detected `delta_B` whose upper bound is below 0.002 is **small incremental information with material gain ruled out**, not endpoint sufficiency and not a null result. If one materiality measure passes and the other does not, report discordance; do not force a global supportive/adverse label.

### Axis C: decision utility

At each threshold separately classify the bootstrap interval for `NB(T1)-NB(S0)` as positive, nonpositive, or imprecise. Positive net benefit at one threshold is reported at that threshold only. No requirement for two of three thresholds is imposed.

### Axis D: observation-process versus measured-value dependence

Report paired intervals for:

- `Brier(O1)-Brier(T1)`: whether measured-value trajectories beat a detailed observation-only expansion;
- `Brier(T1_permuted)-Brier(T1)`: whether destroying patient-specific values while retaining masks/timing harms performance;
- `Brier(S0)-Brier(T1)` in the dense stratum;
- M and masks-only L1 performance.

Value-dependent, physiology-compatible information requires positive T1-over-O1 and permutation-harm intervals plus a positive dense-stratum direction. Failure of these conditions does not erase any Axis A gain; it labels attribution as mixed or observation-process-compatible. An O1 gain with no T1-over-O1 gain, or persistence of performance after value permutation, supports observation-process dominance in the record. It does not prove absence of biological instability. Each diagnostic is reported separately; no arbitrary vote or post hoc subgroup rescues the hypothesis.

### Axis E: endpoint/component meaning

Report composite, `R48`, and `D48` directions and intervals; event chronology; PACU-proxied sensitivity; and treatment-limit strata.

- **Component-coherent:** T1-versus-S0 directions are concordant for `R48` and `D48`, without reversal after PACU-proxy removal or in no-documented-limitation analysis.
- **Component-specific:** evidence is concentrated in only ICU return or only death; conclusions name that component.
- **Endpoint-heterogeneous:** component directions conflict, or the composite direction reverses in a prespecified PACU/treatment-limit sensitivity. The composite score remains reportable, but generic “deterioration” language is prohibited.
- **Not clinically adjudicable:** planned status or goals-of-care meaning remains unknown even if statistical components are precise.

### Axis F: precision and execution sufficiency

The primary comparison is inconclusive for precision if the test set has fewer than 200 composite events, intervals span both zero and the 0.002 materiality threshold, bootstrap failure exceeds 5%, calibration remains grossly inadequate after validation-only recalibration, or more than 30% of transfers lack at least four distinct observed hours in any one of HR, BP, RR, or SpO2. Component conclusions are individually inconclusive if fewer than 100 component events occur in test or their intervals span clinically opposed interpretations. Sparse death precision does not make the ICU-return estimate inconclusive, and vice versa.

Calibration slope 0.8–1.2 is a quality flag, not proof of increment. More than 30% of transfers lacking enough core-channel bins for trajectory estimation is an execution/transportability warning; it does not turn a null into falsification.

### Allowed conclusion language

- **Supportive:** “Final-24-hour trajectory summaries added [detected/material] prognostic information for the observed 48-hour composite beyond the process-aware endpoint snapshot,” followed by the independent materiality, utility, artifact, and component results.
- **Adverse:** “The data rule out the prespecified 0.002 Brier gain” only when its upper confidence bound is below 0.002. If a smaller signal is detected, state it.
- **Observation-dominant:** “Recorded observation processes explain the computable gain better than measured-value trajectories” only when the O1/permutation outputs support that pattern; do not claim physiology is absent.
- **Inconclusive:** name the exact axis—increment, materiality, utility, attribution, component meaning, or precision—rather than labeling the whole study inconclusive unless the primary computation fails.

No computed result can establish biological mechanism, preventability, benefit of delayed transfer, benefit of enhanced monitoring, true planned return, goals-of-care alignment, or transportability.

## Feasibility evidence and resource estimate

Existing bounded audits found 94,458 ICU stays; 94,444 exact successor transfers; 53,001 broad eligible general-ward transfers; and 49,157 first eligible transfers per admission. Within 48 hours there were 2,002 ICU returns, 661 in-hospital deaths, and 2,607 unique composite events (5.30%); 10,327 broad-cohort transfers had live discharge within 48 hours. ## Alternatives and revisit conditions

- **SWIFT alone:** deferred as an intentionally weak comparator; revisit only as a secondary benchmark if required local inputs have adequate coverage.
- **Gradient-boosted summaries:** defer while nested T1 gives the clearest scientific test; revisit if T1 gains but L1 optimization is unstable.
- **Joint latent physiology/observation model:** scientifically attractive but not identified by this single-center record without stronger monitoring/staffing or adjudication data; revisit with external process metadata.
- **Automatic planned-return NLP:** deferred. Discharge summaries are downstream and pre-transfer note intent is not validated here. Revisit after a clinician-labeled note sample and a leakage-safe pre-`t0` text source are available.
- **Seven-day all-destination replication:** deferred because it blurs the immediate ward-transfer decision and largely revisits [K1].
