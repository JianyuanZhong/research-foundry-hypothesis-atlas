# Context-dependent intervention response: does the RA drug transcriptional footprint require the macrophage-fibroblast co-culture context? (episode 20, evolve/repair of D2-T1)

## 1. Unresolved question (the opening)
RA drug discovery and target prioritisation are usually scored in single-cell-type or whole-tissue systems. But in the inflamed joint the myeloid cell is embedded next to fibroblast-like synoviocytes (FLS) and T cells. [K1] showed the macrophage subset enriched in RA tissue is *shaped by resident fibroblasts and TNF* and, reciprocally, changes fibroblast behaviour — so macrophage state is partly a property of the local cellular context. That raises a testable question the seed left undefined and that [K1] did not resolve: **is the transcriptional response of synovial myeloid cells to an intervention a fixed, cell-intrinsic property, or does it depend on the macrophage-fibroblast co-culture context?**

This is not answerable by re-describing baseline coculture differences. It is an *interaction* claim: response_to_drug depends on context.

## 2. Clinical importance
- If intervention responses are context-dependent, single-cell or monotypic assays systematically mis-rank targets and mis-estimate pharmacological effect size; the same compound could look active alone and blunt (or reversed) with FLS present.
- If responses are context-invariant (H0), monotypic screens are a valid and cheaper proxy — a useful, decision-changing negative result.
- [K1] already reports that some RA medications redirected the target macrophage into a state "not expected to resolve inflammation", i.e., drug direction is not simply read off target engagement; context-dependence is a concrete mechanism for such a paradox.
- [K3] shows a targeted RA drug's footprint (JAK1/STAT, chemokines, MMP1/3) is measurable in synovial tissue; the question is whether that footprint is co-culture-context-dependent.

## 3. Competing explanations (rivals), and where they diverge
- **H1 Context-dependent response (leading claim):** the drug effect on macrophage effector genes differs between the MT (myeloid+T) and MTF (myeloid+T+FLS) contexts *within the same donor* (interaction ≠ 0), and the difference is not fully explicable by an overall scaling/composition shift.
- **H0 Cell-intrinsic response (strongest rival):** the per-gene drug effect is the same in MT and MTF; any apparent difference is (a) a global library-scale/composition shift, (b) donor/technical variation, or (c) an artefact of the transwell geometry, not context sensing.
- **H2 Composition/attenuation rival:** adding FLS/T cells changes the transcript mixture, attenuating observed fold-changes toward 1 (and, if any RNA mixing occurs, adding FLS-derived counts). This predicts a *uniform shrinkage* of the drug effect in MTF relative to MT, not gene-specific sign/rank changes. [K2] (CIBERSORT) is the methodological anchor that mixtures can be decomposed into cell-subset fractions; this rival is modelled explicitly.
The discriminating observation: gene-specific interaction with sign/rank changes and residual structure after adjusting for a global scale term and estimated cell-fraction covariate (H1), versus a single global attenuation factor that absorbs the interaction (H0/H2).

## 5. Population, estimand, variables, analysis, baseline and alternative
- **Population / unit:** donor × context × condition sample cells in `GSE95588_d1d2d3d4`. Primary population = donors P1,P2,P3 with paired `MT` and `MTF` **drug** columns for the same drug; vehicle references are the same-donor `P#_MT` and `P#_MTF` columns.
- **Estimand (primary):** for a pre-registered response gene g and drug d, the **context×drug interaction** on log2 expression:
  Δ_g,d = [log2(MTF_d) − log2(MTF_veh)] − [log2(MT_d) − log2(MT_veh)] within donor, then summarised across donors P1–P3 (and reported per donor because n is small).
  Secondary: a pathway-level scalar (mean Δ over the pre-registered gene set).
- **Pre-registered gene set (effector/intervention response; informed by [K3] readouts):** CXCL9, CXCL10, CXCL11, CCL2, CCL5, IL6, CXCL8, SOCS1, SOCS3, MMP1, MMP3, IL1B, TNF, TNFAIP3, NFKBIA. (Set fixed before computing.)
- **Normalisation:** log2(CPM+1); genes with adequate counts retained. Report both on the stated panel and, as sensitivity, on detected genes.
- **Baseline model (simple):** per-gene interaction Δ with a donor sign-flip permutation null (and a donor-clustered/bootstrap interval). Reports whether any drug shows context dependence.
- **Substantive alternative (mechanistic/composition-aware, the reason to prefer more than the baseline):** a mixture-aware linear model
  log2(CPM_g) ~ donor + context + drug + context:drug + α·f_myeloid,
  where `f_myeloid` is an estimated per-sample myeloid fraction from cell-type-restricted markers (myeloid: CD14, CD68, CSF1R, SPI1, AIF1, MRC1, CD163; T cell: CD3D, CD3E, CD2; FLS/stromal: COL1A1, COL1A2, PDGFRB, DCN, LUM, PRG4, THY1), estimated by the [K2]-style deconvolution/non-negative regression. The alternative distinguishes **gene-specific context sensing** (interaction survives with gene-specific structure) from **global attenuation/scale or composition** (interaction collapses to a single scale/composition term). *What the alternative reveals that the baseline loses:* the baseline cannot separate a real interaction from mixture attenuation and library-scale shifts; the mixture model can, and it also localises the interaction to myeloid vs FLS/T-restricted genes.
- **Orthogonal anchor leg:** apply the same interaction construct in the non-GSE95588 system (E-MTAB-8316: macrophage/monocyte response to a compound), reported as exploratory directional concordance only.
- **Split/held-out logic:** because the design is a small within-donor factorial (not a predictive ML task), there is no train/test split; instead the interaction is required to hold **per donor** (P1,P2,P3) and be robust to the composition adjustment before any positive claim. Pre-register the drug list before computing; avoid peeking for the best drug.

## 6. Falsification / outcomes
- **Supportive (H1):** ≥1 pre-registered drug shows a donor-consistent, sign-stable context×drug interaction (donor-clustered interval excluding 0) that survives the composition/scale adjustment; the interaction concentrates in myeloid/effector genes.
- **Adverse (H0/H2):** interactions are ≈0, or the apparent interaction is absorbed by a single global scale/attenuation term or by `f_myeloid` (no gene-specific residual) — i.e., the drug response is context-invariant up to composition. This is a meaningful negative: monotypic screens would be a valid proxy.
- **Inconclusive:** interaction significant in only one donor; or explained by the unlabelled `3`/`AG` wells; or only present for a single unreliable drug; or the anchor leg conflicts and no resolution is possible.
- **Explicitly not established regardless of outcome:** clinical efficacy, causality (transwell, in-vitro, cross-sectional, 1-day), generality beyond these donors/drugs/assay, or protein-level mechanism. These require additional data, expert review, or another study.

## 8. Method-exploration note (baseline vs alternative; deferral reason)
A simple per-gene interaction baseline and a composition/mixture-aware alternative are both specified on the *same question*; the alternative is scientifically necessary because the baseline cannot distinguish a context-sensing interaction from mixture attenuation/composition (the central rival). No GPU is needed: the design is a small count matrix (70 samples × ~23.7k genes); per-gene linear/robust models + deconvolution are CPU-scale (minutes). The larger solver envelope may add bootstrap/permutation and the anchor leg; not executed in discovery.
