# Is monitoring de-escalation a patient-level deterioration signal or a hospital practice fingerprint?

Lead working proposal — eICU 2.0 island, episode 9. Operation: **evolve** of true parent
`[prior hypothesis]` (valid, mu 29.40, episode-4 selection). Seed origin:
`[starting question]` ("Reduced testing and subsequent deterioration"). Status: design
frozen by Lead for compilation; verified bounded probe executed (numbers below); no reference
solution executed at discovery.

## Unresolved question and clinical importance

ICU patients are monitored by repeated lab draws, and clinicians decide how often to draw.
[K1] showed, across 272 lab tests in 669,452 patients, that the *presence and timing* of a
test order — not only its value — carries survival signal (order presence significant in
233/272; order timing beat the value in 118/174). So monitoring intensity encodes a care
process, not only patient state. The consequential, unresolved question is whether a *sudden
drop* in a patient's own draw intensity while they remain physiologically unstable is a
patient-level leading indicator of imminent deterioration, or merely a fingerprint of local
surveillance practice (order sets, protocols, staffing) that will not transport. Resolving it
changes a real modeling/deployment decision: whether monitoring-intensity features are safe to
deploy in cross-hospital deterioration alerts, or require target-site recalibration. [K3]
warns that internal performance is a poor guide to usefulness elsewhere; [K2] documents that
eICU is a 208-hospital telehealth sample, so between-hospital practice variation is real and
inspectable.

## Strongest claim the evidence already supports

Order presence/timing carries survival signal ([K1]) and ICU models often degrade under
hospital transport ([K3]). That does **not** establish that a within-patient *de-escalation*
of draws predicts deterioration, nor that any such signal is patient-level rather than
practice-level. That is the unresolved claim this experiment tests.

## Hypothesis (tested) vs leading rival

- **H1 (patient-level leading indicator):** within a hospital, a larger fall in a patient's
  own draw rate between 0–12h and 12–24h, at hour-24 instability in the upper tertile, is
  associated with higher (24,48]h deterioration, with a monotone draw-rate-ratio
  dose-response, and the association persists after conditioning on concurrent severity and
  instability trajectory.
- **R1 (severity/improvement confounding):** the fall marks clinician-judged improvement;
  conditioning on hour-24 instability + APACHE severity + trajectory attenuates the
  association to the null.
- **R2 (hospital practice fingerprint):** the association is largely explained by hospital
  identity / surveillance intensity; between-hospital variance dominates and the
  within-hospital effect is near null; a model using it degrades under leave-one-hospital-out
  (LOHO) transport.
- **R3 (measurement/artifact):** charting density, artifact handling, or EOL/discharge/transfer
  suppression of draws produces the pattern (addressed by outcome window, disposition checks,
  and a `carePlanEOL` sensitivity analysis).

## Population, temporal boundaries, variables, outcome

- **Population:** adult eICU unit stays, `age>=16`, `unitdischargeoffset>=1440` min, decisive
  unit outcome, restricted to hospitals with `>=200` eligible stays. Verified in
  `patient.csv.gz`: 200,859 rows; 10,907 Expired; **128,148 eligible**; **208 hospitals**;
  **135 hospitals with >=200 eligible**.
- **Landmark / windows:** hour-24 landmark. Exposure window 12–24h vs reference 0–12h;
  outcome window (24,48]h.
- **Outcome observability (primary):** the (24,48]h mortality outcome is only observed when the
  unit stay extends past 48h (`unitdischargeoffset>2880`) or the patient dies within the window.
  Stays alive-discharged/transferred before 48h are excluded from the primary mortality outcome
  and reported separately; the secondary escalation outcome is evaluated only in still-admitted
  patients, and disposition (`unitdischargelocation`, `carePlanEOL`) is checked to prevent
  discharge/EOL suppression of draws from masquerading as the exposure (rival R3).
- **Exposure:** continuous draw-rate ratio `R = (draws in 12–24h + 1)/(draws in 0–12h + 1)`
  on the bound analyte panel; secondary pre-specified binary `R<0.5`. Analyte panel bound by
  exact `labname` strings: `creatinine, BUN, WBC x 1000, Hgb, Hct, platelets x 1000, sodium,
  potassium, chloride, bicarbonate, glucose, calcium, magnesium, anion gap`.
- **Instability:** MEWS-like score from `vitalPeriodic` (heartrate, respiration,
  systemicsystolic, temperature, sao2) at the hour-24 landmark; upper-tertile flag.
- **Severity:** `apachePatientResult` `acutephysiologyscore`, `apachescore`,
  `predictedicumortality`.
- **Primary outcome:** unit death in (24,48]h (decisive; from `unitdischargestatus` +
  `unitdischargeoffset`). Secondary: composite escalation (new vasopressor or new organ
  support in (24,48]h) — drug-name binding deferred to run-time.

## Baselines, learned alternative, discriminating test

- **Baseline (simple):** logistic regression on instability flag + APACHE score + age +
  admission-diagnosis group.
- **Learned alternative (substantive):** gradient-boosted trees on full 24h lab + vital
  time-series features (draw-counts by analyte, missingness, draw-rate ratio, vital
  trajectory slopes and min/max/mean). What it adds that the baseline loses: nonlinear
  interaction between the *instability trajectory* and the draw-rate change (e.g. a fall
  matters only when instability is rising), which a single linear `R` term cannot represent.
- **Discriminating test (H1 vs R2):** multilevel model with hospital random intercept **and**
  random slope on `R` vs a within-hospital fixed effect; report the within-hospital effect and
  the between-hospital variance. H1 predicts a stable within-hospital fixed effect and small
  random-slope variance; R2 predicts the opposite.
- **Transport:** leave-one-hospital-out over the 135 largest hospitals; report ΔAUROC,
  calibration slope, O:E, Brier, net benefit vs baseline.

## Falsification criteria (pre-specified)

- **Supportive (H1):** within-hospital adjusted OR for `R` quintile 5 vs 1 > 1 with monotone
  dose-response across quintiles and a CI excluding 1; LOHO ΔAUROC of the learned model over
  baseline >= 0.02 with CI excluding 0; random-slope variance small relative to the
  within-hospital effect.
- **Adverse (R1):** association attenuates to the null (CI includes 1) after conditioning on
  hour-24 instability + APACHE + trajectory.
- **Adverse (R2):** between-hospital variance dominates and the within-hospital effect is near
  null; LOHO transport degrades to baseline.
- **Inconclusive:** wide CIs / too few events in (24,48]h, or large random-slope heterogeneity
  without a stable within-hospital effect.

## What a result would and would not establish

Supportive results would show a within-hospital, dose-responsive association and transport
behavior consistent with a deployable patient-level signal; they would **not** establish a
mechanism or a causal treatment target. Adverse/inconclusive results would inform the
modeling decision (do not deploy monitoring-intensity features cross-hospital without
recalibration) without refuting patient-level pathophysiology. Mechanism requires order-set,
staffing, and bed-capacity data absent from eICU → another study.
