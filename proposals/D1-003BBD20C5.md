# Observed creatinine/Foley discordance after an early ICU renal insult

## Scientific deliverable

Newly derive and freeze a one-row-per-admission-first-ICU-stay MIMIC-IV v3.1 landmark cohort, then estimate a noncausal prognostic contrast: among adults with an observed early creatinine rise and early observed oliguria who survive to 72 hours with adequate paired measurements, does creatinine recovery without Foley-output recovery identify higher subsequent in-hospital mortality than concordant recovery?

Completion requires:

1. a provenance and support manifest with flow through every eligibility gate, item/stay support, missingness and Foley-density strata;
2. unadjusted and pre-recovery-adjusted phenotype contrasts with 95% uncertainty intervals and event timing;
3. first-event competing-risk results for death versus new RRT, and a separate readmission-versus-death analysis;
4. a fitted transparent non-urine baseline and a substantive temporal nonlinear alternative on the same subject-held-out split, with a urine/fluid ablation, held-out discrimination, Brier score, calibration and uncertainty;
5. prespecified threshold, coverage, source, early-RRT and coverage-weighting sensitivities, plus falsification and adverse/inconclusive reporting.

No cohort counts or fitted clinical result are asserted here.

## Unresolved question and clinical importance

The strongest inspected evidence supports a measurement concern, not this hypothesis. Haines et al., CJASN 2023, DOI 10.2215/CJN.0000000000000203, studied 38 selected mechanically ventilated patients with paired creatinine, cystatin C, iohexol clearance and muscle measurements. Their reported findings support concern that creatinine-based kidney-function assessment can overestimate measured function during prolonged critical illness and can separate from a muscle-sensitive marker. That study does not establish Foley-output discordance, its prevalence or prognosis in MIMIC-IV.

The unresolved claim is narrower and falsifiable:

> Among the explicitly observed, measurement-supported landmark population defined below, patients with an observed early creatinine rise, early Foley oliguria, subsequent creatinine recovery, and persistent low charted Foley output have higher post-72-hour in-hospital mortality than patients with creatinine recovery and Foley-output recovery.

This matters because clinicians may regard improving creatinine as reassuring even when urine output remains low. A reproducible signal could justify prospective validation with better renal-function and volume-status ascertainment, and potentially inform monitoring research. It would not show that creatinine recovery is harmful, that Foley output measures true GFR, or that any treatment should be started, stopped, delayed or continued. The substantive advance is a tightly timed, measurement-aware prognostic test of discordance, rather than a KDIGO claim or a treatment-effect claim.

## Exact population and time boundary

Use adults with \`patients.anchor_age >= 18\`, each admission's earliest valid ICU stay (minimum \`intime\`, tie-break by \`stay_id\`). The unit is an ICU episode keyed by \`subject_id,hadm_id,stay_id\`; multiple admissions are distinct episodes but all episodes for a \`subject_id\` stay in one train/test partition. Exclude invalid intervals, missing \`intime/outtime\`, and \`intime+72 hours >= outtime\`. Exclude death at or before \`t0\`; define \`t0 = intime + 72 hours\`. This survival/continued-stay restriction is explicit conditioning, not immortal-time bias in post-landmark follow-up.

Use half-open windows W0=[intime,intime+24h), W1=[intime+24h,intime+48h), and W2=[intime+48h,intime+72h). Only event time, never delayed \`storetime\`, determines inclusion. Join \`icustays\` to \`admissions\` on \`subject_id,hadm_id\`, patients on \`subject_id\`, ICU events on \`stay_id\`, and hospital labs on \`subject_id,hadm_id\` with \`charttime\` inside the index ICU interval.

## Observed creatinine-rise and recovery phenotype

This is an observed creatinine-rise screen, not KDIGO AKI. MIMIC-IV lacks a reliable pre-admission baseline and clinician-adjudicated AKI/urine-output labels for this question. Use:

- \`icu/chartevents.csv.gz\`, item 220615 (“Creatinine (serum)”);
- \`hosp/labevents.csv.gz\`, item 50912 (“Creatinine”, blood chemistry);

with \`chartevents\` columns \`stay_id,hadm_id,charttime,itemid,valuenum,valueuom\` and \`labevents\` columns \`subject_id,hadm_id,itemid,charttime,valuenum,value,valueuom\`. Join dictionary labels through \`icu/d_items\` and \`hosp/d_labitems\`. Retain numeric values in 0.2–20 mg/dL after source/unit checks; report a 0.3–15 sensitivity and preserve source-specific results.

Let C0 be the minimum valid creatinine in W0, C1 the maximum in W1, and C2 the maximum in W2. Require support in all three windows and C1-C0 >=0.3 mg/dL or C1/C0 >=1.5. The required W1 maximum prevents an early-only maximum from being mislabeled as a later rise. Define Cpeak=max(C0,C1), Clate=min valid creatinine in W2, and creatinine recovery as Clate <= Cpeak-0.3 or Clate/Cpeak <=0.75. Preserve continuous changes and each absolute/relative component.

## Selective Foley measurement and discordance

Foley output uses \`icu/outputevents.csv.gz\`, item 226559 (“Foley”), columns \`stay_id,charttime,value,valueuom\`, joined to \`d_items\`. Retain nonnegative numeric mL values and aggregate multiple rows into relative hourly bins. An unobserved bin is missing, never zero. Require at least 12 distinct observed hourly bins in both W0 and W2 (18-bin sensitivity).

Use a single positive admission weight: item 226512 (“Admission Weight (Kg)”) or, if absent, item 224639 (“Daily Weight”), charted in W0. Do not use a weight first charted after W0 in the primary analysis. The primary output quantity is the charted Foley rate: summed observed mL divided by observed hours and admission kg. It is a charting-dependent proxy, not complete 24-hour urine output. Require weight support for the primary analysis; unweighted mL/hour is a sensitivity only.

Require W0 charted Foley rate <0.5 mL/kg/hour (early observed oliguria). Define late Foley recovery as W2 rate >=0.5 and non-recovery as <0.5. The primary contrast is:

- discordant: creatinine recovery plus late Foley non-recovery;
- concordant: creatinine recovery plus late Foley recovery.

The comparison is conditional on the same early observed oliguria and measurement gates. Report all four creatinine/output combinations descriptively. Thresholds 0.3, 0.5 and 1.0, 18-bin coverage, unweighted output, source-specific creatinine and early-RRT exclusion are prespecified sensitivities.

This selective-coverage cohort is itself the estimand. Report all flow counts and characteristics of excluded or lower-density patients. Optionally estimate stabilized inverse-probability weights for adequate Foley coverage from variables observed by 48 hours, only if positivity and effective sample size are adequate; this is a missingness sensitivity, not causal correction.

## Outcomes and competing risks

The primary endpoint is all-cause in-hospital death after the landmark: \`t0 < admissions.deathtime <= admissions.dischtime\`. Use \`deathtime\` as event time; \`hospital_expire_flag\` only as a consistency check; report unresolved discrepancies and classify the primary result inconclusive if they cannot be reconciled.

For secondary competing outcomes, define new RRT as the first listed RRT procedure in \`icu/procedureevents.csv.gz\` with \`starttime >= t0\` and before discharge, using items 225441, 225802, 225803, 225805, 225809 or 225955. This excludes ongoing pre-landmark RRT. Analyze first post-landmark event as death versus new RRT; report cumulative incidence and cause-specific checks. Among index ICU discharges alive, define the first later same-admission ICU stay with \`later.intime > index.outtime\` and \`later.intime < dischtime\`; analyze readmission versus death before readmission as competing risks. Death/RRT composite and length of stay are descriptive only.

## Adjustment and leakage controls

For the phenotype association, the adjusted model uses covariates available by 48 hours and excludes every W2 value that defines late recovery: age, sex, first care unit/admission context, C0/C1/rise severity, W0 output and coverage, and pre-48 summaries/missingness for MAP (220181), HR (220045), RR (220210), SpO2 (220277), lactate (50813/52442/53154), vasoactive input items 221906/221662/221749/229630/229631/229632/221289/229617, ventilation procedures 225792/225794, and RRT items above. Do not adjust away the phenotype with Clate, late output or late-window features. Do not use \`diagnoses_icd\` as a primary predictor because it has no onset/availability timestamp; a CKD-coded sensitivity is retrospective and not a deployable predictor.

The predictive baseline is penalized logistic regression using age, sex, first care unit/admission context, creatinine summaries, pre-landmark physiology/support indicators and missingness, excluding all urine/fluid trajectory features. The substantive alternative is a CPU HistGradientBoostingClassifier (or equivalent Harbor tree booster) with six-hour bins over 0–72 hours for creatinine, Foley output/observation density, weight-normalized output, input-minus-output proxy, MAP, HR, RR, SpO2, lactate, vasoactive exposure, ventilation and RRT. Each bin retains last/mean/min/max/slope/variability/count/missingness. Fit full and urine/fluid-ablation models on identical subject-grouped training folds and a deterministic held-out 20% subject split. Report held-out AUROC, AUPRC, Brier, calibration intercept/slope and bootstrap intervals. The learned alternative can expose timing, nonlinearities and interactions that a snapshot loses; it is not a mechanism test and cannot rescue a failed phenotype contrast.

Use robust logistic/binomial risk differences or ratios and subject-clustered bootstrap/sandwich intervals for the association, with cause-specific time-to-event checks. The primary adjusted association is not claimed causal. The prediction model's t0 boundary permits W2 features as predictors, while the phenotype adjustment model deliberately stops at 48 hours to avoid circular adjustment. Multiple admissions per subject remain in one split and are clustered in uncertainty.

## Falsification and interpretation

Supportive evidence requires higher discordant post-t0 death risk with uncertainty excluding the null, direction preserved under 18-bin coverage and thresholds, no material reversal after an adequately supported coverage-weighting sensitivity, and reproducible urine/fluid calibration or discrimination improvement beyond the non-urine baseline. It supports only a conditional observed prognostic signal.

Adverse evidence is a precise null or reversal, especially with dense coverage, or disappearance under source/threshold/coverage checks. A temporal model gain without the prespecified phenotype signal is adverse for the discordance claim, not a rescue.

Inconclusive evidence includes fewer than a predeclared minimum of 10 deaths in either primary phenotype group, sparse/imbalanced phenotype support, failed or unstable calibration, unstable coverage weights, unreconciled death fields, failed item/support audit, or inability to fit within the configured budget. The minimum is fixed before fitting and is not relaxed after results are seen.

The verifier can check archive provenance, schemas, joins, time boundaries, cohort flow, item support, phenotype computation, fits, uncertainty, sensitivity results and conclusion-to-output consistency. It cannot establish true GFR, muscle-loss mediation, volume status, clinician-intended Foley placement/charting, treatment indications, causality, bedside thresholds or external validity. Those require cystatin C/measured GFR, muscle and volume assessment, clinical adjudication, expert review, prospective monitoring and external validation.

## Alternatives and provenance

The prior assessed ICU-discharge residual-instability proposal remains the fallback if paired renal support is inadequate. It targets a different 48-hour post-ICU-discharge prognostic question and must not be silently substituted. Diuretic/treatment and transfusion branches remain deferred because indications and time-varying confounding are incompletely recorded. The asynchronous-organ-recovery seed is retained but not chosen because it lacks an equally fixed, computable renal/respiratory phenotype. The three demonstrations informed only dated-trajectory versus snapshot comparisons; this is an adaptation, not reproduction. Their unavailable UKB/Danish cohorts, genetic inputs, chest-X-ray images and the cancer paper's unavailable main text/STAR Methods are not claimed here.
