# Branch A — temporally ordered patient-state versus workflow transport in eICU

Status: proposed child of `[prior hypothesis]`; no study result is claimed. This branch preserves the parent’s eICU mortality question and exact source lineage while replacing one coarse 0–360-minute feature summary with prespecified repeated landmarks, rolling windows, vital trajectories, and a value/process separation.

## Scientific opening and falsifiable hypothesis

The parent’s strongest supported claim is narrower than its motivating interpretation: laboratory value-plus-recording-process features may transport differently between hospitals, and a within-hospital process permutation or timing shift can test whether patient linkage matters. That design still conditions every primary feature on the first six hours, summarizes a physiologic trajectory coarsely, and uses an ICU-discharge label without a death timestamp. Thus a positive process increment could reflect current acuity, selective measurement, persistence of recording practice, or the six-hour/long-stay selection rule.

The unresolved question is whether *patient-linked, temporally ordered physiologic state* explains a reproducible part of the transport signal after recording intensity is separated, or whether the apparent signal is primarily a hospital workflow/documentation signature. This is clinically important because a model that relies on state trajectories can support transportable monitoring review only after validation, whereas a model that relies on local observation density requires feed harmonization, site validation, or recalibration. It does not authorize testing, treatment, or a causal interpretation of measurement practice.

Leading explanation: recent and changing vital/laboratory values carry patient-state information. At a fixed landmark, state-only trajectories should retain useful discrimination across held-out hospitals; a temporal model should improve over a summary baseline if transient excursions and ordered changes matter; and adding process features should contribute less after within-hospital patient-process permutation or within-window timing displacement.

Strong rivals:

1. Workflow/documentation: measurement counts, analyte availability, and timing encode hospital-specific recording intensity. Process-only prediction may transport or degrade by site even when values are permuted across patients. A process increment that survives permutation is evidence for this descriptive rival, not proof that documentation caused the outcome.
2. Severity and selection: sicker or more intensively observed patients both have abnormal values and more measurements, and later landmarks retain only stays still in the ICU. A state increment can therefore be prognostic without identifying a mechanism; a later-landmark change can be due to risk-set selection rather than recovery dynamics.
3. Timing/label artifact: because eICU has ICU-relative observation/result offsets but no order status, specimen collection time, interface-outage flag, or death timestamp, apparent timing effects may be charting latency or the inability to align death to a landmark. A time-shift/null result narrows interpretation but cannot establish order or causality.

Primary falsifiable hypothesis: in adult first ICU admissions at the 6-hour landmark, a values-only model using temporally ordered vital/lab summaries has a non-zero and hospital-transportable contribution beyond demographics, while the incremental contribution of measurement-process features is attenuated by patient-process permutation and by within-window process-time displacement. At 12, 24, and 48 hours, the same contrast should be directionally consistent among stays still under ICU observation, but these are explicitly secondary dynamic risk-set estimands. A learned temporal model should add information over a prespecified summary baseline only if ordered changes, not merely greater record density, matter.

The strongest evidence that would support the patient-state interpretation is a positive, held-out state increment at the primary landmark; a temporal-model improvement over the summary baseline on the same patients, hospitals, target and split; attenuation of the process increment after patient-process permutation; and a selective attenuation after shifting process timing. A positive process increment that survives permutation, or a process-only model with similar site degradation, supports the workflow rival. Intervals spanning zero, unstable analyte selection, or inadequate event/hospital support are inconclusive. None establishes a physiologic mechanism, causal effect, or clinical utility.

Secondary estimands are conditional on continued ICU observation to each later landmark and must not be pooled as if they represented one fixed population.

The later risk sets are a boundary analysis, not a solution to discharge-time selection. The patient table has no death timestamp usable for landmark censoring. A death before a later landmark cannot be distinguished from an early discharge without using the unavailable event time; later landmarks therefore exclude stays by recorded discharge offset and can induce selection. Report risk-set sizes, outcome prevalence, and hospital/event support at each L; do not call a later-landmark difference causal or evidence of recovery.

## Feature construction and leakage rules

All transformations are fit separately inside each outer training partition and then frozen for its held-out hospitals. The same hospital fold assignment is used at every landmark and for every model. No feature may use `unitdischargeoffset`, discharge status, Apache fields, or any row with an offset greater than L.

Use 2-hour bins at primary L=360: [0,120), [120,240), and [240,360]. For later landmarks use consecutive 2-hour bins from 0 through L, with the final interval closed at L. The rolling-window implementation must additionally expose the recent 6-hour window [max(0,L-360),L] as a prespecified sensitivity view. No forward filling across a landmark is allowed. A measurement at a bin boundary belongs to the later bin except the final endpoint.

State variables are the finite numeric vital candidates and the training-fold selected lab analytes. For each variable and bin calculate last, first, median, minimum, maximum, standard deviation, and count of valid observations; calculate a within-stay slope only when at least two distinct offsets exist, with slope fit using the actual ICU-relative observation/result offsets. The state-only matrices use training-fold median imputation for missing values and do not include missing indicators, counts, or timing metadata; this makes the intended state/process contrast explicit, although observed-value selection remains a limitation. Robust training-fold 0.5th/99.5th percentile clipping is allowed and must be recorded. No clinically invented plausibility range is permitted.

Select at most 30 lab analytes using distinct eligible-patient coverage in the training hospitals and observations available by that landmark, requiring >=1% training-patient coverage. The selected set is frozen for the corresponding landmark and reported. A variable is retained only if the readiness report establishes numeric coverage; report fewer rather than substitute unavailable measures. Vital selection follows the fixed primary candidates above and the same training-only coverage rule.

Process variables are kept separate from state values: per-variable presence, valid-row count, distinct-offset count, first and last offset, time since last measurement at L, number of active bins, total valid-row count, total distinct-offset count, and counts of rows revised by L. These are recording proxies, not confirmed orders. Process-only features contain no lab result or vital value. The combined model concatenates state and process matrices. The state-only temporal model receives values and a fixed imputation mask only as an input-independent padding mask; it must not receive an observed/missing indicator. A separate sensitivity model may receive masks, but its result cannot support a pure state claim.

Permutation control: within each fitting/scoring partition and hospital, sort stays by SHA-256(`"eicu-process-null-v2\\0"+patientunitstayid+":"+str(L)`) and cyclically assign each stay the complete process vector from the next stay in that hospital. Keep original state values and labels. The permutation uses no outcome and preserves hospital-level process distribution while breaking patient-process linkage. Omit singleton hospitals from permutation-only summaries and report them.

Timing control: within each patient and landmark, replace each process offset by a cyclic displacement within [0,L], using `t_shift=t-180` when t>=180 and `t_shift=t+180` otherwise for L>=360; for L<360 use modulo displacement `(t+L/2) mod (L+1)` on integer offsets. Recompute first/last/time-since-last from shifted process offsets while preserving variable presence and row counts. State values and their original offsets remain unchanged. This is a negative control for timing alignment, not an order-time intervention.

A temporal reversal control reverses the sequence of 2-hour state bins for T-State while preserving the multiset of values. A drop in T-State after reversal is consistent with directional information; no drop is a falsification of a directional-order claim. Both controls are secondary and are not used to tune the primary result.

## Baseline, learned alternative, split, and uncertainty

D-Logit is elastic-net logistic regression on demographics only. S-Logit is the same elastic-net model on demographic variables plus the state summary features above, with alpha/l1 ratio chosen by nested training-only cross-validation and class weighting specified before fitting. D-Logit supplies the demographics-only baseline for the state increment; S-Logit tests whether clinically legible summaries and additive effects suffice.

D-GBM is histogram gradient boosting on demographics only. S-GBM is the identical learner on demographics plus state summaries. SP-GBM is the primary learned model: histogram gradient boosting with a fixed, capacity-matched feature budget, using the identical state/process matrices, analyte selections, imputation, clipping, class policy, and outer split. Its nonlinear interactions can reveal whether process intensity modifies the value signal, but improvement alone is not the scientific endpoint. P-GBM is process-only. Permuted-SP-GBM replaces only process channels using the control above.

T-State is the substantive temporal alternative: a small gated recurrent/temporal-convolution encoder over the ordered 2-hour state vectors, with a fixed hidden width and dropout chosen before the outer evaluation. It is fit on the same landmark target, same hospital-held-out folds, same state values, and same uncertainty procedure as S-Logit. T-State+P appends the process channels at each bin and has a matched parameter budget; a process-permuted T-State+P and reversed-bin T-State are controls. T-State can reveal transient excursions, persistent direction, and value interactions that fixed first/last/min/max summaries lose. It is not proposed as a complexity bonus and cannot rescue an inconclusive process contrast.

Primary learned/baseline comparison is paired T-State versus S-Logit on state inputs at L=360. The primary state contrast is A_state = AUROC(S-GBM,360) - AUROC(D-GBM,360), with paired calibration and utility contrasts. Primary patient-state/workflow comparison is the process increment of SP-GBM over S-GBM versus its increment after process permutation, with T-State as the ordered-state sensitivity. This keeps baseline/learned comparisons matched while preserving a transparent model. If the temporal alternative cannot meet readiness gates or the resource envelope, retain S-Logit and S/ SP-GBM and mark T-State deferred; do not silently replace the question.

Use hospital-held-out folds: sort retained hospital IDs by SHA-256(`"eicu-transport-v2\\0"+hospitalid`) and greedily balance eligible-stay counts into five contiguous folds. Each outer fit uses four hospital folds and predicts the fifth. A fixed 20% patient-stratified internal sample from the four training hospital folds is reserved only for internal reporting, assigned by SHA-256(`"eicu-internal-v2\\0"+patientunitstayid+":"+str(L)`). No patient split is called external validation. The manifest must show that the same stay cannot cross hospital folds and that all landmarks for a stay inherit its hospital fold.

Report per-hospital and pooled AUROC (primary discrimination), AUPRC, Brier score (primary probability accuracy), calibration intercept/slope and calibration-in-the-large, and decision-curve net benefit at thresholds 5%, 10%, and 20% against treat-none/treat-all. The modeled action is senior review/monitoring review, not treatment. Report prevalence and effective sample size by hospital, fold and landmark.

Let `I_m(L)=metric(m,L)-metric(S-GBM,L)`, with Brier/calibration error signs oriented so positive means worse error. The primary 6-hour process contrast is `A_process = I_SP-GBM(360)-I_perm-SP-GBM(360)`, which equals the paired difference between original and permuted process augmentation when the same S-GBM reference is used; estimate it in held-out hospitals and report direct AUROC, Brier and net-benefit differences. The primary state contrast is `A_state = AUROC(S-GBM,360)-AUROC(D-GBM,360)`, with the same paired calibration/utility outputs. The temporal gain is `A_time = AUROC(T-State,360)-AUROC(S-Logit,360)` with matched calibration and uncertainty. The state transport contrast is the held-out/internal change in S-GBM or T-State, compared with the process-augmented change. At later landmarks report `A_state(L)`, `A_process(L)` and the landmark interaction descriptively, never as one pooled causal estimate.

Use 2,000 hospital-cluster bootstrap resamples with replacement, identical resample indices across paired models and landmarks where the risk set permits. A cluster bootstrap interval is required for every primary contrast. If a landmark has fewer than five hospitals with at least 20 events and 20 non-events, label its estimates unstable and do not use it for a confirmatory interpretation. A readiness gate also requires at least 5,000 primary-risk-set stays, at least 100 primary events, and at least 5 retained hospitals; these are feasibility gates, not observed facts.

## Falsification and interpretation rules

Technical falsification requires: no post-landmark row enters any feature; no fold-selected analyte or imputation statistic uses held-out hospitals; duplicate keys and revised lab rows are handled deterministically; process permutation leaves hospital-level process marginals within numerical tolerance; timing displacement preserves per-stay variable presence and row count; reversed bins preserve the state-value multiset; shuffled-label tests have AUROC near 0.5 with intervals covering 0.5; and a feature-manifest audit can reproduce every feature from source key/time fields.

Supportive patient-state result: at L=360, the held-out state model has a positive state contribution with a 95% cluster-bootstrap interval excluding zero, T-State improves over S-Logit on discrimination or calibration without a corresponding process-only gain, the process increment is attenuated toward the state-only model after permutation, and any timing attenuation is coherent. The result supports a descriptive patient-linked state interpretation, not a mechanism.

Supportive workflow result: the process increment persists after within-hospital permutation, P-GBM shows comparable held-out discrimination or site degradation, and timing shifts do not materially change it. This supports hospital-preserved recording intensity as a plausible explanation and motivates harmonization/site validation; it does not prove that documentation caused predictions or outcomes.

Adverse result: the state/process addition worsens held-out Brier/calibration or net benefit and its upper interval is below zero while the baseline remains stable; do not retain process features for a putative monitoring use. Mixed result: state value and process features help in different hospitals, permutation and timing controls disagree, or the temporal model improves without transportability. Inconclusive result: intervals span zero, event/hospital gates fail, risk-set coverage collapses at later landmarks, analyte selection is unstable, or technical audits fail. A small AUROC gain without calibration, utility, and control coherence does not confirm the hypothesis.

## Substantive advance, missing evidence, and defer criteria

Relative to the parent, this child removes the single coarse 0–360 summary as the only temporal representation, adds a primary three-bin trajectory and rolling six-hour view, tests repeated landmarks, incorporates verified vital tables, and gives the state-only temporal alternative a matched target/split/uncertainty evaluation. It can show whether a process increment is tied to the same patient, to timing, or merely to site-preserved intensity. It cannot remove confounding by acuity or selection induced by requiring continued ICU observation, and it cannot reconstruct orders, collection times, interface outages, clinician intent, or death time.

Clinical adjudication or another study is required for claims about why a test was ordered, whether an interface failed, whether an alert changes care, and whether monitoring review benefits patients. Prospective silent deployment with timestamped order/collection/interface data is required before a clinical action claim. An independent health system or later eICU release is required for external replication beyond the hospital-held-out estimand.

Continue if readiness verifies the bindings and primary risk-set gates and the temporal model fits within the solver envelope. Revise the feature dictionary only as a proposed child if vital/lab coverage is inadequate or a missing dependency changes the estimand. Defer T-State, but retain the landmark state/process experiment, if the bounded implementation check fails while the tabular design remains feasible. Abandon the transport interpretation if technical falsification fails or if the design cannot retain at least five hospitals with adequate events; do not manufacture a patient-level split.

## Alternative record and compute

The baseline and learned alternative answer the same 6-hour question with the same held-out hospitals, risk set, labels, metrics, bootstrap clusters, and calibration/utility evaluation. S-Logit is estimated CPU-light; S/ SP-GBM plus controls are expected to fit on 16 CPUs with 64–128 GiB RAM in approximately 2–6 hours, planning estimates not measured. T-State is expected to require 16 CPUs, 64–128 GiB RAM, and approximately 4–8 hours including five outer fits and controls; this is unverified. A single bounded readiness fold should measure feature extraction and one temporal fit. The approved solver-planning envelope is 16 CPUs, 256 GiB, and 8 hours; no GPU is required for the tabular models. If T-State materially exceeds the measured envelope, defer it rather than shrink the clinical population or change the estimand. GPU use is a tradeoff, not a scientific requirement; the deployment has allocated A100 capacity through managed jobs, but no GPU feasibility result is claimed here.

Not chosen: a larger transformer over raw event sequences was deferred because the immediate uncertainty is patient-state versus workflow, and the available fields lack orders/collection/interface semantics; the compact temporal encoder tests ordered physiology directly at lower cost. A purely cross-sectional six-hour GBM was not selected as the sole child because it repeats the parent’s coarse temporal limitation. A causal treatment or testing intervention study is not feasible with these sources and is explicitly out of scope.
