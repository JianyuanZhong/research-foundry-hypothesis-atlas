# UKB-30: Night Noise × Sleep Disturbance Interaction and Incident Atrial Fibrillation — A UK Biobank Prospective Cohort Study

## 1. Unresolved Question and Clinical Importance

Atrial fibrillation (AF) affects approximately 33 million people worldwide and is the most common sustained cardiac arrhythmia, carrying a 5-fold increased risk of stroke and substantial mortality. While traditional risk factors (age, hypertension, valvular disease, heart failure) explain most AF risk, a significant proportion of AF cases occur in individuals without these factors, suggesting that environmental and behavioral contributors remain underappreciated.

**The specific unresolved question:** Does chronic night-time environmental noise exposure interact with sleep disturbance (insomnia) to produce a synergistic (beyond-additive) increase in incident AF risk?

This question is clinically important for three reasons:

1. **Mechanistic gap:** The "noise reaction model" [K1] establishes that noise activates sympathetic and HPA-axis pathways, and that adverse effects are "most pronounced at night, when noise fragments restorative sleep and amplifies neurohormonal imbalance." However, no prospective cohort study has quantified whether the *combination* of noise and sleep disturbance produces a multiplicative hazard for AF beyond what either factor alone predicts. If the interaction is synergistic, it identifies a modifiable exposure combination (noise abatement + sleep intervention) with potentially greater AF prevention benefit than either intervention alone.

2. **Causal pathway evidence:** Mendelian randomization using noise-related DNA methylation instruments has provided causal evidence that environmental noise influences AF/flutter and other CVD outcomes through epigenetic mechanisms [K3]. Our prospective cohort design complements this by testing the epidemiological interaction in a large population with direct exposure measurement.

3. **Sleep-circadian axis:** UK Biobank accelerometer studies confirm that sleep timing misalignment (social jet lag) independently predicts incident AF and other CVD [K2], and that sleep duration itself was not independently associated with risk in that cohort—suggesting that sleep *quality/disturbance* (insomnia) rather than duration is the relevant pathway. Our study tests whether insomnia (a quality measure) interacts with noise, providing a complementary test to the duration-based findings.

**What answering this question would change:** If a significant noise×insomnia interaction exists, it would (a) identify a high-risk subgroup (high noise + insomnia) for targeted AF screening, (b) support combined noise abatement and sleep medicine interventions, and (c) provide prospective epidemiological evidence complementing the causal MR evidence for noise→AF. If no interaction exists, it would suggest that noise and sleep disturbance act through independent (additive) pathways, redirecting prevention toward addressing each factor separately.

## 2. Specific Falsifiable Hypothesis

**Primary hypothesis (H1):** In UK Biobank participants with measured night noise exposure and self-reported insomnia, the hazard ratio for incident AF (ICD10 I48) is significantly higher in the combined high-noise + high-insomnia group than predicted by the additive combination of their individual risks, after adjustment for PM2.5, age, sex, smoking, and BMI.

Formally: In a Cox proportional hazards model with a noise×insomnia interaction term, the interaction coefficient is significantly positive (p < 0.10) after full adjustment.

**Secondary hypothesis (H2):** The association between night noise and incident AF is modified by sleep duration, with stronger noise effects observed among those with shorter sleep duration (<6 hours) compared to normal duration (6-8 hours).

**Tertiary hypothesis (H3):** Among AF cases, night noise exposure is associated with increased mortality hazard (AF death), independent of sleep factors.

## 5. Method Comparison

### 5.1 Primary Analysis: Cox Proportional Hazards with Interaction
- **Model:** `hazard(AF) ~ noise + insomnia + noise:insomnia + PM2.5 + age + sex + smoking + BMI`
- **Software:** R `survival` package (coxph) or Python `lifelines`/`statsmodels`
- **Interaction test:** Wald test or likelihood ratio test for the noise×insomnia coefficient
- **Proportional hazards assumption:** Schoenfeld residuals check; if violated, use time-varying coefficients or stratified analysis

### 5.2 Baseline Comparison: Univariate and Additive Models
- **Model A (univariate):** `hazard(AF) ~ noise` (no adjustment)
- **Model B (main effects only):** `hazard(AF) ~ noise + insomnia + PM2.5 + age + sex + smoking + BMI` (no interaction)
- **Comparison:** Compare Model B (additive) vs. primary model (interaction) using likelihood ratio test. The interaction model is justified only if it significantly improves fit over the additive model.

### 5.3 Sensitivity Analyses
1. **Noise as categorical (tertiles):** Test interaction with ordinal noise categories
2. **1-year lag:** Exclude AF in first year post-baseline
3. **PM2.5 restricted cohort:** Analysis limited to 461,073 with PM2.5 (primary cohort)
4. **Sleep duration as effect modifier:** Stratify by sleep duration (<6, 6-8, >8 h)
5. **Competing risk (Fine-Gray):** For AF death outcome, with non-AF death as competing event
6. **E-value:** Quantify unmeasured confounding needed to explain away the interaction
7. **Missing data:** Complete-case analysis (primary) vs. multiple imputation for PM2.5 (sensitivity)

### 5.4 Why Cox PH is Appropriate
- AF is a time-to-event outcome with censoring (death, end of follow-up)
- Cox PH is the standard method for hazard ratio estimation in large cohort studies
- The interaction term directly tests the multiplicative-scale interaction hypothesis
- With ~494K participants and ~40K AF events, the model is well-powered for main effects; interaction tests require the large N to detect modest interaction effects
- **Alternative considered:** Accelerated failure time (AFT) model — would test interaction on the time scale rather than hazard scale. Deferred because hazard ratio is the more interpretable and clinically standard measure for AF risk.

## 6. Alternative Explanations and Sensitivity Analyses

### 6.1 Leading Explanation (if interaction is positive)
Night noise disrupts sleep architecture, and pre-existing insomnia reduces the sleep "buffer" against noise-induced sympathetic activation. The combined effect exceeds the sum of individual effects because:
- Noise fragments already-poor sleep → greater HPA-axis activation
- Insomnia reduces sleep duration → less time for parasympathetic recovery
- Synergistic autonomic imbalance → atrial electrical instability → AF

### 6.2 Rival Explanations

| Rival | Mechanism | Test |
|-------|-----------|------|
| **Residual confounding by urbanicity** | Noisy areas are more urban; urban residents have different AF risk (healthcare access, air pollution, lifestyle) | PM2.5 adjustment (proxy for urbanicity); E-value calculation |
| **Reverse causation** | Pre-subclinical AF causes sleep disturbance (palpitations, nocturnal symptoms) → insomnia is a consequence, not cause | 1-year lag; exclude early AF; temporal ordering (baseline insomnia → later AF) |
| **Healthcare access bias** | Noisy urban areas have better AF detection → higher AF diagnosis rate | Sensitivity: restrict to inpatient AF (41280 dates only) |
| **PM2.5 collinearity** | Noise and PM2.5 are correlated (both from traffic); interaction may be driven by unmeasured PM2.5×sleep interaction | Include PM2.5 in model; test PM2.5×insomnia interaction as comparison |
| **Measurement error in noise** | Single noise estimate (model-based, not personal dosimetry) → non-differential misclassification biases toward null | Acknowledged limitation; interaction unlikely to be created by non-differential error |

### 6.3 Specific Sensitivity Tests
1. **PM2.5×insomnia interaction:** If this interaction is also positive, the noise×insomnia interaction may be driven by shared environmental exposure
2. **Stratified by sleep duration:** If noise effect is stronger in short sleepers, supports the sleep-fragmentation mechanism
3. **Stratified by age:** If interaction is stronger in older adults, supports cumulative damage mechanism
4. **Sex-specific interaction:** Test whether the noise×insomnia interaction differs by sex (AF is more common in men, insomnia more common in women)

## 7. Falsification Criteria

### 7.1 Supportive Result
- Noise×insomnia interaction p < 0.10 (two-sided) in the fully adjusted Cox model
- Interaction hazard ratio > 1.0 (e.g., HR for high-noise + high-insomnia > product of individual HRs)
- Consistent direction in sensitivity analyses (1-year lag, PM2.5-restricted, categorical noise)
- **Interpretation:** Evidence for synergistic effect of noise and insomnia on AF risk. Supports combined intervention strategy.

### 7.2 Adverse Result (hypothesis refuted)
- Noise×insomnia interaction p > 0.10 in the fully adjusted model
- OR: Interaction HR < 1.0 (protective interaction, biologically implausible)
- **Interpretation:** Noise and sleep disturbance act through independent (additive) pathways. Prevention should target each factor separately. The mechanistic model of synergistic autonomic activation is not supported.

### 7.3 Inconclusive Result
- Interaction p between 0.05 and 0.10 (borderline)
- OR: Significant interaction in unadjusted model but attenuated to non-significance after full adjustment
- **Interpretation:** Insufficient evidence to confirm or refute synergy. May reflect (a) true null, (b) residual confounding, (c) underpowered interaction test. Additional data (larger cohort, better noise measurement, objective sleep data) would be needed.

### 7.4 What Would NOT Be Established
- Causality: Even a positive interaction does not prove causation (observational study, no randomization)
- Mechanism: Does not identify the specific biological pathway (sympathetic vs. inflammatory vs. epigenetic)
- Generalizability: UK Biobank is a self-selected, predominantly white cohort; results may not generalize to other populations
- Dose-response: Single noise measurement cannot establish a dose-response relationship

## 9. Limitations

1. **Single noise measurement:** Night noise (24022-0.0) is a model-based estimate at the residential address, not a personal dosimetry measurement. It does not capture occupational noise, recreational noise, or temporal changes in noise exposure. Non-differential misclassification would bias results toward the null, making a positive interaction finding more credible.

2. **Self-reported insomnia:** Insomnia (1200-0.0) is a single self-reported item (1-3 scale), not a validated clinical diagnosis or actigraphy-derived measure. Subjective sleep disturbance may be influenced by mood, pain, or other factors. However, it is the only sleep quality measure available in the full UKB cohort (accelerometer data is limited to ~90K participants).

3. **ICD10 AF coding:** AF is identified from ICD10 codes in 41270 (diagnoses) and 41280 (first inpatient diagnosis dates). Outpatient AF may be undercounted. The 41280 field records only inpatient diagnoses, so some AF events may lack a precise date. Sensitivity analysis using 41270 presence (without date) is planned.

4. **Residual confounding:** Despite adjustment for PM2.5, age, sex, smoking, and BMI, unmeasured confounders (socioeconomic status, noise sensitivity, sleep environment, other environmental exposures) may remain. The E-value analysis will quantify the strength of unmeasured confounding needed to explain away the interaction.

5. **UK Biobank selection bias:** UKB participants are healthier and more health-conscious than the general UK population. AF incidence may be lower, and the noise×sleep interaction may differ in a more representative population.

6. **No objective sleep data:** The full cohort lacks actigraphy or polysomnography. The interaction is tested with self-reported insomnia, which is a proxy for sleep disturbance. Accelerometer data (available for ~90K) could be used in a sensitivity analysis but would substantially reduce the cohort size.

7. **Single time-point exposure:** Both noise and insomnia are measured at baseline. No information on temporal changes in exposure or sleep. The analysis assumes stable exposure over the follow-up period.

8. **Cannot establish causality:** This is an observational study. Even a positive interaction does not prove that noise and insomnia causally interact to increase AF risk. The MR evidence [K3] provides complementary causal support for the noise→AF pathway but cannot address the interaction.
