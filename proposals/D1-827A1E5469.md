# Result-bearing successor: 24-hour lactate trajectory adds a small but unresolved cross-hospital prognostic increment

## Result, unresolved question, and clinical importance

This successor executes [prior hypothesis] without changing its age-inclusive population, norepinephrine-entry clock, 24-hour landmark, continuous M1-versus-M0 leave-one-hospital-out estimand, subsequent hospital-mortality outcome, +0.02 AUROC materiality threshold, proper-score/calibration/site-stability gates, or noncausal limits.

The locked hypothesis was that, among the parent's selected adults still hospitalized at 24 hours with an early recorded norepinephrine/Levophed infusion entry, baseline lactate at least 2 mmol/L, and a qualifying 12–24-hour lactate, adding continuous trajectory `T=ln(F/B)` and `T²` to a model already containing the absolute late lactate would improve out-of-hospital discrimination for eventual hospital mortality by at least +0.02 AUROC without worsening Brier score.

The complete result is **inconclusive about that prespecified material-gain hypothesis**. M0 AUROC was 0.72139 and M1 AUROC was 0.72619, a point gain of **+0.00479**. The 2,000-replicate copied-hospital refit-bootstrap 95% percentile interval was **−0.00849 to +0.02286**. Because the interval includes +0.02, the result is neither supportive nor adverse under the frozen hierarchy. It also includes zero, so even the direction of the incremental discrimination is unresolved after site-clustered refit uncertainty.

This resolves an important part of the prior uncertainty without overstating it. The observed gain is much smaller than the materiality target, proper scores improve slightly, calibration is acceptable, and every site-deletion contrast is positive; nevertheless, the upper uncertainty bound remains just above +0.02. The strongest justified conclusion is therefore that the selected trajectory produced a small, stable point improvement under this model, while the available 33-hospital sample did not precisely exclude a clinically material improvement. It does not establish that serial lactate is useful, useless, causal, mechanistically distinct, or beneficial to display or act upon.

The question is clinically consequential because the 24-hour clinician already has the late value. The extra burden and interpretive complexity of retaining the earlier value should be justified by incremental information, not by a crude mortality contrast between groups mathematically derived from the same two values. Inspected full-text evidence supports only a narrower prior claim. Baysan et al. (2022, DOI 10.1097/CCE.0000000000000750; frozen full-text XML source `[source checksum]`) reported that adding 24-hour lactate to a recalibrated APACHE IV model in sepsis increased C-statistic from 0.62 to 0.64, and that admission plus 24-hour lactate had the best fit. That is evidence that late and prior lactate can contribute to prognosis, but it does not test trajectory beyond a model already containing late lactate in this EICU phenotype. The inspected 2026 review by Levy et al. (DOI 10.1016/j.aicoj.2026.100061; full-text XML source `[source checksum]`) emphasizes serial interpretation while documenting adrenergic, inflammatory, ischemia-reperfusion, hepatic, and renal influences. That supports clinical importance and caution, not this experiment's untested mechanism or decision benefit.

It required known hospital discharge status and `hospitaldischargeoffset >= t0+1440`.

The full flow was 200,859 patient rows; 200,234 adult rows after the corrected age rule, including 7,081 mapped older-adult rows; 16,299 early-pressor stays; 14,786 deterministically selected people; 14,672 with a known hospital outcome; 13,023 still hospitalized through L24; 5,077 with a valid baseline lactate; 3,117 with baseline at least 2; and **1,506 measured people in 96 hospitals** with a valid late lactate. Thirty-one retained people had age `> 89` correctly mapped to 90. All 33 sites had both outcomes; the largest site contributed 115 people (9.36%), below the 15% gate.

Late F used the same analyte, unit, range, revision/conflict and tie rules, collection in [t0+720,t0+1440], and revision by L24, selecting nearest t0+1080 and then earlier collection. At each collection, only the greatest eligible revision was retained; disagreeing values at that revision invalidated the collection and identical ties used the smallest `labid`. The run found one invalid baseline collection conflict and no late conflict.

The age-inclusive binary overlap diagnostic also reproduced the parent's anticipated structural problem. Across corrected retained-cohort F quintiles, X=0/X=1 counts were 233/0, 229/30, 156/79, 133/120, and 37/212. The at-least-20-in-each-group gate therefore fails in the lowest quintile. ## Locked models, estimand, and execution

The sanity baseline predicted each held-out person using training-set mortality prevalence. M0 used age and age squared; Female and Male indicators with Other/Unknown omitted; t0/1440 and its square; baseline collection lag `(t0-B_offset)/360`; late-measurement age at the landmark `(L24-F_offset)/720`; and `ln(F)` and its square. M1 added `T=ln(F/B)` and `T²`. M2 added binary X to M0 instead and remained secondary.

Each continuous design column was winsorized at its training-fold 1st and 99th percentiles and standardized using its training-fold mean and population SD. Those constants were applied unchanged to the held-out hospital. Models were unweighted L2 logistic regressions with intercept, `C=1.0`, `lbfgs`, maximum 10,000 iterations, tolerance `1e-8`, and no class weights. Hospital identity was never a predictor. All 33 M0 and M1 fits converged and emitted finite probabilities, one per person.

The primary estimand was pooled out-of-hospital `ΔAUC=AUROC(M1)-AUROC(M0)`. Positive `ΔBrier=Brier(M0)-Brier(M1)` and positive `Δlogloss=logloss(M0)-logloss(M1)` favor trajectory. Calibration was assessed on pooled unrecalibrated held-out predictions.

For uncertainty, seed `20260924` generated 2,000 samples of 33 hospitals with replacement. Each sampled original hospital remained one held-out group; its copies were excluded together when it was held out, other sites entered training with sampled multiplicity, preprocessing and both models were refit, and test rows received the held site's multiplicity. All **2,000/2,000** replicates converged with finite metrics. Site deletion recomputed pooled fixed-prediction differences after dropping each original hospital.

M1 met both pooled calibration guards: slope 0.940 was within 0.80–1.20 and absolute calibration-in-the-large 0.00278 was below 0.05. All 33 fixed-prediction site deletions were finite and positive for ΔAUROC (100%; range +0.00301 to +0.00797), exceeding the 80% stability guard. Every source, population, event, site concentration, original-fit, bootstrap, and deletion computability gate passed.

The secondary binary M2 performed slightly worse than M0: AUROC 0.72033, Brier 0.20449, and log loss 0.59404; M2−M0 differences were −0.00106 AUROC, −0.000370 Brier improvement, and −0.000781 log-loss improvement. Because X is a thresholded function of B and F and fails its F-support diagnostic, this is only a model descriptor, not evidence for a binary nonclearance effect.

The frozen classification hierarchy yields **inconclusive**, specifically because the ΔAUROC interval includes +0.02. The result is not “adverse”: that label required the upper bound to be below +0.02. It is not “supportive”: that required the lower bound to be at least +0.02 and nonnegative lower-bound Brier improvement. It is not “opposite/harmful”: both difference intervals include zero and point estimates favor M1.

A supportive result would have justified external/prospective validation of retaining prior lactate as a prognostic feature. An adverse result would have argued that this model and selected EICU population rule out the prespecified material gain, without proving serial lactate useless. An opposite result would have indicated worsened transport under the locked procedure. This inconclusive result leaves a narrow residual uncertainty: a +0.02 AUROC gain is still compatible with the upper tail, but the observed point estimate and proper-score differences are small. Any additional same-snapshot modeling, changed threshold, larger feature set, or alternate bootstrap would be a new Lead-approved study, not a repair or rescue of this result.

## Verification and evidence limits

An automatic verifier can check source hashes and headers; joins and unique-person selection; age mapping; landmark eligibility; collection/revision/conflict/tie rules; B, F, T, and X arithmetic; outcome-blind site selection; exact feature matrices and fold-only preprocessing; absence of hospital predictors and leakage; estimator settings and convergence; one held-out prediction per person; copied-hospital grouping and refits; pooled, bootstrap, calibration, and deletion metrics; every gate; and whether the inconclusive label follows from an interval crossing +0.02.

Verifier fixtures must reject a supportive label for these results, an adverse label while the upper bound exceeds +0.02, any definitive label after a failed computability gate, and any causal or treatment-benefit conclusion even if computational outputs are correct. They must accept appropriately bounded supportive, adverse, opposite, and inconclusive interpretations, including this one. The verifier establishes computational fidelity and conclusion-to-output consistency, not clinical truth.

EICU cannot establish actual norepinephrine administration or dose, treatment intent, arterial versus venous lactate, assay indication, fluids, beta-agonists, source control, hepatic or renal mechanism, tissue perfusion, treatment limitations, exact death time or cause, what clinicians knew, or what they did. Clinical record adjudication is required for those interpretations. External prospective validation is required for transport beyond these selected measured EICU patients, and a prospective impact study—ideally randomized if a trajectory-triggered action is proposed—is required to claim that displaying or acting on trajectory improves decisions or outcomes.
