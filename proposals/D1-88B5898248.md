# Episode 13: acidemic 94–95% versus 96–97% two-boundary oxygenation mapping

## Unresolved bedside question and substantive advance

Among invasively ventilated adults with concurrent acidemia, do released monitor SpO2 observations of 94–95% rather than 96–97% map at least 10 percentage points more often into observed PaO2 60–89 mm Hg after restricting to the same hospital and similar measured pH, FiO2, PaCO2, and ICU-time context?

This is a consequential next calibration question because the parent showed that, at SpO2 96–97%, acidemic observations may map into PaO2 >=90 more often than normal-pH observations, but its estimate was too imprecise to resolve a 10-point margin. That result does not answer whether the adjacent lower monitor category moves acidemic observations into a two-boundary arterial range or simply replaces one uncertain concurrent contrast with another. The proposed outcome counts both tails: PaO2 <60 is outside the window, as is PaO2 >=90. It therefore cannot be made favorable merely by reducing high PaO2 while producing an equal increase in very low PaO2.

The 60-mm Hg lower boundary is a prespecified severe-low-arterial-oxygenation guardrail, and the 90-mm Hg upper boundary is anchored to the liberal-target category used by the inspected OXY-BREATHES review. Neither boundary defines tissue oxygen delivery, toxicity, a universally optimal target, or an individual treatment rule. The +10-point margin means one additional observation per ten maps inside the two-boundary window; it is interpretable but is not a validated minimum clinically important difference.

The experiment compares concurrent observed categories, not assigned oxygen targets. SpO2, pH, PaO2, and charted FiO2 can reflect severity, treatment, measurement, and clinician selection. Even a supportive result cannot show that lowering FiO2 to target 94–95 causes benefit or is safe.

## Existing evidence, strongest supported claim, and unresolved hypothesis

The inspected full-text XML of Delgado et al. (Antioxidants 2026; DOI 10.3390/antiox15020235; PMCID PMC12938712) describes 21,406 adults and 717,064 aligned measurements at one tertiary center. It reports higher PaO2 in acidotic states at comparable SpO2, modification by FiO2, frequent hyperoxemia at very high saturation, and generally physiologic PaO2 around SpO2 96–97. This supports physiologic plausibility and a large single-center concurrent association. It does not establish the EICU contrast, a target effect, safety, or clinical utility.

The inspected full-text XML of Nguyen et al. (OXY-BREATHES, Critical Care Medicine 2026; DOI 10.1097/CCM.0000000000007031; PMCID PMC13134654) defines conservative trial targets as SpO2 88–94% or PaO2 <80 and liberal targets as SpO2 >=94% or PaO2 >=90. Across nine randomized trials and 20,447 mechanically ventilated adults, 90-day mortality did not substantially differ (RR 1.01, 95% CI 0.94–1.09); adverse-event evidence was limited by outcome-specific imprecision and open-label designs. This establishes continuing target uncertainty, not equivalence of 94–95 and 96–97, not an acidemia-specific effect, and not harm from one PaO2 >=90 observation.

The strongest EICU claim currently supported is descriptive and computational. The all-source audit of snapshot `[source checksum]` reproduced the parent's 329 acidemic SpO2 96–97 observations and found 227 corresponding acidemic SpO2 94–95 observations. Together these are 556 observations from 440 stays, 439 people, and 41 hospitals; 45 people (and 45 stays) have observations in both bands. No sampling was used.

The frozen measured-overlap construction contains 199 observations in 55 cells from 171 stays, 171 people, and 11 hospitals: 85 observations at 94–95 and 114 at 96–97. Symmetric weighted probabilities for PaO2 60–89 are 77.077% and 64.199%, respectively, a lower-minus-higher difference of +12.878 points. A 5,000-replicate copied-hospital/copied-person bootstrap was defined in every replicate and produced a 95% percentile interval of −7.973 to +28.521 points. The interval contains zero and +10; the reference result is therefore inconclusive about direction and the material margin.

The component tails explain why this is not a safety claim. Weighted PaO2 >=90 probabilities are 14.513% versus 31.675% (difference −17.161 points; 95% interval −31.600 to +12.000), while PaO2 <60 probabilities are 8.409% versus 4.126% (difference +4.283 points; 95% interval −10.724 to +12.525). Only 7 and 5 raw PaO2 <60 events occur in the overlap groups. PaO2 <=70 is more common in the lower band (45.217% versus 15.090%; difference +30.127 points; 95% interval +1.697 to +48.596), which is a lower-arterial-mapping signal, not proof of hypoxic harm. The available data cannot distinguish a clinically safer lower target.

**Primary hypothesis, frozen in this candidate for compilation:** in the prespecified measured-overlap population, the symmetric overlap-weighted lower-minus-higher risk difference for `YW = 1[60 <= observed PaO2 < 90 mm Hg]` is at least +10 percentage points.

The hypothesis concerns a concurrent two-boundary mapping. It is not that an assigned SpO2 target of 94–95 causes PaO2 to enter the window, improves oxygen delivery, reduces oxidative injury, or improves outcomes.

## Falsification and interpretation

**Supportive for a material two-boundary mapping difference:** lower 95% bound for RDW >=+10 points and every mandatory diagnostic passes. The strongest allowed conclusion is: “Within this retrospective measured-overlap construction, acidemic observations at released SpO2 94–95 mapped at least 10 points more often than observations at 96–97 into observed PaO2 60–89.” This is not a target, safety, or treatment conclusion.

**Adverse to +10:** upper 95% bound <+10. If the interval is wholly above zero, report a smaller positive mapping difference adverse to the material margin. If it spans zero, report unresolved direction and adversity to +10. If wholly below zero, report an opposite-direction association.

**Inconclusive about +10:** interval contains +10 or any mandatory diagnostic fails. If the interval is wholly above zero, a positive association may be reported while material magnitude remains unresolved. A positive point estimate, favorable unweighted result, or consistent sensitivity signs alone is not support.

Tail discordance must be explicit:

- Window support with an increase in PaO2 <60 means more observations map into the window but the very-low tail also increased; never call the lower band safer.
- Window support with no resolved reduction in PaO2 >=90 means the gain can arise from the low tail and category redistribution; report the full vector.
- Reduced PaO2 >=90 with no window gain means observations moved partly below 60; this is adverse to the two-boundary hypothesis.
- A resolved increase in PaO2 <=70 is evidence of lower arterial mapping only, not hypoxic injury.
- Normal-pH interaction, P/F, restricted mean, or repeated-person findings cannot rescue the primary.

No outcome establishes clinician target, delivered oxygen, causal titration effect, tissue oxygen delivery, oxidative injury, ischemia, benefit, harm, or a treatment rule.

## Missing evidence and stronger claims

EICU lacks assigned SpO2 target, intended target, titration protocol, delivered FiO2, waveform synchronization/artifact, device make/calibration, measured arterial saturation, specimen identity, temperature, 2,3-DPG, reliable ventilation persistence, and tissue oxygen delivery. Blood-gas testing is clinician-selected and not missing at random. Public narrative text is removed and structured notes cannot recover these facts.

Clinical adjudication is required for arterial specimen and ventilation truth and for interpretation of implausible/extreme measurements. Prospective synchronized measurement is required to validate the category mapping. A trial that assigns 94–95 versus 96–97 with protocolized titration, exposure-duration measurement, rescue criteria, and patient-centered outcomes is required to decide safety or benefit. External validation is required for transportability.
