# HCC post-TACE trajectory: episode-4 endpoint-validity and competing-event child

Status: proposed Harbor experiment. No cohort count, model fit, adjudication, or clinical result has been executed in this branch. This is a substantive child of `[prior hypothesis]`, preserving its HCC population, procedure time zero, laboratory window, days 8–90 target, all-eligible primary population, and baseline-versus-dynamic comparison.

## Scientific opening, hypothesis, and boundary

The parent’s strongest evidence-supported claim is only that an early post-TACE laboratory trajectory could carry incremental prognostic information for a later coded decompensation admission, after accounting for baseline risk and observation intensity. The unresolved clinical claim is stronger: that the trajectory reflects clinically meaningful loss of hepatic/renal reserve rather than a coding/documentation convention, a care-intensity signal, early acute-care selection, or ascertainment after discharge.

The primary hypothesis remains: in adults with HCC undergoing a first observed TACE, with no documented high-specificity decompensation before or at index, a 0–7-day trajectory showing coordinated deterioration in synthetic/renal reserve predicts a new post-discharge decompensation event during days 8–90 beyond a baseline-only model. An isolated AST/ALT flare is the leading biological rival. This episode adds two non-biological rivals:

1. discharge and early acute-care selection may determine who is observed and who receives a coded endpoint; and
2. a diagnosis code may be present without clinical decompensation, while clinically meaningful decompensation may be documented without the prespecified code.

A supportive result would therefore require more than incremental discrimination against the original code. It would require compatible timing, competing-event analyses, and a blinded endpoint-validation sample in which code-positive events are clinically credible across availability and care-regime strata. An adverse result would localize the signal to documentation or observation practice. An inconclusive result would show that the source can estimate coded prediction but not clinical validity; it would not refute a biological reserve-loss hypothesis.

Closest inspected evidence is [K1] Sanai et al., which reports post-locoregional hepatic decompensation and baseline-reserve associations but does not test this routine-laboratory trajectory or endpoint validity; [K2] Piscaglia and Ogasawara, which emphasizes TACE benefit/risk selection and preservation of hepatic reserve but does not provide a days-8–90 trajectory test; and [K3] Yang et al., a protocol documenting inconsistent post-TACE syndrome risk-factor evidence and the need for systematic endpoint definitions, not a completed validation result. All three were inspected abstract-only; no inaccessible full text or supplement is claimed. The new contribution is an endpoint-validity and competing-event test of the parent’s prediction question, not a claim that these works establish the present hypothesis.

## Frozen population, time zero, and temporal estimand

Use only the HCC dataset. Identify each patient’s first procedure row whose `手术` contains TACE, `肝动脉化疗栓塞`, `经导管肝动脉栓塞`, or `动脉化疗栓塞`, with parseable `开始时间` and a matching encounter. Require age >=18 and an HCC diagnosis (at minimum `肝细胞癌` or `肝恶性肿瘤`) before/on the index encounter. Exclude the parent’s high-specificity pre/index terms: `腹水`, `肝性脑病`, `消化道出血`, `食管胃底静脉曲张`, `肝肾综合征`, `急性肝衰竭`, `肝功能衰竭`, `自发性腹膜炎`, and `黄疸`; report broader cirrhosis/decompensation flags separately.

Time zero is the earliest qualifying `手术.开始时间`. Baseline is the closest assay-specific quantitative result in [-14, 0] days, with [-30, 0] as sensitivity analysis. Dynamic laboratory information is restricted to [0, 7] days after time zero, with raw timestamps, assay/name regime, specimen type, and duplicate status retained. The primary source-follow-up rule is unchanged: use only index dates with a verifiable 90-day observation horizon, using 2025-10-03 or a recomputed equivalent cutoff after checking source maxima.

The primary binary outcome remains the first distinct encounter after the index `出院时间` whose admission time is in [8, 90] days after time zero and whose diagnosis contains a prespecified high-specificity decompensation component. Report the composite and ascites, encephalopathy, gastrointestinal/variceal bleeding, renal/hepatic failure, and jaundice components separately. Any admission and repeat TACE remain non-equivalent secondary outcomes. Do not silently turn missing discharge into an event, censoring time, or new time zero.

The primary estimand is still the paired chronological-test-set difference in Brier score, calibration, discrimination, and decision-curve net benefit between the baseline-only and dynamic models for the all-eligible population. It is incremental prognostic value for an operational endpoint, not a treatment effect, causal effect, mechanism, or action threshold.

## Endpoint construction and blinded validation substudy

### Operational strata

Construct the primary diagnosis endpoint from `诊断名称` on the qualifying post-discharge encounter using frozen, component-specific lexical rules. Before looking at model predictions, create four mutually reported endpoint strata:

- diagnosis-only/clinical-document unavailable: the diagnosis rule is positive, but no linked usable clinical-document row is available for the encounter;
- document-confirmed: the diagnosis rule is positive and the same component is present in one or more of the document fields above, with document time/encounter linkage valid;
- discordant: diagnosis and an available document disagree, including a diagnosis-positive/document-negative record or a document-positive/diagnosis-negative record;
- negative/other: neither source supports the component.

The first category is intentionally called diagnosis-only rather than clinically true. A diagnosis-positive record with an available document that does not support the component belongs to discordant, not document-confirmed. Keep missing-document cases separate from discordance. Repeat this table for each component and for the composite.

### Sampling and blinding

Freeze a deterministic validation sample from the chronological test period before adjudication. Sample across coded outcome status, A0/A1/A2 laboratory availability, calendar era, department, index discharge completeness, and early acute-care status. Use a fixed cap of 30 records per nonempty cell of (coded positive/negative × availability stratum × calendar tertile × department group), include all coded-positive records when a cell has fewer than 30, and cap the total at 600 by a fixed seeded random draw if the cross-product is larger. Report sampling fractions and use inverse sampling weights for validation estimates. If a cell is too sparse, collapse only adjacent calendar strata or clinically similar departments using a rule frozen before inspecting labels; otherwise report it as unestimable.

The adjudication packet is assembled from diagnoses, encounters, clinical_documents, medications, and, when present, orders/examinations. It contains index and outcome encounter times, all relevant text fields, medication/order timing, and the component-specific code, but no model prediction, laboratory trajectory, feature importance, or model-derived risk. Adjudicators are blinded to model outputs and to the primary endpoint label; the packet’s raw source fields cannot be hidden because they are the evidence being adjudicated. A second clinician independently reviews a fixed subset, with disagreements resolved by a prespecified third review. Record agreement, missing fields, and reasons for unresolvable labels.

Adjudication labels are: (a) definite/probable new or worsening ascites, encephalopathy, bleeding, renal/hepatic failure, or jaundice; (b) no clinical support; (c) competing illness or HCC progression better explaining the encounter; (d) isolated expected/TACE-related injury without decompensation; (e) text/document invalid or temporally mislinked; and (f) indeterminate. Also label whether the episode appears planned, unplanned, or unknown. “Planned” is accepted only when explicitly supported by the encounter/document/order/procedure record; scheduling cannot be reconstructed reliably from these tables. Unknown planned status is retained, not forced into unplanned. Medication patterns are corroboration, not proof of a syndrome.

Validation outputs are sensitivity, positive predictive value, and weighted agreement of the code composite against adjudicated definite/probable decompensation, with 95% design/bootstrap intervals and separate diagnosis-only, document-confirmed, discordant, missing-document, availability, calendar, department, and planned-status-unknown results. Do not use adjudicated labels to tune the primary model. If the sample is too small for precise validation, call the endpoint clinically unvalidated rather than treating code performance as truth.

### Timing and competing early events

Preserve the primary binary target exactly, but create an independent event-history audit. From index time, derive:

- index admission discharge lag and whether `出院时间` is parseable;
- any distinct post-discharge admission/acute-care encounter in days 0–7;
- repeat TACE or another procedure in days 0–7;
- first days 8–90 decompensation encounter;
- first days 8–90 non-decompensation acute admission;
- first days 8–90 repeat TACE/procedure; and
- whether a qualifying decompensation record occurs after an earlier non-decompensation encounter.

For the primary target, earlier non-decompensation care does not erase a later qualifying decompensation: the binary estimand asks whether any qualifying decompensation occurs in the frozen days 8–90 window. For a secondary competing-risk estimand, define entry as `max(index + 8 days, discharge time)` and exit at the first days 8–90 event, end of the 90-day horizon, or the source follow-up boundary. A discharge after day 90 produces no estimable post-discharge risk interval and is reported separately. Competing first events are non-decompensation acute admission and repeat TACE/procedure; early days 0–7 acute-care return is a prespecified state/stratum before entry, not silently treated as censoring. Death is unavailable and cannot be treated as a competing risk.

Fit cause-specific discrete-time hazards and cumulative-incidence curves only as a secondary analysis. The event-history alternative must distinguish the estimand “first observed competing event” from the primary “any qualifying decompensation by day 90.” Report both rather than relabeling one as the other. Examine whether dynamic-model performance changes after stratifying by early acute-care return, discharge lag, or missing discharge. Do not use any post-day-7 observation in either prediction model.

## Baseline/dynamic model and substantive alternative

The transparent models are fit on one row per eligible index patient:

- **B0 baseline-only:** L2-regularized logistic regression for the primary binary composite using age, sex, index department, HCC/cirrhosis/portal-vein-thrombus/prior-decompensation flags, procedure context, pre-index utilization, baseline assay-specific values, and baseline missingness. A no-lab B0 is a sensitivity comparator.
- **B1 dynamic:** B0 plus prespecified day-0–7 assay features: baseline-to-last change, robust slope, extrema, last value, bin coverage, first-lab lag, observation count, and separate AST/ALT-flare blocks. Labs are transformed within training-set assay/name regimes because units are unavailable. B1 does not condition its primary score on complete trajectory, discharge, or a post-outcome record.
- **Care-intensity audit:** add pre-index and day-0–7 encounter/admission/department/lab/procedure/medication/order/examination counts, discharge lag, and early-return indicators in a locked sensitivity model. This is not the primary dynamic claim; it tests whether the gain is explained by the observation/care process.

The substantive alternative is an **observation-aware competing-risk landmark model**. It is selected because the unresolved scientific issue is endpoint timing and observation selection, which B1’s summaries cannot represent. Inputs are the same baseline variables and day-0–7 laboratory timestamps/assay regimes plus bin-level lab indicators/counts, first-lab lag, index discharge lag/completeness, early acute-care return, repeat TACE/procedure indicators, index department, calendar era, and pre-index utilization. The outcome head models discrete-time transitions from post-discharge entry to days 8–90 decompensation, non-decompensation acute admission, or repeat TACE/procedure; the observation head models laboratory availability and care encounters through day 7. Use partial pooling by department/era and assay/name regime, and a pattern-mixture sensitivity parameter for informative missingness. Generate both a predicted probability of the primary binary outcome and a cause-specific cumulative-incidence curve for first decompensation. No post-day-7 data enter the inputs.

The alternative can reveal whether the apparent trajectory gain remains after modeling who is discharged, who returns early, and who is observed, and whether a risk signal predicts a clinically specific event or simply first acute-care contact. It also exposes whether the conclusions depend on treating non-decompensation admission or repeat TACE as censoring. It cannot identify a biological mechanism. Risks are stronger assumptions about missingness, transition ordering, sparse department/era cells, and planned status; convergence or positivity failure is an informative feasibility limitation.

The neural sequence alternative is deferred. It could represent irregular timestamps, but it would add capacity without resolving whether endpoint ascertainment and early-care selection generate the association. Revisit it only if the transparent and observation-aware models show a reproducible residual pattern that is scientifically about nonlinear trajectory structure rather than data capture.

Do not interpret a wide null as refutation.

The actual scientific deliverable is newly fitted B0 and B1, the care-intensity audit, and the observation-aware competing-risk model; frozen all-patient test predictions; event-history and discharge-selection flow; diagnosis/document/discordance tables; blinded adjudication labels and weighted validity estimates; component and planned-status-unknown results; paired uncertainty; and negative-control outputs. ## Decision-curve interpretation

Decision-curve net benefit is computationally checkable only as a standardized comparison under an assumed threshold probability and an assumed relative harm of false positives versus false negatives. In this dataset it is not, by itself, evidence that clinicians should act on a risk score: there is no observed intervention triggered by the score, no validated action threshold, no reliable planned/unplanned label, no adjudicated endpoint for the full cohort, no mortality linkage, and no prospective calibration or external validation.

Therefore report DCA as exploratory model-utility analysis against the operational coded endpoint and, separately, against adjudicated labels in the validation substudy. It may support a candidate triage aid only if adjudicated endpoint validity is acceptable, calibration is stable across availability/calendar/department strata, the threshold and action are specified in advance, and an expert team confirms that false-positive and false-negative consequences are clinically plausible. Even then, it would justify prospective decision-impact validation, not clinical deployment. No DCA result can establish mechanism, treatment benefit, causal TACE harm, or a safe intervention threshold.

## Falsification and interpretation

Mandatory controls are:

1. within-patient timestamp permutation preserving values, assay names, and counts;
2. a matched pre-index pseudo-window with the same feature construction;
3. measurement/care-only prediction from counts, lag, discharge/early-return, department, and era without lab values;
4. AST/ALT-only versus reserve-panel-only blocks;
5. any-admission and repeat-TACE outcomes as non-equivalent outcomes;
6. a future-observation audit proving that no post-day-7 value, document, medication, diagnosis, or encounter is used as a feature;
7. endpoint-stratum analysis comparing diagnosis-only, document-confirmed, discordant, and missing-document events;
8. competing-risk comparison with and without early days 0–7 acute-care returns; and
9. a regime-label shuffle for department/calendar observation heterogeneity.

Supportive evidence requires a non-negligible paired dynamic gain on the untouched chronological test in the all-eligible population; no material timestamp-permutation or pre-index reproduction; persistence after care-intensity adjustment and the missingness sensitivity range; compatible B0/B1 and observation-aware calibration; endpoint PPV/sensitivity and adjudication agreement that are not confined to one availability, department, or calendar stratum; and directionally consistent component results. This would support a clinically credible incremental prognostic regularity, not a mechanism or clinical decision aid.

Adverse evidence includes disappearance after care-intensity or observation-aware adjustment; dominance by discharge lag, early return, counts, or a single documentation regime; timestamp or pre-index reproduction; code-positive events judged as planned care, HCC progression, isolated TACE injury, or unsupported text; major diagnosis/document discordance; calibration failure in held-out regimes; or an effect limited to first acute-care contact rather than decompensation. This would revise the claim to a local documentation/observation association or abandon the trajectory direction if an adequately observed adjudicated test repeatedly fails.

Inconclusive evidence includes missing/late discharge, too few adjudicated records, unresolvable planned status, sparse competing-event cells, unknown assay units, high unmapped assay names, weak common support, unstable competing-risk convergence, or wide intervals. These defer clinical interpretation; they do not refute reserve loss. Continue only if the primary computation and a sufficiently informative validation sample are complete. Narrow to a prespecified regime if only that regime is estimable; defer clinical utility if adjudication or assay review is missing; require prospective/external study before acting on the score.

Computationally checkable claims are source hashes/headers, composite-key joins, timing bounds, split integrity, model inputs, predictions, code/document strata, adjudication sampling and agreement calculations, calibration, paired uncertainty, competing-risk transitions, DCA under declared assumptions, and leakage/negative-control behavior. Clinical truth, planned status, mechanism, causality, mortality, action thresholds, and external validity require clinician adjudication, assay/unit review, prospective decision-impact work, or another study.

## Alternatives and resource decision

B0/B1 is the transparent baseline/dynamic comparison: inputs are the frozen baseline and [0,7] assay summaries; target is the primary days-8–90 binary composite; fit is chronological train/validation/test; uncertainty is paired patient bootstrap. It is retained as the interpretable reference and is adequate for the incremental-prognostic estimand.

The observation-aware competing-risk landmark model uses the same labs and baseline inputs plus explicit availability, discharge, early-care, and competing-event processes; fit targets are transition hazards, availability, and the primary binary risk; it uses the same split and reports transition-specific and primary-risk uncertainty. It is selected because it can distinguish a reserve trajectory from a pathway through discharge and acute-care observation, information B1 loses.

A neural sequence model using irregular assay/time tokens is deferred for the scientific reason that greater capacity would not identify whether a coded endpoint is clinically valid or whether missingness is informative. GPU use is therefore not required. B0/B1 likely fit with 4–8 CPU cores and <32 GB RAM in tens of minutes; the competing-risk/observation fit and 2,000-replicate bootstrap are estimated at 16 CPUs, 64–128 GB RAM, no GPU, and up to 4–8 hours (28,800 seconds). These are planning estimates, not measured results. A bounded train-only convergence probe may use 4 CPUs/16 GB and cannot be reported as study evidence.
