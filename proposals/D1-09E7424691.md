# Time-weighted hypotension burden and subsequent acute kidney injury in eICU

## Scientific question

Among adult ICU patients with systolic blood pressure measurements in the first 24 hours of ICU care, does a prespecified time-weighted hypotension burden measure predict subsequent acute kidney injury (AKI) better than the minimum systolic blood pressure alone?

The unresolved claim is that duration and time-weighting of hypotension add prognostic information beyond the nadir, after accounting for baseline illness severity, vasopressor exposure, kidney function at baseline, and hospital. A null result would weaken the claim that duration is clinically useful; a positive result would support further validation and prospective study.

## Why it matters

Minimum systolic blood pressure is a common bedside summary, but it can understate sustained hypoperfusion. If time-weighted burden improves early AKI risk stratification, it may inform monitoring and resuscitation decisions. If it does not, clinicians and researchers should not add a more complex measure without evidence.

## Prior evidence

- [K1] The eICU 2.0 data dictionary provides the source files and table metadata for the study, including vitalPeriodic, vitalAperiodic, lab, infusionDrug, patient, and hospital tables. The vital measurements are not continuous; the interval rule is an approximation.
- [K2] Lehman et al. (2010) showed that hypotension is a risk factor for AKI in ICU patients, with duration and time-weighting being relevant measures. This proposal extends that work by testing whether time-weighted burden adds prognostic information beyond the nadir, with hospital transportability analysis.
- [K3] KDIGO criteria define AKI staging using creatinine and urine output thresholds. This proposal uses KDIGO creatinine criteria for the AKI outcome, with a creatinine-only fallback when urine output is unavailable.

## Population and temporal design

Use eICU 2.0 adult ICU admissions with at least one systolic blood pressure measurement in the first 24 hours after ICU admission. Define the exposure window as 0–24 hours from ICU admission. Define the outcome window as 24–72 hours after ICU admission to avoid immediate outcome overlap. Exclude patients with missing ICU admission time, age under 18, or no creatinine measurement available for baseline or follow-up.

## Exposure definitions

- **Nadir baseline**: minimum systolic blood pressure (SBP) during the 0–24 hour window.
- **Time-weighted burden**: for each SBP measurement below 90 mm Hg, weight by the duration of the interval during which the patient is assumed to remain hypotensive. Use a prespecified interval rule: each measurement represents the preceding interval up to the next measurement, capped at 6 hours. This is an approximation because eICU vital measurements are not continuous.
- **Alternative burden**: cumulative minutes below 90 mm Hg using the same interval rule, without time-weighting.

All three measures are computed from the same set of SBP measurements to ensure a fair comparison.

## Outcome definition

Primary outcome: AKI stage ≥1 by KDIGO creatinine criteria during the 24–72 hour window. Define baseline creatinine as the first creatinine measurement in the 0–24 hour window. AKI is defined as creatinine ≥2× baseline, or creatinine ≥2× baseline plus urine output <0.5 mL/kg/h for 6 hours, or creatinine ≥3× baseline plus urine output <0.5 mL/kg/h for 6 hours or urgent renal replacement therapy. If urine output is unavailable, use creatinine-only criteria and report this limitation.

## Covariates and adjustment

Adjust for:
- Age, sex, race (from patient table)
- ICU admission type (from patient table)
- Baseline APACHE II score (from apachePatientResult table)
- Baseline creatinine (from lab table)
- Vasopressor exposure in the first 24 hours (from infusionDrug table)
- Hospital (from hospital table)

## Analysis plan

1. **Descriptive analysis**: report the distribution of SBP measurements, hypotension burden, and AKI incidence by hospital.
2. **Univariate analysis**: compare the three exposure measures using logistic regression for AKI.
3. **Multivariable analysis**: fit logistic regression models with each exposure measure, adjusted for the covariates above. Compare models using likelihood ratio tests and AUC.
4. **Hospital transportability**: fit a hierarchical logistic regression model with hospital-level random intercepts to assess whether the association is consistent across hospitals.
5. **Sensitivity analysis**: repeat the analysis with a 12-hour exposure window and with a 48-hour outcome window.

## Falsification criteria

- If the time-weighted burden measure does not improve AUC or likelihood ratio test over the nadir baseline after adjustment, the hypothesis is not supported.
- If the association is driven by a small number of hospitals or by patients with extreme measurement density, the result is inconclusive.
- If the association disappears after adjusting for vasopressor exposure, the result suggests that the signal is mediated by treatment rather than by hypotension itself.

## Limitations

- eICU vital measurements are not continuous; the interval rule is an approximation.
- Urine output may be unavailable for some patients, limiting the KDIGO criteria.
- Hospital-level confounding cannot be fully addressed with observational data.
- The study is prognostic, not causal; a positive result does not establish that reducing hypotension duration prevents AKI.
