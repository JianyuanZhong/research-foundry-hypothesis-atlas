# Nighttime ICU discharge and residual physiologic instability: a hospital-held-out eICU observational interaction study

## Decision question and status

This is a substantive Episode 8 successor using the assessed valid transportability parent `[prior hypothesis]`, the repairable native-eICU seed-06 import `[prior hypothesis]`, and the repairable seed-10 recovery-speed foundation `[prior hypothesis]`.

The clinical decision is whether an ICU discharge occurring overnight deserves a different level of post-ICU surveillance when the patient still has recorded physiologic instability immediately before discharge. The expert seed-06 question is retained exactly in substance:

> Is the association between nighttime ICU discharge and subsequent recorded hospital death concentrated among stays with residual instability before discharge?

This is an observational, conditional-association and transportability study. It is not a causal estimate of the effect of discharging at night, a recommendation to delay discharge, a staffing or bed-capacity study, or a hospital-quality ranking.

No results are claimed in this proposal.

## Evidence-supported claim, unresolved claim, and advance

The strongest claim supported before execution is structural and data-bounded. The frozen eICU snapshot contains patient-stay and hospital identifiers; ICU-relative offsets; recorded ICU and hospital discharge clock times, statuses and locations; structured vital-sign observations; respiratory-support status fields; and infusion/treatment records. The source guide, complete eICU catalog, relevant schema JSONs and raw headers were inspected. Representative rows show recorded `Alive`/`Expired` discharge status values and clock/offset fields, but the execution must enumerate all encountered values before freezing inclusion rules.

The unresolved claim is the prespecified interaction, not the existence of any main effect:

> Among adult eICU unit stays discharged alive from the ICU, the adjusted association between a discharge in the recorded local-clock interval 23:00–07:00 and subsequent recorded hospital death within 48 hours is more positive for stays with high residual instability in the final six ICU hours than for stays with low residual instability, and this interaction is reproducible in hospitals held out from model fitting.

The null is no concentration of the nighttime association by the instability definition, including a zero or reversed interaction after adjustment and hospital-held-out evaluation. A negative interaction is also a legitimate adverse result.

The substantive advance over the earlier renal transportability line is a distinct disposition decision: whether the safety signal around an ICU disposition event is heterogeneous by the patient’s immediately preceding recorded state. It retains the prior line’s exact frozen-source discipline, hospital-held-out uncertainty, missingness safeguards and strict noncausal interpretation, but moves the primary estimand to a clinically consequential ICU discharge interaction. Seed 10 contributes only the need to represent support/recovery state and disposition timing explicitly; no hospital practice effect or quality effect is assumed.

## Population and index

The observational unit is an ICU unit stay identified by `patientunitstayid`.

Primary cohort:

- adult stays with numeric `patient.age >= 18`; report missing age and the eICU `> 89` age convention rather than treating it as exact age;
- nonmissing, finite `patient.unitdischargeoffset = D` with `D >= 360`;
- `patient.unitdischargestatus` exactly equal to the audited alive code `Alive`;
- valid `patient.unitdischargetime24`, parsed as HH:MM:SS, with no unrecognized clock value;
- enough pre-discharge physiology for the prespecified instability definition below;
- hospital-discharge status and offset sufficient to classify the primary 48-hour outcome, unless retained in the flow as unknown ascertainment.

To avoid giving a person or hospitalization several primary discharge opportunities, the primary analysis takes the earliest eligible alive ICU discharge within each `patienthealthsystemstayid`, ordered by `unitdischargeoffset` and then `unitvisitnumber`. A sensitivity retains all eligible unit stays and clusters uncertainty by `patienthealthsystemstayid` and, separately, `uniquepid`. The all-stay sensitivity is descriptive if the same hospital admission has multiple ICU discharges.

The index is the recorded ICU discharge at minute `D). The pre-index physiology window is the half-open ICU-relative interval `[D-360, D)`, exactly the final six hours before discharge. No observation at or after `D` enters the exposure or adjustment variables. This prevents the discharge row itself, post-discharge care, or a later hospital outcome from leaking into the predictor.

Exclude only after reporting the full flow:

- nonadult or unparseable age;
- missing/invalid discharge offset or clock;
- ICU discharge status not exactly Alive;
- insufficient measured physiology for the primary instability ascertainment;
- duplicate or conflicting key rows that cannot be resolved by the frozen tie rule.

Insufficient physiology is an instability-unknown state, not stability. Missing discharge outcome fields are an outcome-unknown state, not survival.

## Nighttime exposure with exact clock boundaries

Parse `patient.unitdischargetime24` using its recorded local clock:

- Night: `23:00:00 <= unitdischargetime24 < 24:00:00` or `00:00:00 <= unitdischargetime24 < 07:00:00`.
- Day: `07:00:00 <= unitdischargetime24 < 23:00:00`.

The primary exposure is `night=1) for night and `night=0) for day. Calendar date, time zone, daylight-saving status and staffing schedule are unavailable; the clock is used exactly as recorded, without reconstructing dates from offsets. A prespecified sensitivity uses 22:00–06:00, but it cannot replace the primary definition. Invalid or missing clock times remain exposure-unknown.

Report night/day counts by hospital, unit type, unit stay type, discharge location and ICU length of stay. Do not call a time-of-day association an organizational effect.

## Residual pre-discharge instability

The primary instability phenotype is a recorded physiologic state, not expert adjudication.

Use the final six-hour window `[D-360,D)), partitioned into twelve half-open 30-minute bins. In each bin, retain finite values after a preparation audit and fixed plausibility filters:

- MAP: `vitalAperiodic.noninvasivemean`, accepted only in 20–200 mmHg;
- heart rate: `vitalPeriodic.heartrate`, 20–250 beats/min;
- respiratory rate: `vitalPeriodic.respiration`, 3–80/min;
- oxygen saturation: `vitalPeriodic.sao2`, 50–100 percent;
- temperature: `vitalPeriodic.temperature`, 25–45 degrees C.

If several values for one variable fall in a bin, use their median. A bin is covered if at least one accepted component is present. Require at least six covered bins and at least three bins with an accepted MAP or heart-rate value. No missing value is imputed to normal for the exposure. The audit must report component-level coverage and invalid-value exclusions.

A covered bin is unstable if any available component meets one of these fixed thresholds:

- MAP <65 mmHg;
- heart rate >=120/min;
- respiratory rate >=24/min or <=8/min;
- oxygen saturation <92%;
- temperature >=38.5 C or <35 C.

Define:

`instability_burden = unstable covered bins / covered bins`.

The primary modifier is `high_instability = 1` when `instability_burden >= 0.25`, otherwise 0. With the minimum six covered bins, this requires at least two unstable bins. The continuous burden and each component are prespecified secondary representations. A sensitivity uses burden >=0.50 and another uses a continuous spline; neither may be chosen after seeing outcomes.

The primary interaction is `night * high_instability`. “Residual” means only that the window is immediately before the recorded ICU discharge; it does not mean unresolved disease, inappropriate discharge, or a clinician-adjudicated instability syndrome.

## Outcome and ascertainment

Let `H = patient.hospitaldischargeoffset` and `S = patient.hospitaldischargestatus`. The primary outcome is subsequent recorded hospital death within 48 hours after ICU discharge:

`Y48 = 1` if `S = Expired` and `0 < H-D <= 2880` minutes.

The strict lower bound requires death after, rather than at the same timestamp as, the ICU discharge. A hospital discharge status of Expired is a database-recorded status; it is not independently adjudicated mortality.

Primary ascertainment states are mutually exclusive:

1. **Death within 48 hours:** `S=Expired`, valid `H), and `0 < H-D <= 2880`.
2. **Alive hospital discharge before 48 hours:** `S=Alive`, valid `H), and `0 < H-D < 2880`; this is a competing discharge/censoring event in a time-to-event representation and not a death.
3. **Known no death by 48 hours:** valid `H>D` and either `S=Alive` or `S=Expired` with `H-D>2880`. The latter is a death after the primary horizon and is not a 48-hour event.
4. **Outcome unknown:** missing/invalid `S` or `H), noncanonical status, `H<=D` without a resolvable same-stay explanation, or internally conflicting discharge fields.

A valid alive hospital discharge exactly at 48 hours is classified as known no death by the closed primary boundary; the audit must report endpoint ties. A same-time death is not accepted as a subsequent event and is reported as an inconsistency/unknown state.

The secondary full-hospitalization endpoint is `YH=1` when `S=Expired` and `H>D), with `S=Alive` and `H>D` as known no event. This secondary endpoint asks whether the interaction persists through the recorded hospital stay and is not a substitute for the 48-hour decision window. Deaths after hospital discharge, post-acute mortality and unrecorded transfers are unavailable.

For the primary model, unknown outcomes are retained in the cohort flow and stratified by hospital and exposure group. They are not silently coded as non-events. The main estimand is evaluated in the ascertainable cohort, with an inverse-probability-of-ascertainment sensitivity using only pre-discharge variables; unstable weights or informative missingness make the result inconclusive rather than “fixed.”

## Adjustment blocks and models

The primary estimand is the adjusted standardized association interaction:

`Delta_int = (p_hat[night, high_instability] - p_hat[day, high_instability]) - (p_hat[night, low_instability] - p_hat[day, low_instability])`.

This is a model-standardized association contrast. Setting night/day or instability values in a prediction model does not create causal counterfactuals.

### Transparent baseline

Fit a prespecified regularized logistic model for `Y48` in the ascertainable cohort, with:

- `night`, `high_instability`, and their product as the prespecified primary terms;
- age, gender, ethnicity, hospital admission source, unit type, unit stay type and audited admission-diagnosis category;
- log-transformed `unitdischargeoffset` and hospital stratum descriptors `numbedscategory`, `teachingstatus`, and `region);
- final-six-hour median, minimum, maximum, last value and linear slope for accepted MAP, heart rate, respiratory rate, oxygen saturation and temperature, with coverage indicators;
- the continuous instability burden and component indicators only in a secondary expanded baseline, not simultaneously with arbitrary duplicated encodings in the primary model.

Categorical maps, scaling, imputation and regularization are fitted in training hospitals only. If an adjustment field is missing, retain an explicit missing category or training-only imputation and report the missingness; do not use post-discharge fields. No hospital ID is fitted as a predictor in held-out evaluation.

The primary reporting is the interaction coefficient with its hospital-clustered uncertainty plus the four standardized predicted-risk cells and `Delta_int`. The coefficient alone is not called a clinical risk ratio.

### Bounded model-learning alternative

Fit a fixed, CPU-bounded gradient-boosted tree classifier as a model-learning stress test using the same training-only preprocessing and the same pre-discharge feature blocks:

- 100 boosting iterations;
- learning rate 0.03;
- maximum depth 3 or, in implementations without that parameter, maximum 15 leaves;
- minimum leaf size 100;
- L2 regularization 1.0;
- no post hoc hyperparameter search.

The alternative may use the primary physiology summaries, instability burden/components, admission context and audited recorded-support flags if the semantic gate passes. It must not use discharge status, hospital discharge offset/status/location, post-discharge rows, `labresultrevisedoffset`, future observations or hospital ID. The tree model tests whether the interaction and transport pattern survive a bounded nonlinear representation; it does not turn a predictive pattern into a causal effect.

## Hospital-held-out computation and uncertainty

Assign hospitals to five deterministic folds using a documented hash of `hospitalid`; every stay from a hospital remains in one fold. The primary results are out-of-hospital predictions from models fit without the held-out hospital. A leave-one-hospital-out sensitivity is required for hospitals meeting the event/overlap gate. A random patient split is descriptive only and cannot be the primary result.

For each held-out hospital and each model, report:

- ascertainment-state counts, event rate and exposure-by-instability cell counts;
- standardized four-cell risks and `Delta_int`;
- Brier score, calibration intercept and slope, AUROC and precision-recall area where estimable;
- sensitivity, false-negative rate and alert rate at a 0.90 sensitivity threshold selected in training hospitals only; if the threshold cannot be transported without extrapolation, report threshold failure;
- difference between bounded model and transparent baseline.

The primary transport summary is the hospital-weighted mean `Delta_int`, its distribution across hospitals, and the hospital-weighted paired difference in Brier/calibration error between models. Use hospital bootstrap intervals by resampling hospitals, not rows; report fold ranges and a `uniquepid`-clustered sensitivity. Do not present a single pooled confidence interval as if hospitals were independent patients.

Ascertainment weighting is a sensitivity, not a rescue. Estimate the probability of a known primary outcome using only pre-discharge variables, including admission context, final-six-hour coverage, instability, hospital stratum, unit type, ICU duration and recorded support/measurement density. Fit the observation/unknown model inside training hospitals. Report positivity, effective sample size, truncation and sensitivity to truncation. Large unknown fractions, nonpositive weights or materially divergent weighted/unweighted conclusions are inconclusive.

- all encountered discharge status/location values, clock parse failures, offset inconsistencies and same-time discharge records;

- vital coverage by component, bin coverage, invalid-value exclusions and threshold-component counts;
- respiratory/treatment label and offset inventories before any support flag is used;
- held-out overlap, event support, threshold positivity and missingness/weight diagnostics.

The primary interaction is interpretable only if at least five hospitals contribute eligible stays, at least five hospitals have at least 10 primary events, and no exposure-by-instability cell is empty in a hospital included in the interaction summary. A cell count below 20, an extreme hospital concentration, or a high-sensitivity threshold unsupported in held-out hospitals requires hospital-specific results to be labeled unstable and the global interaction to be inconclusive. If fewer than five hospitals pass, the computation can still report descriptive pooled estimates but cannot claim hospital transportability.

The study is also inconclusive if essential columns are absent, exact status or clock semantics remain unresolved, more than one prespecified ascertainment state dominates without a plausible sensitivity, instability coverage is too sparse, outcome-unknownness is differential and not weightable, or the bounded model cannot be evaluated under the same temporal rules. No gate may be repaired by turning unknown into stable/no-death, changing the night window after seeing results, using post-discharge fields, or substituting Apache aggregates.

## Falsification and interpretation rules

Computational patterns that falsify or weaken the concentration hypothesis include:

- the interaction estimate is near zero, reverses sign, or its interval includes no interaction across the main and held-out analyses;
- nighttime association is as large or larger among low-instability stays than high-instability stays;
- the result is present only in a random patient split, one hospital, one unit type, one discharge location or one support label;
- the interaction disappears after adding basic pre-discharge severity and measurement-coverage controls, or changes materially under the ascertainment-weighted analysis;
- the interaction depends on including observations at `D`, post-discharge data, hospital ID leakage, an unreviewed status synonym, or a single physiologic component;
- the same interaction appears after permuting night labels within hospital, permuting instability labels within stay, or assigning a placebo index at `D-360` while preserving the same outcome boundary;
- an earlier pre-discharge window `[D-720,D-360)` produces the same or stronger effect, which weakens the claim that the association is concentrated in residual immediate instability;
- changing the prespecified night window, instability threshold, or support-label handling causes an unreported sign change;
- the transparent model and bounded model disagree qualitatively and the disagreement cannot be explained by calibration or sparse cells;
- excluding stays with recorded EOL/support indicators changes the result in a way consistent with unmeasured goals-of-care selection;
- the observed interaction is not reflected in computed held-out risks, calibration, or hospital-specific contrasts.

A supportive result requires a positive `Delta_int` in the primary transparent model, broadly concordant held-out hospital estimates, uncertainty excluding no interaction under the prespecified hospital bootstrap, no single-hospital dominance, and qualitatively similar results from the bounded model and timing/threshold sensitivities. It would support the narrow observational statement that the recorded nighttime-discharge association with recorded 48-hour hospital death is concentrated among stays with the defined recorded residual instability. It would justify clinical review of discharge-transition surveillance and a prospective validation study.

An adverse result is a null or negative interaction, instability-stratified association that does not reproduce across held-out hospitals, or a pattern that disappears under ascertainment and leakage controls. It rejects or weakens this data-defined concentration hypothesis; it does not prove nighttime discharge is safe, that instability is irrelevant, or that no discharge policy could help.

An inconclusive result follows from sparse events, empty cells, large outcome-unknownness, unstable observation weights, poor hospital overlap, unresolved clock/status semantics, or model/endpoint instability. It should lead to better data acquisition or another study, not a more permissive threshold.

Computationally checkable claims include source hashes/headers, joins, filters, time windows, clock parsing, instability formulas, outcome state assignment, feature leakage checks, model construction, held-out predictions, calibration, uncertainty and falsification permutations. Clinical review is required for whether a patient was truly unstable, whether discharge was appropriate, whether the recorded death was clinically attributable to the transition, and whether a surveillance or delayed-discharge policy would improve outcomes.

## Evidence and unavailable clinical mechanisms

The data cannot establish:

- bed capacity, staffing levels, handoff quality, nighttime monitoring intensity or discharge intent;
- clinician assessment of readiness, goals of care, treatment limitations or family preference;
- whether a recorded vital abnormality was artifact, transient and clinically accepted, or biologically meaningful;
- actual delivered oxygen, medication administration, support liberation, post-ICU nursing response or ward-level monitoring;
- deaths after hospital discharge, complete post-acute mortality, or outcomes for patients transferred outside ascertainment;
- a causal effect of changing discharge time, a safe discharge rule, hospital quality, or operational policy benefit.

A stronger causal claim requires measured discharge decision factors and quasi-experimental or prospective variation in discharge timing. A clinical operational recommendation requires expert adjudication of readiness and instability, staffing/bed/monitoring data, prospective external validation, and an impact evaluation with harms, delayed-discharge costs and alert burden.
