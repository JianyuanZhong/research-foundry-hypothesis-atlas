# Asynchronous organ recovery after circulatory stabilization: a severity-matched MIMIC-IV study

Status: active draft, not yet a registered hypothesis. Parent: [prior hypothesis].

## Question and clinical importance

Among adults in the ICU whose vasopressor support is stopped or substantially reduced, does delayed recovery of renal or respiratory proxies identify a higher risk of subsequent ICU deterioration or death than recovery of those proxies, after accounting for severity and treatment intensity at the stabilization decision point?

This is clinically consequential because a single SOFA assessment or a binary improved/not-improved label can miss delayed organ failure. A patient may appear hemodynamically stabilized while creatinine, urine output, or oxygenation remains abnormal. If delayed recovery is prognostic beyond current severity and treatment, clinicians would have a concrete reason to monitor the recovering organ more intensively after circulatory support is withdrawn, rather than treating vasopressor-free status as full recovery.

## Evidence and unresolved claim

The strongest claim supported by the available evidence is that longitudinal laboratory and support patterns can distinguish ICU patients with different prognoses. The expert seed proposes that recovery after circulatory stabilization is asynchronous across organs and that delayed or incomplete recovery predicts later deterioration. The unresolved claim is whether delayed renal or respiratory proxy recovery is an independent prognostic signal or is explained by current severity, treatment intensity, or selective measurement.

The leading claim is that delayed recovery reflects a genuine recovery process with prognostic value. The strongest rival is that delayed recovery is a marker of current severity, treatment selection, or observation intensity rather than an independent recovery process. The study is clinically consequential only if the phenotype predicts subsequent outcomes beyond standard severity and time-varying perfusion measurements.

The inspected literature supports the clinical importance of this question. Precision septic shock care requires integration of bedside phenotyping with repeated reassessment and de-escalation [K1]. Dynamic trajectory patterns of serum sodium and urine output in the first 96 hours after ICU admission are associated with 30-day mortality in sepsis patients, with persistent oliguria representing a high-risk prognostic phenotype [K2]. Early hemodynamic responsiveness to dobutamine identifies a subgroup with a more favorable shock trajectory [K3]. However, none of these studies directly tests whether delayed renal or respiratory proxy recovery after circulatory stabilization is an independent prognostic signal beyond current severity and treatment intensity.

## Population and temporal design

- Source: MIMIC-IV v3.1, `baidu_downloads/eicu_mimic/mimic数据库/mimic-iv-3.1.zip`.
- Population: adults (age >= 18) with an ICU stay in `icu/icustays` and a recorded vasopressor discontinuation or substantial reduction during the first 72 hours of the ICU stay.
- Stabilization decision point: the first time at which vasopressor dose is zero or reduced by at least 50% from its peak, with no restart for at least 6 hours.
- Recovery window: 24 hours after the stabilization decision point.
- Outcome window: 7 days after the stabilization decision point.

## Variables and data bindings

- Vasopressor administration: `icu/inputevents` joined to `icu/d_items` on `itemid`; filter for norepinephrine, epinephrine, vasopressin, and phenylephrine. Use `rate` and `starttime`/`endtime` to define peak dose and discontinuation.
- Creatinine: `hosp/labevents` joined to `hosp/d_labitems` on `itemid`; filter for creatinine (`component='Creatinine'`). Use `valuenum` and `charttime`.
- Urine output: `icu/outputevents` joined to `icu/d_items` on `itemid`; filter for urine output. Use `value` and `charttime`.
- Oxygenation: `icu/chartevents` joined to `icu/d_items` on `itemid`; filter for FiO2 and P/F ratio. Use `valuenum` and `charttime`.
- Severity: `icu/icustays` `stay_id`, `first_omr` for SOFA; `hosp/admissions` `admission_time`, `discharge_time`; `icu/chartevents` for GCS, MAP, and heart rate.
- Treatment intensity: `icu/inputevents` for vasopressors and fluids; `icu/procedureevents` for mechanical ventilation; `icu/ingredientevents` for fluid balance.
- Outcomes: `icu/icustays` `discharge_to_deceased_in_hospital` and `hosp/admissions` `discharge_to_deceased_in_hospital`; ICU deterioration defined as restart of vasopressors, initiation of mechanical ventilation, or transfer to a higher level of care.

## Recovery phenotype

- Renal recovery: creatinine at 24 hours after stabilization is within 1.5 times the admission creatinine AND urine output at 24 hours is >= 0.5 mL/kg/hr.
- Respiratory recovery: P/F ratio at 24 hours after stabilization is >= 200 AND FiO2 at 24 hours is <= 0.4.
- Discordant recovery: renal or respiratory recovery is absent while the other organ has recovered.

## Analysis

1. Define the stabilization decision point and recovery phenotype as above.
2. Compare discordant recovery with concordant recovery using a prespecified logistic regression model with adjustment for age, sex, SOFA at admission, vasopressor peak dose, fluid balance, and mechanical ventilation status at the stabilization decision point.
3. Use a temporal split: train on the first 70% of ICU stays by `icu/icustays` `stay_id` and validate on the remaining 30%.
4. Report the adjusted odds ratio for 7-day ICU deterioration or death, with 95% confidence intervals.
5. Perform a sensitivity analysis excluding patients with missing creatinine or P/F ratio measurements at 24 hours.

## Falsification criteria

- If the adjusted odds ratio for discordant recovery is not significantly different from 1.0 (95% CI includes 1.0), the hypothesis is not supported.
- If the association is explained by baseline SOFA or vasopressor peak dose (i.e., the odds ratio becomes non-significant after adjustment), the hypothesis is not supported.
- If the association is driven by a small number of patients with extreme values, the hypothesis is not supported.

## Method comparison

- Baseline: prespecified logistic regression with adjustment for severity and treatment intensity.
- Learned alternative: a gradient-boosted model with the same inputs and a prespecified temporal split. The learned model can reveal whether nonlinear interactions between severity and recovery phenotype improve prediction beyond the linear baseline.
- Selection: if the learned model improves validation AUC by at least 0.01 over the baseline, report the improvement and the feature importance of the recovery phenotype. If not, report the baseline as the primary analysis.

## Limitations

- Organ recovery is inferred from laboratory and support measures rather than direct organ function.
- The study is prognostic, not causal.
- The results are limited to the MIMIC-IV population and may not generalize to other ICU populations.
