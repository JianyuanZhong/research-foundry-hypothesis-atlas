## Unresolved question and clinical importance

ICU patients are monitored by repeated lab draws and clinicians decide how often to draw.
[K1] showed, across 272 lab tests in 669,452 patients, that the *presence and timing* of a
test order \u2014 not only the value \u2014 carries survival signal (order presence significant in
233/272; order timing beat the value in 118/174). So monitoring intensity encodes a care
process, not only patient state. The consequential, unresolved question is whether a *sudden
drop* in a patient's own draw intensity while they remain physiologically unstable is a
patient-level **leading indicator** of imminent deterioration, or merely a fingerprint of
local surveillance practice / clinician-judged improvement that will not transport. This
changes a real modeling/deployment decision: whether monitoring-intensity features may be
used in cross-hospital deterioration alerts without target-site recalibration. [K3] warns
that internal performance is a poor guide to usefulness elsewhere; [K2] documents that eICU
is a 208-hospital telehealth sample, so between-hospital practice variation is real and
inspectable.

## Strongest claim the evidence already supports vs the untested claim

- **Supported now:** order presence/timing carries survival signal ([K1]); ICU models often
  degrade under hospital transport ([K3]); eICU provides a 208-hospital sample with
  `patientUnitStayId`/`hospitalId` keys ([K2]).
- **Unresolved claim this experiment tests (H1):** that a **within-person** fall in the
  patient's own draw rate between (0,12]h and (12,24]h, while the patient is still
  physiologically unstable at the hour-24 landmark, predicts (24,48]h unit deterioration
  *within hospital* (not explained by between-hospital practice), and is **absent** in
  physiologically stable patients (negative-control prediction).

## Leading claim and strongest rival

- **H1 (patient-level leading indicator).** Within a hospital, a larger fall in a patient's
  own draw rate has a monotone dose-response with higher (24,48]h deterioration; the effect
  is concentrated in unstable patients and near-null in stable patients; the within-hospital
  fixed effect is stable and between-hospital random-slope variance is modest; the effect
  survives leave-one-hospital-out (LOHO) transport.
- **R1 (severity/improvement confounding).** The fall marks clinician-judged improvement;
  conditioning on hour-24 instability + APACHE severity + instability trajectory attenuates
  the association to the null.
- **R2 (hospital practice / severity fingerprint) — primary rival.** The association is
  largely explained by hospital identity / surveillance intensity; between-hospital variance
  dominates, the within-hospital effect is near null, and the drop predicts *equally* in
  stable and unstable patients (it tracks practice/prognosis, not measured patient state).
- **R3 (measurement / reverse-causation artifact).** Draws fall because a deterioration or an
  end-of-life (EOL) decision already started in the exposure window; the deficit is
  coincident/after, not before, the outcome.

H1 and R2 differ on a specific, observable comparison: the **stable-stratum effect** and the
**within- vs between-hospital decomposition**.

## Primary estimand (within-person, person-centered draw-rate ratio)

For eligible stay *i* let `d1_i` = panel lab draws with `labresultoffset` in (0,720] min and
`d2_i` = draws in (720,1440] min. Define the **person-centered draw-rate ratio**

    R_i = (d2_i + 0.5) / (d1_i + 0.5),   log R_i = log(d2_i+0.5) - log(d1_i+0.5).

Both numerator and denominator are the *same patient's* counts, so any hospital-level
ordering intensity that scales both windows multiplicatively (practice level) cancels in
log R; the estimand is a within-person change, not a between-patient level. Half-count
smoothing (+0.5) is fixed a priori to keep zero-draw windows finite.

**Primary model** (multilevel logistic, random intercept and random slope on log R):

    logit P(Y_i=1) = \u03b2\u2080 + \u03b2\u2081 logR_i + \u03b2\u2082 center(log d1_i) + \u03b2\u2083 MEWS24_i
                   + \u03b2\u2084 APACHE_i + u_{h(i)} + v_{h(i)}\u00b7logR_i

with `u_h ~ N(0,\u03c4_int\u00b2)`, `v_h ~ N(0,\u03c4_slope\u00b2)`. **H1 predicts \u03b2\u2081 < 0** (a lower R,
i.e. a bigger fall, predicts higher risk), i.e. OR per halving of R (= 0.693-unit decrease in
log R) > 1. Report the within-hospital fixed effect, `\u03c4_slope\u00b2`, and the hospital-level ICC
of log R.

## Pre-specified negative-control falsification (H1 vs R2)

Define instability tertile from a MEWS-like score in `vitalPeriodic.csv.gz` at the hour-24
landmark (heartrate, respiration, systemicsystolic, temperature, sao2). Let `S_i` = 1 for the
**lowest (stable) instability tertile**. Add the interaction `logR_i \u00d7 S_i` and estimate the
stratum-specific ORs.

- **H1 prediction:** the unstable-stratum OR (per halving of R) > 1 with CI excluding 1 and
  |log OR| \u2265 0.30; the **stable-stratum** OR falls in a pre-specified null zone
  [0.80, 1.25] (a same-magnitude drop carries ~null risk when there is little impending
  deterioration for monitoring to lead); interaction \u03b2_{R\u00d7S} > 0 (attenuation).
- **R2 / practice-artifact prediction:** the stable-stratum OR also excludes 1 at a similar
  magnitude, and the interaction CI covers 0 (`\u03b2_{R\u00d7S} \u2248 0`).
- **Test that separates them:** Wald test of H0: \u03b2_{R\u00d7S}=0 plus the stable-stratum OR
  against the null zone. H1 survives only if both the attenuation interaction and the
  stable-stratum null hold; otherwise R2 is favored.

## Shape / reverse-causation test (R3)

Split (12,24]h into 3-hour sub-bins and estimate the within-patient draw deficit shape.

- **Leading-indicator shape (H1):** risk rises with drop magnitude and the deficit appears
  *before* the outcome and is not confined to the final sub-bin; risk increases monotonically
  across R quintiles.
- **Reverse-causation shape (R3):** the deficit is concentrated in the final sub-bin (monitoring
  stops just before/at the event) or is explained by EOL care-plan activation in the window.
- **Pre-specified tests:** (a) exclude stays with `carePlanEOL.cpleoldiscussionoffset` (or
  `cpleolsaveoffset`) in (0,1440] min and re-estimate \u03b2\u2081; (b) compare the (12,18]h vs
  (18,24]h deficit; (c) condition on the hour-24 instability slope sign. R3 is favored if
  \u03b2\u2081 is carried by the last sub-bin or disappears after EOL exclusion.

## Explicit method comparison: simple baseline vs substantive alternative

- **SIMPLE baseline.** Single-level fixed-effect logistic regression on *measured hour-24
  state only*: MEWS-like score and its components; labs `creatinine, BUN, WBC x 1000, Hgb,
  platelets x 1000, sodium, potassium, chloride, bicarbonate, glucose, calcium, magnesium,
  anion gap` (nearest value at/before the landmark); plus APACHE IVa
  (`apachePatientResult`: `acutephysiologyscore`, `apachescore`, `predictedicumortality`).
  No random effects; no use of draw *timing*.
- **SUBSTANTIVE alternative.** (i) The multilevel model above (hospital random intercept + **random
  slope on log R**); optionally (ii) a **counting/point-process** model of draw times
  (inhomogeneous Poisson: intensity \u03bb_i(t) = exp(\u03b1 + \u03b3\u00b7(t>720) + \u03b4\u00b7unstable_i + \u03b4\u00b2\u00b7unstable_i\u00b7(t>720)))
  fitted to the observed panel-draw event times in (0,1440] min to separate a true intensity
  shift from censoring/discharge.
- **What the alternative reveals that the baseline loses:** the baseline *conflates*
  between-hospital practice with the patient signal (it cannot estimate `\u03c4_slope\u00b2`, the
  heterogeneity of the drop effect across hospitals), cannot shrink/transport a hospital-specific
  drop effect for a new site, and cannot separate a genuine intensity change from a change in
  observation censoring. The person-centered ratio plus hospital random slope is exactly the
  structure that decides H1 vs R2.
- **Comparison metric (not a small AUC gain):** report difference in discrimination (\u0394AUROC
  with bootstrap CI), **calibration slope**, **O:E ratio**, **Brier score**, and **net benefit**
  across thresholds, plus internal (test-split) and LOHO transport. The decision-relevant
  claim is the within-hospital decomposition and transport, not a marginal AUC change.

## Pre-specified falsification criteria

- **Supportive (H1):** within-hospital adjusted OR per halving of R > 1 with CI excluding 1;
  monotone dose-response across R quintiles (Q5 vs Q1); stable-stratum OR in [0.80,1.25] and
  interaction \u03b2_{R\u00d7S} > 0 with CI excluding 0; `\u03c4_slope\u00b2` small relative to the fixed
  effect; LOHO \u0394AUROC of the substantive alternative over baseline \u2265 0.02 with CI excluding 0
  and no material calibration/ net-benefit loss.
- **Adverse (R1):** \u03b2\u2081 attenuates to the null (CI includes 1) after conditioning on hour-24
  instability + APACHE + trajectory.
- **Adverse (R2):** within-hospital effect near null; between-hospital variance dominates
  (high ICC of log R); LOHO transport degrades to baseline; stable-stratum OR also excludes 1
  with interaction CI covering 0.
- **Adverse (R3):** effect carried by the last sub-bin or removed by EOL exclusion.
- **Inconclusive:** wide CIs / too few events in (24,48]h; large random-slope heterogeneity
  without a stable within-hospital effect; probe shows insufficient within-person variation.

## What outcomes would mean

- **Supportive:** would indicate a transportable, within-person monitoring-de-escalation
  signal (still association/transport, not mechanism or a treatment target) and support using
  monitoring-intensity features cross-hospital.
- **Adverse (R2/R1/R3):** would indicate that monitoring-intensity features should not be
  deployed cross-hospital without recalibration, and that apparent de-escalation signals are
  practice/severity/reverse-causation artifacts \u2014 a directly actionable negative result.
- **Inconclusive:** would defer the deployment decision and specify the missing evidence.
  Stronger causal/mechanistic conclusions would require order-set, staffing, and bed-capacity
  data absent from eICU and/or a prospective study, i.e. another study, not this dataset.

## Honest limitations

- Association and transport only; **no causal/mechanistic claim**. eICU lacks order-set,
  staffing, and bed-capacity data, so the mechanism behind any de-escalation signal cannot be
  identified here.
- Vasopressor `drugname` strings for the composite escalation outcome are **not bound** at
  discovery; the primary outcome is decisive unit death, with escalation as a secondary
  outcome requiring run-time binding of `infusionDrug.drugname` / `treatment.treatmentstring`.
- Threshold sensitivity (tertile/quintile cut points, null-zone bounds, sub-bin width) is
  pre-specified but untested at discovery and must be reported with sensitivity analyses.
- No reference/solver execution at discovery: this is a readiness design, not a solved result.
- eICU is de-identified; `carePlanEOL` and unit disposition are proxies for EOL/withdrawal.
