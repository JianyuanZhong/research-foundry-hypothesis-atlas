# D3-M1: Composition versus Within-Cell Regulation of Inflammatory Signaling in RA Synovial Tissue

## Scientific Opening

In rheumatoid arthritis (RA), synovial tissue exhibits elevated expression of inflammatory genes (IL6, TNF, IL1B) that drive joint destruction. However, the origin of this signal is ambiguous: it could reflect (a) an increase in the proportion of inflammatory cell types (composition-driven), (b) a change in the transcriptional state of resident cells (within-cell regulation), or (c) both. This distinction is clinically consequential: if the signal is composition-driven, depleting the infiltrating cell type may suffice; if within-cell regulation drives it, the resident cell type itself must be reprogrammed.

The D3-M1 expert seed asks precisely this: "Is a signal better explained by measured cell abundance, within-cell state, or both?" We address this using three complementary datasets that provide single-cell resolution of FLS and macrophages in RA synovial tissue.

## Hypothesis

**Hypothesis**: In RA synovial tissue, the upregulation of inflammatory signaling genes (IL6, TNF, IL1B) in fibroblast-like synoviocytes (FLS) is driven primarily by within-cell transcriptional reprogramming rather than by shifts in macrophage composition.

**Formal statement**: After adjusting for macrophage proportion, the expression of IL6, TNF, and IL1B in FLS remains significantly elevated in RA compared to healthy controls, indicating that within-cell regulation is the dominant driver.

## Competing Explanations

| Explanation | Prediction | Clinical Implication |
|---|---|---|
| **H1: Composition-driven** | FLS expression unchanged; macrophage proportion increases | Target macrophage infiltration/depletion |
| **H2: Within-cell regulation** | FLS expression changes; macrophage proportion stable or secondary | Reprogram FLS transcriptional state |
| **H3: Both** | Both FLS expression and macrophage proportion change | Combined strategy needed |

The leading explanation (H2) is supported by prior work showing FLS acquire a pathogenic, inflammatory phenotype in RA. The strongest rival (H1) is supported by the well-documented increase in synovial macrophage infiltration in RA. The composition-controlled reanalysis by Amato et al. [K3] demonstrates that composition adjustment can remove up to one-third of naive differential expression, highlighting the importance of this distinction.

## Population and Temporal Boundaries

- **Population**: Adult human synovial tissue from RA patients and healthy controls
- **Temporal boundaries**: Cross-sectional (no longitudinal component); tissue sampled at a single time point per donor
- **Disease states** (E-MTAB-8322): healthy synovium (HC0547), undifferentiated arthritis (UPA), treatment-naive RA, treatment-resistant RA, disease remission
- **Coculture conditions** (E-MTAB-8873): FLS-only, FLS+MerTK/CD206pos macrophages (Mpos), FLS+MerTK/CD206neg macrophages (Mneg), with optional LPS stimulation or MerTK inhibitor

## Molecular Signal of Interest

**Primary signal**: Expression of the inflammatory gene panel {IL6, TNF, IL1B} in FLS

**Secondary signals**:
- Matrix degradation genes {MMP1, MMP3, MMP13} in FLS
- Macrophage activation markers {CD68, CD14, FCGR3A, TREM2} in macrophages
- FLS identity markers {COL1A1, COL1A2, THY1, FAP}

## Falsifiable Test

### Primary Test: Within-Cell Expression in FLS (E-MTAB-8873)

1. **Load** all RSEC MolsPerCell matrices from E-MTAB-8873
2. **Identify** FLS cells (all cells in FLS-only and FLS+macrophage conditions are FLS)
3. **Compute** mean expression of {IL6, TNF, IL1B} per cell in each condition
4. **Compare** FLS_only vs. FLS+Mpos (same donor) using a donor-aware paired test
5. **Adjust** for macrophage proportion (measured from the mixed conditions)
6. **Decision**:
   - If FLS expression significantly increases after macrophage addition → **supports H2** (within-cell regulation)
   - If FLS expression unchanged but macrophage proportion increases → **supports H1** (composition-driven)
   - If both change → **supports H3** (both)
   - If neither changes → **inconclusive** (no detectable effect)

### Secondary Test: Macrophage Population Composition (E-MTAB-8322)

1. **Load** all matrix.mtx files from E-MTAB-8322
2. **Cluster** cells into macrophage subpopulations (e.g., inflammatory vs. homeostatic)
3. **Compute** cell-type proportions per sample
4. **Compare** proportions across disease states (healthy, UPA, naive RA, resistant RA, remission)
5. **Decision**:
   - If proportions shift significantly between states → **supports H1** (composition-driven)
   - If proportions stable but within-cell expression changes → **supports H2** (within-cell regulation)

### Tertiary Test: Bulk Signal Decomposition (E-MTAB-8316)

1. **Load** bulk read counts from E-MTAB-8316
2. **Deconvolute** using cellGeometry [K1] or BayesPrism-DWLS [K2] with E-MTAB-8873 as reference
3. **Decompose** the bulk inflammatory signal into composition and within-cell components
4. **Decision**:
   - If composition explains >70% of the signal → **supports H1**
   - If within-cell explains >70% → **supports H2**
   - If both contribute substantially → **supports H3**

## Method Alternatives

### Baseline: Simple Pseudobulk Comparison

**Approach**:
1. Aggregate scRNA-seq data by sample and condition (pseudobulk)
2. Compute mean expression of inflammatory genes per pseudobulk sample
3. Compare conditions using t-test or Wilcoxon test
4. Report fold-change and p-value

**Limitation**: Does not separate composition from within-cell effects. A change in pseudobulk expression could be driven by either mechanism.

**Resource**: CPU, ~10 minutes for data loading and comparison.

### Alternative: Donor-Aware Mixed Model with Composition Adjustment

**Approach**:
1. Fit a linear mixed model: `expression ~ condition + macrophage_proportion + (1 | donor)`
2. Donor is a random effect to account for repeated measures
3. Macrophage proportion is a fixed covariate to adjust for composition
4. Test the condition effect after adjustment

**Advantage**: Separates composition-driven from within-cell effects. The condition coefficient represents the within-cell effect after accounting for composition.

**Resource**: CPU, ~1-2 hours for model fitting (lme4 or statsmodels in Python/R).

**Selection rationale**: The mixed model is the scientifically substantive alternative because it explicitly tests the hypothesis (within-cell regulation after composition adjustment) rather than just describing the data. The pseudobulk comparison is the simple baseline that establishes whether a signal exists at all.

## Expected Outcomes and Interpretation

| Result | Interpretation | Next Step |
|---|---|---|
| FLS expression increases after macrophage addition, persists after composition adjustment | **Supportive of H2**: Within-cell regulation is the dominant driver | Investigate FLS transcriptional reprogramming mechanisms |
| FLS expression unchanged, macrophage proportion increases | **Supportive of H1**: Composition-driven signal | Investigate macrophage infiltration mechanisms |
| Both FLS expression and macrophage proportion change | **Supportive of H3**: Both mechanisms contribute | Design combined intervention strategy |
| No significant change in either | **Inconclusive**: No detectable effect in this system | Consider alternative signals or conditions |
| FLS expression decreases after macrophage addition | **Adverse to H2**: Macrophages suppress FLS inflammation | Investigate macrophage-derived suppressive signals |

## Limitations

1. **Cross-sectional design**: No longitudinal data to establish causality
2. **Coculture system**: E-MTAB-8873 is an in vitro coculture, not in vivo tissue; composition may differ from native tissue
3. **Macrophage-only scRNA-seq**: E-MTAB-8322 profiles macrophages only, not FLS; cannot directly measure FLS composition changes
4. **Bulk deconvolution accuracy**: E-MTAB-8316 deconvolution depends on the quality of the scRNA-seq reference
5. **Donor variability**: RA is heterogeneous; results may not generalize across all patients
6. **No spatial information**: None of the datasets provide spatial context for cell-cell interactions
