# eICU care-plan ventilation labels versus an independently defined respiratory patient-state trajectory

## Assigned evolution and substantive advance

This proposal is a targeted child of assessed-valid `[prior hypothesis]` and `[prior hypothesis]`. It preserves their frozen source-bound denominator, exact labels, landmark schedule, and warning that vocabulary audits cannot establish bedside meaning. It does not repeat a lexical audit. The parents establish that the two literal `carePlanGeneral` ventilation labels are sparse, hospital-concentrated, and not safely transportable as a common phenotype; exhaustive expansion to the four exported `Ventilated -` values adds almost no coverage and does not rescue held-out prediction of a charted setting reduction.

The remaining clinically consequential gap is endpoint validity. A label could fail as a transportable vocabulary yet still mark a meaningful patient-state trajectory, or an apparent association could merely indicate that a clinician documented more often when documentation was already dense. The proposed experiment tests whether label receipt at a fixed decision time is associated with a subsequent, persistent, multi-domain respiratory state trajectory assembled from sources independent of the care-plan label. It explicitly separates patient-state evidence from the opportunity to document it.

The strongest evidence already supported is limited to exported structured-documentation use and proxy associations. The untested claim is: conditional on the locked cohort and availability gates, the label at D adds information about a later, independently constructed respiratory state trajectory beyond the pre-D patient state and documentation opportunity, with a mapping stable enough to be clinically interpretable. The experiment cannot decide whether a label denotes an actual daily extubation evaluation, SBT, readiness, appropriate care, benefit, or harm.

## Hypothesis and estimand

For each person-landmark, let D be the first qualifying exact-label row in `[L,L+360]`, where `L` is 1,440, 2,880, 4,320, 5,760, or 7,200 minutes after ICU admission. Compare the two literal strategies:

* `evaluation`: `Ventilated - with daily extubation evaluation`
* `no_trial`: `Ventilated - with no daily extubation trial`

The primary estimand is the descriptive, hospital-held-out difference in risk and predictive information for a **persistent respiratory-state improvement** during `(D+360,D+1800]`, conditional on a target-independent pre-D state and explicit source-opportunity covariates. This is not a causal treatment effect. Repeated landmarks remain clustered within `patientunitstayid`/`sid`; the first qualifying stay per `uniquepid` and the parent exclusions are retained.

The falsifiable positive hypothesis is that evaluation-label receipt predicts a higher probability of the trajectory, and improves held-out log loss and calibration over the same model without label features, in at least the unbounded and two airway-recency variants, without a comparable pre-D placebo association or documentation-opportunity-only association. The null/adverse alternative is that the contrast disappears after opportunity adjustment, is present before D, is driven by one chart component or one hospital, or fails independent corroboration.

## Independent patient-state endpoint

The endpoint is constructed without `carePlanGeneral` values and without generic care-plan counts. Numeric respiratory-chart values are parsed only when numeric and physiologically admissible under fixed field-specific bounds. A component is available only when it has at least two observations separated by at least 30 minutes in the relevant pre or post period; a single copied value cannot create a state transition.

### Primary composite: persistent multi-domain state improvement

A row is an event only if both of the following domains are present in the post window and the qualifying change persists at the post-window median (using the two separated observations and any additional valid observations):

1. **Support-intensity domain.** At least one non-peak ventilator support component falls from the pre-D median to the post-D median by a fixed threshold: FiO2 decreases by at least 0.05, PEEP/PEEP-CPAP by at least 2 cm H2O, pressure support by at least 2 cm H2O, or set respiratory rate by at least 2 breaths/min. Peak inspiratory pressure is excluded from the primary endpoint because the parent showed that it dominates the earlier composite and its bedside semantics are uncertain.
2. **Physiology/safety domain.** The post-window median has no clinically adverse deterioration in either available safety signal: `sao2` does not fall by more than 2 percentage points from the pre-D median and `respiration` does not rise by more than 4 breaths/min. If neither safety signal is available in both periods, the row is endpoint-missing, not a non-event.

The event also requires corroboration by an airway/ventilation state record in `respiratoryCare` during the post window: a row with a nonmissing `airwaytype` and/or a ventilator interval (`ventstartoffset`, `ventendoffset`, `priorventstartoffset`, `priorventendoffset`). This is an availability/corroboration requirement, not proof that a setting was delivered at the bedside. A setting-only event without this corroboration is a prespecified secondary endpoint.

For each component, retain direction, magnitude, observation count, first and last post times, and missingness. Report component-specific events and the intersection composite so a positive result cannot be attributed to one noisy field. No unit conversion is inferred beyond the published eICU convention; field-specific units and chart-interface semantics remain limitations.

### Independent secondary endpoints

* **Persistent support reduction without the physiology requirement**, using the same two-observation and post-median rules.
* **Safety-preserving oxygenation trajectory**, defined separately as no adverse `sao2`/respiration change plus a decrease in FiO2 or PEEP, to avoid hiding an oxygenation deterioration inside the composite.
* **Airway-transition proxy**, requiring a time-ordered respiratoryCare airway change from an invasive airway to `No Artificial Airway` if that literal value occurs, or a treatment row whose normalized `treatmentstring` is an exact pre-frozen ETT-removal candidate followed by no invasive-airway respiratoryCare record for 48 hours. This is a validation sensitivity only because the parent endpoint audit found sparse markers and incomplete observation; it must not be used to force a positive result.
* **No-event and missingness outcomes**, including post-window observation density and competing discharge/truncation, to expose informative ascertainment.

The airway-transition sensitivity is not interchangeable with extubation or liberation. If its support fails the predeclared minimum, report it as unavailable/inconclusive and do not estimate an association from a tiny subset.

## Documentation-opportunity separation

Construct opportunity variables using only rows in the same pre-D and post-D windows, never care-plan values. For each stay-window report counts, distinct labels, number of unique measurement times, median inter-record gap, and whether each source family is present:

* respiratoryCharting rows and distinct `respchartvaluelabel` values, separated into the five component families and peak-pressure labels;
* respiratoryCare rows and distinct `airwaytype` values;
* vitalPeriodic and vitalAperiodic observations;
* treatment rows and distinct treatment strings;
* `respchartentryoffset-respchartoffset` lag summaries.

The primary patient-state model includes pre-D measurement availability and density as covariates and has a complete-window analysis requiring the same minimum opportunity in each label arm within a held-out hospital. A companion opportunity-only model uses counts, distinct labels, timestamps, and source presence but excludes all respiratory values and label semantics. If the opportunity-only model predicts the endpoint as well as the state-plus-label model, that is adverse to the claim that labels add patient-state information. Because conditioning on post-D observation can itself induce selection, report both complete-window and inverse-observation-weighted sensitivity; weights are estimated only from pre-D variables and hospital/landmark, with truncation fixed at the 1st/99th percentiles.

## Falsification and robustness plan

1. **Pre-D placebo trajectory.** Apply exactly the same two-domain, two-observation, median-based endpoint to `(D-1440,D-720]` versus `(D-720,D]`. A label contrast comparable to the post-D contrast is adverse to a newly emerging post-label state interpretation.
2. **Lead-time placebo.** Shift the exposure time to `D-720` while keeping the outcome window fixed relative to D; a similar association suggests pre-existing trajectory or label timing leakage.
3. **Component ablation.** Refit results excluding each one of FiO2, PEEP, pressure support, rate, `sao2`, and respiration in turn; a result dependent on peak pressure, one component, one source family, or one hospital is not supportive.
4. **Documentation-only negative control.** Predict post-window source-row density and entry lag from labels; strong label association with opportunity, without corresponding improvement in the value-based state endpoint, is evidence of documentation opportunity rather than state information.
5. **Permutation control.** Within hospital-by-landmark strata, permute labels across eligible rows while preserving arm counts and all source availability; repeat the complete pipeline with a fixed seed and 1,000 permutations. The observed held-out increment must exceed the 97.5th percentile of the permuted increment. Permutation is a falsification check, not a replacement for clustered uncertainty.
6. **Transport and clustering.** Primary evaluation is leave-one-hospital-out with row-weighted micro and equal-hospital macro log loss, Brier score, AUROC where defined, calibration slope/intercept, and risk differences. Repeated landmarks for one `sid` move together in stay-cluster bootstrap/resampling; no row-level bootstrap is allowed. Report hospital directions and leave-one-hospital-out reversals.
7. **Label-source separation.** Do not use care-plan counts, care-plan group/value strings, or any feature measured after D in the baseline. The state endpoint is assembled from respiratoryCharting, respiratoryCare, and vitalPeriodic; treatment is reserved for the secondary airway check. A sensitivity adding care-plan opportunity must be explicitly marked post-exposure and cannot be the primary adjustment.

## Supportive, adverse, and inconclusive gates

These are validity and falsification gates, not p-value thresholds.

### Supportive

The label adds patient-state information only if all of the following hold in the primary unbounded definition and at least two of the three airway bounds (latest invasive evidence within 24, 12, and 6 hours before L):

* at least five hospitals have at least ten endpoint-observed rows per label, at least 50% of analyzed rows lie in these hospitals, and no single hospital supplies more than 40% of either label arm;
* the evaluation-minus-no-trial risk difference is positive, its hospital-specific directions agree in at least 4/5 estimable hospitals, and the equal-hospital macro held-out log-loss increment over baseline-plus-opportunity is negative;
* the label model improves both micro and macro log loss over the opportunity-only model by at least 0.01 and its 95% stay-cluster interval excludes zero in the favorable direction, without a direction reversal in leave-one-hospital-out estimates;
* the state-plus-label increment remains after the primary component ablations and is not explained by the pre-D placebo, lead-time placebo, or permutation distribution;
* at least 10% of rows in both arms have the independent airway corroboration; otherwise the corroboration gate is inconclusive and the overall result cannot be called fully supportive.

Even a supportive computational result supports only incremental information in this eICU chart construct, not actual SBT, extubation readiness, treatment choice, or benefit.

### Adverse

The patient-state-information claim is adverse if any of these prespecified patterns occurs in the primary analysis and is reproduced in one bounded sensitivity: endpoint support falls below the five-hospital/ten-per-label requirement; the label increment is no better than opportunity-only; the risk difference reverses across hospitals or leave-one-hospital-out folds; the observed increment is no larger than the pre-D placebo or lies within the permutation null; the result disappears after removing one component/source family; or the label predicts post-window documentation density while not improving the value-based trajectory.

An adverse result means the labels are not validated as incremental patient-state features for this endpoint in this snapshot. It does not prove that clinicians did not perform evaluations or that the labels are purely documentation conventions.

### Inconclusive

The result is inconclusive when endpoint availability, independent corroboration, or hospital overlap is too sparse; confidence intervals span both a clinically meaningful increment and no increment; bounded definitions disagree; or missingness/competing exits differ so strongly that weighting cannot distinguish state from ascertainment. Inconclusive is the correct result if the raw data cannot support the prespecified support comparison; no relaxed threshold or outcome-guided relabeling is permitted.

## What can and cannot be concluded

A verifier can check the frozen denominator and exposure, source hashes, exact columns and joins, offsets/windows, numerical parsing bounds, duplicate handling, two-observation separation, medians, component thresholds, opportunity features, missingness, competing exits, hospital-held-out splits, stay-cluster resampling, permutation implementation, and gate logic. It can test whether reported conclusions follow from computed outputs and whether an unsupported positive claim is made when a gate is adverse or inconclusive.

It cannot establish that `cplitemvalue` expresses bedside intent, that respiratoryCharting values were delivered settings rather than copied/interface measurements, that airwaytype proves an invasive device at every minute, that the endpoint equals clinical readiness or liberation, or that the association is causal. The configured eICU release lacks raw waveforms, bedside device logs, complete narrative notes, direct SBT/extubation/reintubation adjudication, local interface dictionaries, and post-ICU outcome follow-up. Those stronger conclusions require blinded clinician adjudication, site workflow/interface metadata, device-linked measurements, and prospective or external multicenter validation.
