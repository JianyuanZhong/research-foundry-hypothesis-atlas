# Does discordant AKI recovery add prognostic information beyond observation intensity?

## Unresolved question and clinical importance

A fall in serum creatinine during critical illness can reflect improved filtration, but reduced creatinine generation during muscle loss or dilution from fluid accumulation can also lower the measured concentration. In a small prospective critical-illness cohort with iohexol comparison, creatinine-based eGFR overestimated measured kidney function as ICU stay lengthened [K1]. In PICARD, fluid overload was associated with mortality and lower recovery, but the authors explicitly could not determine whether fluid overload caused or resulted from more severe renal failure [K2]. A recent Research Square preprint directly studies joint creatinine/urine-output criteria at a 24-hour ICU landmark and subsequent kidney progression in MIMIC-IV and eICU, demonstrating the proximity of landmarked joint-criteria work and the importance of ascertainment, denominator choice, and competing events [K3]. It does not test post-AKI improvement in creatinine paired with persistent oliguria.

**Hypothesis to test:** Among adults with creatinine-defined incident AKI who are alive, still in ICU, and not receiving kidney replacement therapy (KRT) 48 hours after AKI onset, an observed decline in creatinine combined with adequately documented persistent oliguria over the preceding 24 hours is associated with higher subsequent 7-day in-hospital death risk than concordant improvement, after accounting for pre-landmark severity, treatment/fluid proxies, and measurement intensity.

The clinical value is to determine whether apparent biochemical improvement should be interpreted cautiously when the independent urine-output signal remains adverse, and whether that pattern warrants prospective validation. This is a **conditional prognostic association**, not a causal or physiological diagnosis. The 48-hour landmark deliberately defines a survivor/in-ICU population; it does not represent early deaths or patients discharged before the landmark. The novelty claim is limited to this recovery-discordance comparison plus explicit observation-process and landmark-flow checks, not joint AKI criteria generally.

## Strongest rival and distinguishing observations

The strongest rival is that discordance is produced by severity and care/measurement processes: sicker patients receive more frequent creatinine testing and urine charting, diuretics alter urine output, fluid accumulation dilutes creatinine, output events may be missing or interval semantics may be ambiguous, and conditioning on survival and ICU retention selects a nonrepresentative subset. Adjustment cannot remove unmeasured severity or unrecorded output. A robust association should persist in a prespecified adequately observed subset and after measured pre-landmark observation counts/lags and severity are accounted for. Attenuation or reversal under those checks, or concentration of the effect in poorly observed patients, favors the measurement/severity rival. Neither pattern proves a renal mechanism.

**Cohort and exposure.** Identify first incident creatinine-defined AKI during the first adult ICU stay, using only measurements available at each time: serum creatinine rise `>=0.3 mg/dL` within 48 hours or `>=1.5x` the lowest preceding available value within 7 days. This is creatinine-defined AKI, not complete KDIGO AKI ascertainment. Time zero is 48 hours after onset. Eligibility requires alive, still in the index ICU stay, and no prior KRT at that landmark. Report the full flow from detected AKI through deaths, ICU discharge, KRT, insufficient labs, and insufficient urine observation; the primary estimand applies only to eligible landmark survivors.

Within the 24 hours before time zero, define creatinine improvement from the onset value to the lowest observed value by a prespecified threshold (primary: decline `>=0.3 mg/dL`; also report continuous slope/change). Define persistent oliguria only if item mapping and interval/weight eligibility pass the pre-outcome audit: `urine output <0.5 mL/kg/hour` for at least 12 hours in the final 24 hours, using only valid observed intervals and a validated contemporaneous weight. Do not convert unrecorded intervals to zero. The four creatinine-change/urine-output states remain visible; the primary contrast is falling creatinine with persistent oliguria versus falling creatinine without oliguria among adequately observed patients. Poorly observed urine is its own unclassified/coverage state, never grouped with non-oliguria. Freeze all mappings and thresholds before examining outcome associations.

**Primary outcome/estimand.** Seven-day cumulative incidence of in-hospital death after time zero, with live hospital discharge before day 7 as a competing event. Use `admissions.deathtime` and `dischtime` relative to time zero; death after the 7-day horizon is not an event. Report the standardized 7-day risk difference and ratio, with 95% uncertainty intervals, for the discordant versus concordant-improvement groups. New KRT is a prespecified secondary outcome only after clinical code/item validation; report it separately and do not let an unvalidated RRT flag define the primary result. If RRT mapping is validated and event support permits, report a death-or-new-KRT composite as secondary, not as a mechanism claim.

**Adjustment.** Prespecify age, gender, pre-landmark AKI creatinine level/change, baseline chronic kidney disease where reliably derived, available pre-landmark physiologic severity, pre-landmark fluid/medication proxies, and observation counts/latest-measurement lags for creatinine, urine, and key charted vitals. Use only variables temporally available before the landmark; do not adjust for post-landmark mediators. Show both minimally adjusted and expanded estimates and overlap/event counts. Do not interpret the expanded adjusted association as causal.

## Analysis, alternatives, and computational deliverable

The required new scientific deliverable is a frozen cohort/measurement audit plus a fitted, patient-held-out prognostic comparison: counts and exclusions at every flow step; verified item/code mappings and coverage distributions; group-specific 7-day cumulative incidence; adjusted risk contrasts and confidence intervals; observation-process/coverage sensitivity results; and, if there is adequate event/overlap support, baseline-versus-trajectory held-out calibration and proper-score outputs. If measurement gates fail, document the no-go and do not manufacture an effect estimate.

**Simple baseline (primary).** Cause-specific/competing-risk regression with prespecified covariates above, using Fine-Gray or equivalent competing-risk model for 7-day death with live hospital discharge competing. Estimate absolute standardized cumulative incidence and risk difference. Compare an adjusted baseline without the discordance indicator against the same model with that indicator; report calibration, Brier score, and bootstrap confidence intervals in the held-out patient split. This directly tests the conditional association and information beyond current measured state.

**Substantive learned alternative (same cohort, endpoint, split).** Fit a small gated recurrent unit (GRU) competing-risk model on aligned 6-hour pre-landmark bins (up to eight bins for 48 hours): creatinine levels/change, validated urine outputs only when interval-covered, fluid/medication/vital summaries where mapped, and explicit missingness/coverage masks and time-since-last-observation. Train to predict the same 7-day death-versus-live-discharge outcome, not to infer renal physiology. A patient-level split by `subject_id` (70/15/15 train/validation/test; fix seed, no same patient across splits) prevents leakage. Compare held-out calibration, Brier/log score, and risk calibration with the simple model; use patient bootstrap intervals. The GRU can reveal whether sequence order or changing coverage carries information lost by landmark summaries; any gain is predictive/descriptive only, and strong performance driven by observation masks would reinforce the care-process rival. If population/event support is too small for stable sequence fitting, defer this alternative and report the baseline only rather than oversell it.

**Resources (planning estimates, not measured).** Expected fit on CPU with 4 CPUs and 16 GiB memory; about 1-3 hours for cohort construction/fits and up to 4 additional hours for a compact GRU plus bootstrap, pending a bounded size/import probe. No GPU is required by scientific design; request an allocated GPU only if profiling shows the bounded CPU sequence fit cannot meet the configured future solver envelope. The cited demonstration papers are not a method quota: Delphi is a broad disease-sequence transformer and ALADYNOULLI a latent longitudinal model; neither should be transplanted without improving this fixed prognostic contrast. A small GRU is retained only to compare ordered signals/missingness with the transparent summary model.

## Falsification, interpretation, and next decision

- **Supportive:** the pre-specified discordant group has greater 7-day death risk with uncertainty excluding no difference, the estimate persists in adequately observed patients and after measured-severity/observation adjustment, and the result is not dominated by one output mapping or thin overlap. This supports only a conditional prognostic association in MIMIC-IV and motivates prospective validation with protocolized hourly urine, measured kidney function (for example cystatin C/iohexol), and independent outcome review.
- **Adverse to the hypothesis:** a precise estimate shows no clinically material excess risk or the discordant group has lower risk under validated measurement. Attenuation toward null when restricting to adequate coverage or accounting for measurement intensity supports ascertainment/severity as the better explanation. Predefine a clinically meaningful risk-difference margin with nephrology/clinical reviewers before outcome fitting; without that margin, a nonsignificant estimate alone is not falsification.
- **Inconclusive:** intervals include both no effect and clinically important benefit/harm, sparse KRT/outcome counts or poor overlap make estimates unstable, or urine-output mappings/interval coverage cannot support classification. State no biological null conclusion when precision or measurement is inadequate.
- **Abandon/revise:** if the audit cannot validate urine interval meaning or adequate coverage, do not call the exposure oliguria and defer the discordance claim. If RRT mapping is weak, retain mortality as primary and omit RRT from primary/composite interpretation. If only association disappears after measured covariates, do not assert that adjustment proves the rival; revise toward measurement-validation work.

The available data cannot computationally establish true filtration/recovery, complete fluid balance, meaning of absent urine rows, causal effects of fluids/diuretics/oliguria, clinical utility, or transportability. These require clinical/nephrology adjudication, better prospective measurement and/or external replication. Computation can check source bindings, chronology, cohort flow, item/code lists, coverage rules actually applied, split integrity, fitted estimates and uncertainty, and whether stated conclusions match outputs. It cannot adjudicate physiology or treatment decisions.

## Inspected key references

[K1] Haines et al. directly compared creatinine/cystatin C estimates with iohexol in 38 mechanically ventilated patients and found creatinine-based eGFR overestimated measured function after prolonged critical illness; small single-center cohort with cystatin C/iohexol unavailable in MIMIC. Full text retrieved through Ovid HTML and inspected; proposal uses the abstract-level measured finding only.

[K2] Bouchard et al. reported fluid overload associated with mortality and non-recovery at peak creatinine in a multicenter observational AKI cohort, while explicitly leaving causal direction unresolved. Abstract inspected via Europe PMC metadata; the work motivates fluid/severity adjustment, not a causal fluid claim.

[K3] Wang, Lu, and Jiang's Research Square v1 preprint is the closest inspected work: a 24-hour MIMIC-IV/eICU cirrhosis landmark study of number of positive creatinine/urine criteria and 48-hour severe AKI/KRT progression, with death/discharge competing events. It is not peer reviewed; population and contrast differ (criterion count, not creatinine improvement with persistent oliguria). It bounds novelty and motivates careful selection/measurement reporting rather than establishing our hypothesis.
