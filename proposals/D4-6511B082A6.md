## Substantive successor change

The parent’s verified primary question is whether prior values improve pooled probabilistic prediction, especially in its frozen central 60% current-only risk band, and whether that improvement transports from assessments through 31 December 2012 to assessments in calendar year 2013. This child retains that endpoint, population, split, nested contrasts, and parent estimand as an anchor.

The new scientific question is decision-specific and distributional:

> At a fixed 10% review capacity within each current-only-risk stratum of the parent’s ambiguous band, does retaining the prior eight-field panel capture more subsequent target events than current values plus verified repeat spacing, and is that incremental capture concentrated in the highest-risk part of the ambiguous band?

The advance is not a smaller pooled score difference. It tests whether historical information changes the composition of a constrained review list, and whether its value is heterogeneous across people who look similarly ambiguous under the current panel. A positive answer would identify where retaining a historical panel could matter for triage research; a negative answer would argue against spending review capacity or system complexity on historical values for this recorded-risk task. Neither answer establishes that a clinician should adopt the policy.

The parent’s pooled Brier and interaction estimands remain mandatory secondary/continuity outputs. The successor’s new primary estimand is the fixed-capacity event-capture heterogeneity contrast defined below.

## Evidence-supported versus untested claims

The strongest available evidence is the frozen local data and schema evidence: the literal panel cells, assessment-date fields, position-matched health-outcome code/date arrays, death-date fields, and one-to-one `eid` relationships exist in the named source files. The local catalog also documents that field-instance indices are not elapsed time and that complete field-level coding, units, specimen timing, and five-year ascertainment certification are not generally available.

The parent’s endpoint repair establishes a reproducible recorded endpoint rule, conditional on certification of the code domain and date semantics. It does not establish disease onset, outpatient disease, severity, assay validity, or complete follow-up. The unresolved claims tested here are:

1. prior values improve held-out target-event capture at a fixed review capacity beyond current values and spacing;
2. that incremental capture differs across frozen current-risk strata; and
3. the pattern is stable when early fits are transported without refitting to the 2013 assessment cohort.

No fitted result, event count, clinical utility, causal effect, or policy recommendation is asserted in this proposal.

## Selection, split, and frozen risk strata

Create a complete participant flow from the population anchor through age/sex, T0/T1, interval, paired-panel completeness, prior-event exclusion, era, ascertainment, parent risk band, new risk strata, and T/D/N outcome. Report every count, including all malformed outcome positions, target events, deaths, exclusions, and the eight era-by-stratum cells.

Preserve the parent’s selection sensitivity. Among T0-complete participants, define S=1 as valid paired T1 plus complete panel and accepted endpoint eligibility. Fit a regularized selection model in early data using only pre-T1 T0 panel values, age, sex, and T0 calendar year. Apply it unchanged to late T0-complete participants. Use stabilized sensitivity weights w_i=P(S=1)/p-hat(S=1|X_i), truncating at the early 1st and 99th percentiles. Report overlap, maximum weight, standardized differences, and effective sample size (ESS) by era and new stratum. This is a missing-at-random sensitivity analysis, not causal correction.

Use one deterministic participant-level five-fold hash split on `eid` within early data. All imputation, scaling, regularization, hyperparameter selection, calibration, selection modeling, and any cutpoint estimation are training-fold-only. Early out-of-fold predictions are the only basis for frozen risk bands and strata.

First fit M0 as the parent’s current-only model and obtain its held-out three-class probability for T, r_i. Define q20 and q80 from all eligible early out-of-fold M0 T probabilities using one recorded quantile algorithm:

- L: r < q20;
- A: q20 <= r < q80;
- U: r >= q80.

Apply exactly the same q20/q80 and half-open labels to late predictions from early-fitted models. No late outcome, late quantile, M1b prediction, learned prediction, or permuted field may define A.

For the new heterogeneity analysis, estimate q25, q50, and q75 from the early out-of-fold M0 T probabilities among early A participants only. Define four within-A strata Q1 through Q4 using half-open intervals with those frozen cutpoints, with the final interval closed on its upper end. Apply those cutpoints unchanged to late A participants. If any cutpoint is nonfinite, orderedness fails, a stratum is empty, or parent A is nonidentifiable, fail closed. All cutpoints and counts must be recorded before the new-policy outcomes are inspected.

The 10% review capacity is fixed before fitting results are examined. Within each era and Q stratum, a model’s policy selects exactly the lowest-risk-tie-broken 10% by its held-out predicted T probability (or the prespecified nearest integer count); ties are broken by a deterministic hash of `eid`. This is a capacity-normalized research policy applied separately within each frozen stratum, not an approved clinical rule. The all-A top-10% policy is also reported as an operational aggregate, but Q-stratum contrasts are the primary heterogeneity analysis.

Require at least 200 participants and 30 T events in every early/late Q1-Q4 cell, at least 100 T events overall, finite weights, late weighted ESS >= 200 and at least half the unweighted cell ESS in each Q stratum, and adequate early-to-late overlap. If any gate fails, the new heterogeneity claim is inconclusive and strata/capacity may not be redefined.

## Models and substantive alternatives on identical inputs

All models use the same eligible participants, endpoint, fixed split, frozen bands/strata, policy capacity, weights, and uncertainty procedure.

- M0: regularized multinomial current panel plus age at recruitment, sex, and T1 calendar year.
- M1a: M0 plus verified repeat spacing (L).
- M1b: M1a plus all eight literal prior-panel values. The parent’s primary prior-information contrast is M1b versus M1a.
- M1c: M1a plus eight current-minus-prior changes, only as a prespecified representation sensitivity after timing, units, and missingness certification. It cannot replace M1b versus M1a.
- M2: shallow gradient-boosted multinomial model with exactly M1b’s inputs.
- M2a: matched shallow gradient-boosted multinomial model with exactly M1a’s inputs.

M0/M1a/M1b/M1c use fold-local preprocessing and regularized multinomial probabilities. M2/M2a use a small prespecified depth, learning-rate, and regularization grid, fixed tree limit, and training-only early stopping or inner validation. No model uses outcome labels, event dates, late data, or post-T1 information in feature construction.

The transparent baseline answers the parent question and provides the counterfactual ranking policy: M1a’s current-plus-spacing T risk. The substantive learned alternative tests whether nonlinear current/prior discordance or spacing interactions alter a capacity-limited ranking in ways additive M1b averages away. Its scientific value is judged by the same fixed-capacity capture, yield, calibration, fold stability, and early-to-late transport outputs—not by a small predictive improvement alone. M2 cannot rescue a failed endpoint, timing, ascertainment, or transparent M1b-versus-M1a contrast.

## New decision-specific estimands and parent comparison

For model M, era e, and Q stratum h, define selected indicator A_M,i from the held-out T probability and fixed 10% capacity. With fixed participant weights (unweighted primary; selection-weighted sensitivity), define:

C(M,h,e) = sum_i [w_i * 1(Y_i=T) * A_M,i] / sum_i [w_i * 1(Y_i=T)]

as target-event capture among the stratum’s recorded T events, and:

P(M,h,e) = sum_i [w_i * 1(Y_i=T) * A_M,i] / sum_i [w_i * A_M,i]

as target-event yield among review slots. Report corresponding D capture/yield and T/D/N frequencies among reviewed and non-reviewed participants as safety and competing-event descriptions. These are recorded-outcome decision proxies, not clinical utility measures.

The incremental policy value of prior values is:

Delta(h,e) = C(M1b,h,e) - C(M1a,h,e)

with the analogous yield contrast reported. The new primary heterogeneity estimand is:

Gamma(e) = Delta(Q4,e) - Delta(Q1,e)
tau_Gamma = Gamma(late, no-refit) - Gamma(early).

The primary directional hypothesis is Delta(Q4, late) > 0.05 and Gamma(late) > 0.05: at identical within-stratum review capacity, adding the prior panel captures at least five additional percentage points of that stratum’s recorded T events in Q4 than the current-plus-spacing baseline, and the Q4-minus-Q1 difference is at least five percentage points. The 0.05 is a prespecified operational decision margin for event capture, not a validated clinical-benefit threshold. Also report Delta(Q1) through Delta(Q4), all-A capture/yield, and simultaneous uncertainty intervals; the Q4 claim cannot be inferred from a pooled average.

The parent continuity estimand remains mandatory:

d(g,e) = BS(M1b,g,e) - BS(M1a,g,e)
D(e) = d(A,e) - [d(L,e) + d(U,e)]/2
tau_A = D(late, no-refit) - D(early).

Report parent Brier/log-loss/calibration outputs alongside Delta, Gamma, and tau_Gamma. A favorable pooled Brier contrast with no fixed-capacity or heterogeneity signal does not support the new hypothesis. Conversely, a policy contrast does not erase a poorly calibrated pooled model or establish clinical utility.

## Interpretation boundaries

Supportive results require all semantic, code-domain, timing, ascertainment, join, and integrity gates; all Q-cell support gates; calibrated and stable held-out predictions; positive Q4 incremental capture and positive Q4-minus-Q1 heterogeneity beyond the 0.05 operational margin; consistent direction under selection weighting; acceptable parent continuity outputs; and no falsification failure. This supports a conditional, capacity-normalized association between prior-panel retention and capture of the specified recorded T endpoint in the supported UKB analysis population. It suggests where a future triage study might focus.

Adverse results include no Q4 improvement, Gamma at or below zero, capture below the operational margin, benefit explained by spacing rather than prior values, reversal under weighting, poor calibration, fold instability, early-to-late reversal, or failed permutation/random-policy controls. This weakens the case for retaining prior values for this recorded-risk review task; it does not prove no value in another population, outcome, capacity, or clinical setting.

Inconclusive results include missing or failed code-domain/timing/ascertainment certification, malformed/unpaired positions, death-date disagreement, fewer than 100 T events, sparse Q cells, poor overlap/ESS, nonidentifiable cutpoints, wide intervals, conflicting endpoint or missingness sensitivities, or an unstable learned policy. “No qualifying record in the available source” under incomplete follow-up is inconclusive, not a negative clinical result.

Computationally checkable claims are source/schema/header integrity, full-array position handling, joins, flow counts, endpoint construction, event ordering, model fitting, held-out predictions, policy selection, capture/yield arithmetic, calibration, bootstrap intervals, overlap/ESS, and permutation behavior. Clinical adjudication or another study is required for true disease onset, outpatient events, severity, assay/specimen validity, complete ascertainment beyond supplied evidence, causal effects of repeat testing, validated clinical utility, clinical thresholds, external validation, and prospective policy evaluation. No automatic verifier can establish those claims.

## Alternatives considered, deferred, and revisit conditions

A current-only/spacing baseline was selected because it is the parent’s transparent counterfactual and makes the new policy contrast interpretable. Additive prior values (M1b) were selected as the primary information comparison because they preserve literal measurements and isolate prior information from repeat spacing. The boosted M2/M2a pair was selected as a substantive learned alternative because the same inputs can reveal nonlinear current-prior discordance and risk-ranking interactions relevant to fixed-capacity capture. The split is the deterministic participant-level five-fold `eid` split; target is held-out three-class T/D/N prediction and the derived fixed-capacity policy metrics; uncertainty is paired participant bootstrap with a refit sensitivity.

M1c raw change is retained only as a representation sensitivity: it may reveal deterioration-related information but is not the primary estimand because difference interpretation depends on certified units, specimen timing, and comparable assays. Physical/body-composition extensions are deferred despite available-looking assessment fields because local metadata does not certify their labels, units, missing meanings, or clinical relevance. Genomic/latent-GP and disease-sequence models are deferred because the local genomics file is derived phenotype/metadata rather than a raw sequence/variant bundle, and the outcome history lacks a verified event-level date/code dictionary adequate for a mechanistic disease trajectory. A larger neural sequence model is not needed to answer this fixed-capacity question; it would be reconsidered only if verified dated multi-event histories and a distinct clinically meaningful estimand became available. These are evidence-bound deferrals, not blanket exclusions of structured data, neural methods, GPUs, or mechanistic models.

Approximate future solver resources are up to 16 CPUs, 262144 MiB memory, and 28800 seconds, unmeasured for this exact implementation. The work is tabular, with five folds, selection weighting, paired bootstrap, and a shallow boosted comparator; CPU is the default. The discovery budget is 7200 science seconds and is separate from this future solver envelope. The deployment has eight A100-SXM4 80-GB GPUs, but an ordinary shell has no allocation; GPU use is neither required nor rewarded.
