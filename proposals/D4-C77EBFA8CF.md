# UKB-36 repaired: pre-diagnostic host reserve and competing mortality after incident colorectal cancer

## Deliverable and falsifiable claim

The scientific deliverable is a newly fitted, held-out competing-risk prognostic comparison: (1) a prespecified routine-data baseline, (2) the same baseline plus pre-diagnostic reserve, and (3) a learned reserve model. Completion requires a frozen cohort/exclusion table, participant-level split, fitted models, 1/3/5-year cause-specific risk and cumulative-incidence predictions, calibration, IPCW Brier scores, time-dependent AUCs with uncertainty, reserve effect estimates, missingness/selection diagnostics, and the prespecified falsification table. A planned analysis is not an observed result.

Strongest available support is that functional frailty/reserve has been associated with mortality or short-term vulnerability in colorectal-cancer populations [K1, K2], and that competing-risk analyses separate tumour-driven from vulnerability-related deaths [K3]. The unresolved claim is narrower and testable: among incident colorectal-cancer cases, does reserve measured before diagnosis add reproducible prognostic information for non-cancer death beyond diagnosis timing, age, sex, comorbidity and cancer subsite? This is prognostic prediction/descriptive association, not a treatment-tolerance effect or a mechanism.

Primary hypothesis: in the incident cohort, lower pre-diagnostic reserve will be associated with higher 5-year cumulative incidence of non-cancer death, and adding reserve will improve held-out non-cancer risk prediction over the routine-data baseline. The increment should be larger for non-cancer death than for colorectal-cancer-specific death. The leading rival is occult-cancer/cachexia or selection: reserve measured close to diagnosis may simply mark already advanced disease, while missing stage/treatment and UKB volunteer selection may create the apparent signal.

## Population, time axis and estimand

Use only the UKB/ukb672073 namespace. Eligible participants have first assessment date '53-0.0' between 2006-01-01 and 2010-12-31, no cancer-registry ICD-10 code C00-C97 before that date, and a first paired cancer-registry record in '40005-*' (date) and '40006-*' (ICD-10) for C18, C19 or C20 after assessment and by 2022-12-31. The incident diagnosis date 't0' is the earliest valid paired C18-C20 date after assessment. The primary cohort requires 't0 - assessment_date >= 2 years' to reduce reverse causation and 't0 <= 2017-12-31' so a 5-year outcome can be observed by the administrative boundary. A prespecified timing sensitivity includes diagnoses 0-2 years after assessment; a distant sensitivity requires at least 5 years. Do not use post-diagnosis measurements or codes as predictors.

Follow-up starts at 't0', ends at the earliest of death '40000-*', 2022-12-31, or the last source-supported follow-up date after an explicit audit. Deaths before 't0' are excluded. Events are mutually exclusive: (1) colorectal-cancer death, underlying cause '40001-*' beginning C18-C20; (2) other cancer death, cause beginning C00-C97 but not C18-C20; (3) non-cancer death, underlying cause not beginning C00-C97; and (0) censoring. Unknown or unusable cause is reported separately and is not silently classified as non-cancer.

The primary estimand is the difference in held-out 5-year cumulative-incidence prediction and prediction error for non-cancer death between the routine baseline and baseline-plus-reserve models, with CRC-specific and other-cancer death treated as competing events. Report cause-specific Cox and Fine-Gray subdistribution estimates as descriptive association summaries, not causal effects. The primary confirmation is a paired participant bootstrap confidence interval for the change in IPCW Brier score and time-dependent AUC at 1, 3 and 5 years, plus calibration-in-the-large/slope. A reserve increment is supportive only if its uncertainty is compatible with improved prediction and calibration, is not confined to the 0-2-year window, and is not explained by selection diagnostics. Avoid declaring a small uncalibrated AUC change clinically useful.

## Baselines, learned alternative and analysis

B0 is a prespecified cause-specific/Fine-Gray baseline with age at diagnosis, diagnosis timing, sex, Charlson score and C18/C19/C20 code. B0+D adds deprivation only if the missing dependency is admitted. B1 adds the reserve components and their missingness indicators to B0. Use restricted cubic splines for age and timing, standardization learned in training only, and multiple imputation fitted inside training folds; complete-case and missingness-pattern analyses are mandatory. B0 and B1 use exactly the same eligible cases and split.

The substantive learned alternative is a CPU gradient-boosted multiclass horizon model (one model each for 1-, 3- and 5-year outcome, classes CRC death, other-cancer death, non-cancer death and no event) using the same B0/B1 inputs, nonlinear reserve thresholds, reserve-by-timing interactions, repeat-measure change when available, and missingness patterns. It is not included to chase a small score: it tests whether clinically meaningful information is nonlinear or interactive and whether missingness/trajectory itself distinguishes occult-disease timing from stable reserve. Compare B0 vs B1 and the learned B1 model on identical held-out cases with IPCW Brier score, time-dependent AUC, calibration curves/slope, cumulative-incidence calibration and decision curves at prespecified risk thresholds. Use a fixed participant-level 70/15/15 split (seed 36), stratified by outcome and diagnosis year; no resampling-driven outcome or subgroup selection. The 5-year test set is restricted to diagnoses through 2017; 1/3-year analyses use all diagnoses through 2020 when supported by the fixed censor date.

A small regularized linear/spline model is the primary interpretable baseline. A temporal transformer/GP sequence model is deferred, not rejected categorically: the verified binding has sparse repeated reserve values and dated diagnosis arrays but no validated treatment/stage sequence, and a complex sequence representation would not distinguish a stronger scientific explanation under the current budget. Revisit it only if event support and dated longitudinal measurements justify a prespecified trajectory question. CPU is appropriate for the tabular baseline and boosted alternative (approximately 4 CPUs, 8-16 GiB RAM, one bounded job; expected fitting is minutes to tens of minutes after Parquet projection). No GPU is scientifically required; a GPU would not repair missing deprivation, stage or treatment evidence. The future solver must use the configured resource envelope rather than this discovery estimate.

## Falsification, adverse and inconclusive outcomes

1. **Occult-disease timing:** repeat B0/B1 in diagnosis windows 0-2, 2-5 and >=5 years after assessment, with the >=2-year cohort primary. A reserve association confined to 0-2 years, or one that collapses after excluding near-diagnosis cases, supports cachexia/measurement timing rather than stable pre-diagnostic reserve. Do not rescue it by changing the endpoint.
2. **Cause specificity:** compare reserve increments for non-cancer, CRC-specific and other-cancer death. A similar or larger reserve signal for CRC death is adverse to the proposed separation and is compatible with unmeasured stage/tumour burden; it does not prove mechanism. A non-cancer-only signal supports prognostic specificity but still not treatment tolerance.
3. **Negative control:** among the same incident CRC cohort, external-cause death (ICD-10 V01-Y89 where coded) is a negative-control endpoint for a reserve signal attributed specifically to cancer survivorship; a strong identical association suggests general selection/frailty or coding artefact. This is a diagnostic, not proof of absence of confounding.
4. **Measurement/coding:** require a paired cancer date/code and repeat the incident cohort using '41270/41280' inpatient diagnosis records where feasible. Report date discordance, impossible orderings, duplicate eids, and unknown causes. If C18-C20 counts or non-cancer deaths are too sparse for the prespecified horizon, report an inconclusive feasibility result and do not pool all cancer sites.
5. **Selection/missingness:** compare all incident C18-C20 cases with reserve-complete cases on age, sex, timing, BMI, ethnicity, centre and comorbidity; model probability of reserve availability and use inverse-probability weighting as a sensitivity. Report missingness by diagnosis window and outcome. If complete-case and weighted/imputed estimates disagree materially, the result is selection-sensitive and not confirmatory.

Supportive results mean reserve improves calibrated held-out non-cancer prediction, survives the >=2-year check, is not reproduced by the negative control, and has a smaller/different pattern for CRC death. Adverse results mean the increment is absent after timing/selection checks, only near diagnosis, or dominated by CRC death; revise toward occult disease, stage or selection and do not claim host reserve. Inconclusive results mean sparse events, unavailable deprivation, wide intervals, poor calibration, or outcome/cause coding instability; obtain longer follow-up or a linked cancer registry before deciding. Automatic computation can verify joins, cohort counts, model outputs, uncertainty and these stated comparisons. Clinical adjudication is still required for stage, treatment, actual toxicity, functional reserve, cause-of-death validity and external validity.

## Clinical advance and limits

If robust, this study would establish a pre-treatment prognostic boundary: whether a low-cost, pre-diagnostic reserve profile adds information about competing non-cancer mortality after CRC is diagnosed, beyond when the diagnosis occurred and routinely observable patient/cancer variables. That could justify prospective geriatric/nutritional assessment and a treatment-planning study. It would not justify withholding chemotherapy, claim that reserve causes death, identify a biological pathway, or infer tolerance from creatinine/albumin/grip. The dataset lacks TNM stage, grade, treatment assignment, dose intensity, toxicity, performance status, detailed frailty assessment, adjudicated competing causes and external validation; those claims require expert review, linkage or a prospective study.
