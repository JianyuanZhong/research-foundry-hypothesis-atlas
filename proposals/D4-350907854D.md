# UKB-36 repaired proposal: prediagnostic host reserve and competing mortality after colorectal-cancer diagnosis

Status: proposed, not executed. Parent repaired: [prior hypothesis]. Dataset snapshot: `ukb-completed-subset-20261002`; catalog [source checksum].

## Scientific opening and hypothesis

The closest inspected CRC prognostic work [K1] reports that functional status and frailty add most for overall and nondisease-specific survival, whereas a model containing tumor stage/location already performs well for CRC-specific outcomes. A prospective multi-cancer cohort [K2] associates prediagnostic frailty with mortality after cancer diagnosis. The unresolved clinical question is narrower and consequential: among people with incident CRC, does objectively measured host reserve before diagnosis identify a specifically larger risk of non-CRC death than CRC death, and does that signal persist when the measurement is separated from diagnosis far enough to reduce occult-cancer reverse causation?

Primary falsifiable hypothesis: in incident CRC, lower prediagnostic reserve, measured at least two years before the CRC diagnosis, is associated with a larger five-year cumulative incidence of non-CRC death than CRC death after diagnosis, after adjustment for age, sex, BMI, smoking, and baseline calendar time. Formally, for a one-standard-deviation lower reserve score R, the primary contrast is
`log(HR_nonCRC) - log(HR_CRC) > 0`
in cause-specific hazard models, with the corresponding non-CRC CIF contrast exceeding the CRC CIF contrast at five years. This is an association/description estimand, not a causal effect of changing grip strength or renal biomarkers.

The strongest evidence already supports only an association between prediagnostic frailty/reserve and later mortality [K2], and the parent’s bounded diagnostic reported 4,732 C18 diagnoses plus 108,481 rows with baseline handgrip, creatinine and cancer-diagnosis fields. No complete cohort fit or effect estimate has been computed here. The proposed experiment tests the unresolved differential endpoint and temporal-boundary claim. It does not test treatment tolerance, stage, recurrence, or a biological mechanism.

The principal rival is cancer-associated decline before diagnosis: [K3] shows that >10% prediagnostic weight loss predicts worse CRC survival and attenuates an apparent obesity-survival association. If the UKB reserve signal is confined to assessments within two years of diagnosis and disappears with a five-year lag, occult disease/timing is more plausible than durable host reserve. Other rivals are age/comorbidity selection, measurement error, and differential CRC stage or treatment. UKB does not contain the required stage/treatment variables, so these rivals can be bounded but not fully separated.

Clinical value: a reproducible prediagnostic signal that is stronger for non-CRC mortality could justify prospective validation of a reserve screen at CRC diagnosis and different survivorship/supportive-care pathways. A null or proximity-only result would prevent overinterpreting grip or laboratory values as a durable reserve phenotype.

## Population, temporal anchors and outcomes

Unit: one participant with one `eid` in the `ukb672073` namespace.

Baseline `b_i`: the date in `53-0.0` (Date of attending assessment centre), using only the initial assessment instance. Participants missing `53-0.0` or with non-parsable dates are excluded. The primary exposure is therefore prediagnostic and cannot use any measurement after the baseline assessment.

CRC diagnosis date `t_i`: among paired columns `40005-k.0` (date of cancer diagnosis) and `40006-k.0` (type of cancer: ICD10), k=0,...,21, select the earliest date after `b_i` whose paired ICD-10 code matches the prespecified colorectal definition `^C(18|19|20)` after trimming and upper-casing. This includes colon (C18), rectosigmoid junction (C19), and rectum (C20); it excludes C00-C17, C21 and non-ICD10 values. Do not treat an unpaired date or a missing code as CRC. Exclude anyone with a CRC code/date on or before baseline. The primary cohort requires `730 <= t_i-b_i < 3653` days (approximately 2 to <10 years); the five-year-lag sensitivity cohort requires `t_i-b_i >= 1826` days. A prespecified timing analysis retains eligible CRC cases with `t_i>b_i` and reports strata 0-<2, 2-<5 and >=5 years; it is not used to redefine the primary cohort.

Cancer-diagnosis dates and codes are registry-derived fields in the frozen source. They are not an adjudicated pathology record. If more than one paired CRC record has the same earliest date, retain one participant and record the tie.

Post-diagnosis time origin: `t_i), not assessment date. Follow-up time is days from `t_i` to the first valid death date in `40000-0.0` or `40000-1.0`, or administrative censoring at 2026-10-02, the frozen snapshot boundary. The solver must assert that no accepted death date is after 2026-10-02; if the source documentation establishes an earlier outcome-linkage cutoff, that documented cutoff supersedes the snapshot boundary and must be recorded in the output. Death dates on or before `t_i` are data-quality exclusions, not events.

Competing outcomes, using the underlying primary cause paired by death record in `40001-0.0`/`40001-1.0`:

1. CRC death: cause code matches `^C(18|19|20)`.
2. Non-CRC death: a valid nonmissing ICD-10 cause code that does not match `^C(18|19|20)`.
3. Unknown-cause death: a death date exists but the cause is missing or cannot be parsed.

No death by the administrative boundary is censored. Unknown-cause death remains a third competing event in the primary analysis; a sensitivity analysis classifies it as non-CRC and a second sensitivity censors it at death. Report all three event counts and do not silently discard unknown causes. The primary estimand is the five-year CIF for causes 1 and 2, with cause 3 displayed as a data-quality/competing outcome.

## Exposure and covariate construction

Primary host-reserve score R is fitted only in the training split and then applied unchanged to validation/test data:

- `grip_max = max(46-0.0, 47-0.0)`, using the available side only if one side is missing; both missing is missing.
- Standardize grip_max within sex; standardize log(albumin), -log(creatinine), and -log(cystatin C) within sex using training parameters.
- `R` is the mean of available standardized components when at least three of four are present. Record component count and missingness. The primary complete-reserve analysis requires all four; an imputation-plus-missing-indicator sensitivity uses training medians.
- Higher R means greater measured reserve. Do not call it biological reserve or sarcopenia without validation.

Adjustment covariates are age at recruitment `21022-0.0` (or derived age from `34-0.0` only as a check), sex `31-0.0`, BMI `21001-0.0`, smoking `20116-0.0`, and calendar year from `53-0.0`. Do not adjust for variables measured after diagnosis. The diagnosis-lag `t_i-b_i` is used for timing strata and a sensitivity adjustment, not as a post-diagnosis covariate.

## Baseline versus substantive learned alternative

Both methods use exactly the same participant-level features above, the same incident-CRC population, the same diagnosis-anchored competing outcomes, and the same participant-level split. The comparison addresses a scientific uncertainty—whether reserve acts as a simple approximately linear scalar or through nonlinear threshold/joint patterns—not a leaderboard objective.

Baseline: a prespecified discrete-time competing-risk hazard model with one-year intervals 0-1, 1-2, ..., 4-5 years; interval indicators; R as a linear one-SD term; and the adjustment covariates listed above. Fit cause-specific complementary-log-log hazards (or an equivalent multinomial discrete-time hazard with a documented link), with no data-driven feature selection. Transform interval hazards into cause-specific CIFs using the product-limit recursion. This is interpretable and estimates the primary differential association.

Learned alternative: a regularized gradient-boosted tree multinomial discrete-time hazard model over the same one-year person-period rows and four outcomes (no event, CRC death, non-CRC death, unknown death), with interval as an input and monotonicity not imposed. Use a fixed, small hyperparameter grid decided before test evaluation (e.g., 200/400 trees, depth 2/3, learning rate 0.03/0.05); select by validation integrated Brier score. The substantive output is not only a score: report endpoint-specific permutation importance and held-out partial-dependence/accumulated-local-effect curves for R, grip, albumin, creatinine and cystatin C, plus the joint low-grip/high-cystatin-C pattern. This can reveal thresholds or reserve-component combinations that the scalar baseline erases. If the learned model does not improve or gives unstable effects, retain the baseline as the scientific answer rather than escalating complexity.

Split: hash `eid` with a fixed documented hash and allocate 70% train, 15% validation, 15% locked test, stratified only by event type after participant-level assignment; no person-period row may cross splits. All scaling, imputation, feature selection and tuning use train/validation only. Lock the test file and compute it once.

Evaluation and uncertainty: at 1, 3 and 5 years report cause-specific CIF calibration intercept/slope, integrated and horizon-specific IPCW Brier scores, time-dependent AUC, and a paired participant bootstrap (at least 500 resamples; target 1,000 if within the solver envelope) for contrasts and model differences. For the primary scientific estimand, report adjusted cause-specific hazard ratios for one-SD lower R, the difference between non-CRC and CRC log effects, and standardized five-year CIF contrasts with 95% bootstrap intervals. Calibration must be assessed separately for CRC, non-CRC and unknown death. The solver must emit the cohort flow, missingness table, event table, model coefficients, learned feature-effect table, predictions and random seeds.

Resource estimate: CPU-only is adequate for this tabular, <=5-year discrete-time study; no GPU is required. The future solver planning envelope is 16 CPUs, 262,144 MiB and 28,800 seconds. ## Falsification, interpretation and stopping rules

Supportive pattern: in the primary >=2-year lag cohort, lower R has a positive non-CRC association and the non-CRC-minus-CRC log-effect contrast is above zero with a 95% interval excluding zero; the direction is similar in the >=5-year sensitivity cohort; and the learned model either exposes stable nonlinear reserve interactions or improves held-out non-CRC CIF calibration/Brier score by a prespecified practically meaningful amount (absolute integrated Brier improvement >=0.01, with bootstrap interval above zero). This supports a robust prognostic regularity and motivates external validation and prospective reserve measurement, not a treatment or mechanism claim.

Adverse pattern: the contrast is zero/reversed, CRC death is at least as strongly associated as non-CRC death, or the reserve association disappears in the >=5-year lag cohort while appearing only at 0-<2 years. That pattern supports competing explanations such as occult cancer-associated decline, age/comorbidity selection, coding/timing artifacts or stage differences; abandon the durable-reserve interpretation and report the boundary.

Inconclusive pattern: sparse deaths, wide intervals crossing clinically relevant values, unstable cause coding, or large test missingness/miscalibration. Do not call this a null. Continue only after improving outcome adjudication, obtaining a documented linkage cutoff, or acquiring independent CRC stage/treatment data.

Falsification checks required before interpretation:

1. Refit by lag strata 0-<2, 2-<5 and >=5 years; a durable host-reserve claim must not be confined to the nearest stratum.
2. Compare complete-case R with training-median/missing-indicator analysis and with grip-only and laboratory-only scores. A result driven by a single assay or missingness flag is not evidence for a unified reserve construct.
3. Repeat with age/sex/BMI/smoking adjustment, then add an available baseline prior-cancer indicator only if its field definition is verified; report how much attenuation occurs.
4. Replace the three-cause outcome with a two-cause analysis (unknown as non-CRC; unknown censored at death) and compare CIFs.
5. Compare diagnosis-anchored competing-risk estimates with a naive analysis that censors competing deaths; a material discrepancy demonstrates why the competing-risk estimand matters.
6. Use a negative-control exposure consisting of shuffled R within sex and a negative-control date feature from assessment-date ordering; the shuffled model must lose the held-out association. Permute outcomes once with a fixed seed; apparent retained signal indicates leakage.
7. Test the learned model only after the baseline specification is frozen. A predictive improvement without stable endpoint-specific reserve effects is a model-performance result, not evidence for host reserve.

Computationally checkable claims are the event definitions, cohort counts, lag strata, model fits, calibration, uncertainty and whether conclusions match locked outputs. Clinical adjudication or another study is required for histologic CRC confirmation, stage, recurrence, treatment receipt/dose/tolerance, cause-of-death validity, generalizability beyond UKB, and any causal or mechanistic statement. The available data cannot establish that low reserve causes non-CRC mortality or identifies patients unable to tolerate therapy.

## Alternatives not chosen and revisit evidence

A simple reserve-score model was retained because it directly tests the differential endpoint and is transparent. The learned boosted hazard model was retained because it can reveal thresholds and component interactions lost by a scalar score while remaining feasible on CPU. A transformer/longitudinal sequence model was deferred: the primary question is baseline reserve, the required dated repeated reserve series is not bound, and complexity would not answer a distinct scientific uncertainty. A mechanistic renal-muscle model was deferred because no validated mechanistic labels or treatment outcomes are available. Revisit these alternatives only if repeated prediagnostic measurements, stage/treatment data, or an independent cohort are admitted, or if the learned model demonstrates stable nonlinear patterns that need external mechanistic testing.
