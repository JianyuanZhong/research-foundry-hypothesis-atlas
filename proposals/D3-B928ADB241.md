# Temporal Feedback Structure of Macrophage-FLS Inflammatory Signaling in RA Synovial Tissue

## Scientific Opening

In rheumatoid arthritis (RA), synovial tissue exhibits a self-reinforcing inflammatory loop between macrophages and fibroblast-like synoviocytes (FLS) [K1]. The crosstalk is well documented: FLS secrete IL-34, CCL2, and CXCL12 that recruit and polarize macrophages toward the M1 phenotype, while M1 macrophage-derived TNF-α, IL-6, and IL-1β further activate FLS [K1]. However, the **temporal ordering** of this feedback is unresolved. Three non-equivalent explanations are compatible with the current evidence:

1. **Unidirectional macrophage-driven model**: Macrophage activation precedes and drives the FLS inflammatory response. FLS are passive recipients of macrophage-derived signals.
2. **Bidirectional feedback loop**: Macrophage and FLS activation are mutually reinforcing with no clear temporal precedence. Both cell types can initiate the loop.
3. **Composition-driven model**: The apparent "feedback" is an artifact of changes in cell composition (e.g., increased macrophage infiltration) rather than a genuine dynamic interaction between cell types.

This distinction is clinically consequential: if the loop is unidirectional, targeting macrophage activation may suffice; if bidirectional, both cell types must be addressed; if composition-driven, the apparent feedback may not reflect a genuine mechanistic interaction.

## Hypothesis

**Hypothesis**: In RA synovial tissue, macrophage and FLS inflammatory activation form a bidirectional feedback loop with no clear temporal precedence, rather than a unidirectional macrophage-driven or composition-driven process.

## Evidence Opening

The available datasets provide single-cell resolution of FLS and macrophages in RA synovial tissue:

- **E-MTAB-8873**: Targeted assay with 445 measured feature columns. Pseudobulk summarizes each recorded FLS/coculture sample. Multiple contributing or reused donors require explicit dependence modeling.
- **GSE95588**: Coculture data with duplicate and date-like gene labels. Donor-aware inferential comparisons need verified column-to-GSM/donor mapping.
- **E-MTAB-8316**: Four FLS donor clusters, six nested monocyte donors, twelve condition samples. Average repeated context measurements within each donor/condition before paired exploratory inference.
- **E-MTAB-8322**: Pseudobulk contains measured macrophages only. SA149/SA149P relatedness unresolved; excluded from simple independent-donor screens.

The Mpos vs Mneg conditions in these datasets provide a discriminating test for the temporal feedback structure: if macrophage activation drives FLS activation, Mpos conditions should show elevated FLS inflammatory gene expression; if the loop is bidirectional, both cell types should show elevated expression in Mpos conditions; if the apparent feedback is composition-driven, the signal should be explained by changes in cell composition rather than within-cell regulation.

## Falsifiable Test

A donor-aware mixed model on Mpos vs Mneg conditions in E-MTAB-8873, GSE95588, E-MTAB-8316, and E-MTAB-8322, with specific observable predictions for each competing explanation:

- **Unidirectional macrophage-driven**: Mpos conditions show elevated FLS inflammatory gene expression (IL6, TNF, IL1B) but not elevated macrophage inflammatory gene expression.
- **Bidirectional feedback loop**: Mpos conditions show elevated inflammatory gene expression in both FLS and macrophages.
- **Composition-driven**: The apparent feedback is explained by changes in cell composition (e.g., increased macrophage infiltration) rather than within-cell regulation.
- **Donor-specific**: The signal is driven by a subset of donors rather than a consistent pattern across donors.
