# Creatinine rise during observed decongestion: a selection-ready MIMIC-IV restart

## Status and scientific deliverable

This is a substantive adaptation of expert seed `[starting question]` (imported candidate `[prior hypothesis]`), not a reproduction of a published analysis. It is a fresh restart direction to compare with mature candidate `[prior hypothesis]`, which studies fixed pre-exit lactate-by-perfusion prioritization. No cohort count, fitted result, prevalence, or clinical conclusion is claimed here.

The future Harbor solver must newly fit and estimate:

1. a reproducible record-defined acute-care heart-failure cohort and flow;
2. a prespecified creatinine-rise/observed-decongestion phenotype with an indeterminate category and row-level clinical-time/recording-time provenance;
3. separate 24-hour cumulative-incidence contrasts for renal replacement, respiratory escalation, and timed death, plus their record-defined rescue-versus-death decomposition;
4. matched latest-state/additive, phenotype-interaction, care-context-only, and chronological learned models on one subject-level split;
5. calibration, fixed-capacity capture, uncertainty, coverage/ascertainment sensitivity, and phenotype/timing/permutation falsifications; and
6. a conclusion ledger that distinguishes prognostic association from treatment effect, harmlessness, congestion diagnosis, diuretic response, and transportability.

Completion is either a gate-passing held-out study with all outputs or a complete, prespecified gate-failure audit. Readiness and a favorable direction do not establish the hypothesis.

## Unresolved question and clinical importance

The strongest available evidence supports only a narrower claim. A recent retrospective MIMIC-IV study of 3,267 critically ill patients with HF reported that first-24-hour negative fluid balance was associated with lower 28-day mortality and 48-hour AKI but higher AKI progression; it used broad fluid-balance groups and adjusted logistic models, and explicitly called for clinical-trial validation. A separate critical-illness study of 38 mechanically ventilated patients found that creatinine-based eGFR overestimated measured kidney function relative to cystatin C and iohexol clearance during prolonged illness. These findings motivate, but do not establish, the question below. Neither proves that a creatinine rise during successful decongestion is benign, nor that persistent congestion causes a worse outcome.

The falsifiable hypothesis is:

> Within adults with a record-defined HF admission who received a loop diuretic and then developed a prespecified creatinine rise during the first 48 ICU hours, patients with improving observed decongestion have a lower 24-hour risk of record-defined rescue and timed death than patients with persistent observed congestion, after standardization to the same baseline severity and care-context variables.

The primary contrast is not “diuresis is safe.” It is the standardized difference in first-event risk between two observed trajectory groups among creatinine risers. The expected direction is lower rescue and death for improving versus persistent congestion; the new renal-replacement component is reported separately and is not assumed to have the same direction. A null or reversed contrast is scientifically useful: it would challenge the common interpretation that the clinical meaning of creatinine rise depends primarily on whether fluid status is improving.

This advances existing knowledge by conditioning on creatinine rise, requiring a joint trajectory rather than a single net-balance category, and decomposing a composite into renal rescue, respiratory escalation, and death. It also tests whether the apparent physiology signal survives a prespecified care-context/observation decomposition. It is clinically relevant to whether a creatinine rise should be interpreted alongside decongestion evidence, but it cannot determine whether clinicians should continue, reduce, or stop diuretics.

## Population, landmark, and exact phenotype

Use adults with age `anchor_age >= 18` in the first valid ICU stay for an admission: non-null `subject_id, hadm_id, stay_id, intime, outtime` and `intime < outtime`. Define the HF-coded acute-care phenotype using `hosp/diagnoses_icd` rows with `seq_num=1` and ICD-10 `icd_code` beginning `I50`, or ICD-9 `icd_code` beginning `428`. Because diagnoses have no clinical timestamp and an HF code is not an adjudicated acute decompensation label, call this “record-defined HF admission,” not acute HF. A sensitivity definition uses any qualifying HF code on the admission.

Exclude a stay if a qualifying dialysis procedure starts before the landmark, if death is known by the landmark, if `hospital_expire_flag` is inconsistent with an absent `deathtime`, or if the admission does not remain observable at the landmark. Require at least one delivered loop-diuretic input before the landmark: item 221794 Furosemide (Lasix), 228340 Furosemide (Lasix) 250/50, or 229639 Bumetanide (Bumex). Do not convert bumetanide to furosemide-equivalent dose; retain drug-specific dose and exposure as separate context variables.

The fixed landmark is `t0 = intime + 48 hours`. Require `t0 < outtime`, `dischtime > t0`, and live status at `t0`: `deathtime > t0`, or null `deathtime` with `hospital_expire_flag=0`. A missing death time with a missing/nonzero flag is unresolved, not survival. Keep an all-candidate flow before any coverage or outcome exclusion.

### Creatinine rise

Primary creatinine is serum chemistry item 50912 (`Creatinine`) in `hosp/labevents`, with finite `valuenum`, clinical `charttime` inside the ICU interval, recording `storetime <= t0`, and canonical `valueuom=mg/dL`. If a solver uses ICU charted creatinine items 220615 or 229761, that is a declared sensitivity, not a silent merge. Define:

- C0: median creatinine in `[intime, intime+24h)`;
- C1: median creatinine in `[intime+24h, t0)`;
- Rise: `C1-C0 >= 0.30 mg/dL`.

Each window must have at least two distinct clinical-time creatinine observations, and every selected row must pass the dual clinical-time/recording-time audit. Sensitivities are a 1.5-fold increase and absolute thresholds of 0.20 and 0.50 mg/dL. These are record-defined creatinine patterns, not adjudicated KDIGO stage or measured GFR.

### Observed decongestion

Calculate two adjacent 24-hour windows using clinical event times.

- Recorded mL input is the sum of finite `icu/inputevents.amount` rows whose `amountuom` is mL and whose valid `starttime,endtime,storetime` are available. Continuous intervals are clipped to the window and integrated from the recorded rate where a valid mL/hour rate exists; otherwise a valid mL amount is assigned at its clinical start, with interval-clipping rules logged. Non-mL doses are never converted.
- Recorded mL output is the sum of finite `icu/outputevents.value` rows with `valueuom` mL and clinical `charttime` in the window. It is an operational recorded-output measure, not complete physiologic fluid loss.
- Net0 and Net1 are recorded input minus recorded output for the first and second windows.
- Urine0 and Urine1 are the sum of output items 226559 Foley, 226560 Void, 226561 Condom Cath, 226563 Suprapubic, 226564 R Nephrostomy, 226565 L Nephrostomy, 226566 Urine and GU Irrigant Out, 226567 Straight Cath, 226627 OR Urine, 226631 PACU Urine, and 227489 GU Irrigant/Urine Volume Out. If no urine observation exists in a window, the phenotype is indeterminate; missing is not zero.

The primary mutually exclusive physiology-proxy groups, restricted to Rise, are:

- Improving observed decongestion: `Net1-Net0 <= -500 mL` and `Urine1-Urine0 >= +250 mL`.
- Persistent observed congestion: `Net1-Net0 >= 0 mL` and `Urine1-Urine0 <= 0 mL`.
- Indeterminate/discordant: all other patterns or failed endpoint-independent coverage.

These thresholds are prespecified operational proxies. They do not diagnose intravascular volume, venous congestion, or adequate source control. Sensitivities vary the net threshold to 0 and -1,000 mL, the urine threshold to 0 and +500 mL, use urine-only or net-only phenotypes, and require non-worsening respiratory burden. A failed coverage or event-support gate makes the corresponding estimand not estimable rather than permitting post hoc redefinition.

## Outcome and estimands

Follow `(t0,t0+24h]`. The first record-defined outcome is one of:

- K: new renal replacement, the first `icu/procedureevents.starttime > t0` among item IDs 225441 Hemodialysis, 225802 CRRT, 225803 CVVHD, 225805 Peritoneal Dialysis, 225809 CVVHDF, or 225955 SCUF;
- E: respiratory escalation, the first start after `t0` of item 224385 Intubation, 225792 Invasive Ventilation, or 225794 Non-invasive Ventilation;
- D: timed in-hospital death from `hosp/admissions.deathtime` in the window, ordered before a tied K/E event;
- S: live ICU outtime before K/E/D, an administrative competing boundary;
- N: no K/E/D/S through 24 hours while ICU observation is complete;
- U: unknown death timing, incomplete ICU event ascertainment, invalid ordering, or other prespecified follow-up failure.

An active procedure crossing `t0` is not a new event. A live ICU exit is not evidence of recovery, and post-exit ward rescue is not captured by this structured ICU endpoint. Report K, E, D, S, N and U separately; R=K-or-E is a record-defined rescue summary only.

On the strict estimable set E*, the primary estimands are 24-hour cumulative-incidence contrasts standardized to the distribution of baseline covariates in the two Rise groups:

`theta_R = CIF_R(24 | Rise, Persistent) - CIF_R(24 | Rise, Improving)`

and, as a clinically distinct secondary,

`theta_D = CIF_D(24 | Rise, Persistent) - CIF_D(24 | Rise, Improving)`.

Report `theta_K` and `theta_E` separately, plus all unstandardized group CIFs and U/S/N rates. Use cause-specific discrete-time hazards with competing K/E/D/S states and covariate standardization, or an equivalently documented Aalen-Johansen/G-computation implementation. Covariates are C0, baseline Net0/Urine0, age, gender, admission type, first care unit, loop-drug indicators and dose variables, oxygen/ventilation status at t0, and measurement counts. No post-t0 covariate may enter a model.

The primary directional support criterion is a lower 95% simultaneous subject-bootstrap bound for `theta_R` above 0.05, with no material reversal of `theta_D`; `theta_D`, `theta_K`, and `theta_E` remain separately reported and are not collapsed to rescue a failed primary contrast. A null or reversed estimate, a persistent signal in the care-only control, or a phenotype-permutation signal is adverse to the proposed record-defined interpretation. Broad intervals, sparse cells, high U, absent overlap, or failed coverage are inconclusive.

## Physiology versus care-context decomposition

The physiology-proxy block contains only C0, C1, their change, Net0/Net1, Urine0/Urine1, and the derived Rise/decongestion group. It is explicitly named a proxy block because recorded fluid delivery and urine measurement are not pure physiology.

The care-context/observation block contains loop-drug identity and drug-specific dose, oxygen flow item 223834, SpO2 item 220277, O2 device item 226732, respiratory-rate item 220210, ventilation status before t0 from procedure starts, counts and distinct clinical times of creatinine/fluid/urine/oxygen observations, missingness masks, `first_careunit`, the `hosp/transfers` careunit interval covering t0, `anchor_year_group`, age, gender, admission type, and elapsed ICU time. Dose is context, not an instrument or treatment effect.

Fit the following on exactly the same E* subject split, outcome definition, horizon, and available pre-t0 rows:

- B0: penalized cause-specific discrete-time model using baseline demographics, admission context, C0/C1, Net0/Urine0, and t0 respiratory status.
- B1: B0 plus all continuous pre-t0 trajectories and observation masks/counts, but no Rise-by-decongestion interaction.
- Bphen: B1 plus the locked Rise-by-improving/persistent/indeterminate phenotype terms; this is the primary interpretable test.
- Bmask: care-context and observation variables only, with all physiologic values masked; this tests whether documentation intensity, diuretic selection, oxygen care, or unit mix can reproduce the signal.
- J1: a small masked chronological GRU over six 8-hour bins from ICU admission to t0, with the same physiology domains, care-context domains, and missingness masks as B1/Bphen. It predicts K/E/D/S hazards and is trained only on the training subjects.

The learned alternative tests a substantive uncertainty: whether the ordering and persistence of fluid/urine/creatinine/oxygen trajectories contain information that fixed summaries discard. It may reveal reversal, delayed response, nonlinear coupling, or care-process/physiology interactions. It cannot convert an observational association into a treatment effect and cannot rescue a failed Bphen phenotype test.

Report group-standardized CIFs and contrasts as the main scientific outputs. Model outputs are secondary: held-out Brier score, calibration intercept/slope and O:E for K/E/D, R/D-specific AUPRC, and fixed 5%, 10%, and 20% review-capacity capture with a random-allocation benchmark. Treat a small AUC change without calibrated R/D improvement or a stable phenotype contrast as scientifically insufficient.

## Split, uncertainty, gates, and falsification

Freeze a subject-level 70/15/15 train/validation/test split by `subject_id` before fitting or test inspection. Select thresholds, regularization, GRU hidden size, early stopping, and any context grouping on training/validation only. Use one locked test evaluation. Use 1,000 subject bootstrap replicates for contrasts, CIFs, calibration, and fixed-capacity capture with the split preserved; if the solver envelope forces fewer, report the actual number and missing components.

Before test outcomes are used, require:

- valid source/member/header/schema and dictionary audits;
- no duplicate or reversed interval failures after the declared handling;
- dual-time/storetime leakage audit and zero post-t0 predictor rows;
- at least 100 subjects in both Improving and Persistent groups, at least 30 R events overall, and at least 10 D events in each primary group; otherwise the corresponding contrast is not estimable;
- complete endpoint-independent creatinine/fluid/urine coverage, with U <=20% for the primary analysis;
- positivity/overlap for standardization and effective sample size above 50 in each primary group;
- all-candidate, eligibility, phenotype, event-support, and U/S/N flow tables.

Prespecified falsifications and sensitivities are:

1. permute Improving/Persistent labels within Rise and coverage strata; the contrast should vanish;
2. permute all outcome labels within the frozen split; no model should retain signal;
3. fit Bmask and compare it with Bphen; comparable performance indicates care-context explanation rather than physiologic information;
4. shuffle chronology within J1 bins and compare with chronological J1;
5. shift the phenotype window 12 hours earlier while retaining the same outcome window as a timing placebo;
6. repeat with urine-only, net-only, alternate thresholds, charttime-only versus dual-time filtering, and ICU-charted creatinine;
7. remove loop-dose and oxygen variables from B1/Bphen to quantify context dependence;
8. prohibit and scan for `last_careunit`, `deathtime`, `hospital_expire_flag`, `dod`, post-t0 procedure rows, and post-t0 discharge fields as predictors.

Support requires the source/time/coverage/event gates, a positive `theta_R` meeting the locked threshold, no material contradiction in `theta_D`, calibrated held-out output, and disappearance under phenotype and label permutation. Adverse evidence is a null/reversed contrast, Bmask equivalence, timing-placebo persistence, or calibration failure; it does not prove that diuresis harms patients. Inconclusive evidence includes sparse groups, failed overlap, U >20%, or uncertainty spanning clinically important directions.

## Evidence limits and comparison with the mature lactate branch

No local cystatin C, iohexol clearance, measured GFR, echocardiographic congestion adjudication, reliable clinician intent, diuretic indication, complete ward rescue record, or external validation is available in the configured sources. The MIMIC timestamp convention is within-subject shifted; cross-subject calendar alignment is invalid. Diagnosis rows lack onset times. Radiology text exists but is excluded; radiology images and raw waveforms are unavailable. Clinical review is required to decide whether a procedure was necessary, whether fluid status truly improved, whether a creatinine rise was hemodilution/hemoconcentration or intrinsic injury, and whether a treatment policy would improve outcomes.

The mature lactate parent is currently stronger for selection: it already has a frozen 12-hour pre-exit landmark, exact leakage-safe lactate/MAP/pressor bindings, a locked context-shift evaluation, explicit R/D handling, matched models, and established validity assessment. This restart is clinically complementary and selection-ready as a design, but it is not worth replacing the mature parent unless its coverage and event gates pass and it demonstrates a stable R/D contrast that survives Bmask, timing, and phenotype permutation. If HF coding, complete fluid/urine coverage, or group/event support fails, park this direction as a valuable negative feasibility result rather than weakening the question. If it passes and shows a robust contrast, it merits a parallel study because it addresses a different bedside ambiguity: interpretation of creatinine during decongestion rather than pre-exit lactate/perfusion prioritization.

## Compute and method rationale

The primary B0/B1/Bphen/Bmask fits are small tabular competing-risk models and should be CPU-first. J1 is a bounded learned alternative, not a mandatory method: six bins, a 32/64-unit masked GRU, dropout, early stopping, five training seeds, and the same data whitelist. Future solver planning envelope: up to 16 CPUs, 262,144 MiB, eight A100-SXM4-80GB GPUs, and 28,800 seconds; current discovery budget is 7,200 seconds. These are planning limits, not measured runtimes. GPU availability was verified for allocated jobs, while no model runtime, convergence time, coverage fraction, or event count was measured here. A GPU may be allocated only if repeated J1 fits make CPU runtime materially limiting, using the allocated device as `cuda:0`; no proposer or solver-weight training is involved.

The simple baseline is scientifically necessary because it answers whether the joint phenotype adds information beyond absolute creatinine and the same care context. The learned alternative can reveal temporal order and nonlinear coupling that aggregate summaries lose, but model complexity or a small predictive gain is not evidence of clinical value. Large transformers, latent disease mixtures, causal treatment-effect models, and image/waveform models are deferred: this snapshot lacks adjudicated congestion, treatment intent/counterfactual support, full external validation, images, and raw waveforms. Revisit them only if the future solver establishes adequate phenotype support and clinical review.

## Completion and interpretation

A supportive result establishes only that, in the record-defined HF-coded ICU population and estimable pre-t0 risk set, the joint observed creatinine/decongestion pattern is associated with different short-term record-defined event risks and may improve calibrated retrospective stratification. An adverse result challenges that association or shows it is mainly care-context/documentation signal. An inconclusive result identifies failed data support or ascertainment.

None of these results establishes that a creatinine rise is harmless, that a clinician should continue diuresis, that decongestion caused the outcome, that a procedure was appropriate or preventable, that the phenotype measures true congestion or kidney function, or that the finding transports outside this MIMIC snapshot. Those claims require clinical adjudication, additional biomarkers/physiologic measurements, a treatment-effect study or randomized trial, and external validation.
