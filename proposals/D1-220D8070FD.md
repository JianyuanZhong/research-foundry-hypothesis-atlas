# Live ICU discharge review: incremental value of the preceding 12-hour instability history

## Scientific deliverable and material evolution

The future Harbor solver must newly produce:

1. a reproducible first eligible live-ICU-discharge cohort and timed 48-hour label audit, including coverage exclusions and untimed-death unknowns;
2. the fixed numeric late-instability index (LII) and twelve-bin feature provenance from the final 12 hours before ICU outtime;
3. a prespecified held-out decision-support comparison between a latest-state/context baseline and an augmented model that adds the same patient's pre-discharge history;
4. a separate covariate-standardized association estimate for LII, with competing-risk and ascertainability sensitivities;
5. paired held-out predictions from an order-blind aggregate model and a chronological masked GRU using the same context, source whitelist, split, and endpoint;
6. bootstrap uncertainty, calibration, endpoint/coverage sensitivities, and chronology, mask-only, and label-permutation falsifications; and
7. an interpretation audit mapping every conclusion to computed outputs and explicitly separating prognostic usefulness from discharge appropriateness, preventability, and causal benefit.

Readiness, a favorable metric, and confirmation of the directional hypothesis are not completion.

This is a substantive child of assigned assessed-valid parent `[prior hypothesis]`. The clinically important question and exact MIMIC bindings are retained. The material repair is to state the clinical decision target and incremental estimand explicitly: the study evaluates whether a risk estimate available at live ICU outtime can prioritize enhanced post-ICU review or monitoring, and whether the pre-discharge history improves prognostic accuracy beyond the latest state and admission/ICU context. It does not estimate the effect of delaying ICU transfer, adding monitoring, or changing treatment.

## Unresolved question, clinical importance, and evidence boundary

At the decision to transfer a patient out of the ICU, can a risk estimate for first same-admission ICU readmission or timed in-hospital death within 48 hours identify patients who merit enhanced post-ICU review or monitoring beyond a baseline using the latest recorded physiologic state and admission/ICU context? This is clinically consequential because early ICU return or death may prompt review of handoff, monitoring intensity, and ward escalation pathways. In this retrospective record, however, those actions are not observed as a randomized policy and their benefit is not identifiable.

The primary falsifiable decision-support hypothesis is:

> Among first eligible live ICU discharges with adequate pre-discharge measurement coverage and an ascertainable timed outcome, adding the prespecified twelve-hour pre-outtime history to a baseline containing admission/ICU context and the terminal recorded state improves held-out probabilistic prediction of the 48-hour composite, with better or non-inferior calibration.

The primary incremental estimand is the paired test-set difference in Brier score,
`Delta_Brier_history = Brier(augmented history model) - Brier(latest-state/context baseline)`, with negative values favorable, plus the paired differences in calibration intercept/slope and an outcome-free, prespecified decision-threshold sensitivity table. The baseline and augmented models use the same population, endpoint, subject split, and source whitelist. A positive result means incremental prognostic information for this recorded endpoint; it is not a treatment effect, net clinical benefit, or proof that a different discharge decision would improve outcomes.

For interpretability, the augmented model has two matched representations:

- an order-blind aggregate logistic model using context, terminal state, and prespecified order-blind summaries of all twelve bins; and
- a chronological masked GRU using the same context and the same per-bin inputs.

The secondary representation estimand is `Delta_Brier_order = Brier(GRU) - Brier(aggregate)` on untouched test patients. A chronology-dependent advantage should attenuate when preterminal bin order is shuffled within patient while the terminal bin and all measurements are preserved. The LII is a separate descriptive exposure estimand, not a substitute for the incremental decision comparison:

`logit P(Y48=1 | LII, Z) = alpha + beta LII_IQR + gamma Z`.

Report the standardized observational risk contrast `RD75-25 = mean_i[p_i(LII=Q75)-p_i(LII=Q25)]` and per-IQR association, but do not call either a benefit of delaying discharge.

Available evidence supports only that this frozen snapshot contains ICU episode boundaries, admission outcomes, timestamped structured observations, and the audited dictionary items/units. It does not establish residual physiology as causal, that events were preventable, or that a review/monitoring policy would help. Related MIMIC-IV ICU-readmission prediction literature (Remoundou et al., 2026, PMCID `PMC13409962`; the acquired full-text XML was read for relevant abstract/introduction/methods passages) supports the existence of related predictive work, not this live-discharge population, endpoint, incremental estimand, chronology test, or intervention effect. The three research-ambition demonstrations are rigor/method examples, not evidence for this hypothesis and are not reproduced.

## Population, index, and temporal boundaries

The source unit is an ICU stay linked to one hospital admission.

1. Read every `icu/icustays` row with non-null `subject_id, hadm_id, stay_id, intime, outtime`. Reject `outtime <= intime`, `outtime < admittime`, and rows without a matching admission.
2. Inner join `hosp/admissions` on `(subject_id, hadm_id)`; require non-null `admittime` and `dischtime`.
3. Define an eligible live ICU discharge at `t0 = outtime` when `outtime < dischtime` and either `deathtime` is null or `deathtime > outtime`. The primary index is the earliest eligible discharge per `(subject_id, hadm_id)`, ordered by `outtime`. A secondary all-eligible-discharge analysis clusters by subject and hospitalization.
4. The primary analytic population is eligible first discharges with at least four of twelve bins containing at least two valid core numeric channels and an ascertainable timed primary outcome. Retain an all-eligible flow table, including coverage exclusions and untimed-death unknowns.
5. Split all primary observations by `subject_id`; no person occurs in more than one partition. Cross-subject calendar alignment is prohibited because MIMIC timestamps are subject-specifically shifted.

The feature window is exactly `[t0 - 12 hours, t0]`, in twelve backward bins `[t0-12h,t0-11h), ..., [t0-1h,t0]`. Use `charttime` for charted measurements and `starttime/endtime` for intervals. `storetime` is never used for ordering, inclusion, or missingness. A row with clinical time after `t0` is prohibited even if it was stored earlier.

## Primary outcome and ascertainability

For each index, define the first event in `(t0, min(t0+48h, dischtime)]`:

- ICU readmission: a later `icu/icustays` row with the same `subject_id, hadm_id`, a different `stay_id`, and `intime > t0`; 
- timed in-hospital death: `hosp/admissions.deathtime > t0`, within 48 hours and no later than `dischtime`.

The binary primary outcome is one if either occurs. For competing-cause tables, a same-time tie is assigned death first; the composite is unchanged. A later ICU stay after `dischtime` is not a same-hospitalization event.

If `hospital_expire_flag=1` and `deathtime` is null, the record cannot establish whether death fell inside the timed window. Treat it as an untimed-death unknown, exclude it from the timed primary analysis without imputation, and report its count and covariate/coverage distribution. A flag of zero with no `deathtime`, or a non-null death time outside the window, is not a timed event. Sensitivity bounds assign every untimed flag to event and to non-event; the latter is not the primary estimate.

Secondary outcomes are the analogous 7-day composite; readmission alone at 48 hours and 7 days; timed death at 48 hours and 7 days; and in-hospital death through `dischtime`, where `hospital_expire_flag` may be a label-only indicator if `deathtime` is absent. Report readmission-first, death-first, and no-event categories, cause-specific cumulative incidence, and cause-specific hazards for the timed horizons. Treat alive hospital discharge before a horizon as administrative censoring for the same-admission risk set; as sensitivity, treat it as a competing terminal event or use inverse-censoring weights only if positivity and diagnostics support it. No endpoint is called preventable.

## LII and per-bin inputs

Core channels are HR, MAP, RR, SpO2, temperature, FiO2, O2 flow, and PEEP. Use `valuenum` and `valueuom`. Reject fixed impossible ranges: HR 20-250 bpm, MAP 20-200 mmHg, RR 2-80/min, SpO2 50-100%, temperature 30-43 C, O2 flow 0-100 L/min, and PEEP 0-40 cmH2O. For FiO2, divide numeric values above 1.5 by 100, then retain 0.21-1.00.

Within each bin, deduplicate each item at a clinical timestamp by median of valid numeric values. For MAP, use highest-priority available source in order arterial `220052`, non-invasive `220181`, ART `225312`, then median within that source. A valid bin has at least two observed core channels; do not impute an unobserved channel as normal.

Abnormal indicators are fixed: HR <50 or >120; MAP <65; RR <8 or >24; SpO2 <92; temperature <36 or >=38.5 C; FiO2 >0.40; O2 flow >=4 L/min; PEEP >5 cmH2O. Store numeric values, masks, abnormal flags, observed-channel count. Define `LII_i = mean_b(abnormal_core_count_b / observed_core_count_b)` over valid bins and require at least four valid bins. Define Q25/Q75 from LII in the analytic cohort without outcome labels and report continuous per-IQR association. A secondary sensitivity adds pressor/inotrope presence and overlap minutes, grouped by presence/duration rather than incomparable dose.

For clipped `inputevents`, use the listed vasoactive IDs. If interval timing is missing or reversed, do not impute duration; count malformed intervals and use only a separate presence flag when clinical timing is usable. Support text/device items are not required for primary LII.

## Decision baseline, incremental analysis, and matched alternatives

The clinical decision target is a prognostic triage signal available at `t0`:  whether a patient falls into a predeclared risk stratum for enhanced post-ICU review/monitoring. Because MIMIC does not record a standardized review policy, the study estimates predictive information and threshold operating characteristics, not clinical utility or policy effect.

Define the latest-state/context baseline B0 using only:

- `C`: anchor_age, gender, anchor_year_group, first_careunit, last_careunit, ICU duration at `t0`, admission_type, admission_location, earlier ICU-stay count in the same admission, valid-bin count, and core-channel observation coverage; and
- terminal-bin numeric/mask values and terminal observed-channel count for the eight core channels, with the same prespecified preprocessing.

The augmented feature representation B1 adds the full pre-outtime history through exactly the order-blind summaries below. It does not add any source table, field, note, label, or post-outtime information.

Both B0 and B1 are penalized logistic models with the same training/validation/test split, preprocessing rules, and endpoint. B1 is the primary incremental comparator. Report test-set `Delta_Brier_history = Brier(B1)-Brier(B0)`, paired calibration differences, AUROC/AUPRC as secondary, and risk-stratum sensitivity/specificity/PPV/NPV at thresholds fixed before test outcomes are viewed. The threshold grid is determined from training/validation predictions and a clinically interpretable absolute-risk grid (for example 2%, 5%, 10%); no threshold is selected from test outcomes. These are operating characteristics, not net benefit.

The matched representation alternative compares B1 with a small masked GRU. B1 uses context, terminal state, and order-blind summaries of the twelve hourly vectors: per-channel mean/median/minimum/maximum/SD/IQR, abnormal-bin fraction, valid-bin count, observation count/missingness, pressor presence, and clipped pressor minutes. It excludes slopes, reversal counts, bin IDs, sequence positions, future fields, labels, discharge location, and note text.

The GRU uses twelve chronological hourly vectors containing the eight numeric channels, masks, fixed abnormal flags, observed-channel count, and clipped pressor presence/minutes, with the identical C concatenated to the final hidden representation. Numeric scaling, imputation constants, categorical encodings, hidden size (32 or 64), dropout, early stopping, and calibration choices are fit using training/validation only; use five fixed random seeds and one sigmoid head for Y48. No model is causal.

The GRU can reveal persistence, abrupt recovery, fluctuation, and coupled channel order when patients have similar aggregate summaries. B1 establishes whether any added information beyond terminal state/context is reproducible without relying on chronology. The GRU is scientifically useful only if it adds validated, calibrated information beyond B0/B1; a tiny ranking gain or neural complexity is not a clinical advance. A last-bin-only descriptive comparator and a mask-only GRU diagnose terminal state and documentation-process signals.

The secondary chronology estimand is `Delta_Brier_order = Brier(GRU)-Brier(B1)` on untouched test patients. Shuffle bins 1-11 within each test patient while holding terminal bin 12, measurements, masks, C, and endpoint fixed. A genuine order-dependent gain should attenuate. Shuffle all twelve bins as a secondary check and report the changed terminal state. If mask-only performance matches the full GRU, documentation intensity rather than physiology is a likely signal. A fixed within-split outcome-label permutation is a computational null; discrimination and calibration improvement should collapse to chance. Export assertions that every feature clinical time is <= t0 and no prohibited endpoint/disposition field appears in either matrix.

## Split, uncertainty, decision rules, and falsification

Before fitting, freeze one deterministic approximately stratified 70/15/15 subject split. All preprocessing, scaling, regularization, vocabulary, hyperparameters, calibration maps, and operating thresholds use training/validation only. LII quartiles are outcome-free and their calculation is logged. No test outcome chooses features, thresholds, or model settings.

For B0, B1, LII association, and the GRU, report 1,000 subject-level bootstrap replicates when within the declared execution cap; otherwise report the exact completed number and label the interval execution-limited. Report paired test predictions, calibration intercept/slope, Brier, AUROC, AUPRC, and 95% subject-bootstrap intervals. Preserve seeds/checkpoints and successful replicate counts.

Supportive incremental evidence requires Delta_Brier_history < 0 with a 95% interval excluding zero, no important calibration degradation, and consistent direction in the 7-day/coverage sensitivity analyses. The 2-percentage-point risk-difference rule applies only to the separate LII Q75-versus-Q25 descriptive association, not to a claim of utility. Supportive chronology evidence additionally requires Delta_Brier_order < 0 with interval excluding zero and attenuation under preterminal shuffle. These rules do not establish net benefit or a policy effect.

Adverse evidence is no history increment, reversed/unstable incremental performance, calibration worsening, disappearance after coverage adjustment, a gain confined to care-unit/documentation strata, a shuffle-resistant gain, or mask-only performance matching the full GRU. These support the narrower conclusion that terminal state, charting intensity, or treatment selection—not recoverable temporal physiology—accounts for the apparent signal.

Inconclusive evidence includes low event counts, inadequate coverage, many untimed-death unknowns, malformed intervals, unstable calibration, failed unit validation, or discordant endpoints/sensitivities. Inconclusive is neither confirmation nor refutation.

## Alternative scientific methods and deferred questions

The substantive simple baseline is B0 penalized logistic regression: latest state plus fixed context, transparent, CPU-feasible, and directly aligned with the proposed decision target. B1 is the matched learned/representation alternative: it asks whether the entire pre-discharge history contains reproducible information beyond B0; the GRU further asks whether chronological order contains information beyond aggregate history. These are not complexity contests.

The exact inputs, target, split, uncertainty evaluation, and resource plan are the definitions above: same Y48, same subject split, training/validation fitting only, paired held-out predictions, calibration/Brier/AUROC/AUPRC, and 1,000 subject bootstraps. Compact logistic fits and feature aggregation are expected CPU-feasible. The GRU is expected to fit on CPU for the bounded sequence, with one allocated A100-SXM4-80GB optional if measured profiling shows repeated fits exceed the solver envelope; ordinary shell CUDA visibility is not evidence of absent GPUs. The future solver envelope is 16 CPUs, 262,144 MiB, up to 8 GPUs, and 28,800 seconds. Full ZIP extraction and bootstrap duration remain unmeasured. Discovery used only read-only schema/header/dictionary checks; no scientific fit or outcome result is claimed.
