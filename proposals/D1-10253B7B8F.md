# Identifiable observation-aware hepatic reserve after first TACE: episode 4 HCC child

## Scientific question and falsifiable hypothesis

For adults with hepatocellular carcinoma who undergo a first recorded transarterial chemoembolization (TACE), does an early within-person change in routine liver-function assays from before TACE to days +1 through +7 provide prognostic information for a new coded hepatic-decompensation encounter on days +8 through +90 beyond pre-TACE current state, history, and the observation process that generated the tests?

The primary falsifiable hypothesis is that an adverse multi-assay trajectory—lower albumin and platelets and/or higher total bilirubin and INR—has an adverse association with a new coded decompensation encounter and improves held-out proper scoring over a current-state/history baseline, after stabilized adjustment for assay availability and 90-day follow-up. The association must remain directionally similar when post-TACE testing frequency and timing are removed from the outcome model. This is a prognostic hypothesis for a coded endpoint. It is not a claim that TACE causes decompensation, that a diagnosis label is adjudicated liver failure, that missing follow-up means no event, or that a model defines a treatment threshold.

The episode-4 child makes one substantive repair to the episode-3 parent: it replaces an unconstrained joint latent-state/observation model with an anchored, low-dimensional assay-specific random-effects trajectory model and separately estimated observation/follow-up weights. This is required because the source audit found sparse outcomes and incomplete trajectories: the exact cohort had 59 coded events overall, 21 events among patients with at least one primary assay in both windows and observable 90-day status, and 12 events under all-four-assay coverage. The repair preserves the question while making the latent trajectory estimand auditable and explicitly allowing a bounded no-go result.

## Established evidence, unresolved claim, and clinical advance

The available evidence supports the clinical plausibility that hepatic reserve and its deterioration matter after liver-directed therapy, and it supports the mechanical availability of dated TACE procedures, encounter dates, repeated laboratory results, diagnosis labels, and follow-up encounters in this snapshot. It does not establish that the local diagnosis labels are adjudicated decompensation, that death is completely captured, or that a post-TACE laboratory change is independent of selective testing.

The unresolved claim is narrower: under a prespecified observation and follow-up assumption, does a reproducible early within-person trajectory add prognostic information beyond pre-TACE current state and history? The substantive advance is a transparent separation of current state, anchored multi-assay change, and care-process observation. A supportive result would justify only incremental prognostic information for this coded outcome; it would not establish causal injury, biological reserve as a validated construct, or clinical utility.

## Baseline and substantive alternative

### Simple current-state baseline

Fit two nested penalized rare-event logistic baselines:

- B0: pre-window assay medians, age, sex, prior encounter count, prior coded liver-disease/decompensation history, diagnosis-type stratum, and pre-index medication/treatment context.
- B1, the primary comparator: B0 plus post-window assay availability indicators, counts, and median timing. B1 is the current-state/history model with care-process summaries held constant; the primary comparison is alternative versus B1.

Pre-window medians and missingness categories are computed separately by exact assay. Imputation, scaling, regularization and rare-event correction are estimated in train/tune only. B0 describes the total value of post-window information; B1 tests whether value is trajectory rather than simply being tested.

### Identifiable learned/mechanistic alternative

Fit an assay-specific, one-factor random-effects reserve model to the same patients and windows:

- robust-standardize each exact assay with training-fold median and IQR;
- model irregular observations at actual 检验时间 with assay-specific intercepts and residual variances;
- estimate a patient reserve intercept and post-TACE reserve change/slope, with separate assay-specific loadings;
- anchor the albumin loading to +1 and constrain bilirubin and INR loadings to the adverse direction and platelet loading to the opposite direction, fixing factor scale/sign and encoding only the stated direction;
- retain assay-specific measurement error, posterior/empirical-Bayes reserve-change uncertainty, residuals, and discordant assay recovery.

The alternative outcome model uses fitted reserve change and uncertainty plus exactly the B1 covariates. Use Firth logistic or a weakly regularized Bayesian rare-event logistic fit, selected before test evaluation. The alternative is scientifically useful because it estimates whether discordant, irregularly measured multi-assay recovery adds information beyond current state while quantifying trajectory uncertainty. A black-box sequence model would not resolve the observation/identifiability question and is deferred.

Fit separate stabilized inverse-probability models for: (1) assay availability in each window, using pre-index assay history/count/timing, prior encounter history, age/sex, index department, and treatment context; and (2) 90-day follow-up, using only information known by T0 and the same index covariates. A sensitivity weight model may add observed post-index history only up to each subsequent assay decision. No weight model may use a future diagnosis, future follow-up anchor, or latent outcome. Truncate weights at prespecified training-fold percentiles and report effective sample size and positivity. This is a conditional observation assumption, not proof that unmeasured care drivers are absent. A pattern-mixture sensitivity varies event odds among unknown-follow-up patients over a prespecified multiplier; unstable weights or failed positivity make the weighted result inconclusive.

The parent’s fully joint observation/latent model is deferred because its latent scale, assay loadings, observation mechanism, and outcome association are not reliably identified by the sparse event and window support documented in the source audit.

## Evaluation, uncertainty, and falsification

Use deterministic patient-level 60/20/20 train/tune/test folds within discovery buckets. Fit preprocessing, weights, priors, loading constraints, regularization and model selection using train/tune only. Evaluate B0, B1, the unweighted alternative, and the weighted alternative on identical untouched test patients.

Primary estimands are: adjusted odds ratio and patient-bootstrap 95% interval for an adverse IQR-equivalent reserve change; paired test-set log-loss and Brier-score change for alternative versus B1; and calibration intercept/slope and reliability. AUROC/AUPRC are descriptive because events are rare. Use 200 patient-level bootstrap replicates, preserving all rows for a patient. Report weighted/unweighted estimates, effective sample size, and calibration uncertainty. Decision-curve net benefit at 5%, 10%, and 20% is exploratory and not a treatment threshold.

A confirmatory interpretation requires at least 30 primary events overall and at least 8 test-set events, positive/negative support in diagnosis-type strata used for a claim, stable weights with no severe positivity failure, and successful reserve-recovery/identifiability diagnostics on simulated data generated from the fitted training model. If these conditions are not met—as the source audit makes plausible—the result is a bounded feasibility/inconclusive report, not a null biological conclusion.

Falsification and sensitivity checks are prespecified:

- a process-only model using observation counts/timing but no lab values; comparable gain indicates care-process signal;
- removal of all post-window count/timing features; disappearance of gain indicates measurement intensity rather than trajectory;
- within-fold permutation of post-window lab patient assignments while preserving assay identity, window, and count; retained gain indicates leakage or split failure;
- strict all-four-assay coverage versus primary any-primary-assay coverage;
- unweighted versus stabilized-weighted analysis and pattern-mixture range for unknown follow-up;
- diagnosis-type strata and admission-time versus discharge-time event proxies;
- reserve sign/scale and parameter-recovery checks on simulated irregular observations with the observed assay/window pattern.

Supportive results require adverse-direction association, useful uncertainty, improvement in both proper scores over B1 without material calibration harm, no comparable process-only gain, and consistent direction across ascertainment sensitivities. This supports only incremental prognostic information for a coded decompensation endpoint under the stated observation assumption.

Adverse results are a precise null/reversal, no proper-score improvement, worse calibration, a gain eliminated by process adjustment, a gain reproduced by the process-only model, or a gain that fails the time-permutation control. This falsifies the incremental claim in this HCC snapshot, not the biological importance of liver reserve.

Inconclusive results include fewer than event thresholds, sparse assay windows, unstable weights, failed parameter recovery, materially discordant diagnosis-type strata, or intervals too wide to distinguish meaningful improvement. No monitoring or treatment recommendation follows.

## Deliverable, compute, and evidence limits

Completion requires newly fitted B0/B1 and anchored reserve models, cohort flow and exact counts, fold hashes, transformed assay summaries, fitted loadings and reserve parameters, observation/follow-up weight diagnostics, simulation-based recovery diagnostics, held-out predictions, proper-score/calibration estimates with patient-bootstrap intervals, and every falsification output. Reference execution establishes computational feasibility only; it cannot establish clinical truth.

Use streaming extraction over read-only CSVs, 2 CPU cores, up to 8 GiB memory, and at most approximately 2 hours of science compute. Extraction and B0/B1 should remain under 10 minutes after parsing; the anchored random-effects and weight models target 30--120 CPU minutes. No GPU is required. If the alternative exceeds this limit, preserve the diagnostic and report the comparison inconclusive rather than changing the estimand.

The full HCC source audit and method-selection rationale are recorded in work/episode4_hcc_repair_support.md. The earlier demonstrations were used only as bounded methodological context: dated structured histories motivate trajectory comparison, and longitudinal latent models motivate reserve representation. This local HCC experiment is not a reproduction; images, waveforms, validated staging/response, timestamped diagnoses, complete death capture, and external validation are unavailable. Expert adjudication of coded decompensation, death linkage, and an independent HCC cohort would be required for stronger clinical conclusions.
