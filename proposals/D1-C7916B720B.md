# Treatment-stream corroboration of a documented respiratoryCare start

Status: delivery-ready design repair of the Episode 15 child of [prior hypothesis]. This is not a completed analysis. No cohort count, fitted coefficient, prediction, metric, interval, or clinical outcome is claimed.

## Scientific opening and falsifiable hypothesis

The parent question is whether a pre-landmark state/process vector predicts a later strict documented respiratoryCare interval start and whether that start is reproduced in respiratoryCharting. The unresolved boundary is whether a third, separately stored eICU treatment stream adds operational corroboration beyond availability, workflow, timing, exit, and semantic artifacts. [K1] supports eICU table linkage and the distinction between archived documentation and continuously monitored bedside information. [K2] supports cross-center evaluation but explicitly bounds semantic harmonization. [K3] supports explicit preprocessing and leakage/audit controls for irregular structured EHR data. None validates the semantics or completeness of these eICU rows.

**Hypothesis.** In the assigned adult first-ICU S6 population with no valid documented active respiratoryCare interval at minute 360, the parent pre-landmark state/process vector will show a coherent hospital-held-out increment for a later strict documented respiratoryCare interval start only when that start is corroborated within a prespecified window by a source-labeled respiratory treatment record in the independent `treatment` table. The increment should persist after treatment/chart availability, ICU-exit, end-of-life, conflict, and source-time controls. If the vector predicts treatment-row availability or generic documentation density but not treatment-concordant starts, or concordance disappears under timing/workflow nulls or is concentrated near exit, the treatment stream adds documentation complexity rather than corroborating operational information.

This is a recorded-documentation estimand. A treatment row is never interpreted as an order, administration, delivered ventilation/oxygen, treatment initiation, indication, benefit, or causal effect.

Time zero is ICU admission and all offsets are ICU-relative.

- Require finite `patientunitstayid`, `hospitalid`, `unitdischargeoffset`, and `unitdischargestatus).

- S6 eligibility is `unitdischargeoffset > 360`; do not require survival to minute 720.
- Predictors use exactly the inclusive interval [0,360]. Follow-up is the strict interval (360,720], truncated at the earlier of minute 720 and recorded `unitdischargeoffset`.
- The risk set is the operational-absence group with no valid documented active respiratoryCare interval at minute 360. This is not clinical absence.
- A respiratoryCare interval is valid when `ventstartoffset` is finite and either `ventendoffset` is finite and strictly greater than start or end is missing and treated as open. It is active at 360 when start <= 360 and end > 360 or open.
- For each stay, assign the first follow-up cause among strict `R_start_doc`, alive ICU exit, and expired ICU exit. An exit at the same offset as an event wins under the inherited tie rule. Do not infer a death time from expired discharge. A stay without an event or exit by administrative end is event-free for this recorded-documentation estimand.

## Endpoints and semantic separation

The notation below is repaired and is binding.

- `R_start_doc`: the parent’s strict, unambiguous documented respiratoryCare interval-start boundary after inherited valid/open/invalid interval rules, exact-duplicate collapse, same-start conflict flags, and start-ambiguity exclusion.
- `T_resp_record_doc`: a treatment row whose raw `treatmentstring` belongs to a frozen source-labeled respiratory-treatment vocabulary.

## Artifact rivals and frozen falsification checks

The leading operational explanation is reproducible cross-table documentation: `C_RT_doc` and independently defined `T_start_doc` remain coherent in held-out hospitals and observable strata after controls.

Rivals have distinct predictions:

- **Availability/selection:** baseline state predicts whether treatment is recorded or an opportunity exists; treatment-any/opportunity models rise while `C_RT_doc` attenuates after stabilized inverse-availability weighting.
- **Shared workflow/interface:** treatment and respiratoryCare are separate tables but may be emitted by one workflow; concordance disappears under within-hospital schedule-preserving treatment-offset permutation or is concentrated in a few hospitals/classes.
- **Backfill/timing:** treatmentoffset may be documentation or scheduled-care time; estimates change materially across +/-10, +/-30, and +/-60 minutes or cluster at discharge.
- **Exit/EOL selection:** concordance is concentrated near ICU exit or after EOL signals and attenuates in non-exit-adjacent or EOL-sensitive analyses.
- **Conflict/semantic artifact:** duplicates, same-offset conflicts, unstable vocabulary, or the broad class alone drive concordance. This archive cannot resolve whether a positive label means an administered treatment, ventilator support, oxygen delivery, or indication.

Freeze and report: treatment-any/respiratory-any/candidate/opportunity outcomes; within-hospital schedule-preserving permutation stratified by respiratory/treatment availability, exit band, and coverage bin; the +/-30-minute window as primary with +/-10- and +/-60-minute sensitivities; shifted windows +60 to +120; treatment-to-respiratoryCare lag; chart clinical-offset versus entry-offset comparison; discharge-adjacent and non-exit-adjacent analyses; EOL baseline and post-EOL-follow-up sensitivity; conflict inclusion/exclusion; broad versus candidate vocabulary; and five-viable-hospital positivity. ## Interpretation and falsification criteria

**Supportive** requires reproducible row audits, outcome-blind vocabulary freeze, at least five viable hospitals with positive opportunity/event support, coherent held-out `C_RT_doc` and `T_start_doc` results for both model families, paired process increment beyond the inherited negligible margin where estimable, and no dominant availability, EOL, exit, timing, conflict, class, chart-only, hospital, or schedule-null explanation. It supports only a reproducible cross-table recorded respiratory transition.

**Adverse** includes `R_start_doc` without treatment concordance; an increment confined to generic availability; disappearance after availability weighting or in treatment-observable strata; exit/EOL clustering; strong window sensitivity; schedule-null persistence failure; one-hospital or one-vocabulary dependence; or a boosting-only gain. If `C_RCT_doc` remains positive but `C_RT_doc` does not, retain the parent chart-concordance interpretation and conclude that treatment adds no corroboration.

**Inconclusive/deferral** includes too few strict starts or hospitals, poor positivity/effective sample size, unstable or contradictory vocabulary/offsets, or uncertainty spanning meaningful and negligible margins. An imprecise null is not refutation. Continue only to blinded critical-care adjudication if the operational claim passes; revise to a documentation/availability study if selection dominates.
