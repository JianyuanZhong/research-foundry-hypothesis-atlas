# Does posterior-thigh MFI track later falls-report burden beyond muscle distribution?

## Question and clinical importance

In UK Biobank participants with visit-2 body MRI and a later visit-3 falls response, does higher visit-2 bilateral posterior-thigh muscle fat infiltration (MFI) associate with a higher *reported falls category* over the year before visit 3, beyond visceral adiposity, total thigh fat-free volume, posterior/anterior (P/A) muscle-volume ratio, prior falls and measured function? The prespecified primary hypothesis is a positive conditional association per 1-SD higher posterior MFI with any fall report; the additional, strictly secondary question is whether the association is compatible with a shift from no falls to one and then more than one reported fall.

This matters because falls can cause injury, fracture, disability and loss of independence. If muscle composition carries a distinct prospective signal beyond volume distribution and measured baseline frailty proxies, it would justify external validation of MRI muscle-quality phenotyping; a precise adverse result would lower its priority. This dataset cannot establish individual clinical utility, a screening threshold, or benefit from treatment.

**Evidence already supported:** K1 reports broad UK Biobank thigh-MFI associations with mortality and incident disease but its abstract does not establish a falls-specific prospective association. K2 directly reports cross-sectional UKB P/A thigh muscle ratio associations with falls; hence a general thigh-MRI/falls novelty claim is not warranted. K3 summarizes unresolved aging-versus-disease/disuse explanations and heterogeneous imaging. These works support relevance and limit interpretation, not the proposal hypothesis. The dataset’s raw header and schema contain the required fields. Earlier campaign work reports a date-ordered overlap screen but that is not a category count, complete-case denominator, clinical adjudication, or fitted result. No model result is claimed here.

**Strongest rival:** MFI may proxy age, adiposity, illness, disuse, prior falls or general frailty, while return for imaging/follow-up and differential recall/reporting shape the observed association. The 366-day lag only separates the recalled year from the index visit; adjustment cannot remove unmeasured confounding, selection or report error. The experiment tests a conditional association in selected respondents, not causality or a biological mechanism.

## Methods and scientific comparison

The primary baseline is covariate-adjusted logistic regression for `any fall (2296-3.0 codes 2/3) vs none (code 1)`, with the inherited linear MFI term, fixed covariates, and same complete-case population. Report the conditional OR per 1-SD MFI and 95% confidence interval. The matched substantive alternative is the inherited shrinkage-GAM on the exact same participants, endpoint, exposure scale, covariates and comparator. It asks whether a nonlinear MFI shape materially changes the conditional association; a curve is descriptive and cannot identify a mechanism.

For the secondary burden question, fit a cumulative-logit proportional-odds model on the identical cohort and adjustment, with response `1 < 2 < 3`; estimate common MFI log-odds slope/OR, 95% interval, both cumulative splits, counts, convergence and proportional-odds diagnostics. Before model fitting require at least 100 observations in each response category and at least 10 observations in the smallest category per independently estimated slope/parameter (define the parameter count from the actual expanded design matrix). These are stability gates, not power guarantees. Assess proportional odds using a global test at p<0.01 plus threshold-specific MFI slopes; regard it as materially violated if signs oppose or absolute slope difference exceeds `log(1.25)` per 1-SD MFI. Report both criteria and both threshold slopes regardless. If both violation conditions hold, do not interpret a common ordinal slope; use the prespecified multinomial fallback only if each category passes the same support gate and the model is stable. Use no-fall as reference and report separate one-fall-versus-none and >1-versus-none effects with intervals. Never merge categories or switch the primary endpoint post hoc. Otherwise report category counts and defer inference as inconclusive.

Use bootstrap uncertainty (1,000 participant resamples, same model specification and category definition) for GAM/ordinal contrasts where specified; report successful replicate count and failed fits. A wide interval, sparse category, failed codebook check, material nonconvergence or failed bootstrap is inconclusive, not evidence of no association. Report exact model formula/design matrix rank and all exclusions. The ordinal analysis is worth the limited extra GLM work because it reveals whether the primary any-fall signal is compatible with a shift at both thresholds or driven by one-vs-none reports; binary logistic alone erases that distinction. Multinomial modeling is only an assumption-relaxing fallback and loses precision. No neural/sequence model, GPU or recurrent-event model adds defensible information here: there is no validated event-time record, adequate independent validation set, or deployment prediction claim. Prediction metrics would not answer the conditional-association or reporting-burden question.

## Interpretation and falsification

- Supportive for the *primary association*: positive MFI estimate with interval excluding OR=1, stable direction/precision with the P/A ratio and measured baseline frailty covariates, and no evidence that the estimate is driven by key coding, join, selection or measurement artifacts. This remains respondent-conditional observational evidence.
- Supportive for the *secondary ordered-report pattern*: positive common ordinal slope with interval excluding OR=1, adequate category support, stable diagnostics, and directionally compatible threshold-specific slopes without the prespecified proportional-odds violation. If stable multinomial results alone show >1-versus-none association, describe only that category contrast, not an ordinal gradient or recurrence process.
- Adverse: a precise near-null/negative primary estimate or opposing/threshold-specific secondary pattern weakens the simple positive MFI association/burden account. Because no expert-approved minimally important OR margin is available, do not claim equivalence or absence solely because p>0.05. A signal that disappears after P/A distribution/frailty adjustment would favor the rival explanation that MFI adds little beyond measured muscle distribution or frailty proxies.
- Inconclusive: inadequate event/category support, uncertain code labels, wide intervals, model instability, inconsistent fits, or a selection/missingness pattern too severe to interpret. Do not convert imprecision into falsification.

An adjusted association does not prove that myosteatosis causes falls, that changing MFI changes risk, or that the report categories equal true events. Stronger claims require independently collected prospective fall counts/dates and clinical validation/adjudication, external replication, better measures of fall opportunity and frailty, and expert review; clinical actionability requires validated prediction and intervention/utility studies.
