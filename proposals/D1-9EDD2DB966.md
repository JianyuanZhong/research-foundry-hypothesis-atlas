# Relative Glycemic Elevation (Stress Hyperglycemia Ratio) Independently Predicts ICU Mortality Beyond Absolute Glucose in MIMIC-IV

## Scientific Question

In ICU patients with both an acute glucose measurement and a prior HbA1c measurement, does the STRESS HYPERGLYCEMIA RATIO (SHR = acute glucose / HbA1c-derived estimated average glucose) carry independent prognostic information for ICU mortality and in-hospital mortality beyond absolute glucose level, after adjusting for illness severity and comorbidities?

The key unresolved claim: existing SHR studies have evaluated single baseline values in specific populations (ASCVD, heart failure, AMI). The general ICU population—where stress hyperglycemia is most common and most consequential—has not been systematically characterized for the independent prognostic value of SHR beyond absolute glucose. Furthermore, the non-linear dose-response relationship between SHR and mortality in the general ICU population remains unknown.

## Clinical Importance

- Stress hyperglycemia (glucose >180 mg/dL) affects 20-50% of ICU patients and is associated with increased mortality, infection, and prolonged stay.
- Current clinical practice uses absolute glucose thresholds (e.g., >180 mg/dL) to define stress hyperglycemia and trigger insulin therapy.
- However, a glucose of 200 mg/dL in a patient with chronic diabetes (HbA1c 9%, eAG ~212 mg/dL) represents a DIFFERENT physiological state than 200 mg/dL in a patient without diabetes (HbA1c 5%, eAG ~97 mg/dL).
- The SHR captures the acute glycemic deviation from the patient's chronic baseline, potentially identifying patients with the most severe relative stress response.
- If SHR is independently prognostic beyond absolute glucose, it suggests that the magnitude of acute glycemic perturbation (not just the absolute level) drives harm, with implications for personalized glycemic management thresholds.
- The meta-analysis by Wang et al. 2026 [K3] found pooled OR 2.14 (95% CI 1.74-2.55) for SHR and in-hospital mortality in AMI, but this was limited to AMI patients.

## Evidence Base

- Wu & Wang 2026 [K1]: In MIMIC-IV ASCVD patients, early SHR trajectories (persistently high vs moderate-stable) were independently associated with 28-day mortality (HR 1.37, 95% CI 1.15-1.64), validated in NWICU (HR 2.04, 95% CI 1.39-3.01). This established that dynamic SHR patterns carry prognostic information in a specific cardiovascular population.
- Chen et al. 2026 [K2]: In critically ill heart failure patients (MIMIC-III/IV + NWICU, n=2,488), time-varying SHR was independently associated with 28-day mortality (MIMIC-IV: HR 1.10 per 0.1-unit increase, 95% CI 1.07-1.13) and in-hospital mortality. J-shaped dose-response curves were observed. HGI-stratified analyses suggested stronger associations in patients with low glycation index.
- Wang et al. 2026 [K3]: Dose-response meta-analysis of 11 studies (n=27,343 AMI patients) found pooled OR 2.14 (95% CI 1.74-2.55) for SHR and in-hospital mortality, with non-linear dose-response. This is the strongest quantitative evidence but limited to AMI.

## Unresolved Claim

The general ICU population (not restricted to ASCVD, heart failure, or AMI) has not been characterized for:
1. The independent prognostic value of SHR beyond absolute glucose after full adjustment
2. The dose-response relationship (linear vs J-shaped vs threshold)
3. The incremental predictive value of SHR over standard severity scores

## Data Bindings (MIMIC-IV v3.1)

### Exposure
- **Glucose**: `hosp/labevents`, itemid 50809 (Glucose, Blood), 50931 (Glucose, Blood), 51478 (Glucose, Blood), 51981 (Glucose, Blood), 52569 (Glucose, Blood)
  - Index glucose: first glucose measurement in ICU stay (within first 24h of ICU admission)
  - Units: mg/dL
- **HbA1c**: `hosp/labevents`, itemid 50852 (% Hemoglobin A1c)
  - Must be measured BEFORE the index glucose (prior admission or early in current admission)
  - Units: %
  - eAG = 28.7 × HbA1c - 46.7 (ADA 2009 formula)
  - SHR = index_glucose / eAG
- **SHR validity**: eAG must be > 50 mg/dL (HbA1c > 3.5%) and < 400 mg/dL (HbA1c < 15.5%)

### Outcomes
- **ICU mortality**: `icu/icustays`, dod (date of death) within ICU stay
- **In-hospital mortality**: `hosp/admissions`, discharge_time < death_time (or patients.death_datetime)
- **ICU LOS**: `icu/icustays`, icu_los (hours)
- **Hospital LOS**: `hosp/admissions`, discharge_time - admit_time

### Covariates
- **Age**: `hosp/patients`, anchor_age

- **Charlson comorbidity**: computed from `hosp/diagnoses_icd`

### Population
- ICU patients with ≥1 glucose measurement AND ≥1 HbA1c measurement in the same admission
- HbA1c measured BEFORE the index glucose (temporal requirement)
- Age ≥ 18
- ICU stay ≥ 24 hours
- Exclude: HbA1c > 12% (extreme chronic hyperglycemia, eAG > 298 mg/dL), glucose < 40 mg/dL (severe hypoglycemia, likely artifact)

## Analysis Plan

### Primary Analysis
1. **Cox proportional hazards model** for ICU mortality:
   - Model 1 (baseline): SHR (continuous, per 0.5-unit increase) + age + sex
   - Model 2 (adjusted): Model 1 + SOFA proxy + mechanical ventilation + vasopressors + diabetes history + Charlson + lactate + creatinine
   - Model 3 (incremental): Model 2 + absolute glucose (to test independent value of SHR beyond absolute glucose)
   - Primary estimate: HR for SHR per 0.5-unit increase in Model 3

2. **Logistic regression** for in-hospital mortality (same covariate hierarchy)

3. **Dose-response**: Restricted cubic splines (3-5 knots) for SHR vs mortality; test for non-linearity

4. **Incremental prediction**: Time-dependent AUC for Model 2 vs Model 3; NRI and IDI

### Sensitivity Analyses
1. Exclude patients with known diabetes (ICD-10 E10-E14)
2. Exclude HbA1c > 10%
3. Restrict to non-diabetic patients only
4. Use maximum glucose in first 24h instead of first glucose
5. Use SHR from first 12h window instead of single index
6. Exclude patients with ICU stay < 48h
7. E-value for unmeasured confounding
8. Restrict to patients with ≥2 glucose measurements (measurement reliability)

### Falsification Criteria
- **Supportive**: HR for SHR per 0.5-unit increase > 1.25 in Model 3 (adjusted + absolute glucose), 95% CI excluding 1.0
- **Adverse**: HR < 0.8 (suggests relative elevation is protective or irrelevant)
- **Inconclusive**: HR 0.8-1.25 (no independent prognostic value beyond absolute glucose)
- **Non-linearity**: Significant J-shaped or threshold effect in RCS analysis

### Method Comparison
- **Baseline approach**: Absolute glucose quintiles (standard clinical practice)
- **Alternative approach**: SHR quintiles with absolute glucose as covariate
- **Scientific question answered by comparison**: Does the relative metric (SHR) capture prognostic information that the absolute metric (glucose) misses? If SHR quintiles show stronger or non-linear gradients compared to glucose quintiles, this supports the hypothesis that relative elevation is the biologically relevant signal.
- **What the alternative reveals**: The shape of the dose-response (linear vs J-shaped), the threshold at which risk increases, and whether the association persists after conditioning on absolute glucose level.

## Limitations

1. **HbA1c selectivity**: HbA1c is not measured in all ICU patients; those with HbA1c may differ systematically (more likely to have known diabetes, more stable chronic conditions)
2. **Anemia/transfusion effects**: HbA1c is affected by red cell turnover; recent transfusion or hemolysis can bias eAG
3. **Temporal uncertainty**: HbA1c reflects ~90-day average; if measured during the same admission, it may already reflect acute illness
4. **Single center**: MIMIC-IV is a single academic medical center (Beth Israel Deaconess)
5. **Confounding by indication**: Patients with higher glucose may be sicker; adjustment for SOFA and vasopressors is incomplete
6. **SHR formula**: The ADA eAG formula was derived in non-critically ill populations; its validity in ICU patients with altered red cell turnover is uncertain
7. **Not causal**: This is an observational association; SHR is a marker, not a treatment

## What Results Would Mean

- **Supportive (HR > 1.25)**: Relative glycemic elevation is an independent prognostic marker in the general ICU population. This would support personalized glycemic management thresholds (e.g., targeting glucose < 1.5 × eAG rather than a fixed 180 mg/dL threshold). Would warrant prospective validation and potentially a trial of SHR-guided insulin therapy.
- **Adverse (HR < 0.8)**: Relative elevation is not harmful; absolute glucose is the relevant signal. This would support current practice of fixed glucose thresholds.
- **Inconclusive (HR 0.8-1.25)**: SHR adds no independent prognostic information beyond absolute glucose in the general ICU population. This would suggest that the SHR association seen in specific populations (AMI, heart failure) is disease-specific rather than a general ICU phenomenon.
