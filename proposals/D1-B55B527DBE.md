# Is laboratory-process dependence specific, or a marker of general ICU surveillance?

## Scientific deliverable and substantive advance

The future solver must newly construct an auditable, leakage-safe six-hour eICU stay ledger and estimate whether laboratory-observation behavior changes a fixed 10% expired-unit-exit escalation decision after accounting for a separate, non-laboratory monitoring process. It must fit matched transparent nested models and a matched temporal latent-state model in which the same process information is either restricted to the observation channel or also allowed to enter the endpoint hazard.

The substantive advance over the assigned parent is a channel-specific decomposition. The parent establishes a process-versus-clinical-value decision comparison and threshold-crossing utility, but its process block can still represent a broad surveillance regime. This successor asks a sharper question:

> Is the decision instability attributable specifically to laboratory observation behavior, or does it disappear once general physiologic-monitoring intensity is represented?

Completion requires:

1. a frozen cohort, source/header/schema/unit audit, duplicate and offset audit, exact clinical, laboratory-process and vital-process feature ledgers, and whole-hospital split audit;
2. out-of-fold development and frozen held-out-hospital predictions for B0, BV, BL and BLV, where V is vital-observation process and L is laboratory-observation process;
3. the parent anchor contrast BL versus B0 and the new conditional laboratory contrast BLV versus BV, each evaluated at a prespecified 10% decision threshold;
4. the proportion, event composition and hospital concentration of threshold crossings for BLV versus BV, with the same algebraic net-benefit contribution used for the parent;
5. a temporal latent-state observation-only model and a matched direct-process-hazard model on the same 12-bin inputs, with the direct-process contrast treated as the mechanistic test rather than as a generic accuracy contest;
6. expired and alive competing-exit hazards and cumulative-incidence functions, calibration, decision curves, hospital-wise effects, hospital-held-out transport and cluster uncertainty;
7. channel-specificity permutations, outcome permutations, revision and vital-deduplication sensitivities, process-only diagnostics, and model-stability gates; and
8. a conclusion labelled supportive, adverse or inconclusive and tied to computed outputs. No fitted result is claimed in this proposal.

The actual scientific deliverable is a newly estimated conditional decision contribution and a mechanistic edge-ablation result:

- how much laboratory-process information changes a 10% decision after general monitoring process is included;
- whether that contribution loses net benefit on held-out hospitals;
- whether the contribution is concentrated in stays crossing the action threshold and in particular hospitals; and
- whether the same signal survives a temporal latent-state model when process features are allowed to explain observation but not directly alter the latent-state hazard.

A small AUROC gain, a complex model, or successful optimization alone does not establish this deliverable.

## Evidence boundary, unresolved claim and clinical importance

The strongest claim supported before fitting is data availability and design feasibility. The frozen eICU snapshot contains ICU-relative laboratory result and revision offsets, ICU-relative periodic vital observation offsets, ICU stay disposition, admission severity fields, hospital identifiers and the joins needed to construct a whole-hospital transport experiment. It does not establish that a laboratory row is an order, a clinician decision, test availability, turnaround time, assay equivalence, or physiologic missingness. It contains no clinical-action utility or adjudicated treatment harm.

The unresolved claim is a noncausal, deployment-relevant claim:

> At ICU minute 360, among adult stays with sufficient follow-up, does laboratory observation behavior add a clinically consequential change to a 10% expired-unit-exit escalation decision beyond measured patient-state summaries and general vital-monitoring intensity, and does that laboratory-specific decision contribution fail to transport across hospitals?

The parent claim that process variables change a decision is not assumed here. The new experiment distinguishes three possibilities:

1. A laboratory-specific process artifact: BLV differs materially from BV, the contrast has adverse or unstable held-out utility, and the temporal direct-process edge remains after the observation-only latent-state model.
2. A general surveillance or documentation artifact: BV accounts for most of the BLV gain and BLV versus BV is null or transportable; laboratory rows are then not uniquely implicated.
3. A summary-state artifact: the transparent contrast is present, but the observation-only temporal latent-state model recovers calibration and transport without a direct-process hazard contribution. Six-statistic summaries then lost temporal patient-state information.

This matters because a process-sensitive escalation model can direct attention to a hospital's testing or monitoring regime rather than to patient state. A channel-specific result could justify excluding laboratory-process features, presenting a site-specific deployment warning, or requiring local recalibration. It cannot establish that testing causes death, that escalation harms patients, or that a care policy should change. Action cost, treatment decisions, clinician intent and prospective outcomes would be required for those claims.

## Population, temporal boundary and endpoint

Use each eICU ICU stay as one observation; never concatenate later ICU stays. Include adults with patient.age >= 18, parsing age > 89 as 90 and retaining an age-censored flag. Require nonmissing patient.unitdischargeoffset > 360. The landmark is ICU minute 360. No predictor may use a row, revision, or derived value whose relevant time is after minute 360.

The primary endpoint is documented expired ICU-unit exit by minute 2880, 48 hours from ICU admission. Set event time to patient.unitdischargeoffset only when patient.unitdischargestatus is Expired. An alive ICU-unit exit before minute 2880 is a competing alive exit. A known-status stay with unitdischargeoffset > 2880 is event-free at the administrative boundary. NULL or other status is unknown-status censoring and is never recoded as alive. Exclude stays exiting at or before minute 360.

The primary binary target is expired exit by 2880 among known-status stays. The temporal models retain the ordered expired/alive competing states. This is documented ICU-unit exit, not exact biological death, post-ICU mortality, treatment failure, or a clinical escalation outcome. The alive-exit analysis is a prespecified endpoint-specificity diagnostic: if a process contrast is similar for alive and expired exits, disposition or length-of-stay documentation is a plausible explanation; this does not make alive exit a negative control or establish causality. Any all-hospital-discharge mortality analysis is separate and cannot replace or pool with the primary endpoint.

### Nested transparent models

Fit regularized logistic models with identical cohort, folds, preprocessing, missingness policy, threshold and outcome:

- B0 = C;
- BV = C + V;
- BL = C + L;
- BLV = C + L + V.

The primary laboratory-specific decision contrast at threshold q=0.10 is:
Delta_L_given_V^g = NB(BLV^g, q) - NB(BV^g, q), for g in development out-of-fold or frozen held-out hospitals.

The parent anchor is Delta_L_total^g = NB(BL^g, q) - NB(B0^g, q). The new transport estimand is:

D_L_given_V = Delta_L_given_V^D - Delta_L_given_V^H.

This is a transport penalty for laboratory-process information conditional on general vital-surveillance process, not a causal effect of ordering a test. Report D_L_given_V alongside the old D_L_total and the vital-only contrast NB(BV)-NB(B0). No threshold is selected or recalibrated in held-out hospitals.

At q=0.10 compare BLV and BV decisions. Report crossing-in, crossing-out, each crossing's expired-event rate, hospital concentration and:

CDeltaNB_L_given_V =
 P(Y=1 and crossing-in) - q/(1-q) P(Y=0 and crossing-in)
 - P(Y=1 and crossing-out) + q/(1-q) P(Y=0 and crossing-out).

Report development and held-out values, patient-weighted and equal-hospital weighted, with per-hospital paired contrasts. This exactly measures the decision utility contribution of adding L conditional on V. Repeat decision curves at 0.05 and 0.20, but q=0.10 remains primary.

## Temporal latent-state alternative and mechanistic estimand

Use the same stays, accepted analytes, vital values, process features, splits and primary endpoint. Bin observations into twelve fixed half-hour bins over 0-360, preserving within-bin time and masks. Use a fixed three-dimensional latent severity state, selected before fitting.

The observation-only model Tobs has:

- latent-state transitions over the 12 bins;
- laboratory and vital value emissions from the latent state;
- laboratory and vital observation/missingness likelihoods conditioned on the latent state and the corresponding process channel; and
- expired/alive cause-specific hazards conditioned on the latent state only.

The direct-process model Tdirect has the identical state, emissions, observation channels, regularization, initializations and hazard heads, but additionally allows the prespecified L and V process summaries to enter the expired/alive hazard heads. Hospital identity is never an individual predictor; a development-only hospital random intercept may quantify training-site observation heterogeneity and is excluded from held-out scoring.

The mechanistic estimand is the direct-process decision contrast:
E_direct^g = NB(Tdirect^g, 0.10) - NB(Tobs^g, 0.10),
with its development-to-held-out transport penalty and BLV-versus-BV-style crossing decomposition. The lab-specific temporal contrast is the additional direct L channel conditional on V, defined by an optional paired Tdirect,L versus Tdirect,V fit only if the primary Tobs/Tdirect identifiability gate passes. If resources or identifiability do not support that fourth fit, report Tobs/Tdirect as the prespecified mechanism gate and do not claim a lab-specific temporal decomposition.

Interpretation is constrained:

- If BLV-BV and Tdirect-Tobs are both positive in development, adverse or unstable on held-out hospitals, and the lab-specific crossing pattern persists after V, this supports process-sensitive deployment dependence.
- If BV-B0 explains most of BLV-B0 and BLV-BV is null or transportable, the evidence points to broad surveillance intensity, not a laboratory-specific artifact.
- If the transparent contrast is present but Tobs improves calibration/transport and Tdirect adds no decision utility, summary models likely lost temporal patient-state information; the process-artifact claim is weakened.
- If Tdirect is unstable, poorly calibrated or non-identifiable, the mechanistic comparison is inconclusive; the transparent result is not rescued by selecting a favorable initialization.

The learned/latent alternative is scientifically necessary here because six-statistic logistic summaries discard the order of observations, the relation between missingness and observed values, and competing exit timing. Tobs can reveal whether a latent severity trajectory explains the apparent process signal through state inference; Tdirect tests the stronger, deployment-relevant claim that process metadata adds endpoint information not represented by that state. The baseline remains necessary because it provides an auditable, exact decision contribution and does not require latent-model assumptions.

## Falsification and robustness

Retain all parent checks: outcome permutation within development hospitals; ordinary within-hospital process permutation; process-only diagnostics that are never called patient-risk models; revision-conservative versus revision-ignored laboratory availability; exact vital-ID deduplication; hard rejection of actual* fields, discharge fields and post-360 rows; five deterministic latent initializations; and explicit action-rate, raw-event-rate, calibration and crossing-count reporting.

Add the following channel-specific checks:

1. Stratified laboratory-process swap. Within each development hospital, swap L vectors across stays within fixed development-frozen quintiles of B0 predicted risk and V intensity, preserving the clinical block, outcomes, hospital and broad monitoring burden. The held-out implementation uses only development-defined bins. The BLV-versus-BV incremental gain and crossing concentration should collapse toward zero. Persistence indicates leakage, a join error, or that the purported L block contains an unrecognized patient-state variable.
2. Cross-channel ablation. Compare the conditional L contrast with and without V. A large BLV-BV attenuation relative to BL-B0 is evidence that general surveillance explains the parent signal; it is not evidence that V is harmless or causal.
3. Endpoint specificity. Repeat the process contrasts for alive ICU exit as the competing cause using the same landmark and folds. A similar process contribution for both causes is flagged as disposition/documentation dependence rather than mortality-specific state evidence.
4. Temporal edge ablation. The direct-process gain must be compared to Tobs with identical initialization seeds and state dimension. A direct-process result that disappears when observation likelihoods are correctly represented is not supportive of a direct process artifact.
5. Temporal order sensitivity. As a diagnostic only, reverse the order of the 12 process bins while keeping values and outcomes fixed. Failure to respond to this temporal falsification, together with a large direct-process gain, suggests a static site or data-density proxy.
6. Outcome permutation and split checks must collapse discrimination and decision utility toward their null; any persistent signal is a leakage or implementation failure.

Support for a laboratory-specific process dependence requires all of the following: development Delta_L_given_V >= 0.005; D_L_given_V >= 0.005 with a two-sided 95% whole-hospital interval excluding zero; an adverse held-out CDeltaNB_L_given_V of at least 0.005 or an interval excluding zero in the adverse direction; lower expired-event rate among crossing-in than crossing-out when both are estimable; reproduction in the temporal direct-process comparison; stability under the second split and revision-conservative rule; and at least 75% of evaluable held-out hospitals showing nonnegative laboratory-process transport loss. The result cannot be supportive with fewer than 10 held-out hospitals or 25 expired exits. These are deployment-warning criteria, not clinical-effect thresholds.

Evidence is adverse to the laboratory-specific hypothesis when BLV-BV has no material gain, no held-out loss, and stable calibration/utility no worse than BV, while the channel-specific permutation and temporal checks behave as expected. If the vital-only contrast is large and the lab-specific conditional contrast is null, label the narrower laboratory hypothesis adverse and the broader surveillance explanation supported only as an observational interpretation. Evidence is inconclusive for sparse endpoints, inadequate overlap, unstable accepted analytes, missing accepted values, nonconvergence, poor latent-state identifiability, model-family discordance, wide intervals crossing both zero and the clinical margin, or material changes under revision or deduplication rules.

## What can and cannot be concluded

Computationally checkable claims are the frozen source hashes and headers, exact joins, ordinary-gzip status, row and ID audit, time filters, duplicate handling, accepted units, feature ledger, split integrity, outcome construction, nested model fits, latent-state fits, predictions, decision crossings, CIFs, bootstrap intervals and permutation results.

Clinical adjudication or another study is required to determine whether lab rows represent orders, clinician intent, local test availability, turnaround, assay/specimen comparability, true physiologic missingness, actionable mortality risk, or escalation harm. The public snapshot lacks treatment indication, administered dose and clinician action, reliable order timestamps, raw waveforms, complete narrative context, post-discharge outcomes, adjudicated biological death timing and action-cost utilities. Expert review, prospective evaluation and an independent database would be needed before changing hospital policy. The outcome is ICU-unit exit, and all claims must use that label.

## Method comparison, selection and compute

The transparent B0/BV/BL/BLV family is selected because it is auditable, shares the exact cohort and split, and makes the channel-specific decision estimand algebraically transparent. Its information loss is temporal order, observation timing and the coupling between missingness and latent severity.

Tobs/Tdirect is selected as the substantive alternative because it tests a different scientific mechanism on the same accepted inputs: whether a latent temporal state and an explicit observation likelihood explain the process signal, and whether allowing process metadata a direct hazard edge adds decision utility. It is not selected for complexity or predictive novelty. A generic boosted tree is retained as a revisit option only if residuals show a prespecified nonlinear interaction; it cannot separate observation from state. A transformer is deferred because the fixed 12-bin, six-hour structured sequence is too small to justify a large representation for this mechanism question. vitalAperiodic is deferred because its measurement stream is not part of the fixed parent design and its support/timing semantics have not been audited.

Verified compute facts are the configured discovery limit of 7200 science seconds, two concurrent jobs, and eight NVIDIA A100-SXM4 80-GB devices; ordinary shells have no allocated CUDA, so GPU absence must not be inferred from a shell probe. The future solver plan is CPU-first: 8 CPUs, 32 GB RAM and up to 2 hours for ledger construction, nested regularized fits, two low-dimensional temporal fits with five initializations, 500 hospital bootstrap replicates and declared checkpoints for ledger and predictions. GPU is not required for the transparent fits; one allocated A100 may be considered for the temporal fits only if the separate solver envelope permits it. Full fitting runtime, memory and latent-state convergence are unverified. A bounded GPU probe is optional and would measure infrastructure feasibility, not scientific truth.
