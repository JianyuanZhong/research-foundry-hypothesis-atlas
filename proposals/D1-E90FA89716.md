# Does early laboratory recording practice lose transportability after measured acuity is accounted for?

Status: Episode-4 substantive child of `[prior hypothesis]`; adaptation of expert seed `[starting question]`, not a reproduction of a demonstration paper.

## Scientific question, evidence boundary, and advance

The unresolved question is whether laboratory documentation patterns add a risk signal that is not portable across hospitals after the same measured early ICU state is available. Among adults who are still at risk and observable at six ICU hours, does adding laboratory observation/missingness/timing information produce a larger development-site-to-new-hospital performance loss than a values-only model, and does that contrast persist after adjustment for measured acuity and general monitoring intensity?

This matters because a model used to prioritize escalation, monitoring, transfer, or scarce ICU resources may appear accurate at its development hospitals while relying on local test-ordering, interface, or documentation practice. The substantive advance is a paired, hospital-held-out comparison that separates laboratory values from laboratory process, tests a fixed competing-state outcome, and asks whether process information transports—not merely whether a more complex model predicts better in pooled data. A positive result would identify a portability hazard for a class of early ICU models; it would not identify clinician intent or prove that changing testing practice improves outcomes.

The strongest available evidence before this experiment is narrower: the frozen eICU snapshot contains patient- and hospital-linked ICU stays, ICU-relative laboratory results with revision offsets, and longitudinal vital measurements. A prior feasibility audit of this snapshot reported 140,801 pre-deduplication eligible adult initial stays across 207 hospitals, 2,339,035 early laboratory rows, and at least one eligible early laboratory row in 117,368 stays. Those are source-availability counts, not evidence of mortality prediction, transport failure, workflow causation, or clinical utility. The live source headers and catalog schemas were inspected; no fitted scientific result is claimed.

### Scientific deliverable

Newly fit or estimated outputs must include:

1. A frozen cohort/state manifest, exact canonical laboratory/unit coverage table, revision-safe availability audit, feature manifest, and whole-hospital split assignments.
2. Paired values-only (V) and values-plus-laboratory-process (V+P) predictions for every eligible held-out stay, from both a transparent logistic baseline and a temporal GRU alternative when that branch converges.
3. Internal-versus-new-hospital transport contrasts, hospital-cluster uncertainty intervals, calibration and Brier results, the three-state competing-outcome sensitivity, measured-acuity residualization, and general-monitoring controls.
4. Permutation, timing, revision-leakage, and split-integrity falsification outputs.
5. A conclusion tied to these files, with supportive, adverse, and inconclusive interpretations preserved. A feasibility audit or a model result from an easier replacement cohort does not complete the deliverable.

## Population, time origin, estimand, and outcome

The analysis unit is one eICU `patientunitstayid`. Use all source rows satisfying the rules below; do not sample.

From `patient`, retain records with numeric age >=18, `unitstaytype='admit'`, `unitvisitnumber=1`, `unitdischargestatus` exactly `Alive` or `Expired`, and finite numeric `unitdischargeoffset > 360`. The strict inequality makes the landmark population at risk immediately after minute 360 and ensures that every retained stay has exactly one outcome state. If multiple qualifying stays share a non-missing `uniquepid`, retain the record with the smallest non-missing `hospitaladmitoffset`, breaking ties by smallest `patientunitstayid`; if all such offsets are missing, retain the smallest ID. Missing `uniquepid` cannot be used for patient grouping and must be reported; all non-missing retained `uniquepid` values must have one split role per fit. Deduplicate before any split.

Time zero is ICU-relative minute 360. Predictors may use only observations with clinical/result/observation offset in [0,360]. The primary endpoint is ICU death in the interval (360,3240], represented by `unitdischargestatus='Expired'` and `360 < unitdischargeoffset <= 3240`. The competing endpoint C is alive ICU discharge in (360,3240], represented by `unitdischargestatus='Alive'` and `360 < unitdischargeoffset <= 3240`. R is `unitdischargestatus='Alive'` with a finite discharge offset >3240, meaning alive and still in the ICU at minute 3240. A death or discharge at exactly minute 360 is excluded by the population rule, not silently assigned to R.

The primary clinical estimand is the marginal probability of D within (360,3240] in this landmark population. The primary transport estimand is the paired difference-in-differences in predictive performance for V+P versus V when moving from an internal holdout of development hospitals to entirely held-out hospitals. This is an associational risk/transport estimand: it is not hospital mortality, post-discharge mortality, a causal effect of testing, a treatment effect, or a clinical-utility estimate.

The mandatory outcome sensitivity uses the same cohort, folds, feature blocks, and preprocessing: a three-state D/C/R model, implemented as cause-specific one-versus-rest models and a multinomial model. Report state probabilities, state-specific Brier scores, and hospital-stratified state prevalence/cumulative incidence. If finite discharge offsets or mutually exclusive D/C/R states cannot be constructed, stop the definitive analysis and report prerequisite failure rather than censoring or relabeling outcomes.

## Baseline and substantive alternative before method selection

Both methods target D, compare the same V versus V+P blocks, and use the same hospital-held-out estimand. The method choice is about information and interpretability, not a small predictive leaderboard gain.

### Transparent baseline: paired L2 logistic regression

Use fixed L2 logistic regression with C=1 and a deterministic solver. One-hot encode the specified categorical context; fit numeric median/mode imputation and scaling within each outer training set only. V consists of context (`gender, ethnicity, admissionheight, admissionweight, hospitaladmitsource, unitadmitsource, unittype`), vital summaries, and canonical laboratory value summaries. V+P adds only the predeclared laboratory process features. No hospital identifier is a predictor.

This baseline is auditable and directly estimates the incremental process block. Its limitation is that static summaries lose temporal order, trajectory, recovery/worsening shape, and irregular timing.

### Learned temporal alternative: bounded GRU

Use a one-layer width-64 GRU with dropout 0.1, Adam learning rate 1e-3, batch size 256, weighted binary cross-entropy, at most 30 epochs, and patience 5. Use twelve fixed 30-minute bins over [0,360]. In V, each value channel/bin contains only within-bin mean and last observed value; empty entries are imputed from outer-training medians, with no observation mask, count, timestamp, or time-since-last feature. Thus the V sequence is a learned temporal representation of measured values rather than a disguised measurement-process model. In V+P, add laboratory observation masks, within-bin counts, time-since-latest-result capped at 360, and the fixed early process totals; append context to the final hidden state. The GRU can reveal whether trajectory/order and explicit observation timing contain information that static values lose, while the paired block comparison tests whether that information is transport-unstable.

Use one fixed seed per outer repetition (20260913 through 20260917). Fit V and V+P for both families on the same five repetitions of five whole-hospital folds. All imputation, scaling, residualization, stopping, and threshold choices are isolated to outer training hospitals. The static logistic branch is mandatory. If CPU time or convergence prevents the GRU, preserve checkpoints and completed outputs, label that branch deferred, and do not change the cohort, estimand, or baseline to compensate.

The scientific selection is logistic as the primary interpretable comparison plus GRU as a substantive temporal alternative. A mechanistic or causal workflow model is deferred because the available bindings do not provide reliable order/cancellation semantics, turnaround-time fields, staffing/capacity, treatment intent, or a validated interface-failure label. A GRU is not being used as a complexity bonus, and no GPU is required: the bounded science budget is 7,200 seconds, concurrency 2, with at most one 8-GiB CPU extraction/logistic job; request a GPU only if a bounded GRU diagnostic demonstrates that it is needed. Persist preprocessing objects, checkpoints, manifests, split assignments, predictions, and logs.

## Transport evaluation and uncertainty

Before fitting, report eligible counts by hospital, D/C/R prevalence, canonical coverage, value availability, process distributions, and hospitals meeting support. Retain hospitals with at least 200 eligible stays and at least 10 D events, using one fixed supported-hospital set for every model and block. If fewer than 20 hospitals qualify, report descriptive estimates and prerequisite failure rather than a definitive transport conclusion.

For each repetition, assign supported hospitals to five folds using fixed seeds, approximately balancing eligible stay count and D events. External E is all eligible stays in held-out hospitals. Internal I is a patient-grouped 20% holdout within outer-training hospitals and is only the development-site comparator. Report pooled and hospital-macro metrics separately. Hospitals with one outcome class contribute Brier/calibration but not AUROC.

For each family define:

`T_AUROC = (I_AUROC(V+P)-I_AUROC(V)) - (E_AUROC(V+P)-E_AUROC(V))`.

`T_Brier = (I_Brier(V)-I_Brier(V+P)) - (E_Brier(V)-E_Brier(V+P))`.

Positive T means the laboratory-process increment is less preserved in new hospitals. Report E(V), E(V+P), direct external increments, I values, calibration intercept/slope, Brier, AUROC, AUPRC, fixed-bin calibration, and descriptive decision curves at 5%, 10%, and 20%. Decision curves are descriptive sensitivity analyses only: benefits, harms, actions, and acceptable thresholds are unavailable, so they are not clinical-utility claims.

Use 2,000 hospital-cluster bootstrap resamples of complete hospital prediction sets, preserving V/V+P pairing and all repetitions, for percentile 95% intervals. Directional support requires T>0 with an interval excluding zero. A predeclared non-triviality check is a lower interval bound >0.02 on the relevant AUROC or Brier-contrast scale, reported separately from direction. Robust support requires concordant direction in logistic and GRU, passing integrity checks, and poorer or less stable external calibration/Brier for V+P despite a nonnegative internal process increment. The primary interpretation remains a transport penalty, not a causal process effect. Also report GRU(V)-logistic(V) externally with the same uncertainty; temporal information is supported only by reproducible external calibration/Brier improvement, not a small AUROC change.

## Falsification and integrity criteria

Run these without changing the primary estimand:

- Permute D within hospital and refit. AUROC should approach 0.5 and AUPRC should approach the permuted prevalence. Persistent process-specific signal indicates leakage or split failure.
- Compare the valid revision-safe build with a permissive diagnostic build that ignores `labresultrevisedoffset`. The permissive build is never a scientific result; a material difference triggers leakage investigation.
- Permute each patient's P vector among patients within hospital. Collapse of the process increment toward V supports patient linkage; persistence suggests redundancy or a hospital proxy.
- Shuffle eligible laboratory offsets within stay while preserving values and counts. Timing-sensitive GRU effects should attenuate while static count effects may remain; failure to separate those patterns weakens the temporal interpretation.
- Compare raw P, training-only V-residualized P, V-risk tertiles, and the vital-monitoring control. Shrinkage after controls supports measured acuity/observability as an explanation. Persistence of a lab-specific effect with hospital shifts in P and attenuation under within-hospital rank normalization is consistent with a workflow/documentation proxy, not proof of clinician intent.
- Verify no post-360 or revision-after-360 row, identifier, discharge/APACHE outcome, or outer-test preprocessing object entered a predictor, and that every non-missing `uniquepid` has one split role per fit. A patient-overlapping random split may be reported only as an optimistic diagnostic; an effect present there but absent in hospital holdout is site dependence, not deployment evidence.

Supportive results are positive T with an interval excluding zero, external V+P calibration/Brier worse or less stable than V despite a nonnegative internal process increment, concordance across both families, and all integrity checks passing. If residualized and stratified results persist, the narrow conclusion is that recorded laboratory process contains transport-unstable information beyond measured state; workflow remains a plausible explanation only.

Adverse results are T at or below zero with a sufficiently narrow interval, no internal process increment, or disappearance after acuity/vital-monitoring controls. This weakens or falsifies the hypothesis for this panel and cohort; it does not prove that testing practice is universally transportable or safe.

Inconclusive results include fewer than 20 supported hospitals, a missing canonical combination, inadequate D/C/R support, unstable site metrics, intervals spanning zero and 0.02, disagreement between families, failed leakage or integrity checks, or GRU convergence failure when the temporal claim is being made. Preserve all estimates and failure reasons and do not choose a favorable point estimate.

## Clinical adjudication and limits

Computationally checkable are cohort inclusion, strict landmark boundary, joins, ICU-relative time, revision cutoff, canonical feature construction, split isolation, model fitting, predictions, competing states, metrics, bootstrap intervals, and permutation behavior.

Not established by this experiment are whether a missing laboratory row means no order, an unrecorded order, interface loss, delayed result, or clinical de-escalation; whether residualization removes acuity; whether process-dependent predictions improve triage, escalation, transfer, or bed allocation; whether a threshold has acceptable harms; or whether changing testing policy changes outcomes. These require expert review plus order/cancellation and turnaround data, an interface audit, staffing/bed-capacity context, prospective silent deployment, and independent health-system validation. Hospital ID is not an instrument. This design cannot support a causal testing-policy claim. Removed calendar dates, removed narrative note sections, and absent raw waveforms prevent calendar-drift, text, and waveform conclusions.
