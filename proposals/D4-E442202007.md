## Unresolved question and falsifiable hypothesis

The strongest claim supported by the inspected evidence is narrower: baseline metabolic measurements are associated with later coded cardiometabolic disease, and longitudinal or competing-risk models can represent progression. The local data and references do not establish that an assessment-linked within-person change predicts a subsequent additional disease domain beyond the current panel-1 level, nor whether any such signal reflects coordinated metabolic worsening or a discordant marker pattern. The source contains field-instance values and assessment-date proxies, not verified specimen collection times or adjudicated disease onset.

Primary hypothesis:

> Among UK Biobank participants aged 40–69 years at the first valid assessment-linked panel who have exactly one of diabetes, cardiovascular disease, or chronic kidney disease recorded at both panels, complete paired HbA1c, triglyceride and HDL measures, and remain in that same one-domain state at panel 1, assessment-linked metabolic change adds internal prognostic information for the first subsequent additional cardiometabolic domain beyond panel-1 levels, existing-domain identity, duration and prespecified covariates. Conditional on any change signal, a coordinated deterioration component and a marker-discordance component have different route-specific associations.

This is an internal prognostic hypothesis among observed repeat-assessment participants with complete paired assays. It is not a causal effect of changing a biomarker, a claim about biological slope or clinical onset, a recommendation to repeat testing, a monitoring benefit, a policy threshold, or a result generalizable to non-returners.

The substantive advance is a falsifiable decomposition of dated change into two clinically interpretable explanations under a matched event architecture:

- coordinated worsening asks whether the whole metabolic burden is moving adversely;
- discordance asks whether one marker’s change differs from the common movement, which could identify information lost by a single composite score.

A null result is scientifically useful: it would argue that, in this selected coded-event population, panel-1 current levels are sufficient for the tested prognostic purpose.

## Matched baseline and substantive alternative

All models use the same cohort, competing event architecture, death hazard, time zero, censoring, participant split, preprocessing, weighting, uncertainty procedure and locked evaluation.

B0, the simple baseline, fits route-specific cause-specific Cox hazards for each existing-domain to unique-new-domain transition plus explicit death. Predictors are panel-1 HbA1c, triglyceride, HDL, existing domain, duration, age, sex, gap, BMI `21001-0.0`, SBP `4080-0.0`, DBP `4079-0.0`, smoking `20116-0.0`, and center `54-0.0`, using fit-frozen splines for continuous terms. No d0-to-d1 change enters B0.

Construct fit-partition z-scores for the three worsening-oriented changes:

- z_H = standardized HbA1c change;
- z_T = standardized triglyceride change;
- z_R = standardized reverse-HDL change.

Define the common component C=(z_H+z_T+z_R)/sqrt(3). Define two orthogonal discordance contrasts D1=(z_H−z_T)/sqrt(2) and D2=(z_H+z_T−2z_R)/sqrt(6). Orthogonalization is performed in the fit partition if empirical covariance is nonzero, with the transform frozen before locked evaluation.

The primary alternative M_change fits the identical route-specific hazards and adds C, D1 and D2, with only prespecified C-by-existing-domain and D-by-existing-domain interactions where route support is adequate. This is nested and dimension-matched relative to a model with three unrestricted change dimensions:

- the joint test C,D1,D2 versus B0 tests whether dated change adds information;
- the C-only nested test asks whether coordinated worsening suffices;
- the D1,D2 conditional test asks whether discordance adds information beyond coordinated worsening.

For transparency, fit M_common (B0+C) and M_full (B0+C+D1+D2) on the same split. M_full is the primary change model; M_common is the mechanistic comparator. The transformation makes the scientific comparison explicit: M_common can reveal a coherent burden signal that separate markers obscure, while M_full can reveal marker-specific disagreement that a common score loses. If route support fails for D terms, the discordance claim is inconclusive rather than silently removed.

A two-panel attention/recurrent model is deferred for a scientific reason, not because neural methods or GPUs are disallowed: two observations do not provide meaningful sequence structure, and extra capacity cannot resolve return selection, date-proxy timing or coded-onset uncertainty. A four-instance slope/mixed-effects model is unavailable because verified assay instances 2/3 are absent. Revisit either branch only with at least three verified dated panels or a bounded diagnostic showing stable additional scientific information.
