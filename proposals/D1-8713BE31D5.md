# Discharge-proximal non-recovery after observed TACE in HCC: separating residual laboratory signal from care intensity and coded return

## Version, parent, and substantive change

Successor to `[prior hypothesis]`. This is an observational, incremental-prognostic experiment. It is not a TACE treatment-effect study, a discharge-policy trial, a mortality analysis, or a clinical-utility evaluation.

The validated primary early-trajectory estimand is preserved:

> Among adults with HCC who have a first observed TACE episode and are discharged, does the within-person trajectory in core laboratory values from the 14 days before TACE to the first 72 hours after TACE add reproducible held-out information about a subsequent inpatient admission within 30 days after discharge beyond baseline reserve, prior utilization, demographics, and recorded disease history?

The new, clinically consequential unresolved claim is narrower and discharge-aligned:

> Among the prespecified nested population with at least 96 hours from observed TACE start to discharge and complete core laboratory windows, does the change in core laboratory values from the first 72 hours after TACE to the final 24 hours before discharge add held-out information about a subsequent inpatient admission beyond baseline/early laboratory information and the intensity of recorded testing, ordering, medication activity, and index-stay duration?

The experiment also asks whether that increment is specific to an inpatient admission rather than any later coded encounter, and whether it remains when compared with a non-overlapping same-length late-stay placebo trajectory. A surviving increment is interpreted only as residual prognostic information conditional on measured record-process variables. It is not proof of biologic hepatic non-recovery, treatment-related injury, an unplanned return, or benefit from delayed discharge or extra monitoring.

Substantive changes from the parent:

1. The process block is expanded and made explicit: pre-discharge counts of valid laboratory observations/timestamps, non-drug order activity, medication activity, index department, and TACE-to-discharge duration are fixed covariates in both the no-discharge and discharge models. Laboratory values are not included in this block.
2. A second, stricter specificity cohort requires at least 120 hours. It compares the final 24-hour trajectory with a same-length placebo trajectory from 48 to 24 hours before discharge, with no overlap with the 0–72-hour early window. This directly tests whether the final-day signal is more than a generic late-stay trend.
3. Outcomes are separated before modeling: (a) later encounter of any type, (b) later encounter with a nonmissing admission time, and (c) a later admission carrying a frozen liver-coded label. The diagnosis-coded outcome is never the sole evidence for a clinically meaningful return.
4. Every feature and outcome has an event-time rule, an exact source binding, and a leakage assertion. The proposal corrects the parent’s serialized identifiers: the HCC snapshot is `[source checksum], and all joins use the two-field key `(患者主索引, 就诊号).

## Evidence boundary and clinical importance

The strongest external evidence actually inspected is Sanai et al., *Scientific Reports* 2026, DOI 10.1038/s41598-026-54466-4, PMCID PMC13457603, frozen source ID `[source checksum]`. Its abstract reports a four-center cohort of patients treated with TACE or TARE, defines hepatic decompensation using ascites, variceal bleeding, or portosystemic encephalopathy at baseline or within six months, and reports baseline ALBI, etiology, and intervention type as predictors of post-locoregional-therapy decompensation. This supports the plausibility that hepatic reserve and post-locoregional-treatment complications matter.

That evidence does not establish that a routinely recorded, time-ordered laboratory trajectory in this HCC snapshot adds information beyond baseline values, nor that a discharge-time signal is independent of testing and care intensity. It also does not establish that any such signal identifies patients who benefit from a different discharge decision.

The clinical decision at issue is whether the record at the point of discharge contains reproducible information that should motivate prospective review or monitoring after observed TACE. A result that survives measured process controls and temporal placebos would justify prospective validation with units, treatment intent, adjudicated outcomes, and a specified action pathway. A result explained by process intensity or a generic encounter-return signal would caution against calling routine late laboratory abnormalities “biologic non-recovery.” Neither result alone changes care.

## Laboratory exposure and observation-process construction

Use exact assay labels only:

- core: `总胆红素`, `白蛋白`, `国际标准化比值`;
- expanded sensitivity only: `丙氨酸氨基转移酶`, `门冬氨酸氨基转移酶`, `肌酐`, `血小板计数`.

Parse `定量结果` conservatively as numeric. Retain `定性结果`, `标本类型`, unparseable values, missing timestamps, excluded assay labels, and invalid joins in the audit. A valid lab value must have a parseable `检验时间`, an exact two-key encounter join, and an exact assay label. Use `检验时间`, never `就诊时间`, to assign windows.

For each participant and core assay, calculate medians in these fixed windows, using half-open windows to make boundaries and placebos non-overlapping:

- baseline: [TACE 开始时间 - 14 days, TACE 开始时间);
- early: [TACE 开始时间, TACE 开始时间 + 72 hours);
- discharge: [出院时间 - 24 hours, 出院时间);
- late-stay placebo, only in the 120-hour specificity cohort: [出院时间 - 48 hours, 出院时间 - 24 hours).

The early trajectory is (E_j = median(early_j)-median(baseline_j)). The discharge trajectory is (D_j = median(discharge_j)-median(early_j)). The placebo trajectory is (L_j = median(placebo_j)-median(early_j)). Preserve raw assay-specific differences descriptively; robust-standardize each assay within each training fold using the training median and IQR. Do not pool assays, infer units, impose clinical cutoffs, or compute ALBI. Expected directions (not constraints) are higher bilirubin/INR/ALT/AST/creatinine and lower albumin/platelets as adverse.

The primary early cohort requires index 出院时间 >= TACE 开始时间 + 72 hours and at least one valid value for every core assay in baseline and early windows; this prevents early features from extending beyond discharge. The 96-hour discharge cohort additionally requires TACE-start-to-discharge duration >= 96 hours and at least one valid value for every core assay in the discharge window. The 120-hour specificity cohort additionally requires duration >= 120 hours and at least one valid value for every core assay in the placebo window. Report the number retained and the reason for every exclusion. Do not impute a missing window.

### Process block (P)

The process block contains no laboratory values and no order/medication names. It is measured only before discharge and is included unchanged in both nested models.

For the 96-hour primary discharge cohort, derive, after exact encounter joins:

- for each core assay and for all expanded assays: valid-row count, distinct `检验时间` count, first-to-last valid timestamp span, and maximum gap between sorted valid timestamps in [TACE 开始时间, 出院时间);
- total valid core-lab rows and distinct core-lab timestamps in [TACE 开始时间, 出院时间);
- number of distinct encounter keys represented by those joined lab rows;
- number of `orders` rows with parseable `开立时间` in [TACE 开始时间, 出院时间), plus distinct order-issue timestamps; `医嘱状态`, `开始时间`, and `结束时间` are retained for audit but not used to infer a clinical action;
- number of `medications` rows with parseable `开始时间` and an interval overlapping [TACE 开始时间, 出院时间), plus distinct medication start timestamps; drug names, doses, units, routes, and types are not used;
- TACE-to-discharge duration in hours and index `就诊科室`.

For the 120-hour specificity cohort, use a leakage-clean version of (P) measured only in [TACE 开始时间, 出院时间-48 hours), so neither the late-stay placebo nor final-day discharge window contributes process counts. Counts are process/opportunity markers, not clinical severity, indication, treatment adherence, or causal confounder measurements. Apply log1p to counts and robust-standardize within training folds; preserve missingness indicators for unavailable process fields. Require the same P definition within every compared model.

## Operational outcomes

The primary outcome is (Y_{adm,30}): a later encounter for the same participant with nonmissing `入院时间` strictly after index `出院时间`, `入院时间 <= 出院时间 + 30 calendar days`, and `就诊号` different from the index encounter. Use distinct `就诊号`; select the earliest qualifying admission by `入院时间`. This is an observed subsequent admission, not “readmission,” “unplanned return,” “TACE complication,” or recurrence.

The return-process negative-control outcome is (Y_{any,30}): any later encounter with `就诊时间` strictly after index discharge and no later than discharge plus 30 calendar days, distinct from the index `就诊号`, regardless of whether `入院时间` is present. It may include scheduled outpatient care; therefore it is an administrative encounter-return outcome, not a clinical deterioration endpoint.

The diagnosis-coded secondary outcome is (Y_{liver,30}): the first qualifying (Y_{adm,30}) encounter for which any joined diagnoses row has `诊断名称` containing one of these frozen terms:

- `腹水` or `腹腔积液`;
- `肝性脑病` or `肝昏迷`;
- `食管胃底静脉曲张`;
- `消化道出血`.

If both liver and non-liver labels occur on that first admission, classify it liver-coded and disclose the rule. Otherwise classify the first admission as non-liver-coded. Later diagnoses cannot relabel an earlier first admission. This is a mutually exclusive operational phenotype, not adjudicated hepatic decompensation. Require at least 20 liver-coded events before fitting a liver-coded model; otherwise report counts and timing only.

For event-time resolution, additionally report mutually exclusive first-admission windows 0–7 and 8–30 calendar days after discharge, plus first-admission time in hours. These are descriptive unless the all-cause event gate is met. The 90-day admission outcome uses the same admission-time rule and is secondary only for TACE start no later than `2025-10-03 23:59:59`.

If there is no later observed encounter, report this as no observed event in the available snapshot. It is administrative absence, not proof of no event. A missing mortality table means death is not an observed competing event.

## Estimands and models

Let (B) contain baseline core medians, age, sex, index department, calendar period, and prespecified prior-utilization/history summaries formed only before TACE: counts of prior encounters and prior admissions in the preceding 180 days and 365 days, and counts of prior diagnosis rows after exact encounter-time joins. Do not use post-TACE or post-discharge data in B.

Use four transparent ridge-logistic models for each operational outcome:

- (M_B: B);
- (M_E: B + E), the preserved primary early-trajectory comparison;
- (M_{E+P}: B + E + P), the observation-controlled baseline;
- (M_{E+P+D}: B + E + P + D), the discharge-proximal comparison.

The primary discharge increment is (M_{E+P+D}) versus (M_{E+P}), evaluated on held-out participants. The process increment is (M_{E+P}) versus (M_E). The early increment is (M_E) versus (M_B). In the 120-hour cohort, add (L):

- (M_{E+P+L}: B+E+P+L);
- (M_{E+P+L+D}: B+E+P+L+D).

The final-day specificity increment is (M_{E+P+L+D}) versus (M_{E+P+L}). This is the required same-length late-stay placebo comparison. No claim of causal adjustment is made.

Use participant-grouped 5-fold cross-fitting within the earliest 80% of eligible TACE starts, with the latest 20% as a chronological holdout. Break date ties by the configured participant hash, not by row order. Select ridge penalty only inside training folds from a fixed grid `{10^-4, 10^-3, ..., 10^2}` using training-fold log loss. Estimate standardization parameters, categorical encoding, missingness handling, and any prior-history cutoffs inside training folds. Do not tune on the chronological holdout.

For every nested comparison and outcome, report held-out Brier score, log loss, AUROC only as a secondary discrimination measure, calibration intercept/slope, reliability plots, and the paired participant-level difference in Brier score. Use 1,000 participant-level bootstrap resamples of held-out predictions for 95% intervals, recording the seed and resample count. Report the fitted coefficient table only with non-identifying feature names and fold-specific uncertainty. A likelihood-ratio test may be reported for the corresponding unpenalized nested discovery model as a secondary global test; it cannot override held-out deterioration or an event-adequacy failure.

Require at least 20 all-cause admissions in the analyzed discharge cohort before inferential modeling. Require at least 20 events for each separately modeled outcome. Penalization cannot rescue an inadequate event gate. A small or zero 120-hour cohort makes the specificity estimand inconclusive; it does not authorize lowering the 120-hour rule.

## Prespecified falsification, negative controls, and audits

All checks use the same participant partition and fold-contained preprocessing.

1. **Late-stay placebo.** In the 120-hour cohort compare D with L as specified above. If L adds as much held-out information as D, or the D-versus-L increment has a 95% interval including zero, the final-day-specific interpretation is not supported. If the placebo cohort fails its event or data gate, label final-day specificity inconclusive rather than treating failure as a negative result.

2. **Non-overlapping pre-TACE placebo.** Set a placebo anchor at TACE start minus 28 days. Use placebo baseline [TACE-42 days, TACE-28 days) and placebo early [TACE-28 days, TACE-25 days), with the same assay and paired-window rules, and predict the same post-discharge outcomes. These windows do not overlap the actual baseline, early, discharge, or late-stay placebo windows. A placebo increment comparable to the actual early increment weakens a TACE-timed interpretation. Report its missingness and event adequacy rather than changing the windows.

3. **Timestamp permutation and reversal.** Within participant and assay, retain values, assay labels, and two-key joins but randomly permute valid `检验时间` assignments with a recorded seed for 200 permutations; repeat with chronological order reversed. Recompute windows and all compared metrics. The observed time-ordered increment should exceed the 95th percentile of the permutation increments and should attenuate under reversal. This is a temporal integrity check, not a clinical null experiment.

4. **Process challenge.** Report (M_E) versus (M_{E+P}), and (M_{E+P}) versus (M_{E+P+D}). If P accounts for the apparent D increment, the result is a care-record opportunity signal. Repeat P with (a) laboratory-only counts, (b) orders/medication counts, and (c) the full prespecified P block; these are sensitivity decompositions, not post hoc feature selection. Do not interpret inverse-observation weighting as eliminating informative measurement.

5. **Outcome-specificity challenge.** Run the primary D comparison for (Y_{adm,30}) and (Y_{any,30}) under identical splits and preprocessing. Report standardized Brier-skill increments (Brier improvement divided by the outcome variance) with a bootstrap interval for their difference. A D increment only for (Y_{any,30}) is process/return evidence, not admission-specific evidence. A D increment only for (Y_{liver,30}) is code-specific and requires adjudication; it cannot be called biologic decompensation. If both admission and any-encounter outcomes improve similarly, label the signal mixed rather than biologically specific.

6. **Event-time check.** Report 0–7 versus 8–30 day admission increments descriptively and test whether the direction is consistent in the prespecified chronological holdout. A favorable in-sample result with no held-out event-time consistency is not supportive.

7. **Density/gap sensitivity.** Repeat with a prespecified stricter requirement of at least two distinct valid timestamps per core assay in each required window and a maximum within-window gap of 48 hours when a window has at least two points. Report selection and do not replace the primary complete-case cohort.

8. **Procedure and assay audit.** Repeat only as sensitivity with a narrower TACE-label subset. Preserve the full frozen label dictionary, included/excluded counts, duplicate collapse, parse failures, assay availability, and source provenance. If a label or assay cannot be reproduced, the associated claim is inconclusive.

9. **Leakage and join assertions.** Assert that no lab/order/medication row with time after discharge enters B, E, P, D, or L; no outcome diagnosis row enters predictors; the index encounter is not counted as a return; each source-to-encounter join is exact and does not multiply rows; procedure times satisfy their encounter interval; and all feature windows use their own source time fields. Fail the run if any assertion fails.

## Exact interpretation criteria

### Supportive early-trajectory result

Call the preserved early hypothesis supportive only if all conditions hold for (Y_{adm,30}):

- the all-cause event gate passes;
- (M_E) has a prespecified discovery global likelihood-ratio p-value <0.05 or equivalent nested coefficient test;
- held-out Brier improvement (Brier(M_B)-Brier(M_E)) has a 95% bootstrap interval strictly above zero;
- the latest-20% chronological holdout has the same sign and no calibration reversal;
- the actual early increment exceeds the pre-TACE placebo increment and the 95th-percentile timestamp-permutation increment;
- the increment is not eliminated by the stricter density/gap sensitivity.

This supports a reproducible prognostic increment in this data resource, not biology or treatment benefit.

### Supportive discharge-specific result

Call the discharge-proximal hypothesis supportive only if all conditions hold for (Y_{adm,30}):

- the 96-hour nested cohort passes the all-cause event gate;
- (M_{E+P+D}) improves held-out Brier over (M_{E+P}), with a 95% interval strictly above zero, and does not worsen held-out log loss or calibration;
- the increment has the same sign in the chronological holdout;
- the process-only increment (M_{E+P}) versus (M_E) is reported, and D retains an interval-excluding-zero increment after the full P block;
- in the 120-hour cohort, if its event and data gates pass, D beats the same-length L placebo with a 95% interval for the standardized Brier-skill difference strictly above zero;
- the D increment attenuates under timestamp permutation/reversal and is not reversed by density/gap sensitivity;
- the D increment is present for (Y_{adm,30}) without being confined to (Y_{any,30}). If both outcomes improve similarly, classify the finding as mixed process/return evidence, not discharge-specific biological evidence.

Even when all conditions hold, the highest justified wording is: “a discharge-proximal core-laboratory trajectory provided reproducible incremental prognostic information for an observed subsequent admission conditional on recorded process markers.” It is not “hepatic non-recovery,” “decompensation,” “unsafe discharge,” or “indication for monitoring.”

### Adverse or falsified result

Call the relevant claim adverse/falsified when the held-out increment is absent or reverses in the chronological holdout, the timestamp/placebo increment is comparable or larger, the signal disappears after P, the signal occurs only for (Y_{any,30}), or leakage/join/time assertions fail. A process-only result is not a failed prediction study; it means the data support a care-record opportunity/return signal rather than a residual discharge laboratory signal. A liver-coded-only result is code-specific and cannot support a biological claim.

### Inconclusive result

Call a claim inconclusive when an event/data gate fails, the held-out interval includes zero with materially uncertain calibration, the 120-hour placebo cohort is too sparse, key timestamps or assay labels are not reproducible, the process and temporal controls conflict, or the available snapshot cannot distinguish scheduled from unscheduled care. Do not convert a favorable in-sample p-value into support. Inconclusive means the experiment did not resolve the question, not that no association exists.

## What the experiment can and cannot establish

Harbor can check source paths, table/schema availability, participant partitioning, label dictionaries, exact joins, temporal windows, attrition, process counts, outcome construction, model fitting, held-out metrics, bootstrap uncertainty, placebo/permutation behavior, and whether the conclusion matches those outputs.

Harbor cannot establish that:

- an abnormal laboratory trajectory is true hepatic decompensation or TACE-related injury;
- an admission was unplanned, treatment-related, avoidable, or clinically severe;
- a diagnosis code represents ascites, encephalopathy, variceal bleeding, or decompensation in the clinical sense;
- death did not occur or is not a competing event;
- TACE caused the trajectory or that changing discharge care changes admissions;
- the patient has BCLC stage, tumor burden, vascular invasion, treatment dose/territory, performance status, or treatment intent;
- laboratory values are comparable across assays, because the catalog has no lab unit column;
- outpatient care is completely captured, or the findings transport to another service/system;
- a risk threshold or clinical utility rule is safe.

Clinical documents, examinations, and pathology are available as potential material for a future blinded expert-adjudication sample, but their exact audited limitations matter: `clinical_documents` has narrative fields but no temporal column; `examinations` has `检查`, `检查所见`, `检查诊断`, `开始时间`, and `检查号` but no validated outcome adjudication; `pathology` has narrative fields but no temporal column. They are not used as automatic truth labels here. Vitals, transfers, and front_page cannot fill missing physiology, transfer intent, or staging because their catalog tables contain identifiers only.
